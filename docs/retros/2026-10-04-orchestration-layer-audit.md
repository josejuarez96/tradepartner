# Orchestration layer audit (2026-10-04)

**Date:** 2026-10-04  ·  **Issue:** #769 (refocused brief, second attempt)  ·  **Window:** 2026-09-24 to 2026-10-04  ·  **Scope:** how Jose, the orchestrator, the team windows and their subagents are organised and work. No tooling is proposed; where a change needs a script it is said in one line.

Evidence: 339 PRs and 434 issues read through `gh` (timelines, comment first lines, labels), the ways-of-working pages, ADR 0002, the agent files, the shared team brief, 40 memory notes, and the local transcripts (86 sessions plus 1,072 subagent transcripts), streamed by a script that printed counts only. "Post-cap" below means the 109 PRs merged after #489 landed the two-pass review rule on 2026-10-01.

## Summary: the five biggest structural problems

1. **The orchestrator is a role with no record, and nobody owns the frontier.** Eight sessions acted as orchestrator between 10-01 and 10-04 (6a, e3, 58, 9d, 9c, 03, 1c, 3f), three were live at once on 10-04, and each handover lives in one agent's private memory notes, not in the repo. Teams die with the session that spawned them. The agent-claimable plan frontier is empty, the roadmap's idle-capacity items have no issues, and 36 unclaimed follow-ups fill the gap.
2. **The written merge rule is not the practised one.** Rule 7 says the owner approves every merge; in practice 200 of the last 200 merges were made by orchestrators under a standing approval granted in chat once and re-granted four times. The train, the designed path, has landed nothing: its first build marked every PR ineligible (#764), and the active ruleset's required check did not stop a red "DO NOT MERGE" PR from merging under the owner's admin bypass (#762 → #772).
3. **Reviews repeat more than they catch.** 16% of post-cap (PR, reviewer) pairs ran three to five passes; 20 pairs are `PASS > PASS` (a second pass that changed nothing); a `/code-review` finding on #745 was followed by both reviewers re-posting `PASS` one minute later; 23 verdicts carry no head SHA; only 27 of 109 PRs record a `code-review:` line.
4. **Teams stop without a trace.** Ten claims had to be force-released by the owner, 33 issues were claimed twice or more, and #535 has sat claimed since 10-01 with no PR and no comment. The record of why a team stopped exists only in handover notes.
5. **Six rules in the docs contradict the practice**: subagent depth, who claims, the orchestrator's model, what a "team" is, where the brief lives, and which PRs need the owner's word. New sessions learn the real rules from memory notes and chat corrections.

- **Evidence.** Transcripts: sessions 2edc04a0, 1ce8c928, bf8fd623 (10-04); cc408cad (10-01 16:15 to 10-04 00:49). ### 2. Write the merge policy that is actually practised, and stop re-granting it

- **Current practice.** Rule 7: "The owner approves every merge." Practice since 10-01: the orchestrator merges code PRs that are ready, CI green on the head, verdicts `PASS`, after a trial merge against current `main`; spec, ADR, process and CI PRs wait for Jose's word per PR. Each new orchestrator asks for the approval again.
- **Problem.** The rule and the practice disagree, so every orchestrator learns the real rule from a memory note, and the PRs that wait for the owner are exactly the ones nothing in the docs names. Of the 145 recent merged PRs with a ready event (146 merged; one was never marked ready), seven waited more than eight hours: four spec or process PRs held for the owner's word (#445 71 h, #520 49 h, #528 and #521 25 h) and three code PRs that waited across the owner's pause or a red-`main` day (#601 22 h, #434 12.6 h, #431 12.1 h). Code PRs otherwise merge in a median 0.3 h after ready (p75 1.1 h, 108 of 145 within the hour).
- **Evidence.** 200 of 200 recent merges by the owner's token from orchestrator sessions (272 `gh pr merge` commands across sessions). `standing-merge-approval-9c` (one grant, four re-grants). git-workflow.md rule 7; ADR 0002 amendment; CLAUDE.md non-negotiable 1.
- **Proposed change.** Rule 7 states the two classes as practised: (A) a code, test, docs-in-`docs/research|retros|runbooks` or fragment-only PR that is ready, green on its head and `PASS` on every required verdict may be landed by the orchestrator (by train once item 3 holds, by hand until then); (B) anything under `docs/specs`, `docs/plans`, `docs/decisions`, `docs/ways-of-working`, `.claude/`, `.github/`, `scripts/{ready_pr,merge_train,team}.py`, `pyproject.toml`, `uv.lock`, and any PR labelled `hold`, waits for the owner's word on that PR. The approval becomes the written rule, valid until the owner edits it, instead of a per-session chat grant. **Said plainly: this relaxes git-workflow rule 7 and CLAUDE.md non-negotiable 1 as written, and drops the per-session re-confirmation; it is the owner's decision, made by amending rule 7, non-negotiable 1 and ADR 0002.** It differs from #771's rejected proposal in that no PR lands unattended by a new mechanism and nothing is added to what orchestrators land today; the alternative is to keep rule 7 as written and keep re-granting in chat, which is today's practice.
- **Cost or saving.** One docs PR. Removes the re-grant per orchestrator and the ambiguity that put #445 at 71 h; gives the owner a list of what is his to merge.
- **Decides.** Owner, in so many words (it amends rule 7, non-negotiable 1 and ADR 0002).

### 3. Make the train the path, with what must be true first

- **Current practice.** Spec and ADR say PRs land through the train on "merge train `<id>`"; the orchestrator meanwhile hand-merges after its own per-PR trial merge (`git merge-tree`, a scratch worktree, targeted pytest).
- **Problem.** The train has landed nothing. The first real build (batch `20261004-052201`) marked #745, #755 and #758 `INELIGIBLE` because `fragment_texts` is never filled on the production path (#764), a path only tests had exercised. The AC16 check (#762) added a failing test to prove the ruleset refuses a red PR. The ruleset `protect-main` was active (since 2026-10-04 04:59Z); a plain `gh pr merge` by the owner's admin token at 06:03Z merged it anyway, through the admin pull-request bypass, and turned `main` red (#772). Every token in the repo is the owner's and admin, so the required check binds no one here; rule 7 and the `ask` prompt are the only guard, as git-workflow.md itself says. Meanwhile the orchestrator's trial merges (99 commands over the window; 26 in one session) do by hand what the train does, with no record on the PR.
- **Evidence.** #764, #762, #772, #731 (unresolved-thread eligibility, open owner decision). `gh api .../rulesets`: id 24443727, active, created 2026-10-04. `batch-merge-semantic-conflicts`: `main` red three times on 10-01 from hand-merged batches. Transcripts: `trial_merge_checks` 99.
- **Proposed change.** (a) The train becomes the only class-A path once #764 is merged and one dry build has run green on a real batch; hand merges stay for class B and `main`-red hotfixes. This narrows how class-A PRs land and is the merge-train spec's own intent; it is still an owner decision because it is a spec amendment. (b) A check whose purpose is to be red is run on a branch that is never merged, and never via a PR the owner is asked to merge "to see it refused". AC16 cannot be run as written, since no non-admin token exists here; it is dropped, with a note in the spec that the ruleset is a guard against habit and against a clone without hooks, not against the owner's token. (c) The orchestrator stops the per-PR trial merge when the train is the path; until then it posts one line on the PR saying it was trial-merged at `<main sha>`.
- **Cost or saving.** #764 is in flight (#765). Saves about 15 minutes of orchestrator time per code PR and the 30-minute train run that today runs in addition to, not instead of, the hand checks.
- **Decides.** Owner for (a) and (b) (one spec amendment). Orchestrator for (c), no issue needed.

### 4. Hold the two-pass review rule, and put `/code-review` before the verification pass

- **Current practice.** Pass 1 by each required reviewer, one fix commit, pass 2 verifies the diff since pass 1. `/code-review` once at medium. Verdict comments name the head reviewed.
- **Problem.** Post-cap, 28 of 178 (PR, reviewer) pairs ran three passes or more (#633, #637, #640 five each). 20 pairs are `PASS > PASS`: a verification pass with nothing to verify, usually after a merge of `main` or a `/code-review` fix. On #745 `/code-review` posted a medium finding at 03:35 and both reviewers re-posted `PASS` at 03:36; a one-minute pass is not a read of the fix. 23 of 320 verdicts carry no head SHA, so the next pass cannot tell where to start. Only 27 of 109 PRs carry a `code-review:` line although every brief asks for one. First verdicts are 53% `PASS`, 40% `PASS WITH FIXES`, 7% `FAIL`, so pass 1 does find things; it is the later passes that repeat.
- **Evidence.** gh comment timelines on PRs ≥ 500; #745; agents.md "Review passes" (#489).
- **Proposed change.** agents.md "Review passes" adds three lines: (1) order is reviewers pass 1 and `/code-review` in parallel on one head, one fix commit for all findings, then one verification pass per reviewer over that commit; a `/code-review` fix after the verification pass reopens it. (2) A verdict without `reviewed at <sha>` on its second line does not count, and the window re-posts it with the SHA rather than re-running the reviewer. (3) A pass whose diff since the last verdict is empty on the PR's own files is not run; the window posts "no PR-side change since <sha>" instead.
- **Cost or saving.** None to build. Reviewer runs per merged PR are 2.9 post-cap (320 verdicts on 109 PRs); holding the cap and dropping empty passes brings it toward 2.0, roughly a third of reviewer tokens.
- **Decides.** Owner (agents.md). The orchestrator puts the ordering in the brief now.

- **Current practice.** teams.md: stopping for good means `release` (or `--park`) with a handoff comment. In practice teams are stopped by the owner ("wrap up"), by the 20-subagent limit, by a retired orchestrator, or by a classifier denial, and the state lives in the orchestrator's handover note.
- **Problem.** #535 (ibis) has been claimed since 10-01 with no PR and no comment, although 58's handover says ibis "finished". #585 stalled at `PASS WITH FIXES` for two days before a force-release. #616 was stopped mid-fix with uncommitted work in its directory (9c's note). marknits (#641) was "stopped to free a slot" (03's note). The owner force-released ten claims; 33 issues were claimed twice or more. The good cases exist: edgarscan on #578 and markgap on #678 each posted why they stopped and released.
- **Evidence.** Issue comments on #535, #554, #578, #678; memory `orchestrator-handover-{9c,03,1c}`; 10 "Released by the owner" comments; `claims per issue: 2+ on 33`.
- **Proposed change.** teams.md "End" gets one rule: whoever stops a team posts the stop. If the team is alive it posts done / remaining / gotchas and releases or parks. If the orchestrator stops it (slot limit, owner's "wrap up", rotation) the orchestrator posts the same comment on the issue before its own handover, with the directory name and whether the tree is dirty. A claim with no PR and no comment after one working day is stale and the orchestrator asks the owner to force-release it in the handover.
- **Cost or saving.** One comment per stop. Removes the owner's force-release relay (ten so far) and the re-claim-and-rediscover cost on a third of re-claimed issues.
- **Decides.** Orchestrator (practice) now; owner for the teams.md line.

### 6. Own the frontier: a planning duty on a trigger, not a standing architect agent

The owner asked (2026-10-04) whether an "architect / strategy" agent would help: keep a ready frontier of specced and planned work ahead of the teams, sequence and split issues so teams do not collide on `run.py`, `window.py` or `wrapper.py`, turn open owner questions into decision memos, and advise teams through the plan line they read at claim time.

The depth-1 frontier is seven tasks: three are the owner's (T45b, T48b, T75b), two are gated on owner evidence (T22, T70), two are claimed (T76, T84b). Agent-claimable, unclaimed plan work is zero, and the critical path (T48b → T48c → T67 → T71 → T71b) is owner-gated. Build capacity has gone to the follow-up stream instead: 54 follow-up issues filed in nine days, 36 unclaimed size:S issues open today. None of the roadmap's three idle-capacity items has an issue (only #658, Phase 6 tax policy, exists, as an owner decision). So the returns on future work the owner is asking about are not being prepared by anyone.
- **Is sequencing a bottleneck? Smaller, and already tooled.** Recorded waits on another team's file are rare on the issues (2 comments, #517 and #644) and a handful more in handover notes (#678 markgap blocked on `run.py` held by runfollow; #502 held behind #511's `window.py`; #519 behind #502; #707 behind a spec amendment). ### 10. Fewer follow-up issues; fix the small things in the PR

- **Current practice.** agents.md: non-blocking findings go into one `size:S` follow-up issue per PR, filed before ready. Split a PR above about 400 changed lines.
- **Problem.** 54 issues titled "follow-ups from PR #N's reviews" were filed in nine days (13 open). Each becomes a team, two reviewer runs, a `ready_pr` run and a 30 to 45 minute CI run to land a handful of NITs. 44 of 146 recent merged PRs changed more than 400 lines, which is where the multi-pass reviews and the follow-ups cluster (#633, #637, #640, #736). Commits per PR are a median of 4.
- **Evidence.** `gh issue list --search follow-ups` (54, 13 open); diff sizes from the PR data; the 3+ pass list in item 4.
- **Proposed change.** agents.md "Review passes" rule 2: a NIT or follow-up that touches only the PR's own files and needs no decision is fixed in the one fix commit; an issue is filed only for a finding that needs an owner decision, touches files outside the PR, or is sized above S. development-process.md: a plan line whose Files list implies more than 400 lines is split at planning, not at review.
- **Cost or saving.** None. Roughly halves the follow-up issue stream and the teams it spawns.
- **Decides.** Owner (agents.md, development-process.md).

## The seven questions, answered

**1. Structure.** Orchestrator → team → reviewers is the right depth; the evidence is that it already works that way (499 depth-2 spawns, one or two sessions a day since 10-02 instead of seventeen). What is wrong is the orchestrator's lifecycle: several at once, handovers in private memory, teams orphaned on rotation (item 1). The frontier has no owner once the plan is consumed, which is now; a planning team on a trigger, not a standing architect agent, fills that (item 6).

**2. Briefs and handoffs.** Teams get the claim step wrong because the docs and the brief disagree (item 7). Empty background notices come from reviewers and `/code-review` finders handing back to the orchestrator (item 7). Teams stop without a record because nobody owns the stop comment when the orchestrator, not the team, does the stopping (item 5).

**3. Gates and review passes.** Pass 1 catches real defects (47% of first verdicts are `PASS WITH FIXES` or `FAIL`). Passes three to five repeat; `PASS > PASS` pairs and one-minute re-verifications are the waste; `/code-review` is under-recorded and its fixes bypass the verification pass. A ready code PR waits a median 0.3 h; the waits are on class-B PRs and on the orchestrator's duplicate trial merges. Items 4, 3.

**4. Merge path.** The hand merge under a chat-granted standing approval is the real path; the train is the designed one and has landed nothing. The ruleset does not bind the owner's token. Write the policy as practised (item 2), then make the train the class-A path once #764 is in and one dry batch has run (item 3).

**5. Decision flow.** Decisions are asked on issues, answered in chat, written back by the orchestrator and repeated in handovers. Two kinds can be rules: follow-up sizing and choices the spec already settles; a third, spec-wording PRs that record a decision already made, is a named owner decision. Item 8.

**6. Cost and time.** 98% of tokens are cache reads; 10-01's window-per-team shape cost five times the daily reads of the subagent shape. Fable ran the orchestrator for four days. Reviewer runs are 2.9 per merged PR post-cap. 54 follow-up issues each cost a team and a CI run. Items 9, 7, 4, 10.

**7. Rules that drifted.** (a) "A subagent cannot spawn subagents": 499 did. (b) "Subagents never claim": the brief tells teams to. (c) Orchestrator on Opus: it ran on Fable for four days. (d) Team = a window: teams are subagents. (e) The brief lives in a scratchpad, copied by hand. (f) "Process PRs need the owner's word" is practised but unwritten; rule 7 as written says every PR does. Items 7, 2, 9.

**The owner's addition (architect / strategy agent).** Real bottleneck in one half: the plan frontier is empty for agents and the roadmap's idle-capacity items have no issues, while 36 unclaimed follow-ups absorb the teams. Not a bottleneck in the other half: shared-file collisions are few and already tooled by `team.py graph`. ## Method and caveats

PR metrics come from the GitHub timeline (`ReadyForReviewEvent` to `MergedEvent`) and comment first lines on PRs ≥ 430 (146 merged) and ≥ 500 (109 merged, post-cap). Verdict passes are counted per reviewer per PR from comments whose first line starts `<agent>:`; a verdict posted by hand by a window counts the same. Transcript numbers are counts of record types, model fields and tool-call names from the 86 top-level session files and 1,072 subagent files; no transcript text was read or copied. The owner phrases quoted come from the memory notes, which live outside the repo, and from issue comments. Session roles were inferred from merge and spawn counts, not from a label, so "eight orchestrators" is the set of sessions that both merged and spawned teams. Wake-up counts include subagent completion notices, so they are upper bounds. Nothing here was measured on a window with the train live, because no train has merged yet.
