# Plan: Research labeling, pilot A (departure reason), streamlined

**Spec:** [specs/research-labeling.md](../specs/research-labeling.md) (Accepted 2026-10-05, #964; amended 2026-10-06, #1015: the section "Amendment 2026-10-06 (#1015)" governs where it differs from the body, and this plan is written to it)  ·  **Status:** Draft (Approved only once the owner has answered the amendment's seven questions on #1015 and their answers are folded into the task lines below, because questions 3 to 7 decide what T119, T121b, T123 and T123b build)  ·  **Issue:** #1015  ·  **ADR:** [0013](../decisions/0013-research-measurement-boundary.md)

## Approach (short)

**Validated first, then planned.** A read-only sample test against the real vendor ran before this plan (two runs of 308 calls at the pinned `jev-1.13.0`, $0.036; the amendment's evidence lines E1 to E14; #1016 commits the summaries). It settled the spec's load-bearing assumptions: the population is about 11,150 listing ends, not 5,000 (E1); the whole pilot costs about $1 at list price against a $40 ceiling (E2, C1); the Form 25's own notice exhibit resolves 82% of listing ends and the nearest 8-K lifts coverage to 95% (E3, E4), so the passage is one notice plus at most one 8-K, the 8-K read first where one exists and the notice alone otherwise (C2, the rule the evidence scored); answers are stable on repeat and under a reversed option order (E6), so the drift probe is ADR 0013 point 5's at its smallest, twenty fixed packets re-sent before each batch as a `robustness` run beside the model-id stop (C5); the probabilities sit at 1.0, so calibration stays a reported number and nothing more (E7, C6); and the final answer was right on 15 of 16 cases an agent labelled blind (E10). The build is therefore **nine build tasks of size M in total**, not the body's fifteen: the boundary tests first and alone (T118, ADR 0013 point 3 and req 1); then the config, secret, client and the one scripted double (T119) and the option set with its crosswalk (T121) beside each other; then, beside each other, the EDGAR corpus fetch outside `tradepartner.research` (T120), the packets and the scoring (T121b) and the frame build that computes the rule answer at a recorded `t` (T122); then the job (T123) and the two sheets (T123b); then the CLI and the three experiment files, the terms line, the charter sentence and the roadmap item (T124, class B). The owner's items are two tasks at the edge: the keys and the seven recordings (T125), then the real corpus, frame, gold, pilot and first batch (T126).

**What the pilot measures that the spike could not.** The spike never touched the owner's store. The pilot's actual output is the **disagreement rate between the model's label and the master's rule answer** (the crosswalk of spec req 5) over the whole population, and the shortlist it yields: that needs the rule answer computed from the owner's store at a fixed `t` (T122's frame build, run by the owner after T45b's real-store run per ADR 0013 point 9's roadmap line), then one `full` batch (T126). The gold score (150 blind labels, the Wilson bars of owner decisions 3 and 5) stays the gate before that batch (ADR 0013 point 5); E10 suggests it will pass but is sixteen items chosen for disagreement and labelled by an agent, not a score. The spike's 150 sampled accessions are excluded from the gold draw so that "before any model output exists" holds for every scored item (C10, owner question 7).

**Numbers that matter for the build.** The 4-characters-per-token estimate ran 1.5 to 1.7× low (E9): `chars_per_token` defaults to 2.5 and the pre-call estimate is replaced by the returned usage per record, so the mid-batch spend stop (C4) does not fire late. One whole-population batch under C2 is about 7,250 B calls (the 65% with an 8-K) plus about 4,300 A calls (the rest, and B's `unresolved`), about 11,500 calls, about 1.6 hours at `requests_per_second = 2.0` (E8: p50 latency 175 ms, so the pace is ours, not the vendor's), about $0.85; the corpus fetch is about 35,000 EDGAR requests, about an hour at the configured `edgar.requests_per_second` (default 9; the spike paced at 4, about 2.5 hours), once, then cached (E13, C11). Nothing here schedules anything (ADR 0013 point 8).

**Boundary by construction, then by test.** `tradepartner.corpus` imports the EDGAR adapter and nothing from `tradepartner.research`; `tradepartner.research.labeling` reads the corpus file by path and the runtime store through read-only readers only, in `frame.py` alone; `tradepartner.research.models` is the one module that names the vendor host and the key and is imported only by `job.py`; the review sheet is written by the review module and read back by it, and nothing writes the review file but `finish`. T118's tests hold all of it (reqs 12 to 16 as C13 amends them); (d) and (e) land skipped until the modules exist: T119 removes (e)'s `importorskip`, and T123 and T123b each remove (d)'s `importorskip` of the module they land, so whichever of the two lands second is the one that makes test (d) run and must leave it green (its assertions are T118's and do not change; a call-signature edit to match the landed entry points is allowed and named in that PR) (plan-shape rule 4's declared stub).

**No new dependency** (`httpx`, `polars` and DuckDB are runtime dependencies already; owner decision 2: no SDK, no `research` group), **no schema migration** (datasets and runs live in T80's tables through T81's API), **no env var but the three the spec names** (`TYPESAFE_API_KEY`, `RESEARCH__SPEND_CEILING_USD_MONTH`, `RESEARCH__SPEND_CEILING_USD_TOTAL`, all absent or `0.0` by default; the real client cannot be constructed without them), **nothing under `execution/`**. Agents build and test on fixture stores, the scripted double and temporary research directories only; every run they open is `synthetic`. The seven recorded responses under `tests/fixtures/typesafe/` are copied by the owner from the spike's `calls.jsonl` (C9), never written by an agent.
