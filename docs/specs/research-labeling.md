## Problem & why now

ADR 0013 (Accepted 2026-10-05) lets a pretrained **classification** model label a code-selected passage of a public filing with one option from a set we fix, so that a job can compare the label with what our rules concluded and hand the owner a shortlist of disagreements. The label lives only in the research store; the owner reads the filing and decides; a fix enters the system only as a reviewed code or config change. The ADR names this spec at `docs/specs/research-labeling.md`, makes its existence with status Accepted the key that its tests turn on (point 3 (a)), and says nothing in points 1 to 9 is built before it (point 9, Process).

The owner chose the first pilot: **task A, the security-master departure reason** (owner decision 3). The pain is current. The security master's rules are patched one filing at a time, each fix starting with a person reading a Form 25 or an 8-K: #921 (Valaris: a footnote alias kept a Form-25-delisted span alive; a repair would have deleted 1,298 post-bankruptcy bars), #943 (a repair dry run would delete live Seagate, 1,714 bars, and MiMedx, 1,484 bars, because the rules read their redomicile and relisting filings wrong), #905 (Sotheby's cover page after a stopped line), #828 and #922 (Match Group's 2020 separation, an owner-accepted successor the rules no longer derive). A model that reads the filings and says "redomicile", "bankruptcy" or "compliance delisting" fixes none of these; it turns the pile of every listing end into a shortlist of the ones where the filing and the rule disagree.

Two things make this the right first pilot and shape the spec. The population is small (every Form 25 since 2016, a few thousand filings; the corpus fetch prints the exact count), so the whole population is labelled and the pilot's cost is cents, not dollars. And **nothing in the system holds the filing text**: ingest parses the Form 25 notification and deletes the document (`edgar_source._fetch_delisting`), stores no accession on the `delistings` row, and reads no 8-K body; the event-data collector fetches no 8-K primary document in v1. The pilot therefore starts with a corpus build, not a model call (ADR 0013 Context, "What is already fixed", last bullet; owner question 3 there).

**Why now.** The vendor credit the owner holds ($20, owner decision 2) is the pilot's whole budget, and promotional credits are "subject to any additional terms ... including terms with respect to expiration" (vendor MCA, Data / interfaces); the owner's store has been through three repair rounds this week, each a hand-read of filings; and ADR 0013 point 3 says the ADR 0008 point 2 tests, still unwritten, land with this plan's first task, so the boundary becomes a test before any client exists.

## Users & usage

- **The owner** runs everything that touches the real corpus, the real vendor or his store: the corpus fetch (EDGAR, paced), the gold labelling session (about 300 filings, blind, before any model output exists), the pilot run, every later batch, the review of each shortlist, and the recording of the test fixtures (his key). He sees: a labelling screen for gold labels; a review screen per shortlist item (the passage, two unattributed answers, the filing's EDGAR URL); the registry rows on the dashboard's research view (counts, metrics, cost; never a label or a passage). He spends about 10 to 20 hours on the gold sample (ADR 0013 point 5) and then minutes per shortlist item.
- **Agents** build and test against fixture stores, hand-built packets and a recorded-response fake. They never hold the key, never call the vendor, never run on the owner's store; every run they open is `synthetic` (research-registry Definitions). A fix the shortlist motivates is an ordinary PR a team builds under its own issue, with a pinned test, citing the review file's hash and the filing's accession (ADR 0013 point 4).
- **The research registry** records every run (`benchmark` for the pilot scoring and for every batch with its review, `robustness` for the drift and perturbation probes), every dataset version (corpus, frame and gold sample, inference records, review file) and every holdout spend (the sealed `pilot` period, once).
- **Nothing else.** The running system (ingest, paper, dashboard, alerts) makes no model call and reads no label. `tradepartner.execution` and the dashboard never import `tradepartner.research` (ADR 0013 point 3 (b)).

## Definitions

- **Listing end**: a `delistings` row in the runtime store (a Form 25 or 25-NSE, `known_at` = its acceptance), resolved to a `security_id` and an exchange as `store.delistings` does. The population of task A is every listing end whose Form 25 was accepted on or after 2016-01-01 and whose primary document is the XML `notificationOfRemoval` (pre-XML filings are counted and excluded, as ingest's `pre_xml_delistings` counts them).
- **Passage** (the **packet**'s text): what code selects for one listing end (req 3): the Form 25 notification rendered as labelled fields, and the body items and first press-release exhibit of the issuer's 8-K, 8-K/A and 8-K12B filings accepted in a fixed calendar-day window around the Form 25's filing date, truncated by a fixed rule to `research.labeling.max_packet_tokens`.
- **Option set**: the closed set of answers the model may give (req 4), always including `unresolved`, versioned as an object with a hash (prospective spec §3, `prompt_version`).
- **Rule answer**: what the master and resolver concluded for the listing end, computed by code from the runtime store at a fixed as-of time and `code_version` (req 5): the derived status `listed` / `delisted` / `transferred` (`store.delistings.listing_ends_as_of`), whether the same security relisted after the Form 25's effective day, whether a successor security (`<cik>@<date>`, `config.parse_successor_id`) was created near it, and whether a Form 15 fell inside `master.reorganisation_window_sessions` of it.
- **Crosswalk**: the registered map from each rule answer onto the subset of options it is consistent with (req 5). A **disagreement** is a label outside that subset; `unresolved` is neither agreement nor disagreement and is always on the shortlist.
- **Shortlist**: for one batch, every disagreement, every `unresolved`, and a seeded random sample of the agreements (ADR 0013 point 4).
- **Gold label**: the owner's label for a sampled listing end, made from the filing text alone, before any model output exists for the task, with the passage he relied on (protocol §6, §7).
- **Inference record**: one append-only record per (listing end, configuration, attempt) in the research store (req 7): the full request and response, the selected option, the probability vector, the vendor confidence (stored separately), the requested and returned model id, usage and cost, and `known_at`.
- **Review record**: one append-only record per shortlist item the owner decided (req 9), in the review file in the research store (owner decision 4 (a)).
- **Research store**: `research.data_dir` (default `data/research/`, gitignored by `data/*`), the content-addressed directory #810 chose. Every dataset the registry points at lives under it; nothing under it is a fact.
- **Pilot credit**: the owner's $20 vendor credit. `research.spend_ceiling_usd_total` (req 8) is the config form of it.

## Requirements

1. **Nothing before the boundary tests.** The plan's first task writes, in one test module keyed on the two spec paths, (i) ADR 0008 point 2's tests keyed on `docs/specs/llm-analyst.md` (no `tradepartner.llm` package, no LLM SDK in `project.dependencies`, no import of `tradepartner.llm` outside it) and (ii) ADR 0013 point 3 (a) to (e) keyed on this spec, as specified in reqs 12 to 16. They merge before any other task of the plan, so every later PR is checked by them.

2. **Corpus build: fetch outside `tradepartner.research`, export inside it, registered as a dataset.** (#956 item 3.)
   - **Fetch** (`tradepartner corpus fetch departure-reason [--since 2016-01-01] [--until <date>] [--limit n]`, module `tradepartner.corpus.departure_fetch`, outside `tradepartner.research` because it imports the EDGAR adapter): reads the listing ends from the runtime store read-only, through `store.delistings.delistings_as_of` at `t = now`, with one short-lived connection closed before the first request (research-registry req 4's pattern); finds each Form 25's accession and the issuer's 8-K, 8-K/A and 8-K12B accessions in the window `[filing date − research.labeling.context_before_days, filing date + research.labeling.context_after_days]` (calendar days; a filing window, not a session window) from the quarterly `form.idx` through `edgar_raw.filing_index_quarter`, and each filing's acceptance time and `primaryDocument` from the submissions JSON as the event-data collector does (req 2 (a) there); downloads the Form 25 primary document, each 8-K's primary document and its first `EX-99.1` exhibit through `edgar_raw.download_filing_file` into `edgar.cache_dir/{cik}/{accession}/{filename}`, kept (unlike `_fetch_delisting`, which deletes), at the existing paced rate (`edgar.requests_per_second`, the cross-process limiter once the event-data plan lands it; free-data terms 7a: at most 10 per second), with the existing User-Agent; a file already cached is not requested again. It writes a **manifest** `edgar.cache_dir/corpus/departure-reason/<build_id>/manifest.json`: one entry per listing end with `security_id`, exchange, the Form 25's accession, form, acceptance and effective day, and per document its accession, form, acceptance, `items` string, local path and the SHA-256 of the raw bytes; plus the counts the frame needs (listing ends found, pre-XML excluded, documents fetched, documents missing with the reason). A document EDGAR does not serve is recorded as missing, never retried past `edgar`'s retry policy, and the listing end stays in the frame (protocol §4.5: a failure is counted, not screened out). The fetch imports nothing from `tradepartner.research` and writes nothing under `research.data_dir`.
   - **Export** (`tradepartner research corpus export departure-reason --manifest <path>`, module `tradepartner.research.corpus`, which imports no adapter: it reads the manifest's local paths): extracts text deterministically with a versioned extractor (`corpus_version`; HTML to text, whitespace and entity normalisation, 8-K item segmentation on the `Item N.NN` headings, character offsets on the normalised text whose SHA-256 is recorded), renders the Form 25 notification as labelled fields (`issuer`, `exchange`, `class`, `rule_provision`, `signature_date`), and writes a **directory dataset** under `research.data_dir/corpus/departure-reason/<sha256>/`: one JSON file per listing end holding the documents' normalised texts, items, offsets and hashes, and one `frame.parquet` (or CSV) **tabular file** with one row per listing end: `security_id`, `cik`, `exchange`, `form25_accession`, `form25_accepted_at` (the event column), `effective_on`, `rule_provision`, document counts, and `pre_xml` / `missing_documents` flags. The command prints the `tradepartner dataset register` invocation for the export with `--event-column form25_accepted_at` and the event span it computed, or runs it with `--register`. A rebuilt corpus is a new version (research-registry req 11); nothing edits an export in place.
   - **Survivorship.** The frame is the delisted set by construction; the check that matters is that every listing end in the store is in the frame or counted out with a reason, so the export's row count plus its exclusion counts equals the `delistings` row count at `t`. `quant-auditor` reviews the fetch and the export (frame code; data-foundation domain rules).

3. **Packet builder: pure, from the corpus dataset only.** `tradepartner.research.labeling.packets.build_packet(record, option_set, limits) -> Packet` takes one listing end's corpus record and returns the `state` string and its metadata, deterministically: the Form 25 fields first (with the filing date and the effective day written out in words and ISO form, so the model is not asked to compare dates: TC §4 and the vendor's documented weakness on "Date and time comparison"); then, in fixed priority order, the 8-K12B body, the 8-K items in `research.labeling.eightk_items` (default `["3.01", "2.01", "1.01", "5.03", "3.03", "8.01"]`, in that order), then the first `EX-99.1` of each 8-K, each document labelled with its form, accession and acceptance date; the whole truncated at `research.labeling.max_packet_tokens` (default 8000; a token is estimated as 4 characters before the call, replaced by the vendor's `usage.input_tokens` after it), by dropping whole documents from the lowest priority up, then truncating the last kept document at a sentence boundary; what was dropped and where the cut fell are in the packet's metadata. The text is wrapped between fixed delimiters and labelled as filing text; the question text and the option set are never inside the delimiters. **The builder contains no table name** from `TABLE_NAMES`, `JOURNAL_TABLE_NAMES`, `MASTER_CHECK_TABLE_NAMES`, `REGISTRY_TABLE_NAMES` or `RESEARCH_TABLE_NAMES`, no gold- or review-dataset name, and does not know the rule answer (that is req 5's module), so the ADR 0013 point 3 (c) allowlist for it is empty (#956 item 5). The packet carries nothing but the passage, the filing identifiers and the question: no position, order, decision, price, hypothesis, gold label or review decision (ADR 0013 point 6), and the test in req 14 holds it to that by construction (its only input is the corpus record).

4. **The question and the option set, versioned.** One `choice` question per packet, as a versioned object `tradepartner.research.labeling.questions.OptionSet` with `version`, `instructions`, an **ordered** list of `(option, description)` pairs and a SHA-256 over all of it (the `prompt_version` of prospective spec §3). The pilot's proposed set, frozen at registration (owner question 1):

   | Option | Description the model sees (abridged here; the full text is the versioned object) |
   |---|---|
   | `merger_or_acquisition` | the company was acquired by or merged into another company; holders received cash or the other company's securities |
   | `going_private` | the company was bought out by insiders, a sponsor or through a cash-out reverse split and stopped being publicly traded, without a separate acquirer absorbing it |
   | `redomicile_or_reorganisation` | the same business continued under a new holding company, domicile, legal form or a separation, and holders of the old class hold the new one |
   | `bankruptcy` | the class was cancelled, or trading ended, because of a bankruptcy or insolvency proceeding |
   | `compliance_delisting` | the exchange removed the class for failing a listing standard (bid price, market value, filing delinquency, other), not because of any of the above |
   | `exchange_transfer` | the class moved its listing to another national securities exchange and kept trading |
   | `voluntary_withdrawal` | the issuer withdrew the class from the exchange by its own decision, to trade over the counter or to deregister, with none of the above as the reason |
   | `instrument_retirement` | the instrument reached maturity, was redeemed, called, converted or expired (notes, warrants, units, rights, a SPAC trust liquidation) |
   | `unresolved` | the filings shown do not state the reason |

   Nine options, two more than ADR 0013's table row A (`exchange_transfer` and `voluntary_withdrawal` split "voluntary delisting or exchange transfer", because the resolver treats them differently; `instrument_retirement` replaces "other", because a closed set without "other" forces `unresolved` rather than a bucket). The instructions name the task in one sentence, say the reason must be stated in the text shown, and say to choose `unresolved` otherwise. Option order is fixed for a version; the perturbation probe (req 11) permutes it. Changing one word is a new version and a new registration (ADR 0013 point 5: never the bars after the fact).

5. **Rule answer and crosswalk, in their own module.** `tradepartner.research.labeling.rule_answer` computes the rule answer of Definitions for each listing end from the runtime store through read-only store readers (`store.delistings.listing_ends_as_of`, `store.master` and `store.asof` readers, `config.parse_successor_id` on `securities` ids), at an as-of time `t` fixed in the run's `config_json` (the configuration freeze date for the pilot) and recorded with `code_version`; it imports no adapter and no writer and is **not** a packet builder (req 3). The **crosswalk** is a registered table from rule answer to the consistent options:

   | Rule answer (status, relisted same security, successor created, Form 15 in window) | Consistent options |
   |---|---|
   | `transferred` | `exchange_transfer` |
   | `delisted`, same security relisted after the effective day (the #820 relisting rule: CMPR, CG, WELL, Seagate 2010 and 2021) | `redomicile_or_reorganisation` |
   | `delisted`, a successor security created (the #820 new-equity rule; owner-accepted MTCH) | `bankruptcy`, `redomicile_or_reorganisation` |
   | `delisted`, Form 15 in the reorganisation window, no relisting, no successor | `merger_or_acquisition`, `going_private`, `redomicile_or_reorganisation`, `instrument_retirement` |
   | `delisted`, none of the above | `merger_or_acquisition`, `going_private`, `compliance_delisting`, `voluntary_withdrawal`, `instrument_retirement`, `bankruptcy` |
   | `listed` after the effective day (the line the rule did not end: #921, #943) | none: a disagreement by construction (ADR 0013 table row A) |

   This refines the partition ADR 0013's table sketched ("successor-bearing" there grouped merger and going private with redomicile; the master's rules put a Form 15 after a merger and a successor only after new equity, so the sets above follow the code). The table is registered in the experiment file and hashed into `config_json`; changing it is an amendment. A **deterministic comparison arm** is registered beside the model: the Form 25's `rule_provision` mapped by code (12d2-2(a)(1), (a)(2), (a)(4) to `instrument_retirement`; (a)(3) to {`merger_or_acquisition`, `going_private`, `redomicile_or_reorganisation`, `bankruptcy`}; (b) to {`compliance_delisting`, `bankruptcy`}; (c) to {`exchange_transfer`, `voluntary_withdrawal`}), scored on the same gold sample, so the pilot answers whether reading the 8-K adds anything to a field code already has (TC §9; the backlog's B8 question on our own data). Where the provision alone is a set, the arm's "label" is the set and it is scored as covering or not covering the gold label.

6. **The labeling job: handle first, estimate, refuse or call, record, register.** `tradepartner research label <slug> --dataset <corpus dataset id> --split <split> [--run <id>] [--gold <dataset id>] [--configurations 1]` (module `tradepartner.research.labeling.job`):
   1. opens the run through `research.open_run` (or attaches with `--run`), kind `benchmark`, before anything else (research-registry req 3); a `pilot` split bound to the sealed period needs `--spend-holdout --holdout-reason` on the open and is a spend, once; a second scoring of the sealed period is a flagged repeat (#956 item 6; research-registry req 5);
   2. runs the drift probe of req 11 as its own `robustness` run when a probe set is registered for the slug, and closes this run `failed` with `drift probe failed` before any labeling call if the probe's verdict is `fail`;
   3. loads the corpus through `research.load_dataset(handle)` (the one reader), builds every packet (req 3), and **estimates** the batch: Σ packet token estimates × `research.labeling.price_usd_per_million_input_tokens` × (1 + `research.labeling.estimate_margin`, default 0.25); refuses the batch (closes `failed`, message `spend ceiling`) when month-to-date spend + estimate > `research.spend_ceiling_usd_month`, or cumulative spend + estimate > `research.spend_ceiling_usd_total`, or estimate > `research.labeling.batch_max_usd` (default 2.0), where month-to-date and cumulative spend are **computed from the inference records** in the research store, never configured (ADR 0013 point 7 and owner decision 2); the refusal is a row, with the estimate in its message;
   4. calls the model once per packet through `tradepartner.research.models` (req 7) at `research.labeling.requests_per_second` (default 2.0), writing one inference record per attempt as it goes; stops the batch (`failed`, `model id mismatch`) on the first response whose `model` differs from the requested id, and (`failed`, `runaway spend`) when actual billed tokens so far exceed twice the running estimate for the packets sent;
   5. registers the inference records as a dataset version (`departure-reason-inferences`, event column the Form 25 acceptance) and, with `--gold`, scores against the gold dataset (req 10) and writes the result; without `--gold` (a production batch), computes the shortlist (req 9) and leaves the run **unfinished** for the review command to finish.
   A batch is **never** scheduled: there is no timer, no launchd entry, no `collect` source; the command is run by hand (ADR 0013 point 8).

7. **The model client: plain `httpx`, pinned version, everything recorded.** `tradepartner.research.models` is the only module that names the vendor's host (`research.labeling.api_base_url`, default `https://api.typesafe.ai/v1`, declared in `config.py`) and the key's variable name (`TYPESAFE_API_KEY`, read by `config.Settings.typesafe_api_key: SecretStr | None`, in the `secret_values` redaction set `cli_record` and `clean_message` use). **Decision: no SDK for the pilot** (#956 item 1). The call is one `POST {base}/systemone` with `Authorization: Bearer <key>` and the body `{"state": <packet>, "model": <pinned id>, "questions": {"departure_reason": {"type": "choice", "instructions": ..., "criteria": {<option>: <description>}}}}`, over `httpx` (already a runtime dependency), with a timeout `research.labeling.request_timeout_seconds` (default 60), at most `research.labeling.max_attempts` (default 3) attempts per packet, retrying only HTTP 429 and 529 (and a connection error before any byte was sent) with exponential backoff that honours `Retry-After`, never a timeout after the request was sent (it may have been billed; the record says `timeout` and the packet is skipped, counted, and never silently re-sent). Reasons for `httpx` over `typesafe-sdk`: the vendor's SDK selects the model at client construction and its quick start pins the moving alias (`TypeSafeClient(model="jev")`), where the HTTP body takes a per-request `model`; the SDK retries 408 and every 5xx by default (`RetryPolicy`, 2 retries, 0.5 s to 5 s), which re-sends a possibly billed request; an optional dependency group would need a guarded import or a CI-only extra for `mypy --strict` and the import scan; and ADR 0013 test (c) is written for exactly this shape. The `research` dependency group is therefore **not created** for the pilot; test (a) still holds the forbidden list (req 12). If a later spec adopts the SDK, it lives in that group, is imported only here, and CI installs the group for the type check only.
   - **Pinned model.** `model` is the versioned id from the registration's `config_json` (`jev-1.13.0` at the time of writing; the vendor's Models page lists `jev-latest` and `jev-preview` as aliases that "can shift when releases ship"). The client refuses to send an alias (`jev-latest`, `jev-preview`, `jev`) and any id not matching `^jev-\d+\.\d+\.\d+$`.
   - **Per-call record** (ADR 0013 point 5): `record_id`, `run_id`, `security_id`, `form25_accession`, `document_ids` (accessions) and `passage_sha256`, `option_set_version` and hash, `corpus_version`, `model_id_requested`, `model_id_returned` (the response's `model`), `client_version` (this module's version constant; no SDK), `attempt`, `request_sha256`, `raw_request`, `raw_response`, `selected_option` (`answers.departure_reason.choice`), `probabilities` (the full map, which the vendor states sums to 1), `vendor_confidence` (stored separately, never substituted: TC §5, `(p_max − 1/K)/(1 − 1/K)` for K options), `input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `cost_usd` (= `input_tokens` × the price snapshot; the API returns no billed cost, so the list price in `research.labeling.price_usd_per_million_input_tokens`, default `0.042`, is the snapshot, with its date in `price_snapshot`), `http_status`, `latency_ms`, `inference_started_at`, `inference_completed_at`, `known_at` (= `inference_completed_at`, UTC, tz-aware; never back-stamped), `source_available_at` (the Form 25's acceptance). Written append-only as JSONL under `research.data_dir/inferences/departure-reason/<run_id>.jsonl` by `tradepartner.research.datafiles` (the one module that resolves `research.data_dir`, besides `research.load_dataset`).
   - **Zero default and the fake** (ADR 0013 point 3 (e)): the real client is constructed only when `research.spend_ceiling_usd_month > 0`, `research.spend_ceiling_usd_total > 0` and the key is present; the defaults are `0.0`, `0.0` and `None` in code, and with them `tradepartner.research.models` cannot open a connection (a test monkeypatches `httpx.Client` and asserts it is never called). The module takes a `ModelClient` protocol; tests inject `RecordedModelClient` from `tests/research/fake_model_client.py`, which replays `tests/fixtures/typesafe/<name>.json` by `request_sha256` and raises on a request it has no recording for. **Recording is an owner task** (#956 item 2): `tradepartner record typesafe --packets tests/fixtures/typesafe/packets/` (a new source in `cli_record`, beside the EDGAR and Alpaca recorders) sends the hand-built fixture packets with his key, scrubs the responses (no header is stored; the key never appears in a response body; `cli_record`'s scrub patterns run anyway) and writes the fixtures; agents never write a fixture a recording should produce (ADR 0008 point 3, last sentence). The fixture packets are hand-built from `tests/fixtures/edgar/` (the KLX Energy 25-NSE, `rights`, `12d2-2(a)(2)`) and six short synthetic 8-K passages, one per non-`unresolved` option group, under 1,000 tokens each, so the recording costs under a cent.
   - **Config of the client** is settings, never a registration field: `research.labeling.api_base_url`, `request_timeout_seconds`, `max_attempts`, `requests_per_second`, `price_usd_per_million_input_tokens`. What the model is asked (`model`, option set, packet limits) is registration (`config_json`), so a run is reproducible from its row and the research store without the settings file.

8. **Spend: two ceilings from the owner's `.env`, spend computed from records.** `research.spend_ceiling_usd_month` (ADR 0013 point 7) and `research.spend_ceiling_usd_total` (the pilot credit, owner decision 2) are both `0.0` by default in code; the owner sets `RESEARCH__SPEND_CEILING_USD_MONTH` and `RESEARCH__SPEND_CEILING_USD_TOTAL` in `.env` (recommended `10` and `20`, owner question 4). `tradepartner research spend [--month YYYY-MM]` prints month-to-date and cumulative spend from the inference records, by run, with the count of records, and the headroom under both ceilings; it reads the research store only. A PR that changes either default or the "cannot connect at zero" test is labelled `hold` (ADR 0013 point 3 (e)). The owner records actual billing on #946 after the first batch and, each month, the research spend beside the running system's $0 (ADR 0005). **Cost estimate for pilot A** (list price $0.042 per million input tokens, output free, vendor Models page read 2026-10-05; billing unverified until the first invoice): the population is a few thousand listing ends (ADR 0013 point 7; the fetch prints the count); at the 8,000-token packet cap, 5,000 listing ends are 40 M tokens, **$1.68**, and typical packets (a Form 25 of a few hundred tokens plus one or two 8-K items) will come in well under the cap; the 300-item gold sample is at most 2.4 M tokens, **$0.10**, per configuration; four dev configurations on 60 items, **$0.08**; the perturbation probe (five option orders over the 300) **$0.50**; a 50-packet drift probe before each of twelve later batches **$0.20**; the fixture recording under $0.01. The pilot as registered is therefore **under $3**, about 15% of the credit; the refusal rules in req 6 (two ceilings, a $2 batch cap, the 2× runaway stop) are what actually protect the credit against a repricing, a loop bug or a mis-sized packet, and a 5× misestimate still fits. The corpus is the whole population, so no sampling is needed to fit the budget; sampling in req 10 serves the gold labels, not the cost.

9. **The human path: shortlist, blind review, review file, fix by PR.** For a batch run, `tradepartner.research.labeling.crosswalk.shortlist(inferences, rule_answers, crosswalk, seed, agreement_sample_size)` returns every disagreement, every `unresolved`, and `agreement_sample_size` agreements drawn under the seed (default 30, registered), each with its stratum (`disagreement`, `unresolved`, `agreement_sample`) and the agreement sampling rate. `tradepartner research review --run <id> [--item <n>] [--finish]` (in `cli`, calling `tradepartner.research.labeling.review`) attaches the unfinished run and pages the shortlist: the passage (the packet text, with the documents' EDGAR archive URLs), then two answers labelled `a` and `b`, the rule's and the model's in an order drawn per item from a seed derived from the run id and the item id (stable on re-display, unknown to the owner), **with no probability, confidence or attribution shown**; the owner records `a`, `b`, `both_wrong` or `unresolved`, a reason, and the passage he relied on (accession, character offsets on the normalised text, the quoted text's SHA-256). The command attributes after the save (`decided_for` ∈ `rule`, `model`, `both_wrong`, `unresolved`) and appends the record to the **review file** `research.data_dir/reviews/departure-reason/<run_id>.jsonl` (`review_id`, `run_id`, `security_id`, `form25_accession`, `stratum`, `decision`, `decided_for`, `reason`, `passage_ref`, `reviewer = "owner"`, `code_version`, `known_at` UTC); nothing else writes that file. `--finish` registers the review file as a dataset version (`departure-reason-reviews`), computes the batch metrics of req 10 (yield; stratum-weighted precision and recall), and writes the run's result. The review command never writes to `store.path`, `events.path` or config and offers no "apply": **a fix is a PR** under its own issue, by any team, citing the review file's SHA-256, the accession and the owner's decision as the value's source, carrying the filing's `known_at`, with a pinned test as #921 did (ADR 0013 point 4; ADR 0008 point 3). The gold labelling screen (`tradepartner research gold --dataset <frame id> --sample <n> --seed <s>`) is the same screen without the two answers; it refuses to start when any inference-record dataset exists for the slug (gold is made first), and writes `research.data_dir/gold/departure-reason/<sha>.jsonl` with the protocol §7 fields that apply (`label_id`, `security_id`, `form25_accession`, `label`, `passage_ref`, `annotator_confidence`, `seconds_spent`, `labeled_at`, `manual_version`), registered as `departure-reason-gold` with the split file and the sealed `pilot` period.

10. **Evaluation as a registered experiment, bars before data.** Three experiment files under `docs/experiments/` (research-registry req 2; T82's template), merged and registered before the first model call:
    - **`departure-reason-pilot`** (`kind = benchmark`, `stage = 3`, `provenance = model_historical`, `confirmatory = false` (research-registry req 2 refuses otherwise; ML brief §7), `touches_returns = false`, `claims` naming topic 9b's claim ids and B8's, `splits = ["dev", "pilot"]`, `[dataset] name = "departure-reason-gold"`, `[window]` 2016-01-01 to the configuration freeze date, `[budget] runs = 10, configurations = 4`, `seed` published on #963 before the frame is built). **Gold sample**: 300 listing ends drawn from the frame under the seed, stratified by rule answer (the six rows of req 5's table, so the rare rows are represented) × Form 25 filer (issuer `25` or exchange `25-NSE`) × period block (2016 to 2018, 2019 to 2021, 2022 to freeze), proportional within strata, at most two per CIK, inclusion probabilities recorded; the **seeded error set** is forced in: Valaris (#921; Form 25 2020-09-04), Seagate 2010 and 2021 and MiMedx 2019 (#943), Sotheby's 2019 (#905), the Match Group 2020 separation (#828, #922), each with the rule's historical wrong answer as recorded on its issue, so the model is scored on flagging what the rule got wrong at that fix's parent `code_version` without checking out old code; #893's rename holes are **not** seeds because a rename has no Form 25 passage to read (#956 item 10). **Splits**: `dev` = the sampled listing ends whose Form 25 was accepted 2016-01-01 to 2017-06-30 (about 60; the question text and option descriptions are tuned on them, at most four configurations); `pilot` = 2017-07-01 to the freeze (about 240), sealed by that period at `dataset register`; the sealed `pilot` is scored **once** per registration (the spend of req 6). **Primary metric**: `partition_accuracy`, the share of `pilot` gold items (gold `unresolved` excluded) whose model label falls in the crosswalk subset of the gold label's own partition, `direction = greater`, `threshold = 0.80`, `ci_level = 0.95` one-sided lower bound, `inference = "CIK-cluster bootstrap, 2000 resamples, percentile, lower bound"`, `min_clusters = 100` CIKs. **Secondary (gating in the stop rule, reported by code)**: per-option precision and recall with one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson lower bounds, gate `precision_lb ≥ 0.60` for every option with at least 20 gold items (fewer: reported `underpowered`, not gated); `seeded_recall = 1.0` (every seed on the shortlist); `unresolved_share ≤ 0.15` on `pilot`. **Reported, never gating**: ECE with 15 equal-mass bins, multiclass Brier (summed over classes, not divided by K), classwise reliability, all on the **probability vector**, never on `vendor_confidence` (ML brief §5.5); the deterministic `rule_provision` arm's coverage of the gold partition on the same items (`comparison_set`); first-option bias from the perturbation probe; p50 and p90 latency; cost. `stop_rule`: a missed gate is a negative result and closes the task; the option set may be narrowed in a new registration, the bars never lowered after a `pilot` score. `expected_effect`: partition accuracy 0.80 to 0.90 (a factual reading of a stated reason, easier than TC's 0.73 on sentiment); the `rule_provision` arm alone covers most of the merger and retirement rows and none of the bankruptcy-versus-compliance or redomicile-versus-merger distinctions.
    - **`departure-reason-probes`** (`kind = robustness`, same provenance and window, `splits = ["dev"]`, `budget.runs = 30`): the **perturbation probe** (five fixed permutations of the option order over the 300 gold items; primary `flip_rate` with `direction = less`, `threshold = 0.10`) and the **drift probe** (a fixed set of 50 `dev` packets with their original inference records; before every later labeling run; primary `flip_rate` against the originals, `threshold = 0.10`, and `mean_abs_probability_shift` reported; a `fail` verdict stops the batch, req 6 step 2). A vendor retirement of the pinned id ends the stream: the next pinned id is a new registration, never a substitute (prospective spec §8).
    - **`departure-reason-batches`** (`kind = benchmark`, `splits = ["full", "prospective"]`, `[dataset] name = "departure-reason-corpus"`, `budget.runs = 12`): the production batches after a passed pilot. Each batch and its review is **one run** (ADR 0013 point 4; #956 item 4: `benchmark` fits, the system being the pinned configuration and the outcome the owner's review decisions, which are human labels on a stratified sample; no new registry kind is needed, and this spec declares that rather than amending the registry spec). A batch over the historical population binds `full` with `provenance = model_historical`; a batch over Form 25s accepted after the freeze binds `prospective` with `provenance = model_prospective` and is where a confirmatory record can accrue later (its own registration, `confirmatory = true`, predating its dataset). **Batch metrics** (#956 item 9): `yield` = the share of reviewed disagreement and `unresolved` items decided `model` or `both_wrong` (the rule was wrong), reported with its lower bound as the primary (`direction = greater`, `threshold = 0.30`: fewer than three in ten shortlist items being real errors means the shortlist is not worth his time); stratum-weighted **model accuracy** and **rule accuracy** over the batch, each item weighted by the inverse of its stratum's sampling rate (disagreements and `unresolved` 1.0, agreements `agreement_sample_size / n_agreements`), with the agreement stratum's lower bound reported separately and the estimates flagged `underpowered` when fewer than 20 agreements were reviewed; `unresolved_share`; `n_reviewed`; cost. The batch's corpus dataset name carries no seal, so a batch is not a holdout spend; the gold dataset's seal protects only the gold name.
    All three verdicts are recorded by the owner as `TP-` claims labelled exploratory (research program §6). The `pilot` run's `TP-` claim is the pilot's verdict; until it says `pass`, no `departure-reason-batches` run is opened (ADR 0013 point 5: "no task produces a shortlist for the owner to act on before it has passed").

11. **Probes and provenance honesty.** Every packet sent before the freeze is `model_historical` and exploratory forever (research-registry req 2, ML brief §7); the dashboard and every report label it so. The drift probe runs before every batch (req 6 step 2); the perturbation probe runs once per option-set version on `dev` before the `pilot` score. Probe records are inference records like any other, under their own run ids, and are never counted in a batch's shortlist or accuracy.

## Risks & domain checks

- **Look-ahead and point-in-time.** The packet holds only filings accepted inside a window around the Form 25, each with its acceptance; the rule answer is computed at a fixed `t` recorded in `config_json`; gold labels are made from the filing text alone, blind to the model and to the store (the gold screen shows no rule answer); every record's `known_at` is its completion time and is never back-stamped; a fix carries the filing's `known_at`, never the review's. The model itself may have seen later filings (ML brief §7): every historical result is exploratory by construction, and the honest path to a confirmatory record is the `prospective` split on Form 25s accepted after the freeze.
- **Survivorship.** The frame is the delisted set, by definition; the check is completeness against `delistings` (req 2) and the pre-XML exclusion count. The gold sample is stratified by rule answer so the rare rows (successor, `listed` after a Form 25) are not swamped by plain mergers.
- **Costs.** Under $3 for the pilot at list price against a $20 credit; two ceilings, a batch cap and a runaway stop in code; spend computed from records; billing reconciled on #946 after the first batch (prospective spec §6: advertised price is never treated as observed cost). The promotional credit's expiry is unknown until the owner reads his account.
- **Order path.** None: tests (b) and (d) prove no import path and no store write; the registry stores numbers; the review command writes only the review file. `tradepartner.execution` is untouched.
- **Secrets.** One key, `SecretStr`, redacted everywhere `secret_values` reaches, read in one module, never in a packet or a record. The recorder is the owner's.
- **LLM inputs.** Filing text is untrusted and the vendor documents that the model "can be steered by injected text": the option set is closed and ours, the instructions are outside the delimiters, and the worst case is one mislabelled passage a human then reads. First-option bias is measured by the perturbation probe before the `pilot` score; dates are supplied by code in words so the model is not asked to compare them; the packet cap keeps the state under the vendor's 32k budget and away from the "large state full of irrelevant detail" failure mode; nondeterminism and silent re-routes are visible through the stored raw response, the returned model id check and the drift probe.
- **The research store as a temptation.** A directory of plausible labels next to the master; tests (b), (c) and (d) and the dashboard's rule (no label shown) exist for it. Reversible by deletion (ADR 0013 Consequences).
- **Anchoring.** The review screen shows the two answers unattributed and without probabilities; attribution happens after the save. The gold screen shows no rule answer. The same owner labels gold and reviews shortlists, which is the design's accepted limit (a solo owner); the hindsight check of protocol §6 is not run for a factual label the filing adjudicates.
- **Vendor change.** A repricing changes `price_usd_per_million_input_tokens` by a reviewed config change; a retired id ends the stream and a new id is a new registration; a terms change on training or retention is an ADR 0013 Revisit item.
- **Owner time.** 300 gold labels at two to four minutes is 10 to 20 hours before any shortlist; recorded as review hours in the retro (ADR 0005). Owner question 3 offers a smaller sample at the cost of power.
- **Store locks.** The fetch holds one short read of the owner's store before any request; the rule answer uses short-lived read-only connections; nothing here contends with ingest or the paper window's run beyond that.

## Open questions

Each has options and a recommendation; the owner answers on #963 and the answers are folded into a dated section, after which the status becomes Accepted and the plan can be written.

1. **The option set.** (a) The nine options of req 4. (b) ADR 0013's seven plus `unresolved` (`voluntary delisting or exchange transfer` as one option, `other` instead of `instrument_retirement`). (c) Six: fold `going_private` into `merger_or_acquisition` and `voluntary_withdrawal` into `compliance_delisting`'s complement, fewer options for a model documented to lean on option order. **Recommendation: (a)**; the two splits follow the resolver's own distinctions (`transferred` versus `delisted`) and a closed set without `other` keeps `unresolved` honest; (c) if the dev split shows the finer options are not separable, as a new registration.

2. **Client: plain `httpx` or the vendor SDK.** (a) `httpx`, as req 7 specifies, no `research` dependency group for the pilot. (b) `typesafe-sdk` in a `research` group, imported only in `research.models`, with a CI-only install for `mypy` and the SDK's retries disabled (`RetryPolicy(max_retries=0)`) so our retry rule holds. **Recommendation: (a)**: the API is one POST, the SDK's defaults (alias at construction, retry on every 5xx and 408) work against pinning and spend accounting, and test (c) is written for the `httpx` shape; (b) costs a guarded import and buys nothing the pilot needs.

3. **Gold sample size and split.** (a) 300 (about 60 `dev` to 2017-06-30, about 240 `pilot` sealed from 2017-07-01), 10 to 20 owner-hours. (b) 200 (40 and 160), 7 to 13 hours, with `min_clusters` lowered to 80 and the per-option gates applying to fewer options. (c) 300 with no `dev` split: the question text is frozen from the fixture packets alone and every gold item is `pilot`. **Recommendation: (a)**: the ADR's power arithmetic puts the sample near 300 before design effects, and four tuning configurations on 60 real items are cheap insurance against a question the model reads literally; (c) if the owner would rather not spend an hour on `dev` labels, at the cost of a first `pilot` score on an untuned question.

4. **The `.env` ceilings for the pilot.** (a) `RESEARCH__SPEND_CEILING_USD_MONTH=10` and `RESEARCH__SPEND_CEILING_USD_TOTAL=20`. (b) `5` and `20`. **Recommendation: (a)**, the ADR's own recommendation for the month, with the total at the credit; the pilot as registered spends under $3 either way, and the batch cap of $2 is the figure that stops a mistake.

5. **Primary bar and the unresolved cap.** (a) `partition_accuracy` lower bound ≥ 0.80, `unresolved_share ≤ 0.15`, per-option precision lower bound ≥ 0.60 where powered. (b) 0.85, 0.10, 0.70: closer to the ML brief's planning values. **Recommendation: (a)** for a review aid whose errors a human catches: the shortlist is worth his time at 0.80 if the yield confirms it, and the bars are never lowered afterwards, so the lower first bar is the honest one; (b) if he wants the pilot to double as evidence for the typed-classifier claim.

6. **The context window.** (a) 45 calendar days before and 20 after the Form 25's filing date, 8-K items `3.01, 2.01, 1.01, 5.03, 3.03, 8.01` plus the first `EX-99.1`. (b) 90 before and 30 after, all items. **Recommendation: (a)**: a merger's 2.01 and a compliance notice's 3.01 fall inside it, the packet stays small, and a window the dev split shows too narrow is a config change before the freeze, never after.
