# Spec: Paper trading and journal (Phase 4)

**Status:** Draft  ·  **Issue:** #247  ·  **Related:** [roadmap Phase 4](../roadmap.md), [charter](../charter.md), [ADR 0003](../decisions/0003-data-adapters-local-first.md) (rules 7 and 8), [ADR 0005](../decisions/0005-objective-benchmark-stop-criteria.md) (the four Phase 4 checks), [ADR 0006](../decisions/0006-universe-and-cadence.md) (cadence, missed rebalance, forced exits), [ADR 0007](../decisions/0007-clock-failures-halt-path.md) (halt path), [ADR 0008](../decisions/0008-llm-role.md) (no LLM; overrides never blocked), [ADR 0009](../decisions/0009-price-vendor.md) (gap sign-off gate), [design standard](../design/dashboard.md) (Phase 4 page-map row), [backtest spec](backtest.md), [data-foundation spec](data-foundation.md), [H1](../hypotheses/h1-momentum-12-1.md), research [G7 wash sales](../research/2026-09-25-g7-wash-sales.md), [#86 pre-open fills](../research/2026-09-25-alpaca-open-and-depth.md) (Probe 3 is #182, owner-run the week of 2026-09-28), [free-data terms](../research/2026-09-25-free-data-terms.md), issue #33 (Broker interface gaps)

## Problem & why now

Phase 3 answers "how would the rule have done?". Phase 4 asks whether the system can run the rule **unattended, against a broker, without a human in the loop and without cheating itself**: orders placed by code through deterministic risk rules, every order reconciled against what the broker says, every decision journaled from signal to outcome, and a paper result that can be compared to the backtest on the same dates. This spec builds the risk-gated broker wrapper (ADR 0003 rule 7), the Alpaca paper adapter, the scheduled tracking run, the journal, the operations and override pages, the alerts, the wash-sale lot ledger and the commands that check the Phase 4 exit criteria. It is where charter principles 3, 5 and 6 (code places orders, rules before money, tune on process) and development-process rules 5, 7 and 8 become code with tests.

The paper vehicle is the registered H1 (long-only 12-1 momentum, top decile equal weight, monthly). ADR 0005 makes Phase 4 a test of process, not of returns: its exit criteria are four checks a command can run, and beating a benchmark is not one of them. Phase 4 may not start until the owner has signed off on H1's recorded survivorship gap (ADR 0003 rule 8, ADR 0009 point 3); the `paper start` command enforces that gate.

## Users & usage

- **The scheduler** runs `tradepartner paper run` once per XNYS session, before the open, after the previous session's `ingest` has completed (ADR 0005's machine assumption). On most sessions the run collects order outcomes, reconciles and marks. On a fill session (the session after a month-end) it also plans and submits. Nothing else in the system places orders.
- **The owner** runs `paper start` once (after the gap sign-off), reads the operations page most days, uses the override screen rarely and with a reason, engages or releases the kill switch, records Alpaca paper responses once with their own keys for the fixtures, and runs `paper check` and `paper report` at the phase close. Only the owner holds keys, runs anything against the real store or the paper account, and releases the kill switch.
- **Phase 6** inherits the wrapper, the journal, the ledger and the pages unchanged; it swaps the adapter's endpoint, not the rules. Nothing in this spec runs against a live account.
- **Tests**, on every PR, run the wrapper against `FakeBroker` and the Alpaca adapter against recorded, scrubbed responses with the network disabled.

## Definitions

- **Rebalance session** T_i, **fill session** F_i and **read time** close(T_i) are the backtest spec's definitions (`backtest.schedule`). The **paper window** starts at the first rebalance session on or after H1's frozen `holdout.end` (the backtest spec reserves sessions after it for tracking) and is recorded by `paper start`, never read from live `Settings`.
- **Tracking run**: one execution of `paper run` on session S. Its **kind** is `rebalance` when S is a fill session, `catch_up` when a rebalance was missed and S is inside the catch-up window, `mark` otherwise. Every run has a `paper_runs` row, whatever its outcome (the registry rule applied to paper: no row, no run).
- **Tracking trial**: a backtest of the registered hypothesis over the paper window with `kind=tracking` (backtest spec, Definitions), run by `paper report`, never counted in N or V, and never a holdout spend. Its `trial_weights` and monthly returns are what paper is compared against.
- **Plan** at T: the universe, signal, targets and reasons computed at read time close(T) by the same function the engine uses, over the same `StoreProvider`. A plan is a pure function of rows with `known_at ≤ close(T)` and of the frozen parameters.
- **Decision**: one per name in the plan's held-or-target set: trade (side, notional), skip (with a reason) or override. **Order**: a decision the wrapper submitted. **Chain**: signal → decision → order → {fill | expired | rejected | cancelled} → outcome (ADR 0005), one journal row per link, each with its own `known_at`.
- **Halt**: ADR 0007's minimum (no further `submit` in this run, the exception propagates, the fault is journaled with its type) plus what this spec adds in req 4.
- **System fault**: any `SystemFaultError` (ADR 0007): `ClockError`, `StaleDataError`, `ReconciliationError`, `RejectionCapError`, `KillSwitchEngagedError`, `LimitBreachError`. Every one takes the halt path.
- **Ledger**: the positions and cash the journal implies, rebuilt from journaled fills (split-adjusted by `corporate_actions` with `ex_date ≤ S`), never stored as truth. **Reconciliation** compares the ledger with the broker's `positions()`, `open_orders()` and `account()`.

## Requirements

1. **Broker interface completed (#33).** `Broker` gains `account() -> Account(cash, buying_power, equity, as_of)`, `open_orders() -> list[Order]` (every order not in a terminal state), `get_order(client_order_id) -> Order` (ADR 0007 point 3's fetch), `fills(since=None)` and `assets(symbols) -> dict[symbol, Asset(tradable, fractionable, status)]`. `OrderStatus` gains `EXPIRED` and `REJECTED`; the terminal set is exactly `{FILLED, EXPIRED, REJECTED, CANCELLED}`. `OrderRequest` describes a market DAY order by `notional` or `quantity` (exactly one), with `order_type` and `time_in_force` fields fixed to `market` and `day` in Phase 4; the fill price is no longer a request field. `FakeBroker` implements all of it with an injected price function and can be told to expire, reject or partially fill a named order. ADR 0007 Task A lands here: `SystemFaultError` and `ClockError` in `tradepartner/errors.py`, every adapter's clock call wrapped so any failure becomes `ClockError` before an `Order` or `Fill` is built.
2. **Alpaca paper adapter.** `adapters/alpaca_broker.py` implements `Broker` over `alpaca-py`'s trading client, **paper endpoint only**: `alpaca.paper` is a **guarded setting** whose validator rejects `false` (the `universe.exclude_sic_ranges` pattern), and the adapter reads `ALPACA_PAPER_API_KEY` / `ALPACA_PAPER_API_SECRET`, never the data keys, so a live-capable key is never in the order path in Phase 4. It dedupes `client_order_id` against the broker (`get_order`) per the ABC docstring, maps master tickers to broker symbols through one function (class-share separators tested), times out and retries per `alpaca.trading_request_timeout_seconds` and `alpaca.trading_max_retries`, contains no risk logic (ADR 0003 rule 7) and holds no secret in any exception text. Contract tests run on recorded, scrubbed responses (the Phase 2 recorder pattern) that the owner records once with paper keys.
3. **Risk-gated wrapper.** `execution.RiskGatedBroker` is the **only** object that calls `Broker.submit` or `cancel` (AST check: no module outside `execution/` imports a `Broker` implementation, and nothing outside the wrapper calls `submit`). A run hands it the whole batch of decisions. Before the first `submit` it (a) runs the ADR 0007 clock pre-check: well-formed; not earlier than the `finished_at` of the last `ok` ingestion run; not later than the close of the scheduled session plus `exec.clock_max_sessions_late` sessions; the wrapper and the adapter hold the same clock object; every later reading is monotonic or the run halts with `ClockError`; (b) builds and validates every `OrderRequest` (a `ValueError` here halts, ADR 0007 point 6); (c) checks the batch against the limits, all from config: per-name target weight ≤ `risk.max_position_weight`; per-order notional ≤ `risk.max_order_notional_fraction` × equity; gross exposure after the batch ≤ `risk.max_gross_exposure` (no leverage); every sell quantity ≤ the reconciled held quantity (long-only, no shorts, ever); buys sized after the modelled cost (`costs.*`) within `min(cash, buying_power)` from `account()`; order count ≤ `risk.max_orders_per_run`; every symbol `tradable` and, when the order is notional, `fractionable`; every order notional ≥ `exec.min_order_notional`, else the decision is journaled `skip_below_minimum` and not submitted. A batch that breaks a limit is not partially submitted: the run halts with `LimitBreachError` naming the rule. (d) derives every `client_order_id` deterministically as `f"{paper.order_id_prefix}-{F:%Y%m%d}-{security_id}-{side}"` (canonical symbol form; a test pins the derivation and its length against the adapter's limit), so a re-run on the same session replays and a later session is a new attempt.
4. **Halt path and rejections (ADR 0007).** The rejection allowlist is one frozen constant per `Broker` method, pinned by a test: `submit`, `positions`, `fills`, `account`, `open_orders`, `get_order`, `assets`: **empty**; `cancel`: `{OrderNotOpenError}` (the order finished before the cancel; journaled, not a fault). Every other exception, `SystemFaultError` first, halts. An asynchronous `rejected` status is not an exception: it is a terminal chain state, counted per run; the run halts with `RejectionCapError` when the count exceeds `exec.max_rejections_per_run` or when every order of the run was rejected. A `DuplicateClientOrderIdError` triggers `get_order`: identical fields adopt the order as a **replay** (journaled as such); any difference or a failed fetch halts. **What a halt does here:** journals the fault; best-effort cancels this run's open orders (matched by `paper.order_id_prefix` and session) with each cancel outcome journaled and a cancel failure journaled without changing the halt; engages the kill switch (req 5); writes the alert (req 11); re-raises. An order whose `submit` raised is journaled `unknown` and must be resolved by reconciliation before any resume.
5. **Kill switch, persisted.** An append-only `kill_switch` table holds `engaged` and `released` events with source (`owner`, `fault`, `drawdown`), fault type, reason and run id; the latest row is the state. Engaged by: any halt; the owner (`paper kill --reason`, or the override form); the drawdown trigger, when ledger equity is below its paper-window peak by more than `risk.max_drawdown`. While engaged, `paper run` still collects outcomes, reconciles and marks (read-only broker calls) but submits nothing and journals the session as `skipped_kill_switch`; a fill session so skipped is a missed rebalance. Released **only** by the owner with `paper resume --reason`, which first runs reconciliation and refuses when it fails; the release row records the reconciliation id. No config, flag or page can bypass an engaged switch.
6. **Reconciliation.** Runs at the start of every tracking run (after collecting outcomes) and at the end, and on `paper reconcile`. Compares, per symbol, the ledger quantity with `positions()` within `exec.reconcile_qty_tolerance`; ledger cash with `account().cash` within `exec.reconcile_cash_tolerance`; the journal's non-terminal orders with `open_orders()` (each side must explain the other); and flags any broker order or position the journal cannot explain (a foreign prefix, a symbol never journaled). A delisted name the broker has removed is explained by a listing end known at close(S−1); a quantity change is explained by a split with `ex_date ≤ S` known by then. Any residual is `ReconciliationError`: halt, kill switch, alert. Every reconciliation writes a `reconciliations` row with its status and mismatches, so "reconciled since the last order" is a query.
7. **Tracking run.** `paper run [--session S]` (default: the session the clock is in, from the calendar; a non-session halts). Sequence: (1) read the kill-switch state; (2) collect outcomes for every non-terminal journaled order via `get_order` and `fills(since)`, journaling fills and terminal events; (3) reconcile; (4) staleness: the last `ok` ingestion run must cover the previous session for `ingest.reference_symbol`, else `StaleDataError` (halt; on a fill session this is a missed rebalance); (5) mark: write `positions_daily` at the previous session's close from the ledger and `prices_as_of`; (6) if S is F_i, or a missed rebalance is pending and S is within `paper.max_catch_up_sessions` of its F_i: plan at close(T_i) through the engine's plan function over `StoreProvider` (**the same code and read time as the backtest**); apply the session's overrides (req 9); build decisions: a name leaving the targets is sold whole by quantity; other trades are notional, target minus drifted ledger weight; the `n_orders_below_min_at_live_capital` column is computed for the same decisions at `paper.live_capital_reference` and journaled (no order); hand the batch to the wrapper; (7) forced exits on any session: a held name whose listing has ended at close(S−1) is sold whole (the engine's delisting convention); an untradable one is journaled `untradable` and marked at its last close; (8) reconcile again; (9) write the `paper_runs` row with status `ok`, `halted`, `stale`, `skipped_kill_switch` or `failed` (any other exception) and `invoked_by` (`scheduler` or `tty`). A missed rebalance beyond `paper.max_catch_up_sessions` is journaled `missed_rebalance` and not traded until the next T. A run that finds no `paper_runs` row for the previous session writes a `missed_run` alert.
8. **Journal chain (ADR 0005 check 2).** Append-only tables (below), every row with `known_at` and `ingested_at`, written through `store.journal` (inserts and reads only, like `store.registry`). For every order: exactly one `orders` row, one or more `order_events` rows of which exactly one is terminal in `{filled, expired, rejected, cancelled}`, zero or more `fills` rows, and one `outcomes` row written by the first run after the next rebalance's fill (or the exit): the position's return from its fill to that fill, its contribution, and the fill session's bar open and close (for the fill decomposition in req 10). Fills carry the broker's `filled_at` as `known_at`. `paper check` reports every order whose chain is incomplete once its outcome is due.
9. **Overrides (ADR 0005 check 3, ADR 0008).** The override page is the dashboard's **only write**: a form that appends an `overrides` row through a short-lived write connection (`store.db.open_for_write`, "store busy" when locked), with kind ∈ `{exclude_name, keep_name, engage_kill_switch}`, the rebalance session it applies to, the name, and a reason of at least `paper.min_override_reason_chars` (a `CHECK` on non-empty reason, the length in the form and the CLI). An override applies to the one rebalance it names and is consumed by that run, which journals the decision as `override` with the override id; the resulting order passes the wrapper like any other (ADR 0008 point 4). No memo panel exists in Phase 4; Phase 5 adds one additively and the form must keep working with none.
10. **Tracking comparison (ADR 0005 check 1).** `paper report` opens a `kind=tracking` trial of the hypothesis over [paper start T, last completed T] on the real store with the frozen parameters (the Phase 3 window rule is extended: a `tracking` window must start at or after the first rebalance ≥ `holdout.end`, and `tracking` trials never count in N, V or holdout spends), then computes: per month, paper return (ledger equity at close(T_{i+1}) over close(T_i), from `positions_daily`) minus the trial's month return; **modelled cost per rebalance** = the trial's base-level `cost_paid` at T_i over its equity at close(T_i); the check passes when the maximum absolute difference over months not flagged `missed_rebalance` is ≤ `paper.tracking_k` × that month's modelled cost, with missed months listed separately; per fill session, the paper target set and weights against the trial's `trial_weights` (they must be equal: same store, same code, same read time; a difference is a look-ahead or determinism bug and is reported as such); and the **fill decomposition**: the part of each month's difference explained by paper fill price versus the frozen `execution.fill_price` bar price on F_i, so the owner can see whether a miss comes from execution timing (#86) or from anything else.
11. **Alerts.** An `alerts` table (kind ∈ `{run_failed, halted, stale_data, kill_switch, reconciliation, rejection_cap, missed_run, drawdown}`, run id, message, `delivered_json`) is the source of truth, written **before** the exception propagates. Delivery channels from `alerts.channels`: `store` (always), `macos` (`osascript` notification), `email` (SMTP from `ALERT_SMTP_HOST`, `ALERT_SMTP_USER`, `ALERT_SMTP_PASSWORD`, `ALERT_EMAIL_TO`; spend stays $0). A delivery failure is recorded on the row and never fails the run. One alert per (kind, run). The operations page lists them.
12. **Operations page** (design standard, Phase 4 row; pure `execution.ops` functions over a read-only connection, shared with `paper status`). Header: "as of" (store's last `known_at`) and "last updated" (last `paper_runs` row, with a stale chip when the previous session has no run). KPI row: positions (count, value), open orders, today's signals (targets count), kill-switch state as a status chip. Hero: the latest plan's ranking with in/out reasons (`selected`, `below_cut`, `excluded_no_history`, `not_in_universe`, `override`), the target set in the accent colour. Supporting: fills table; a journal-chain detail view per order; alerts list; reconciliation status. Read-only; renders the shell's empty and busy states; no colour literal in page code.
13. **Wash-sale lot ledger (G7 scope for Phase 4).** Pure `execution.lots` over journaled fills writes `lots` (one per acquisition: account id and type, owner, `security_id`, broker symbol, nullable CUSIP from the asset snapshot, trade date as UTC instant and exchange-local date, quantity, cost basis) and `disposals` (lots closed FIFO, trade date, quantity, proceeds, realised gain or loss before adjustment, tax year by trade date). For each loss disposal it scans acquisitions from `WASH_SALE_WINDOW_DAYS` before to after (a named constant from IRC §1091, not config, like `MONTHS_PER_YEAR`), across every account in the ledger, matches replacement shares to the loss in acquisition order with each replacement share absorbing one loss and losses processed in disposal order (G7 R11, R13), and writes `wash_sale_flags` with the matched lots, quantities and disallowed amount, re-scanning as later acquisitions arrive, including across December 31 (R15). Phase 4 holds one account (paper); the schema carries the account columns so Phase 6 can add others. Basis and holding-period carry-forward, broker 1099-B figures and the substantially-identical policy (G7 items 6 to 9 and 11) are Phase 6, before any taxable live trade.
14. **Entry gate and start.** `paper start --hypothesis <slug>` refuses unless `owner_decisions` holds a `gap_signoff` row referencing an `ok`, non-synthetic, `in_sample` trial of that hypothesis (ADR 0009 point 3), the hypothesis's frozen `holdout.end` is a completed month-end, and `account()` succeeds on the paper endpoint. It records a `paper_windows` row: hypothesis id, first rebalance session, the account's starting equity and id, and the code version. A second `paper start` while a window is open is refused.
15. **Exit-criteria command.** `tradepartner paper check` runs, on the real store, the four ADR 0005 checks plus the roadmap's count: (1) rebalance-kind `ok` runs on fill sessions with `invoked_by=scheduler` ≥ `paper.min_rebalances`; (2) the req 10 tracking check; (3) no incomplete chain; (4) no override without a reason (and, from Phase 3, no engine run without a trial: already true by construction). Exit 0 only when all pass; each line names its query. `paper status` prints the operations-page numbers.
16. **CLI.** `tradepartner paper start|run|reconcile|kill|resume|report|check|status`. No flag selects a live endpoint, sets a store path or bypasses the kill switch or a limit; `--reason` is required by `kill`, `resume` and the override kinds; output contains no secret-shaped string. `paper run` is what the scheduler calls; the runbook from Phase 2 T22 gains its plist and the pre-open timing.

## Acceptance criteria (testable)

Interface and adapter
- [ ] `FakeBroker` and `AlpacaBroker` (on recorded responses) each return `account`, `open_orders`, `get_order`, `fills(since)` and `assets`; `OrderStatus` terminal set is exactly `{FILLED, EXPIRED, REJECTED, CANCELLED}` (test on the enum); an `OrderRequest` with both or neither of `notional` and `quantity` raises.
- [ ] ADR 0007 Task A: a clock that returns a naive value, an overflowing value, a non-`datetime`, or raises makes `submit` raise `ClockError` (not `ValueError`) with no order recorded and the id reusable, and makes `FakeBroker.simulate_fill` raise `ClockError` with the order unchanged and `OPEN`; `ClockError` is not a `ValueError` subclass.
- [ ] `Settings(alpaca={"paper": False})` raises; `AlpacaBroker` constructed without `ALPACA_PAPER_API_KEY` raises a credentials error whose text contains no key; every recorded fixture passes the scrub patterns; the adapter's base URL in every recorded request is the paper endpoint (test on the fixtures).
- [ ] Symbol mapping: a class-share ticker from the master maps to the broker's form and back; a symbol the master does not know at close(S−1) is refused before any request.

Wrapper, limits and halt path
- [ ] AST: no module outside `src/tradepartner/execution/` imports `AlpacaBroker` or `FakeBroker` (tests excepted), and no call to `.submit(` or `.cancel(` exists outside `execution/wrapper.py`; `execution/{risk,ids,reconcile,ledger,lots,plan}.py` contain no numeric literals other than 0, 1, −1, 2 and `WASH_SALE_WINDOW_DAYS`.
- [ ] Each limit, overridden one at a time (`risk.max_position_weight`, `risk.max_order_notional_fraction`, `risk.max_gross_exposure`, `risk.max_orders_per_run`, `exec.min_order_notional`), turns a passing batch into a `LimitBreachError` naming the rule with **zero** `submit` calls (recording fake); a sell above the reconciled quantity, a non-tradable symbol and a notional order on a non-fractionable symbol do the same; a below-minimum decision is journaled `skip_below_minimum` and the rest of the batch submits.
- [ ] Buying power: with `account().buying_power` below cash, buys are sized to buying power after modelled cost; cash never goes negative in the fake.
- [ ] Clock pre-check: a reading before the last `ok` ingest's `finished_at`, one after close(S) + `exec.clock_max_sessions_late` sessions, and a non-monotonic reading mid-run each raise `ClockError` with no further `submit`; the wrapper and adapter share one clock object (identity test).
- [ ] The allowlist constants' exact contents are pinned (`cancel`: `{OrderNotOpenError}`; every other method: empty); `ClockError`, `ValueError`, an adapter transport error and an exception of a type defined inside the test each halt: no further `submit`, the exception propagates, a `paper_runs` row with `halted` and the fault type, a `kill_switch` `engaged` row with source `fault`, an `alerts` row, and best-effort cancels of this run's open orders each journaled (a cancel that raises `OrderNotOpenError` is journaled as finished; one that raises anything else is journaled as a cancel failure and the halt stands).
- [ ] `client_order_id` is a pure function of (prefix, fill session, security id, side); a second run on the same session hits `DuplicateClientOrderIdError`, fetches the order and journals a replay with no new order; a duplicate whose fields differ halts; a fetch that raises halts.
- [ ] Rejections: `exec.max_rejections_per_run` + 1 asynchronous `rejected` statuses halt with `RejectionCapError`; a run whose every order is rejected halts even when the count is below the cap; each `rejected` is a terminal chain event.
- [ ] Every `OrderRequest` of a batch is built before the first `submit`; a `ValueError` from building the last one halts with zero submits.

Kill switch and reconciliation
- [ ] `paper kill --reason` appends an `engaged` row; a subsequent `paper run` on a fill session collects, reconciles, marks, submits nothing and journals `skipped_kill_switch`; `paper resume` without `--reason` is refused; `paper resume` with a failing reconciliation is refused and leaves the switch engaged; with a passing one it appends `released` with the reconciliation id; no `Settings` override changes an engaged state.
- [ ] Drawdown: ledger equity below its window peak by more than `risk.max_drawdown` engages the switch with source `drawdown` and alerts; at exactly the threshold it does not.
- [ ] Reconciliation on the fake: a broker position the ledger lacks, a ledger position the broker lacks, a quantity difference above `exec.reconcile_qty_tolerance`, a cash difference above `exec.reconcile_cash_tolerance`, an unexplained open order and a foreign-prefix order each produce `ReconciliationError`, a `reconciliations` row naming the mismatch, an engaged switch and an alert; a split with `ex_date ≤ S` known at close(S−1) and a delisting known then each explain their difference and pass; a split known only after close(S−1) does not (no look-ahead in the explanation).
- [ ] `tests/lookahead/`: the ledger, the reconciliation explanation and the plan of a run on session S are unchanged when the store is truncated at close(S−1) (`TruncatedStore`, the Phase 2 harness); a bar revision known after close(S−1) leaves them unchanged and changes the run on S+1.

Tracking run and journal
- [ ] On the fixture store with the fake broker and a fixed clock: a `mark` session writes `positions_daily` and no order; a fill session plans, submits and journals one `signals` row per universe member with a reason, one `decisions` row per held-or-target name, one `orders` row per submitted decision; a name leaving the targets is sold by quantity equal to the reconciled holding; the plan's targets for F_i equal the engine's `targets[F_i]` for the same store and frozen settings (the paper-equals-backtest oracle); `n_orders_below_min_at_live_capital` equals the hand count at `paper.live_capital_reference`.
- [ ] Every read of the plan carries `t = close(T_i)` (recording provider); no adapter is imported by the plan (AST, as in Phase 3).
- [ ] Missed rebalance: with no run on F_i, the run on F_i + 1 is `catch_up`, trades to the plan at close(T_i) (not re-planned at a later close), and journals `missed_rebalance`; a run beyond `paper.max_catch_up_sessions` journals `missed_rebalance` and trades nothing; a run that finds no row for the previous session writes a `missed_run` alert.
- [ ] Staleness: a store whose last `ok` ingest does not cover S−1 for `ingest.reference_symbol` halts with `StaleDataError` before any plan read, journals `stale`, alerts, and on a fill session leaves a pending missed rebalance.
- [ ] Forced exits: a held name whose listing end is known at close(S−1) is sold whole on S; an untradable one is journaled `untradable` and marked at its last close; the broker removing a delisted position reconciles.
- [ ] Chain: after the fake fills, expires, rejects and cancels one order each, and partially fills one, `paper check`'s chain query returns zero incomplete chains once outcomes are due, and returns the partially filled order as `expired` with its fill row; a deliberately deleted terminal event makes the query return that order; every journal row has tz-aware UTC `known_at ≤ ingested_at`; fills carry the broker's `filled_at` as `known_at`; `store/journal.py` contains no `UPDATE` or `DELETE`.
- [ ] Overrides: the form (headless) refuses an empty reason and one shorter than `paper.min_override_reason_chars`, writes exactly one row through a write connection and shows "store busy" when the store is locked; `exclude_name` removes the name from the next run's targets and sells it if held, `keep_name` keeps it, `engage_kill_switch` engages the switch; the decision row carries the override id and `override`; the resulting order passes the same limit checks (a limit-breaking override halts); the page renders with no memo table present.

Tracking comparison, report and check
- [ ] `paper report` on the fixture store opens a `kind=tracking` trial with the frozen parameters over the paper window; a `tracking` window starting before the first rebalance ≥ `holdout.end` is refused; the trial leaves N, V and holdout spends unchanged (`family_sharpes`, `family_holdout_spends`).
- [ ] With a synthetic ledger whose monthly returns equal the trial's, the check passes; a difference of exactly `paper.tracking_k` × modelled cost passes and one above it fails, naming the month; a month flagged `missed_rebalance` is excluded from the maximum and listed separately; the fill decomposition on a synthetic fill at the bar open with `execution.fill_price=close` equals the hand-computed open-to-close contribution.
- [ ] Target comparison: equal weights on the fixture store pass; a weight moved by hand in `trial_weights` is reported as a divergence naming the session and name.

The Streamlit-versus-React ADR's content (open question 1; the page requirements here are UI-agnostic through `execution.ops`). Limit orders, OPG, extended hours, bracket orders, shorting, leverage, options. A slippage key for the backtest (#86 report: it belongs with the fill-convention decision, open question 4). ## Risks & domain checks

- **Look-ahead:** the plan is the engine's own function at `t = close(T_i)` over `StoreProvider`; the reconciliation's explanations (splits, delistings) use only rows known at close(S−1); the truncation test covers the ledger, the explanations and the plan; the tracking trial is the Phase 3 engine and inherits its invariance suites; the journal is never a backtest input; the paper-equals-backtest oracle catches a plan that drifts from the engine.
- **Point-in-time journal:** every link of a chain has its own `known_at` (a plan at plan time, a fill at the broker's `filled_at`, an outcome when the next fill is known); nothing is updated in place, so "what did the system know when it decided" is a query.
- **Order path (development-process rules 5 and 7):** limits, the kill switch and the halt path are merged and tested before the first paper order; only the wrapper submits; the allowlist is empty; a batch is all-or-nothing against the limits; ids are deterministic so a crash replays instead of doubling; an order whose submit raised is `unknown` until reconciled; the adapter cannot reach a live endpoint (guarded key, separate paper keys); `safety-reviewer` runs on every Phase 4 PR touching `execution/`, `adapters/alpaca_broker.py`, `store/journal.py`, the override page or `.env.example`.
- **LLM inputs:** none (ADR 0008). The override form carries no memo and is never blocked by one.
- **Costs and fill realism:** paper fills at the NBBO after the open (#86), the frozen convention is the F_i close; ADR 0005's tolerance (`paper.tracking_k` × modelled cost, about 20 bp a month at H1's turnover and 15 bp) may be exceeded by fill timing alone; the decomposition in req 10 makes that visible, Probe 3 (#182) quantifies it before the first paper order, and the Phase 4 retro decides between amending ADR 0005 and registering a hypothesis with `open` fills plus a slippage allowance (open question 4). Paper does not simulate market impact or price improvement (#86 S27), so the paper stage measures the simulator, not live execution.
- **Live sizing (H1 Q1 trigger):** at `paper.live_capital_reference` most H1 orders sit at the fractional minimum; `n_orders_below_min_at_live_capital` reports it per rebalance so Phase 6's ADR decides on evidence.
- **Survivorship:** the tracking trial records the gap per rebalance like any trial; forced exits follow the engine's last-close convention; a paper position in a name the broker removes must be explained by a known delisting or the run halts.
- **Reconciliation drift:** split-adjusted comparison with tolerances; the owner never trades in the paper account by hand (a foreign order is a fault by design); the Alpaca paper account can be reset only through `paper start` on a fresh window.
- **Unattended machine:** a run that never starts cannot alert; the next run's `missed_run` alert and the page's stale chip are the detection; ADR 0006's missed-rebalance rule and `paper.max_catch_up_sessions` bound the damage; the plist is the T22 runbook's.
- **Single writer:** the run opens write connections per chunk (journal writes, the trial) and read-only ones for reads; the override form's write retries per `store.lock_retry_seconds` and shows busy.
- **Secrets:** paper keys and SMTP credentials are `SecretStr`; scrub tests on fixtures and CLI output; no secret in any exception.
- **Spend:** Alpaca paper, `osascript` and SMTP cost nothing (ADR 0009 budget).
- **Precision and limits unverified:** the accepted fractional quantity precision and the `client_order_id` length limit are recorded by the owner's recording task before the adapter is tested against them; the wrapper rounds sell quantities down so a sell never exceeds the holding.
- **Owner-only steps:** recording paper responses, `paper start`, `kill`/`resume`, the gap sign-off, and the phase-close evidence need the owner's keys and machine.

## Open questions

1. **Dashboard technology ADR** (design standard, "Open decision, deferred"). Options: (a) Streamlit with the design standard, the override form as a Streamlit form writing through `open_for_write`; (b) a React front end behind a thin read-only API plus one write endpoint for overrides, amending the roadmap's "no separate API" rule. Recommendation: (a); the roadmap's rule stands, the write is one form, and `execution.ops` keeps the page logic reusable if (b) is chosen later. The ADR is a plan task at Phase 4 start; this spec's page requirements do not depend on it.
2. **Alert channels.** Options: (a) `[store, macos]`: free, no secret, seen only at the Mac; (b) `[store, macos, email]` with SMTP credentials in `.env`; (c) a push service (paid or terms-bound). Recommendation: (a) by default, with `email` available when the owner sets the four `ALERT_*` variables; (c) needs a budget change.
3. **Drawdown trigger** `risk.max_drawdown`. Options: (a) 0.30, a checkpoint below H1's expected worst quarter (−18 to −19%) and above a bad month; (b) no drawdown trigger in paper (a total loss of ~$100 is an accepted outcome, ADR 0005); (c) 0.20. Recommendation: (a): the trigger is a forced owner review of the process, released with a reason, not an exit rule, and paper is where the rehearsal is cheap.
4. **Fill convention for the tracking comparison and what Probe 3 settles.** H1 froze `execution.fill_price=close`, so the tracking trial compares against close fills. Probe 3 (#182) settles: whether paper DAY orders submitted pre-open fill within a minute of the open at the NBBO (fixing the run's submit timing and the `exec.clock_max_sessions_late` reasoning); the observed gap between those fills and the bar open and close (the size of the decomposition term in req 10); and whether an `open` convention with a slippage allowance would track paper better. Options: (a) keep `close`, report the decomposition, decide at the retro; (b) register H1b with `open` and a new `execution.slippage_bps` key before paper starts, and track that; (c) amend ADR 0005's tolerance now. Recommendation: (a); nothing frozen changes on a probe, and the decomposition turns the question into a measured number.
5. **Reconciliation tolerances** (`exec.reconcile_qty_tolerance=1e-6`, `exec.reconcile_cash_tolerance=0.01`). Recommendation: accept, and let the owner's recording of the accepted quantity precision tighten the quantity value if Alpaca reports fewer decimals.
6. **Catch-up window** `paper.max_catch_up_sessions=5`. Options: (a) 5, mirroring `gap.missing_tail_sessions`; (b) 1, so a missed fill session is simply a missed month; (c) unbounded per ADR 0006's "next available session". Recommendation: (a): ADR 0006 says trade at the next available session, and five sessions bounds how stale a plan can be traded.
7. **Paper account equity at start.** The tracking check is scale-free (fractional shares, zero commissions), so the amount only decides how many orders sit near the minimum. Options: (a) Alpaca's paper default, which matches `backtest.initial_capital`'s default, with the live-scale count reported per rebalance; (b) the charter's live amount, rehearsing the minimum-notional problem for real, at the cost of a tracking check that fails by construction at H1's position count. Recommendation: (a); `paper start` records whatever the account holds, and Phase 6's ADR reads the live-scale column.
8. **Halt and open orders.** Options: (a) best-effort cancel of this run's open orders on any halt, each outcome journaled (spec'd); (b) leave them (DAY orders expire at the close) and reconcile at the next run. Recommendation: (a): a batch was validated as a whole, and a half-executed batch is a state no rule approved.
9. **Owner trades in the paper account.** Options: (a) forbidden: any foreign order or position is a reconciliation fault (spec'd); (b) tolerated with a journaled `external` explanation. Recommendation: (a); every override goes through the form so it is logged with a reason (rule 8).
10. **CUSIP for the lot ledger.** The security master has no CUSIP; G7 R22 wants it for broker matching. Options: (a) a nullable column filled from the broker's asset snapshot with `provenance=snapshot` when present; (b) leave it out until Phase 6. Recommendation: (a): cheap now, and the recording task shows whether the field exists.
