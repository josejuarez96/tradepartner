# Spec: Paper trading and journal (Phase 4)

**Status:** Draft  ·  **Issue:** #247  ·  **Related:** [roadmap Phase 4](../roadmap.md), [charter](../charter.md), [ADR 0003](../decisions/0003-data-adapters-local-first.md) (rules 7 and 8), [ADR 0005](../decisions/0005-objective-benchmark-stop-criteria.md) (the four Phase 4 checks), [ADR 0006](../decisions/0006-universe-and-cadence.md) (cadence, missed rebalance, forced exits), [ADR 0007](../decisions/0007-clock-failures-halt-path.md) (halt path), [ADR 0008](../decisions/0008-llm-role.md) (no LLM; overrides never blocked), [ADR 0009](../decisions/0009-price-vendor.md) (gap sign-off gate), [design standard](../design/dashboard.md) (Phase 4 page-map row), [backtest spec](backtest.md), [data-foundation spec](data-foundation.md), [H1](../hypotheses/h1-momentum-12-1.md), research [G7 wash sales](../research/2026-09-25-g7-wash-sales.md), [#86 pre-open fills](../research/2026-09-25-alpaca-open-and-depth.md) (Probe 3 is #182, owner-run the week of 2026-09-28), [free-data terms](../research/2026-09-25-free-data-terms.md), issue #33 (Broker interface gaps)

## Problem & why now

Phase 3 answers "how would the rule have done?". Phase 4 asks whether the system can run the rule **unattended, against a broker, without a human in the loop and without cheating itself**: orders placed by code through deterministic risk rules, every order reconciled against what the broker says, every decision journaled from signal to outcome, and a paper result compared to the backtest on the same dates under a rule fixed before the first order. This spec builds the risk-gated broker wrapper (ADR 0003 rule 7), the Alpaca paper adapter, the scheduled tracking run, the journal, the operations and override pages, the alerts, the wash-sale lot ledger and the command that checks the Phase 4 exit criteria. It is where charter principles 3, 5 and 6 (code places orders, rules before money, tune on process) and development-process rules 5, 7 and 8 become code with tests.

The paper vehicle is the registered H1 (long-only 12-1 momentum, top decile equal weight, monthly). ADR 0005 makes Phase 4 a test of process, not of returns: its exit criteria are four checks a command can run, and beating a benchmark is not one of them. Phase 4 may not start until the owner has signed off on H1's recorded survivorship gap (ADR 0003 rule 8, ADR 0009 point 3); `paper start` enforces that gate.

## Users & usage

- **The scheduler** runs `tradepartner paper run` once per XNYS session, starting before the open, after the previous session's `ingest` has completed (ADR 0005's machine assumption). On most sessions the run collects order outcomes, reconciles and marks, then exits. On a fill session it also plans, submits sells before the open, waits for them to finish after the open, then submits buys sized from the cash the sells raised. Nothing else in the system places orders.
- **The owner** runs `paper start` once (after the gap sign-off), reads the operations page most days, uses the override screen rarely and with a reason, engages or releases the kill switch, resolves the rare order the broker never received, records Alpaca paper responses once with paper keys for the fixtures, and runs `paper check` and `paper report` at the phase close. Only the owner holds keys, runs anything against the real store or the paper account, and releases the kill switch.
- **Phase 6** inherits the wrapper, the journal, the ledger and the pages unchanged; it swaps the adapter's endpoint, not the rules. Nothing in this spec runs against a live account.
- **Tests**, on every PR, run the wrapper against `FakeBroker` (told per order to accept, fill, partially fill, expire, reject, or vanish) and the Alpaca adapter against recorded, scrubbed responses with the network disabled.

## Definitions

- **Rebalance session** T_i, **fill session** F_i and **read time** close(T_i) are the backtest spec's definitions (`backtest.schedule`). **S** is the session a run executes on, from the calendar and the shared clock.
- **Paper window**: opened by `paper start` and closed by `paper stop`. Its first rebalance session T_0 is the first rebalance session **after** `paper start`, so it lies after H1's frozen `holdout.end` (the backtest spec reserves those sessions for tracking) and never touches the holdout. The window row **freezes** the exit thresholds and the comparison rule (req 14); runs and checks read the frozen values, never live `Settings`.
- **Tracking run**: one execution of `paper run` on session S. Its **kind** is `rebalance` when S = F_i, `catch_up` when a rebalance is pending and S ≤ F_i + `paper.max_catch_up_sessions`, `mark` otherwise. A run holds an exclusive **run lock** (`fcntl.flock` on `<store.path>.paper.lock`, shared with `resume`, `resolve` and `reconcile`) for its whole life; a second instance exits non-zero `locked`. It writes its `paper_runs` row **first** and its `paper_run_results` row last; a run without a result row is `unfinished` (the registry rule applied to paper: no row, no run), and only a lock holder may close an unfinished run as `crashed`, because a live run always holds the lock.
- **Plan trial**: every run that plans opens a registry trial of `kind=tracking` before its first provider read and closes it before submitting, so every engine read in Phase 4 sits under a trial id (ADR 0005 check 4). A plan trial is one referenced by a `paper_plans` row; it writes no metrics and never counts in N, V or holdout spends.
- **Tracking trial**: the backtest of the registered hypothesis over [T_0, last completed T] with `kind=tracking`, opened by `paper report`. Its `trial_weights` and monthly returns are what paper is compared against.
- **Plan** at T_i: the universe, signal, targets and reasons computed at read time close(T_i) by the engine's own plan function over `StoreProvider` with the frozen parameters: a pure function of rows with `known_at ≤ close(T_i)`. The plan records the store's `store_max_ingested_at` at plan time.
- **Decision**: one row per name in the plan's held-or-target set, made once per rebalance and journaled before any order: trade (side, planned notional or quantity), skip (with a reason) or override. A `catch_up` or same-session re-run **re-uses** the journaled decisions of the pending rebalance; it never re-plans. **Order**: a decision the wrapper submitted. **Chain**: signal → decision → order → {fill | expired | rejected | cancelled} → outcome (ADR 0005), one journal row per link.
- **Halt**: ADR 0007's minimum (no further `submit` in this run, the exception propagates, the fault is journaled with its type) plus req 4.
- **System fault**: any `SystemFaultError` (ADR 0007): `ClockError`, `ReconciliationError`, `RejectionCapError`, `SkipCapError`, `KillSwitchEngagedError`, `LimitBreachError`, `UnresolvedOrderError`. Every one takes the halt path and engages the kill switch. `StaleDataError` is **not** a `SystemFaultError` subclass: it is a data fault that halts the run without engaging the switch (req 4).
- **Ledger**: the positions and cash the journal implies, never stored as truth. Positions are rebuilt from journaled fills and journaled broker adjustments (req 6), split-adjusted by `corporate_actions` rows with `known_at ≤ close(S−1)` and `ex_date ≤` the instant the quantity is stated for. Cash is the broker cash recorded on the last `ok` reconciliation plus the journaled fill and adjustment deltas since it, so per-fill rounding at the broker can drift by at most one run's fills. **Reconciliation** compares the ledger with the broker's `positions()`, `open_orders()` and `account()`.
- **`known_at` rule for the journal**: `known_at` is the shared clock's reading at the moment the system learned or decided the fact (a plan when it was computed, a fill when the run collected it, a reconciliation when it ran); the instant the fact refers to is a separate column (`rebalance_session`, `session`, the broker's `event_at` or `filled_at`). A broker timestamp is never a `known_at`; a broker timestamp later than the clock reading by more than `risk.max_broker_clock_skew_seconds` is a `ClockError`. Rows written on the halt path after a `ClockError` are stamped with `store.db.utc_now()` and flagged `clock_fault`.

## Requirements

1. **Broker interface completed (#33).** `Broker` gains `account() -> Account(account_id, cash, buying_power, equity, as_of)`, `open_orders() -> list[Order]` (every order not in a terminal state), `get_order(client_order_id) -> Order` (raises `UnknownOrderError` when the broker has no such order; ADR 0007 point 3's fetch), `fills(since=None) -> list[Fill]` (each with a `broker_fill_id`), and `assets(symbols) -> dict[symbol, Asset(tradable, fractionable, status, cusip)]` (`cusip` nullable). `OrderStatus` becomes `{ACCEPTED, FILLED, EXPIRED, REJECTED, CANCELLED}` (`OPEN` is renamed `ACCEPTED`; a partial fill is `ACCEPTED` with `filled_quantity > 0`); the terminal set is exactly `{FILLED, EXPIRED, REJECTED, CANCELLED}`; the journal-only state `pending` lives in `order_events.status`, never in the broker enum. `OrderRequest` describes a market DAY order by `notional` or `quantity` (exactly one); the fill price is no longer a request field. `Order` carries `client_order_id, symbol, side, notional | quantity, status, submitted_at, broker_order_id, filled_quantity, filled_avg_price` (the last three nullable), `Fill` carries `broker_fill_id`, and `Position` is unchanged. `FakeBroker` implements all of it with an injected price function, does **not** auto-fill by default in the wrapper tests, and can be told per order to accept, fill, partially fill, expire, reject, or not exist. ADR 0007 Task A lands here: `SystemFaultError` and `ClockError` in `tradepartner/errors.py`, every adapter's clock call wrapped so any failure becomes `ClockError` before an `Order` or `Fill` is built.
2. **Alpaca paper adapter.** `adapters/alpaca_broker.py` implements `Broker` over `alpaca-py`'s trading client, **paper endpoint only**: `alpaca.paper` is a **guarded setting** whose validator rejects `false` (the `universe.exclude_sic_ranges` pattern), and the adapter reads `ALPACA_PAPER_API_KEY` / `ALPACA_PAPER_API_SECRET`, never the data keys, so a live-capable key is never in the order path in Phase 4. It dedupes `client_order_id` against the broker (`get_order`) per the ABC docstring; maps master tickers to broker symbols through one function (class-share separators tested); paces requests at `alpaca.trading_requests_per_minute`; times out and retries per `alpaca.trading_request_timeout_seconds` and `alpaca.trading_max_retries`; maps every broker order status through one table (`new`, `accepted`, `pending_new` → `ACCEPTED`; `partially_filled` → `ACCEPTED` with fills; `filled`, `expired`, `rejected`, `canceled`; **any status not in the table raises `SystemFaultError`**, never a guess); contains no risk logic (ADR 0003 rule 7: the adapter imports nothing from `execution/`); and holds no secret in any exception text. Contract tests run on recorded, scrubbed responses (the Phase 2 recorder pattern) that the owner records once with paper keys. The recording task also answers, in the PR: the accepted fractional quantity precision; the `client_order_id` length limit; whether the paper `cash` reflects same-session sale proceeds (if it does not, the two-phase funding in req 3 invests nothing, and this spec is amended before the wrapper task starts); how cash is rounded per fill; whether the duplicate `client_order_id` check covers terminal orders and for how long (replay depends on it); whether a whole-position quantity sell below `risk.min_order_notional` is accepted; how paper handles a split, a cash dividend, a spin-off and a cash merger on a held name (the #86 report says dividends are not simulated); whether the `Asset` object carries a CUSIP; how many current universe members are not fractionable; the endpoints behind `fills(since)`; and the documented request limit.
3. **Risk-gated wrapper.** `execution.RiskGatedBroker` is the **only** object that calls `Broker.submit` or `cancel` (AST check: no module outside `execution/` imports a `Broker` implementation, and nothing outside `execution/wrapper.py` calls `.submit(` or `.cancel(`). A run hands it the rebalance's journaled decisions and it executes them in **two phases on the same session**: (i) **sells**, submitted before the open; (ii) **buys**, submitted only after every sell of this run is terminal or `paper.sell_wait_seconds` has elapsed since the open (polling `get_order` every `paper.poll_interval_seconds`; buys may therefore land up to that long after the open, by design), sized from `account().cash` **after** the sells (never `buying_power`, so nothing is ever borrowed; a sell still open at the deadline is journaled `missing_fill` and its planned proceeds are not spent). **Buy sizing:** every buy's notional is its `planned_notional` × min(1, cash / Σ planned buy notionals × (1 + `costs.per_side_bps`/10⁴)), then `costs.buy_notional_after_costs`, so the batch fits the cash the sells raised and every name is scaled alike; the formula is pinned by a test. **Same-session re-run:** decisions that already have an `orders` row for S are re-submitted with their journaled fields exactly (a `DuplicateClientOrderIdError` is then a replay, req 4; a fresh acceptance means the broker never received the first attempt); only decisions with no `orders` row are sized now. Before the first `submit` of each phase it re-reads the derived kill-switch state and the unconsumed `engage_kill_switch` overrides, and if engaged submits nothing more, journals the rest of the rebalance `skipped_kill_switch` and ends the run that way (not a fault); then (a) runs the ADR 0007 clock pre-check: well-formed; not earlier than the `finished_at` of the last `ok` ingestion run; not later than close(S) + `risk.clock_max_sessions_late` sessions; the wrapper and the adapter hold the same clock object; every later reading is monotonic, or `ClockError`; (b) builds and validates every `OrderRequest` of the phase (a `ValueError` here halts, ADR 0007 point 6); (c) checks the phase's batch against the limits, every one from config: per-name target weight ≤ `risk.max_position_weight`; per-order notional ≤ `risk.max_order_notional_fraction` × equity; gross exposure after the batch ≤ `risk.max_gross_exposure` (no leverage); every sell quantity ≤ the reconciled held quantity, rounded **down** to `alpaca.quantity_decimals` (long-only, no shorts, ever); buys sized after the modelled cost (`costs.*`) within cash; order count ≤ `risk.max_orders_per_run`; a **batch** that breaks one of these halts with `LimitBreachError` naming the rule before any submit. Per-name conditions **skip** the name instead of halting, each journaled with its reason: not `tradable` (`skip_untradable`); a non-`fractionable` symbol is bought or trimmed by **whole-share quantity** rounded down, and skipped only when that rounds to zero (`skip_below_one_share`); a notional below `risk.min_order_notional` (`skip_below_minimum`; a full exit below it is `dust`, retried every fill session and not counted toward the cap); a name whose listing ended at close(S−1) (`skip_delisted`, handled by req 7's forced exits). Skips other than `dust` above `risk.max_skips_per_run` halt with `SkipCapError`: many skips are a fault in disguise. (d) writes the phase's `orders` rows and their `pending` events and commits **before** the first `submit`; (e) derives every `client_order_id` as `f"{paper.order_id_prefix}-{S:%Y%m%d}-{security_id}-{side}"` from the **run's session S** (a test pins the derivation and its length against the recorded limit), so a same-session re-run replays and a catch-up on a later session is a new attempt with new ids; (f) after each `submit`, polls `get_order` until the order is `ACCEPTED` or terminal or `paper.accept_wait_seconds` has elapsed, journaling the event with the broker's order id. Every limit and cap above is read from the window's **frozen** `risk.*` values (req 14), never from live `Settings`.
4. **Halt path and rejections (ADR 0007).** The rejection allowlist is one frozen constant per `Broker` method, pinned by a test, and the per-candidate reasoning is: `submit`: **empty** (an adapter's "not tradable" and "not fractionable" are pre-checked in req 3(c), so a rejection for them at submit means state changed mid-run; "insufficient buying power" means the ledger and the broker disagree; "market closed" is a scheduling fault); `cancel`: `{OrderNotOpenError}` (the order finished before the cancel: journaled, not a fault; `UnknownOrderError` on cancel halts, the journal and the broker disagree); `get_order`, `positions`, `fills`, `account`, `open_orders`, `assets`: **empty**. Every other exception, `SystemFaultError` first, halts. An asynchronous `rejected` status is not an exception: it is a terminal chain state counted against the **submitting** run; the run that observes the count exceed `risk.max_rejections_per_run`, or observes every order of a run rejected, halts with `RejectionCapError`. A `DuplicateClientOrderIdError` triggers `get_order`: identical fields adopt the order as a **replay** (journaled as such); any difference or a failed fetch halts. **What a halt does here, in order:** appends the `kill_switch` `engaged` row (retrying the lock per `store.lock_retry_seconds`; if the write still fails, the alert is delivered through every non-store channel with kind `kill_switch_write_failed` and the process exits non-zero); journals the fault; best-effort cancels this run's non-terminal orders with each outcome journaled (a cancel failure is journaled `cancel_failed` and the halt stands); writes the alert (req 11); writes the run's result row `halted`; re-raises. **Exception:** `StaleDataError` is a data fault, not a system fault in the ADR 0007 sense: it halts the run and alerts but does not engage the switch, because the next successful ingest cures it and the next run re-checks. **A `LimitBreachError` abandons the rebalance:** the pending rebalance is journaled `missed_rebalance` so a resume does not re-run the same breaching decisions every session. **Unknown state:** a `pending` order whose `submit` raised, or that a crash left pending, is handled by session: on the **same** session the re-run re-submits it with its journaled fields (req 3); on a **later** session the run's collection asks the broker: found → journaled from the broker's status; `UnknownOrderError` → `UnresolvedOrderError`, halt, and the owner runs `paper resolve <client_order_id> --reason` after checking the broker, which journals the terminal event `cancelled` with reason `not_received`; nothing resumes before that.
5. **Kill switch, persisted and derived.** An append-only `kill_switch` table holds `engaged` and `released` events with source (`owner`, `fault`, `drawdown`), fault type, reason, run id and, on release, the reconciliation id and the equity the drawdown peak resets to. The state is **derived**, and read before anything else a run or a resume does: engaged when the latest row is `engaged`, or when any **earlier** run (never the reading run itself) has a result row `halted`, `crashed` or `failed`, or no result row at all, with no `released` row after that run's `started_at`; so a lost write or a crash cannot leave the switch open. Engaged by: any halt except `StaleDataError`; the owner (`paper kill --reason`, or the override form, effective at the next run and at the next phase boundary of a running one, req 3); the drawdown trigger, evaluated at the mark step: ledger equity below the window's peak (the peak resets at each release) by more than the frozen `risk.max_drawdown`, firing once per crossing. While engaged, `paper run` still collects outcomes, reconciles and marks (read-only broker calls) but submits nothing and journals the session as `skipped_kill_switch`; a fill session so skipped is a missed rebalance. Released **only** by the owner with `paper resume --reason`, which takes the run lock, closes any unfinished run (`crashed`), runs reconciliation, refuses while any order is `pending` or unresolved or the reconciliation fails, and otherwise appends `released`. No config, flag or page can bypass an engaged switch.
6. **Reconciliation and corporate actions.** Runs at the start of every tracking run (after collecting outcomes) and at the end, and on `paper reconcile`. It checks `account().account_id` against the window's; compares, per symbol, the ledger quantity adjusted through S with `positions()` within `risk.reconcile_quantity_tolerance`; ledger cash with `account().cash` within `risk.reconcile_cash_tolerance`; the journal's non-terminal orders with `open_orders()` (each side must explain the other; an own-prefix broker order with a `pending` journal row is resolved through req 4, not flagged foreign); and flags anything the journal cannot explain (a foreign-prefix order, a symbol never journaled). An `ok` reconciliation records the broker's cash, which re-bases the ledger's cash (Definitions). **Explanations**, all from rows with `known_at ≤ close(S−1)`: a quantity change equal to a split's ratio with `ex_date ≤ S`; a removed position for a name whose listing ended (delisting or cash merger), with the broker's cash change journaled as an `adjustments` row of kind `corporate_action_cash` and applied to the ledger; a new position in a name that a spin-off with `ex_date ≤ S` names as the child, journaled `spinoff_receipt` and sold whole by the next run as a forced exit (`untargeted_receipt`). A cash dividend is **not** expected from paper (#86 S27); if the broker credits one anyway it is journaled `dividend_cash` and applied. Any residual is `ReconciliationError`: halt, kill switch, alert. Every reconciliation writes a `reconciliations` row with its status and mismatches, so "reconciled since the last order" is a query.
7. **Tracking run.** `paper run` (the session is always the clock's; a non-session exits `mark`-less with a `no_session` result). Sequence: (1) take the run lock; read the derived kill-switch state; write the `paper_runs` row; close any unfinished earlier run as `crashed`; (2) staleness: the last `ok` ingestion run must cover S−1 for `ingest.reference_symbol`, else `StaleDataError` before any broker call that needs the store's explanations (on a fill session the rebalance stays pending); (3) collect outcomes for every non-terminal journaled order of an earlier session via `get_order` and `fills(since)`, journaling fills and terminal events, and evaluate the rejection cap for their submitting runs; (4) reconcile; (5) mark: `positions_daily` for **every session since the last mark through S−1**, each from the ledger quantities adjusted through that session and `prices_as_of` at its close, so a missed or halted run never leaves a month-end without a mark; then the drawdown check; (6) if S = F_i, or a rebalance is pending and S ≤ F_i + `paper.max_catch_up_sessions`: if no decisions exist for T_i, open the plan trial, plan at close(T_i), apply the overrides naming T_i (req 9), build and journal the decisions and the `paper_plans` row (a name leaving the targets is sold whole by quantity; other trades are notional, target minus drifted ledger weight; names whose listing ended by close(S−1) are dropped as `skip_delisted`; `n_orders_below_min_at_live_capital` is computed once per plan for the same decisions at `paper.live_capital_reference`, no order), close the plan trial; then, only if the **sells phase starts** inside the submit window [open(S) − `paper.submit_window_before_open_minutes`, open(S) + `paper.submit_window_after_open_minutes`], hand the decisions to the wrapper (req 3); a fill session whose run is outside the window journals `missed_rebalance` pending and trades nothing; (7) forced exits on any session, through the same wrapper batch: a held name whose listing ended at close(S−1) and is still tradable is sold whole; an untradable one is journaled `untradable` and marked at its last close; a `spinoff_receipt` is sold whole; (8) reconcile again; (9) write `paper_run_results` with status `ok`, `halted`, `stale`, `skipped_kill_switch`, `crashed` or `failed` (any other exception) and `invoked_by` (`scheduler` when `TRADEPARTNER_INVOKED_BY=scheduler` is set and stdin is not a TTY, else `tty`). A rebalance still pending after `paper.max_catch_up_sessions` is journaled `missed_rebalance` and not traded until T_{i+1}. A run that finds no `paper_runs` row for S−1 writes a `missed_run` alert.
8. **Journal chain (ADR 0005 check 2).** Append-only tables (below), every row with `known_at` and `ingested_at` per the Definitions rule, written through `store.journal` (inserts and reads only, like `store.registry`). For every order: exactly one `orders` row (written before submit, with its `pending` event), one or more further `order_events` rows (`accepted` with the broker's order id, `replay`, fills, cancels) of which exactly one is terminal in `{filled, expired, rejected, cancelled}`, zero or more `fills` rows, and exactly one `outcomes` row, **due** at the first run after close(T_{i+1}) for an order of rebalance i (or after the exit session for a forced exit), whatever the order's terminal state: for a filled buy, the position's return from its fill price to the mark at close(T_{i+1}) and its contribution; for a filled sell, the realised P&L on the FIFO lots closed; for an expired, rejected or cancelled order, kind `not_executed` with the name's return over the same horizon (the cost of the miss); for a partially filled then expired order, both. Fills carry the broker's `filled_at` as `filled_at` and the collection time as `known_at`. `paper check` reports every order whose chain is incomplete once its outcome is due.
9. **Overrides (ADR 0005 check 3, ADR 0008).** The override page is the dashboard's **only write**: a form that appends an `overrides` row through a short-lived write connection (`store.db.open_for_write`, "store busy" when locked), with kind ∈ `{exclude_name, keep_name, engage_kill_switch}`, the rebalance session it applies to (or none for the kill switch), the name, and a reason whose trimmed length is at least `paper.min_override_reason_chars` (a `CHECK` on the trimmed length ≥ 1 in the schema; the frozen minimum in the form, the CLI and `paper check`). `exclude_name` drops the name from the targets and sells it if held, its weight staying in cash (not re-spread); `keep_name` keeps a held name at its drifted weight (no trade). An override is consumed by the run that plans T_i, which journals the decision as `override` with the override id (consumption is visible only through `decisions.override_id`; nothing is updated); the resulting order passes the wrapper like any other (ADR 0008 point 4). `engage_kill_switch` takes effect at the next run of any kind. A month with an override is flagged `override` in the tracking comparison, excluded from the tolerance maximum like a missed month and listed separately; the target comparison reports overridden names as `override`, never as a bug. No memo panel exists in Phase 4; Phase 5 adds one additively and the form keeps working with none.
10. **Tracking comparison (ADR 0005 check 1), rule fixed before the first order.** `paper report` opens a `kind=tracking` trial over [T_0, last completed T] on the real store with the frozen parameters (the Phase 3 window rule is extended: a `tracking` window must start at or after the first rebalance session after `holdout.end`; `tracking` trials never count in N, V or holdout spends), then computes per month i: the **raw difference** = paper return (ledger equity at close(T_{i+1}) over close(T_i), from `positions_daily`) minus the trial's month return; the **dividend term** = Σ over names held on each ex-date in the month of amount × ledger quantity, over equity at close(T_i), from `corporate_actions` rows with `known_at ≤ close(T_{i+1})`, excluding any dividend the broker credited (`dividend_cash`, req 6), so nothing is counted twice (what the backtest reinvested and paper never received); the **fill-timing term** = [Σ over buys of (paper fill price − the frozen `execution.fill_price` bar price on the fill session) × filled quantity − Σ over sells of the same] over equity at close(T_i), so a buy that paid more or a sell that received less than the bar price makes the term **positive**, the amount by which execution timing lowered paper's return; the **residual** = raw + dividend term + fill-timing term (both terms add back what paper lost relative to the backtest for reasons the backtest cannot model); the **modelled cost per rebalance** = the trial's base-level `cost_paid` at T_i over its equity at close(T_i). The check applies `paper.tracking_k` × that month's modelled cost to the series the window's frozen `tracking_rule` names: `raw` (ADR 0005 as written) or `residual` (open question 4; it needs an ADR 0005 amendment merged before `paper start`); months flagged `missed_rebalance` or `override` are excluded from the maximum and listed separately, and months with `skip_*` decisions are listed with their skipped names (not excluded); all four series are printed every time. It also compares, per fill session, the decisions' targets with the trial's `trial_weights`: a difference is reported as `override` when the name was overridden, as `late_data` when any bar, action or filing for the name has `known_at ≤ close(T_i)` and `ingested_at` later than the plan's recorded `store_max_ingested_at` (the trial saw a row the plan could not; ADR 0003 rule 2 stamps late bars at the session close), and as `bug` otherwise (a look-ahead or determinism fault). Backtest conventions paper cannot copy are listed with each report: `stale_exit`, `missing_fill` on the backtest side, the exit at the last close, sells and buys at one price versus two phases.
11. **Alerts.** An `alerts` table (kind ∈ `{run_failed, halted, stale_data, kill_switch, kill_switch_write_failed, reconciliation, rejection_cap, skip_cap, unresolved_order, missed_run, missed_rebalance, drawdown, locked}`, run id, message) is the source of truth, written **before** the exception propagates. Delivery channels from `alerts.channels`: `store` (always), `macos` (`osascript` notification), `email` (SMTP from `ALERT_SMTP_HOST`, `ALERT_SMTP_USER`, `ALERT_SMTP_PASSWORD`, `ALERT_EMAIL_TO`; spend stays $0). Each delivery attempt is an `alert_deliveries` row (channel, outcome, error); a failure never fails the run. One alert per (kind, run). The operations page lists them.
12. **Operations page** (design standard, Phase 4 row; pure `execution.ops` functions over a read-only connection, shared with `paper status`). Header: "as of" (store's last `known_at`) and "last updated" (last `paper_run_results` row, with a stale chip when S−1 has no run). KPI row: positions (count, value), open orders, today's signals (targets count), kill-switch state as a status chip (derived state). Hero: the latest plan's ranking with reasons (`selected`, `below_cut`, `excluded_no_history` from `signals`; `left_universe`, `override`, `skip_*` from `decisions`), the target set in the accent colour. Supporting: fills table; a journal-chain detail view per order; alerts list; reconciliation status. Read-only; renders the shell's empty and busy states; no colour literal in page code.
13. **Wash-sale lot ledger (G7 scope for Phase 4).** Kept in Phase 4 on purpose: paper has no tax effect, but Phase 6 needs the ledger before its first taxable trade and paper fills are the only real fill stream to test it on before then. Pure `execution.lots` over journaled fills writes `lots` (one per acquisition: account id and type, owner, `security_id`, broker symbol, nullable CUSIP from `assets`, trade date as UTC instant and exchange-local date, quantity, cost basis) and `disposals` (lots closed FIFO, trade date, quantity, proceeds, realised gain or loss before adjustment, tax year by trade date). For each loss disposal it scans acquisitions from `WASH_SALE_WINDOW_DAYS` before to after (a named constant from IRC §1091, not config, like `MONTHS_PER_YEAR`), across every account in the ledger, matches replacement shares to the loss in acquisition order with each replacement share absorbing one loss and losses processed in disposal order (G7 R11, R13), keeps same-day loss and gain blocks apart (R14), and writes `wash_sale_flags` with the matched lots, quantities and disallowed amount, re-scanning as later acquisitions arrive, including across December 31 (R15). Phase 4 holds one account (paper); the schema carries the account columns so Phase 6 can add others. Basis and holding-period carry-forward, broker 1099-B figures and the substantially-identical policy (G7 items 6 to 9 and 11) are Phase 6. The ledger's acceptance criteria gate **its task**, not the phase close: `paper check` does not read it.
14. **Entry gate, start and stop.** `paper start --hypothesis <slug>` refuses unless `owner_decisions` holds a `gap_signoff` row referencing an `ok`, non-synthetic, `in_sample` trial of that hypothesis (ADR 0009 point 3); the hypothesis's frozen `holdout.end` is a completed month-end; `account()` succeeds on the paper endpoint; and the account is **flat** (no positions, no open orders). It appends a `paper_windows` row: hypothesis id, T_0, account id, starting equity, code version, and `frozen_json`: the **entire `risk.*` section** plus `paper.tracking_k`, `paper.min_rebalances`, `paper.tracking_rule` and `paper.min_override_reason_chars`, canonicalised and hashed as the registry does for hypothesis parameters. Runs, the wrapper, `paper report` and `paper check` read those values from the window, never from live `Settings`; changing a risk rule mid-window means `paper stop`, an ADR (development-process rule 7) and a new window. A second `paper start` while a window is open is refused; `paper stop --reason` appends a `paper_window_stops` row. Every run checks `account().account_id` against the open window.
15. **Exit-criteria command.** `tradepartner paper check` runs, on the real store, against the open window's frozen values: (1) `rebalance`-kind `ok` runs on fill sessions with `invoked_by=scheduler` ≥ `min_rebalances` (tty runs on fill sessions are listed, not failing); (2) the req 10 tracking check under the frozen rule, read from the **latest `ok` tracking trial that `paper report` opened for this window**, which must end at the last completed rebalance session (else the check fails as `report stale`), over at least `min_rebalances` compared months that are not excluded; (3) no incomplete chain once due; (4) no override whose trimmed reason is shorter than the frozen minimum (and, from Phase 3, no engine run without a trial: true by construction, including plan trials). Exit 0 only when all pass; each line names its query. `paper status` prints the operations-page numbers.
16. **CLI.** `tradepartner paper start|stop|run|reconcile|kill|resume|resolve|report|check|status`. No flag selects a live endpoint, a session, a store path, or bypasses the kill switch or a limit (tests assert the parser has none); `--reason` is required by `stop`, `kill`, `resume`, `resolve` and the override kinds; output contains no secret-shaped string. `paper run` is what the scheduler calls; the Phase 2 T22 runbook gains its plist, `TRADEPARTNER_INVOKED_BY`, and the pre-open start time.

## Acceptance criteria (testable)

Interface and adapter
- [ ] `FakeBroker` and `AlpacaBroker` (on recorded responses) each return `account` (with `account_id`), `open_orders`, `get_order` (raising `UnknownOrderError` for an unknown id), `fills(since)` with `broker_fill_id`, and `assets`; `OrderStatus` is exactly `{ACCEPTED, FILLED, EXPIRED, REJECTED, CANCELLED}` and its terminal set exactly `{FILLED, EXPIRED, REJECTED, CANCELLED}` (tests on the enum; no `PENDING`, no `OPEN`); an `OrderRequest` with both or neither of `notional` and `quantity` raises; a partially filled order is `ACCEPTED` with `filled_quantity` and `filled_avg_price` set.
- [ ] ADR 0007 Task A: a clock that returns a naive value, an overflowing value, a non-`datetime`, or raises makes `submit` raise `ClockError` (not `ValueError`) with no order recorded and the id reusable, and makes `FakeBroker.simulate_fill` raise `ClockError` with the order unchanged; `ClockError` is not a `ValueError` subclass.
- [ ] `Settings(alpaca={"paper": False})` raises; `AlpacaBroker` without `ALPACA_PAPER_API_KEY` raises a credentials error whose text contains no key; every recorded fixture passes the scrub patterns; the base URL in every recorded request is the paper endpoint; AST: `adapters/alpaca_broker.py` imports nothing from `tradepartner.execution`.
- [ ] Status mapping: every recorded status maps per the req 2 table; a status absent from the table (injected) raises `SystemFaultError`; a transport timeout is retried `alpaca.trading_max_retries` times then raised (fake transport counting calls); requests are paced to `alpaca.trading_requests_per_minute` (fake clock).
- [ ] Symbol mapping: a class-share ticker from the master maps to the broker's form and back; a symbol the master does not know at close(S−1) is refused before any request.

Wrapper, limits and halt path
- [ ] AST: no module outside `src/tradepartner/execution/` imports `AlpacaBroker` or `FakeBroker` (tests excepted); no `.submit(` or `.cancel(` call exists outside `execution/wrapper.py`; `execution/{risk,ids,reconcile,ledger,lots,plan}.py` contain no numeric literals other than 0, 1, −1, 2 and `WASH_SALE_WINDOW_DAYS`; nothing under `src/tradepartner/backtest/` imports `store.journal` or names a `JOURNAL_TABLE_NAMES` table (text check).
- [ ] Two phases on a non-auto-filling fake: sells are submitted first; no buy is submitted until every sell is terminal or `paper.sell_wait_seconds` has passed (fake clock); buys are sized from `account().cash` after the sells and never from `buying_power` (a fake with `buying_power` far above cash sizes to cash); the sizing equals the req 3 formula on a three-name hand-computed case, with every buy scaled by the same factor; a sell still open at the deadline is journaled `missing_fill` and the buys total at most the cash the filled sells raised plus prior cash; cash is never negative.
- [ ] Each batch limit, overridden one at a time in the window's frozen values (`risk.max_position_weight`, `risk.max_order_notional_fraction`, `risk.max_gross_exposure`, `risk.max_orders_per_run`), turns a passing phase into a `LimitBreachError` naming the rule with **zero** `submit` calls (recording fake), and the pending rebalance is journaled `missed_rebalance` so a resumed run does not retry it; a sell above the reconciled quantity does the same; a sell quantity is rounded down to `alpaca.quantity_decimals` (never up); an environment override of any frozen `risk.*` key after `paper start` changes nothing in the run.
- [ ] Per-name skips: a non-tradable symbol, a notional below `risk.min_order_notional` and a name delisted at close(S−1) are each journaled with their `skip_*` reason while the rest of the phase submits; a non-fractionable symbol is bought by whole-share quantity rounded down and skipped as `skip_below_one_share` only when that is zero; a full exit below the minimum is journaled `dust`, appears again on the next fill session and does not count toward the cap; other skips above `risk.max_skips_per_run` halt with `SkipCapError` before any submit.
- [ ] Kill mid-run: an `engaged` row (or an `engage_kill_switch` override) written between the sells and the buys phase stops the buys with no submit, journals the remaining decisions `skipped_kill_switch` and ends the run `skipped_kill_switch` without a fault row; a second `paper run` while one holds the lock exits non-zero `locked` with a `locked` alert and closes nothing as `crashed`.
- [ ] Clock pre-check: a reading before the last `ok` ingest's `finished_at`, one after close(S) + `risk.clock_max_sessions_late` sessions, a non-monotonic reading between the two phases, and a broker `filled_at` more than `risk.max_broker_clock_skew_seconds` ahead of the clock each raise `ClockError` with no further `submit`; the wrapper and adapter share one clock object (identity test).
- [ ] The allowlist constants' exact contents are pinned (`cancel`: `{OrderNotOpenError}`; every other method: empty); `ClockError`, `ValueError`, an adapter transport error and an exception of a type defined inside the test each halt in the req 4 order: a `kill_switch` `engaged` row with source `fault` is the first row written, then the fault, then best-effort cancels of this run's non-terminal orders each journaled (`OrderNotOpenError` journaled as finished; any other cancel exception journaled `cancel_failed` with the halt standing), then the alert, then the result row `halted`, then the exception propagates; `StaleDataError` halts, alerts and writes `stale` **without** an `engaged` row.
- [ ] Kill-switch write failure: with the store locked past `store.lock_retry_seconds`, the halt delivers `kill_switch_write_failed` through the non-store channels (fakes) and exits non-zero; the next run derives `engaged` from the unfinished run.
- [ ] `orders` rows and their `pending` events exist before the first `submit` (a fake whose `submit` inspects the store); every `OrderRequest` of a phase is built before its first `submit`; a `ValueError` from building the last one halts with zero submits; the `accepted` event carries the broker's order id.
- [ ] `client_order_id` is a pure function of (prefix, run session, security id, side) and fits the recorded length limit; after a crash midway through a phase, a same-session re-run re-submits every journaled order of S with its journaled fields: the ones the fake already holds raise `DuplicateClientOrderIdError`, are fetched and journaled `replay` with no new order, the ones the fake never received are accepted fresh, and decisions with no `orders` row are sized now and submitted; a duplicate whose fields differ halts; a fetch that raises halts; a catch-up on S+1 derives new ids.
- [ ] Unknown state: a `pending` order from an **earlier** session that the fake reports unknown makes the run halt with `UnresolvedOrderError`; `paper resolve <id> --reason` journals `cancelled` with reason `not_received`; `paper resume` is refused while any order is `pending` or unresolved and accepted afterwards.
- [ ] Rejections: `risk.max_rejections_per_run` + 1 asynchronous `rejected` statuses on one run's orders, observed by the next run's collection, halt that next run with `RejectionCapError` naming the submitting run; a run whose every order is rejected halts even below the cap; each `rejected` is a terminal chain event.

Kill switch and reconciliation
- [ ] `paper kill --reason` appends an `engaged` row; a subsequent fill-session run collects, reconciles, marks, submits nothing and journals `skipped_kill_switch`; `paper resume` without `--reason` is refused; with a failing reconciliation it is refused and the switch stays engaged; with a passing one it appends `released` with the reconciliation id; an earlier run left `crashed`, `failed`, `halted` or without a result row, with no `released` row after it, derives `engaged` even with no `engaged` row, and the reading run never counts itself; no `Settings` override changes a derived state; an `engage_kill_switch` override takes effect at the next `mark` run.
- [ ] Drawdown: ledger equity below the window peak by more than the frozen `risk.max_drawdown` engages the switch with source `drawdown` and alerts; at exactly the threshold it does not; after `paper resume` the peak equals the equity recorded on the release row and the trigger does not re-fire until a new crossing.
- [ ] Reconciliation on the fake: a broker position the ledger lacks, a ledger position the broker lacks, a quantity difference above `risk.reconcile_quantity_tolerance`, a cash difference above `risk.reconcile_cash_tolerance`, an unexplained open order, a foreign-prefix order and a different `account_id` each produce `ReconciliationError`, a `reconciliations` row naming the mismatch, an engaged switch and an alert; a split with `ex_date ≤ S` known at close(S−1), a delisting known then (with the cash change journaled `corporate_action_cash`), a spin-off child known then (journaled `spinoff_receipt` and sold whole by the next run) and a credited dividend (`dividend_cash`) each explain their difference and pass; a split known only after close(S−1) does not (no look-ahead in the explanation); an `ok` reconciliation records the broker's cash and the ledger's cash is that value plus later journaled deltas (a fake that rounds each fill to the cent stays within `risk.reconcile_cash_tolerance` over the frozen `min_rebalances` months of fills); with a stale store the staleness halt comes before any reconciliation, so a split for S missing from the store never engages the switch.
- [ ] Split on the session: a 2:1 split with `ex_date = S` on a held name marks at close(S−1) with the pre-split quantity and raw close (no jump), reconciles on S with the doubled quantity, and produces no sell, no limit breach and no drawdown change (fixture case).
- [ ] `tests/lookahead/`: with the harness extended to cut `JOURNAL_TABLE_NAMES` by `known_at` as well, the plan, the ledger, the reconciliation explanations and the mark of a run on S are unchanged when the store is truncated at close(S−1); a bar revision known after close(S−1) leaves them unchanged and changes the run on S+1.

Tracking run and journal
- [ ] On the fixture store with the fake broker and a fixed clock: a `mark` session writes `positions_daily` and no order, and after two sessions without a run the next run writes the two missing sessions' marks too; a fill session opens a plan trial (`kind=tracking`, referenced by a `paper_plans` row, closed `ok` with no metrics, `family_sharpes` unchanged), journals one `signals` row per universe member with a reason, one `decisions` row per held-or-target name (a held name outside the universe with reason `left_universe`), the decisions **before** any `orders` row, and one `orders` row per submitted decision; a name leaving the targets is sold by quantity equal to the reconciled holding; the plan's targets for F_i equal the engine's `targets[F_i]` for the same store and frozen settings (the paper-equals-backtest oracle); `paper_plans.n_orders_below_min_at_live_capital` equals the hand count at `paper.live_capital_reference`; `paper_plans.store_max_ingested_at` equals the store's value at plan time.
- [ ] Every provider read of the plan carries `t = close(T_i)` (recording provider); no adapter is imported by the plan (AST, as in Phase 3); a plan trial opened but rolled back leaves the run `failed` with no order.
- [ ] Submit window: a fill-session run whose clock is outside [open(S) − `paper.submit_window_before_open_minutes`, open(S) + `paper.submit_window_after_open_minutes`] journals `missed_rebalance` pending and submits nothing; inside it, sells go before the open and buys after.
- [ ] Missed rebalance: with no run on F_i, the run on F_i + 1 is `catch_up`, trades the decisions journaled for T_i (or plans at close(T_i) if none exist, never at a later close), with ids derived from its own session, and journals `missed_rebalance`; a run beyond `paper.max_catch_up_sessions` journals `missed_rebalance` and trades nothing; a run that finds no row for S−1 writes a `missed_run` alert; an unfinished earlier run is closed `crashed` before anything else.
- [ ] Staleness: a store whose last `ok` ingest does not cover S−1 for `ingest.reference_symbol` halts with `StaleDataError` before any plan read, journals `stale`, alerts, leaves the switch as it was, and on a fill session leaves the rebalance pending.
- [ ] Forced exits: a held name whose listing end is known at close(S−1) is sold whole on S through the wrapper (it appears in the batch checks); an untradable one is journaled `untradable` and marked at its last close; a `spinoff_receipt` is sold whole on the next run; a plan target whose listing ended at close(S−1) is journaled `skip_delisted`.
- [ ] Chain: after the fake fills, expires, rejects and cancels one order each, partially fills one, and never receives one (resolved by `paper resolve`), `paper check`'s chain query returns zero incomplete chains once outcomes are due; the outcome rows carry the req 8 kind per terminal state and side (filled buy: return to the mark at close(T_{i+1}); filled sell: realised P&L on FIFO lots; not executed: the name's return over the horizon; partial then expired: both); a deliberately deleted terminal event makes the query return that order; every journal row has tz-aware UTC `known_at ≤ ingested_at`; fills carry the broker's `filled_at` in `filled_at` and the collection time in `known_at`; `store/journal.py` contains no `UPDATE` or `DELETE`.
- [ ] Overrides: the form (headless) refuses an empty, whitespace-only and too-short reason, writes exactly one row through a write connection and shows "store busy" when the store is locked; `exclude_name` removes the name from the next plan's targets, sells it if held and leaves its weight in cash; `keep_name` keeps a held name at its drifted weight with no order; the decision row carries the override id and `override`; the resulting order passes the same limit checks (a limit-breaking override halts); the page renders with no memo table present.

Tracking comparison, report and check
- [ ] `paper report` on the fixture store opens a `kind=tracking` trial with the frozen parameters over [T_0, last completed T]; a `tracking` window starting on or before `holdout.end`'s rebalance session is refused; the trial leaves N, V and holdout spends unchanged (`family_sharpes`, `family_holdout_spends`).
- [ ] With a synthetic ledger whose monthly returns equal the trial's, both rules pass; a raw difference of exactly `tracking_k` × modelled cost passes and one above it fails under `raw`, naming the month; a synthetic dividend on a held name makes the dividend term equal amount × quantity over equity and the residual pass where raw fails, and the same dividend with a `dividend_cash` adjustment contributes zero; a synthetic buy filled above the close and a synthetic sell filled below it, with `execution.fill_price=close`, each make the fill-timing term **positive** and the residual equal the hand-computed value; a month flagged `missed_rebalance` or `override` is excluded from the maximum and listed separately, a month with a `skip_*` decision is listed with the name and not excluded; the rule applied is the window's frozen one, not live `Settings`.
- [ ] Target comparison: equal weights on the fixture store pass; a weight moved by hand in `trial_weights` is reported `bug` naming the session and name; an overridden name is reported `override`; a bar for a name inserted with `known_at ≤ close(T_i)` and `ingested_at` after the plan's `store_max_ingested_at` makes that name's difference `late_data`.
- [ ] `paper check` exits 0 on a fixture with the frozen `min_rebalances` scheduler-invoked `ok` fill-session runs, as many compared non-excluded months, a `paper report` trial ending at the last completed rebalance, complete chains, a passing tracking check and reasoned overrides, and exits non-zero naming the failing check when any one is broken (one short of the run count; one short of the compared months because of an excluded month; a report trial ending a month early, `report stale`; an incomplete chain; a tracking miss; an override row inserted directly with a whitespace-padded reason shorter than the frozen minimum); a `tty` run on a fill session is listed and does not fail the check; the check never reads the wash-sale tables.

Alerts and pages
- [ ] Each alert kind is written before its exception propagates (store inspected in the `except` of the test); the `macos` and `email` channels are called with the row's message (fakes); a channel that raises leaves an `alert_deliveries` row recording the failure and the run's status unchanged; one `alerts` row per (kind, run).
- [ ] Headless render of the operations page shows the header dates and the stale chip, the four KPI tiles with the derived kill-switch state, the ranking with reasons, the fills table, a chain view for one order, the alerts list and the reconciliation status, reading only through the shell's read-only connection; the shell's empty and busy states render; no colour literal in page code (the Phase 2 test extended).
- [ ] The CLI parser exposes no `--live`, `--session`, `--store-path` or bypass flag (test on the parser); `stop`, `kill`, `resume`, `resolve` without `--reason` exit non-zero; output passes the `cli_record` scrub patterns.

Wash-sale ledger
- [ ] Pub 550 Example 1 (G7 R12) as synthetic fills: the 100-share loss of 2025-01-03 is matched 50 to the 2024-12-13 lot and 25 to the 2024-12-20 lot, disallowed amount 750, 25 shares deductible; G7 R15's December sale with a January purchase is flagged across the tax-year boundary; a replacement share that absorbed one loss is not matched to a second (R13); a same-day loss block and gain block are flagged and reported apart, never netted (R14); a later acquisition inside the window re-scans an earlier loss; a gain disposal produces no flag; `lots` rows carry trade date, not settlement date, and the account columns.

Real-store evidence (owner tasks, not unit tests)

## Out of scope

Live trading, a live endpoint, live keys and live sizing (Phase 6; `alpaca.paper` is guarded, and the sizing trigger from H1's Q1 is reported by `n_orders_below_min_at_live_capital`, not settled: open question 11). Account type, employer compliance, and G7 items 6 to 9 and 11 (Phase 6, before any taxable live trade). Any LLM call, memo table or memo panel (ADR 0008; Phase 5 adds them additively). The dashboard-technology ADR's content (open question 1) and the risk-rules ADR's content (open question 12); this spec's page and limit requirements are what either records. Changing H1's frozen fill convention or cost defaults (a new hypothesis, never an edit; the decomposition in req 10 measures the gap instead). Limit orders, OPG, extended hours, bracket orders, shorting, leverage, options. A slippage key for the backtest (#86 report: it belongs with the fill-convention decision, open question 4). Recalibrating `costs.per_side_bps` from paper fills (Phase 3 open question 3: a retro item once fills exist). Weekly cadence (STATUS "Decisions needed"). Intra-month re-planning of any kind: the only intra-month trades are ADR 0006's forced exits and the sale of a spin-off receipt. Dividend receipt in paper (Alpaca does not simulate it; the dividend term accounts for it).

## Risks & domain checks

- **Look-ahead:** the plan is the engine's own function at `t = close(T_i)` over `StoreProvider` under a plan trial; the ledger, the mark and the reconciliation explanations use only rows known at close(S−1); the truncation test cuts the fact **and** journal tables; the tracking trial is the Phase 3 engine and inherits its invariance suites; nothing under `backtest/` can read the journal (text check); the paper-equals-backtest oracle catches a plan that drifts from the engine, and the `late_data` category keeps a late-ingested bar from being mistaken for one.
- **Point-in-time journal:** every link of a chain has its own `known_at` from the shared clock, with the broker's instants in their own columns and a skew bound (ADR 0007's "revisit if" on broker-sourced timestamps is answered: they never become a `known_at`); nothing is updated in place; a plan trial ties every engine read to a trial id.
- **Order path (development-process rules 5 and 7):** limits, the kill switch and the halt path are merged and tested before the first paper order, and every limit is frozen into the window so no environment variable can loosen one mid-phase; only the wrapper submits; the allowlist is empty with its per-candidate reasoning recorded; a phase is all-or-nothing against the batch limits and a breach abandons the rebalance rather than retrying it; ids are deterministic and `orders` rows precede submits, so a crash replays instead of doubling; one run at a time holds the lock; the kill-switch row is the first thing a halt writes, the state is derived from every earlier run's result, and it is re-read at each phase boundary, so a lost write, a crash or a kill during a run cannot be missed; a pending order the broker never received stops everything until the owner resolves it; buys are funded only by cash the sells raised, never by margin; the adapter cannot reach a live endpoint (guarded key, separate paper keys); `safety-reviewer` runs on every Phase 4 PR touching `execution/`, `adapters/alpaca_broker.py`, `store/journal.py`, the override page or `.env.example`.
- **LLM inputs:** none (ADR 0008). The override form carries no memo and is never blocked by one.
- **Costs and fill realism:** paper fills at the NBBO after the open (#86), the frozen convention is the F_i close, and the two-phase execution means sells and buys fill at different times; ADR 0005's tolerance (`paper.tracking_k` × modelled cost, about 20 bp a month at H1's turnover and 15 bp) can be exceeded by fill timing alone, and paper never receives the dividends the backtest reinvests. Req 10 prints the raw difference with both terms every time, and the rule the phase is judged by is **frozen at `paper start`** (open question 4), so no criterion changes after results exist (ADR 0005 "Reversibility"). Paper does not simulate market impact or price improvement (#86 S27), so the paper stage measures the simulator, not live execution.
- **Backtest conventions paper cannot copy:** `stale_exit` and `missing_fill` on the backtest side, the exit at the last close, one fill price for sells and buys; each is listed in every report as a known source of difference.
- **Live sizing (H1 Q1 trigger):** at `paper.live_capital_reference` most H1 orders sit at the fractional minimum; `n_orders_below_min_at_live_capital` reports it per rebalance so Phase 6's ADR decides on evidence; the deferral itself is open question 11.
- **Survivorship:** the tracking trial records the gap per rebalance like any trial; forced exits follow the engine's last-close convention; a paper position in a name the broker removes must be explained by a known delisting or the run halts.
- **Corporate actions at a simulated broker:** how Alpaca paper applies splits, spin-offs and mergers is unverified; the recording task answers it before the adapter task closes, and the reconciliation explanations are written so a simulator that does nothing on a split still halts loudly rather than drifting.
- **Reconciliation drift:** split-adjusted comparison with frozen tolerances; the owner never trades in the paper account by hand (a foreign order is a fault by design, open question 9); the account id is checked every run.
- **Unattended machine:** a run that never starts cannot alert; the next run's `missed_run` alert and the page's stale chip are the detection; the submit window, ADR 0006's missed-rebalance rule and `paper.max_catch_up_sessions` bound how stale a traded plan can be; the plist is the T22 runbook's, extended.
- **Single writer:** the run opens write connections per chunk (journal writes, the plan trial) and read-only ones for reads; the override form's write retries per `store.lock_retry_seconds` and shows busy; the ingest job runs after the close, outside the run's window.
- **Secrets:** paper keys and SMTP credentials are `SecretStr`; scrub tests on fixtures and CLI output; no secret in any exception.
- **Spend:** Alpaca paper, `osascript` and SMTP cost nothing (ADR 0009 budget).
- **Alpaca facts unverified:** quantity precision, id length, same-session proceeds in `cash`, corporate-action handling, CUSIP on assets, non-fractionable share of the universe, the fills endpoint and the request limit are all recording-task questions (req 2); the adapter refuses to construct without the recorded precision, and an unmapped status halts.
- **Owner-only steps:** recording paper responses, `paper start`, `kill`/`resume`/`resolve`, the gap sign-off, ADR 0009's acceptance and the phase-close evidence need the owner's keys and machine.

## Open questions

Answers go on #247. Questions 4 and 12 need an answer before `paper start`; the rest before the task that implements them.

1. **Dashboard technology ADR** (design standard, "Open decision, deferred", due now that the operations page is specified). Options: (a) Streamlit with the design standard, the override form as a Streamlit form writing through `open_for_write`; (b) a React front end behind a thin read-only API plus one write endpoint for overrides, amending the roadmap's "no separate API" rule. Recommendation: (a); the roadmap's rule stands, the write is one form, and `execution.ops` keeps the page logic reusable if (b) is chosen later. The ADR is a plan prerequisite of the page task; this spec's page requirements do not depend on it.
2. **Alert channels.** Options: (a) `[store, macos]`: free, no secret, seen only at the Mac; (b) `[store, macos, email]` with SMTP credentials in `.env`; (c) a push service (paid or terms-bound). Recommendation: (a) by default, with `email` available when the owner sets the four `ALERT_*` variables; (c) needs a budget change.
3. **Drawdown trigger** `risk.max_drawdown`. Options: (a) 0.30, a checkpoint below H1's expected worst quarter (−18 to −19%) and above a bad month; (b) no drawdown trigger in paper (a total loss of ~$100 is an accepted outcome, ADR 0005); (c) 0.20. Recommendation: (a): the trigger is a forced owner review of the process, released with a reason, not an exit rule, and paper is where the rehearsal is cheap.
4. **The comparison rule, fixed before the first order.** H1 froze `execution.fill_price=close`; paper fills at the NBBO after the open in two phases and receives no dividends, so ADR 0005's raw rule may fail on execution timing and dividends alone. Probe 3 (#182) settles, before paper starts: whether paper DAY orders submitted pre-open fill within a minute of the open (fixing `paper.submit_window_*` and `paper.sell_wait_seconds`); the observed gap between those fills and the bar open and close (the size of the fill-timing term); and whether an `open` convention with a slippage allowance would track paper better. ADR 0006 also says the fallback convention is "T+1 close for both backtest and paper, never a mix", and a paper fill at the NBBO after the open against a `close` backtest is a mix. Options: (a) `paper.tracking_rule=raw`, ADR 0005 as written, accepting that the phase may fail its check for a known, measured reason; (b) `paper.tracking_rule=residual` (raw + dividend term + fill-timing term), recorded as one amendment to ADR 0005 and ADR 0006 **merged before `paper start`**, citing Probe 3 and #86 as the reason and stating that paper executes in two phases at the NBBO after the open while the backtest fills at the close, with the raw series always printed beside the residual; (c) register H1b with `open` fills and a new `execution.slippage_bps` key before paper starts, and track that (also an ADR 0006 amendment, since the bar open is not the auction). Recommendation: (b): the amendment cites evidence gathered before any paper result exists, which is what ADR 0005's reversibility clause asks for, and the raw number stays on record. Whichever is chosen is frozen into `paper_windows` and cannot move afterwards.
5. **Reconciliation tolerances** (`risk.reconcile_quantity_tolerance=1e-6`, `risk.reconcile_cash_tolerance=0.01`). The ledger's cash is re-based at every `ok` reconciliation, so the cash tolerance only has to absorb one run's per-fill rounding. Recommendation: accept, set both from the recording task's precision and rounding answers, and freeze them at `paper start` with the rest of `risk.*`.
6. **Catch-up window** `paper.max_catch_up_sessions=5`. Options: (a) 5, mirroring `gap.missing_tail_sessions`; (b) 1, so a missed fill session is simply a missed month; (c) unbounded per ADR 0006's "next available session". Recommendation: (a): ADR 0006 says trade at the next available session, and five sessions bounds how stale a plan can be traded.
7. **Paper account equity at start.** The tracking check is scale-free (fractional shares, zero commissions), so the amount only decides how many orders sit near the minimum. Options: (a) Alpaca's paper default, which matches `backtest.initial_capital`'s default, with the live-scale count reported per rebalance; (b) the charter's live amount, rehearsing the minimum-notional problem for real, at the cost of a tracking check that fails by construction at H1's position count. Recommendation: (a); `paper start` records whatever the flat account holds.
8. **Halt and open orders.** Options: (a) best-effort cancel of this run's non-terminal orders on any halt, each outcome journaled (spec'd); (b) leave them (DAY orders expire at the close) and reconcile at the next run. Recommendation: (a): a phase was validated as a whole, and a half-executed phase is a state no rule approved.
9. **Owner trades in the paper account.** Options: (a) forbidden: any foreign order or position is a reconciliation fault (spec'd); (b) tolerated with a journaled `external` explanation. Recommendation: (a); every override goes through the form so it is logged with a reason (rule 8).

Recommendation: (a): cheap now, and the recording shows whether the field exists.
11. **Live sizing stays deferred.** H1's Q1 answer says this spec "must settle live sizing before any order". Options: (a) settle it here: choose fewer names or more capital now, which is a new hypothesis or a charter amendment; (b) defer explicitly to a Phase 6 ADR, with `n_orders_below_min_at_live_capital` reported every paper rebalance as its evidence. Recommendation: (b): no paper order depends on live sizing, and the number the ADR needs does not exist until paper runs.
