# Spec: Event data, v1 collectors

**Status:** Draft  ·  **Issue:** #306  ·  **Related:** [charter](../charter.md) (Scope, "Deferred beyond the MVP"), [roadmap](../roadmap.md) ("MVP scope"), [ADR 0003](../decisions/0003-data-adapters-local-first.md) (adapters, local-first), [ADR 0005](../decisions/0005-objective-benchmark-stop-criteria.md) (integrity criterion), [ADR 0008](../decisions/0008-llm-role.md) (no LLM, points 1 and 3), [ADR 0009](../decisions/0009-price-vendor.md) (budget USD 0), [data-foundation spec](data-foundation.md) (req 1 store and lock, req 6 adapters, req 9 ingest), [backtest spec](backtest.md) (req 8 trial count, req 9 registry), [strategy-lab spec](strategy-lab.md) (family rules, `FAMILY_PARENTS`, fixture marker, quiet intervals), research [G2 insider purchases](../research/2026-09-26-g2-insider-purchases.md), [G5 search volume](../research/2026-09-26-g5-search-volume.md), [free-data terms](../research/2026-09-25-free-data-terms.md) (7a EDGAR rate)

## Problem & why now

The system knows prices and a few filing facts. It does not know **what happened** to a company or to the market on a given day: an earnings release, an insider purchase, a bankruptcy notice, an executive order. Every later direction the owner has named needs that record, stamped with when it became knowable:

- new systematic signals (insider purchases: G2 found no post-2010 monthly-horizon test and said only our own backtest can close that gap);
- the Phase 5 LLM memo, whose input packet ADR 0008 requires to be fixed and timestamped;
- context and alerts for held names in Phase 4 ("why is this down 20%?", "a held company filed a bankruptcy 8-K");
- a later picks journal and override review;
- **event studies** ("what happened to prices after events like this?"), which get their own spec after Phase 3 closes (see "Follow-up: event studies" at the end).

**Why now, while the MVP is still in build:** some sources can only be collected going forward. EDGAR is point-in-time by construction (every filing has an SEC acceptance timestamp), so it can be backfilled honestly at any time. The Federal Register can be backfilled with a conservative stamp. News cannot: an archive fetched later is not what was knowable then (handoff §5, D3). Every month news is not collected is a month of evaluation history that can never be recovered. News collection is **new scope**: the charter defers news-text *signals* and the roadmap says "revisit after the MVP runs" for signals; collecting metadata forward is what open question 1 puts to the owner.

This spec is a **side track**. It does not block, and must not slow, the Phase 2–4 critical path: one team builds the collectors in parallel with Phase 4, the collectors write their own file, and the main store's ingest keeps its rate and its lock rules (req 6).

## Users & usage

- **The scheduler** runs `tradepartner collect` on its own interval (`collect.interval_minutes`) on every day, trading or not. It fetches new items from each enabled source, stores them raw, parses them and exits. It opens the main store **read-only only** (for the security master, req 2), never the broker or the paper account.
- **The owner** glances at the collection card on the data-health page (coverage and last success per source), runs `tradepartner collect --backfill --source S --since DATE` once per source, and later runs event studies under the follow-up spec. Only the owner runs anything against the real store or the real events file (CLAUDE.md rule 9).
- **Build agents** run collectors against recorded fixtures only.
- **Later phases** (Phase 4 ops page, strategy-lab hypotheses, Phase 5 memo, the event-study engine) read the event tables through the as-of API. None of them does so until its own spec says so (req 8).
- **Tests**, on every PR, run parsers on recorded payloads with the network disabled.

## Definitions

- **Event**: one stored record of something that happened, from one source, with `known_at` (when it became knowable to a market participant, tz-aware UTC), `ingested_at`, `source` and `provenance`, as in the data-foundation spec. An event names a **company by CIK** (or none, for a market-wide event such as an executive order), never a `security_id`. The security it concerns is resolved **at read time** as of `known_at` through the security master, so a later ticker change or class split cannot rewrite history.
- **Fan-out**: a company event resolves to **every** security of that CIK listed as of `known_at`. The as-of API returns the event once per resolved security with the count of securities it resolved to. Which of them a study observes is the study spec's decision. A Form 4 row names its security by the filing's security title; a title that matches exactly one `listings` class as of `known_at` resolves to that class alone, any other title falls back to the fan-out rule and the row is flagged `class_unresolved` and counted.
- **Event session** E of an event: the first XNYS session whose close is strictly after `known_at`. An 8-K accepted at 16:30 ET on a session belongs to the next session; the same rule handles half-days and holidays through `tradepartner.calendar`. Stored on no row; computed by the as-of API.
- **SEC business day**: a weekday that is not a federal holiday, from `tradepartner.calendar`'s federal calendar (added by this spec, never weekday arithmetic). The Form 4 "late" flag and the Federal Register gap rule use it.
- **Chunk**: for EDGAR sources one calendar month of accessions per source; for the Federal Register one calendar month of publication dates; for news one collection run. The unit of atomic commit, resume and idempotence (req 3).

## Requirements

1. **Separate events file.** Event tables live in their own DuckDB file (`events.path`), not in `store.path`. Collectors are its only writers. Every other reader (the health card, the as-of API, the future study engine) opens it with the short-lived `read_only=True` rule of the data-foundation spec req 1, attaching it beside the main store. **Lock rules in both directions:** the collector holds the events file read-write only while committing one chunk and releases it between chunks; a reader that holds it open blocks a commit, so the collector retries the lock for `events.lock_retry_seconds` and then exits non-zero with only its `collection_runs` row written (the ingest rule, data-foundation req 9). **One collector at a time:** `collect` takes a single-instance lock file under `collect.raw_dir` for the whole run; a second `collect` exits non-zero at once with a message naming the holder's pid and start time.
2. **Sources, v1.** Each source is an adapter behind one `EventSource` interface (thin raw fetch + pure parser, as ADR 0003 and data-foundation req 6), with a fixture twin. The security master is read from the main store **read-only** at the start of each run: every CIK the master has ever held, delisted names included, is the collector's company list; a filing by a CIK the master does not know is stored with a null resolution and counted (`unknown_cik`), never dropped.
   - **(a) EDGAR 8-K and 8-K/A**: accession, CIK, form, item numbers, acceptance timestamp. `known_at` = acceptance timestamp (`provenance = filing`). The submissions JSON the master ingest already caches carries every 8-K accession, its acceptance and its `items` string, so the collector **reads that cache** and fetches nothing per accession in v1. Primary documents are **not** fetched in v1 (no consumer yet; a follow-up spec adds them with a size measurement). The submissions cache is written atomically by ingest (temp file and rename, a change this spec makes to the ingest side) so a collector reading it mid-write sees the old file or the new one, never a partial one.
   - **(b) EDGAR Forms 4 and 4/A**: one row per reported non-derivative transaction: reporter CIK, reporter role (director, officer and title, 10% owner), transaction code, transaction date, shares, price, shares owned after, the security title, and whether the filing is late (accepted after the second SEC business day following the transaction date, under the deemed-execution rule G2 records for late-notified trades). Accessions come from the EDGAR **full-index** quarterly form index (`form.idx`) for backfill and the daily index for forward collection, both through the existing raw client; the XML primary document is fetched per accession. `known_at` = acceptance timestamp. G2's point-in-time notes apply: never the transaction date, never the deemed filing date.
   - **(c) Federal Register presidential documents and rules**: document number, type (executive order, proclamation, memorandum, rule, proposed rule), agencies, title, abstract, signing and publication dates, raw JSON cached. Identity is the document number; a document seen by both forward collection and backfill keeps the **earliest** stamp. `known_at`: forward collection = first fetch time (`provenance = snapshot`); backfill = **the close of the first XNYS session on or after the publication date** (the late rule; a session close is always later than publication, so no backfilled stamp precedes public availability). Backfill **skips** any document whose late-rule stamp would be later than the current time, counts it, and picks it up on the next run, so `known_at ≤ ingested_at` always holds.
   - **(d) News metadata**: **only if the owner decides so on open question 1.** Headline, source, URL, source-stated publication time, and entity tags if the source provides them; never full article text, and never a source-computed tone, sentiment or theme field (ADR 0008 point 3: no model-produced value enters the store). `known_at` = our fetch time (`snapshot`), always, and the row stores `stamp_resolution_minutes` = the `collect.interval_minutes` in force at the fetch. The source's publication time is stored as evidence and is never used as a stamp.
3. **Backfill.** `collect --backfill --since DATE --source S` for (a), (b) and (c) only; `--source news` with `--backfill` is refused before any request. Chunks are atomic, resumable and idempotent (data-foundation req 9): a failure commits nothing from that chunk, earlier chunks stand, a rerun resumes from the last committed chunk, and a completed chunk rerun changes nothing. Form 4 backfill defaults to `collect.form4_backfill_start = 2016-01-01`, matching price history; the owner may pass `--since 2013-01-01` to build the three prior years G2's opportunistic-insider classifier needs. The first collector task measures one quarter of Form 4 fetches and records the measured rate and the projected full-backfill duration in the plan before the owner starts it.
4. **Append-only, revisions as new rows.** Amendments (8-K/A, 4/A) are new rows carrying `amends_accession` = the original accession. Nothing is updated in place. As-of reads return rows with `known_at ≤ T`, latest revision as of T.
5. **Raw kept, within a budget.** Every parsed row points at a raw payload on disk (content hash, fetch time), so a parser fix can re-parse history without re-fetching: `collect --reparse --source S` rebuilds the parsed rows of a source from the cache as new rows at a new `ingested_at`, network disabled. Raw bytes are never sent anywhere else. The raw cache has a size budget, `collect.raw_size_budget_gb`, checked **before each fetch**; a run that would exceed it halts non-zero after its `collection_runs` row, rows already committed stand, and the health card shows the fill. The first collector task measures one quarter of Form 4 XML and sets the default from that measurement.
6. **Pace and terms.** EDGAR requests use the existing raw client and User-Agent. The 10 requests per second limit (free-data terms 7a) is enforced **across processes**: the raw client's limiter (`edgar_raw._RateLimiter`) gains a lock file under `edgar.cache_dir` holding the last request time, taken with an OS file lock around each wait, so `ingest`, `collect` and a manual backfill share one budget, `edgar.requests_per_second`, which stays at its current value. No process-local split, no `Settings` validator. The Federal Register is paced by `collect.federal_register_min_interval_seconds`, enforced by the same mechanism under `collect.raw_dir`. Each new source's terms (storage, redistribution, rate) are recorded in the free-data terms report **before its adapter merges**; the Federal Register line is the first task of the plan.
7. **Health and failure.** Each `collect` run writes a `collection_runs` row per source (started, finished, status, items fetched, items parsed, quarantined, `unknown_cik`, `class_unresolved`, skipped-for-stamp, last `known_at` seen, raw bytes added). Per-item parse failures are quarantined with the raw pointer and the error, counted, never dropped silently (the T11h rule, #263). The data-health page gets a collection card: per source, last success, items per day over the last 30 days, **gap days** (a day with zero items whose trailing 30-day median of items per day is above zero; for EDGAR sources only SEC business days are gap candidates, for the Federal Register only federal business days, for news every day), quarantined count and raw-cache fill against the budget. A collector failure of any kind, lock, network, parse or budget, never blocks `ingest`, `paper run` or a backtest: the collector shares no lock with them (req 1) and no code path of theirs imports it (req 8).
8. **Boundaries**, each a case in the tree-wide AST test pattern of `tests/execution/test_boundaries.py`:
   - Nothing under `backtest/`, `execution/` or `dashboard/` other than the health card's own module imports the `events` package or names an event table in SQL, until a spec names that reader.
   - Under `collect/` and `events/`, no import of `store.db`'s read-write opener and no `duckdb.connect` call without `read_only=True` for any path other than `events.path`; only `collect/` may open `events.path` read-write.
   - No event is a signal until a registered hypothesis uses it through the strategy-lab or backtest spec; news-derived signals need an ADR first (charter). ADR 0008 point 1's existing test covers LLM calls.
9. **As-of API.** `events_as_of(T, kinds, cik=None)` and `insider_transactions_as_of(T, cik=None)` over an attached read-only connection: rows with `known_at ≤ T`, latest revision as of T, each resolved to `security_id` as of its own `known_at` per the fan-out rule (so a CIK whose ticker changed between two events resolves each to the security listed at that event's `known_at`), with the event session E computed from the calendar. Pure over the connection; no caching across calls.

## Acceptance criteria (testable)

Stamps and revisions
- [ ] Given recorded 8-K, Form 4 and Federal Register payloads, when parsed, then every row has a tz-aware UTC `known_at` equal to the acceptance timestamp (EDGAR) or the stamping rule (Federal Register), and `known_at ≤ ingested_at` holds for every row of every fixture.
- [ ] Given a Form 4/A amending a Form 4, when read as of a time between the two acceptances, then only the original is returned; as of after, the amendment, with `amends_accession` set. The same for an 8-K/A.
- [ ] Given a Federal Register document published on Good Friday (a federal business day and an NYSE holiday), its backfill `known_at` is the close of the following Monday's session. Given one published today after the last close, backfill skips it, counts it, and the next run after the close stamps it.
- [ ] Given a document collected forward at fetch time t and later seen by a backfill, its stored `known_at` stays t.
- [ ] Given news collection (if enabled), then no news row has `known_at` earlier than its fetch time, each row carries `stamp_resolution_minutes`, and a payload with a tone or sentiment field stores none of it.

Collection mechanics
- [ ] Given an 8-K collection run on a fixture whose submissions cache holds the accession, the collector makes no EDGAR request for it (asserted on the recorded transport) and stores the `items` from the cache.
- [ ] Given a Form 4 whose security title matches one `listings` class as of `known_at`, it resolves to that class; given a title matching none, it resolves to every listed class and is flagged `class_unresolved`; given a CIK absent from the master, the row is stored with a null resolution and counted `unknown_cik`.
- [ ] Given a transaction dated Wednesday and accepted the following Monday, the row is `late`; accepted Friday, it is not (SEC business days, the federal calendar).
- [ ] Given a payload that fails to parse, then it is quarantined with its raw pointer and error, counted in `collection_runs`, and the rest of the chunk commits.
- [ ] Given a backfill interrupted mid-chunk and rerun, then no row is duplicated and none is missing; a completed chunk rerun writes no row. `collect --backfill --source news` exits non-zero before any request.
- [ ] Given a parser change and `collect --reparse`, the new rows are produced from the cache with the network disabled and the old rows remain, superseded as of the new `ingested_at`.
- [ ] Given a raw cache one document below `collect.raw_size_budget_gb`, the next run halts non-zero after its `collection_runs` row, the chunk in progress is not committed, and earlier chunks stand.

Locks, pace and isolation
- [ ] Given a reader holding the events file for longer than `events.lock_retry_seconds`, `collect` exits non-zero with only its `collection_runs` row; given a reader that closes within the window, it completes.
- [ ] Given a running `collect`, a second `collect` exits non-zero at once naming the holder.
- [ ] Given two processes making EDGAR requests through the raw client at once, the recorded request times never exceed `edgar.requests_per_second` in any one-second window; the Federal Register pacing holds likewise.
- [ ] Given a writer holding the main store's lock for the whole run, `collect` still completes (it reads the master through a short-lived read-only connection and retries that read for `store.lock_retry_seconds` like any reader).
- [ ] The boundary test fails on a fixture module under `collect/` that imports the read-write opener or connects without `read_only=True` to a non-events path, on one under `backtest/` or `execution/` that imports `events`, and on a module under `events/` that opens `events.path` read-write.

Health and API
- [ ] The collection card shows a gap on a fixture SEC business day with zero 8-Ks between days with items, no gap on the weekend, and the raw-cache fill; `health --check` warns on a source whose last success is older than `collect.max_age_hours`.
- [ ] Given a CIK whose ticker changed between two 8-Ks on the fixture, `events_as_of` resolves each event to the security listed at its own `known_at`, and an 8-K accepted at 16:30 ET gets the next session as E, including across a holiday and a half-day.

## Out of scope

- The event-study engine, the registry migration and the study page (follow-up spec, below).
- 8-K primary documents and any filing text; 10-K and 10-Q text and diffs ("lazy prices"); company exposure maps. Each is a follow-up once v1 runs and the size is measured.
- Any LLM use: reading filings, classifying events, extracting exposures (Phase 5, ADR 0008).
- Any signal, hypothesis or order that uses an event. That goes through strategy-lab or backtest specs, and for news through an ADR.
- Social media and search-volume collection (charter deferral; G5 graded search attention NOT SUPPORTED in large caps).
- Full news article text, intraday prices, paid data of any kind.
- Alerts from collected events. They need the Phase 4 alert machinery and its own requirement in the paper-trading spec.

## Data / interfaces

- **Files:** `events.path` (DuckDB, gitignored), raw cache under `collect.raw_dir`, the single-instance lock file under it, the EDGAR pacing lock file under `edgar.cache_dir`.
- **Event tables** (all with `known_at`, `ingested_at`, `source`, `provenance`): `filings_8k` (+ `filings_8k_items`), `insider_transactions`, `federal_register_documents`, `news_items` (only if enabled), `collection_runs`, `collection_quarantine`. Schema versioned on its own (`events_schema_version`, starting at 1); no change to the main store's schema or `TABLE_NAMES`.
- **Main store:** read-only reads of `securities` and `listings` for the CIK list and class resolution; no write, no migration.
- **CLI:** `tradepartner collect [--source edgar_8k|edgar_form4|federal_register|news|all] [--backfill --since DATE] [--reparse] [--dry-run]`.
- **As-of API:** `events.asof.events_as_of(conn, T, kinds, cik=None)`, `insider_transactions_as_of(conn, T, cik=None)`.
- **Config keys:** `events.path`, `events.lock_retry_seconds`, `collect.interval_minutes`, `collect.sources`, `collect.raw_dir`, `collect.raw_size_budget_gb`, `collect.form4_backfill_start` (default 2016-01-01), `collect.federal_register_min_interval_seconds`, `collect.max_age_hours`. `edgar.requests_per_second` is unchanged and becomes cross-process.
- **Calendar:** `tradepartner.calendar` gains the federal-holiday calendar for SEC business days.
- **Env vars:** none for EDGAR (existing User-Agent) and the Federal Register (no key). A news source may add one to `.env.example`.
- **Scheduling:** one more launchd job, `collect`, at `collect.interval_minutes`. It writes only the events file, so it needs no quiet interval against ingest or paper runs; EDGAR pacing is shared by the lock (req 6). The runbook of T22 gets its plist.

## Risks & domain checks

- **Look-ahead through stamps.** The biggest risk is a stamp earlier than true public availability. The rules are: acceptance time for EDGAR; fetch time for anything forward-collected; the late rule for Federal Register backfill, with the skip so no stamp lands in the future. Late stamps bias later studies toward "priced in", the safe direction. Executive orders are usually public at signing, days before Federal Register publication; the row keeps both dates so a study can say which it measures.
- **Survivorship.** Events for companies that later delisted must resolve to their securities. CIK-first storage, the master's full CIK history including delisted names, and read-time resolution handle that. An unknown CIK is kept and counted, never dropped.
- **Store contention.** Solved by the separate file, the chunked commit, the lock-retry in both directions and the single-instance lock. The cost is a second file to back up.
- **Terms and rate.** EDGAR is covered by the existing research and the cross-process lock. The Federal Register (US government, public API) and any news source need a terms line before merge (req 6).
- **Disk growth.** Measured before it is assumed: one quarter of Form 4 XML sets `collect.raw_size_budget_gb`; the collector halts at the budget rather than filling the disk. 8-K documents are deferred until measured.
- **Backfill duration.** Form 4 volume is large (hundreds of thousands of filings per year, an estimate to be replaced by the first task's measurement); at the shared EDGAR rate a full backfill runs for days and shares the budget with the nightly ingest. The owner starts it knowing the projection, and it resumes across reboots.
- **Order path / secrets / LLM.** None touched. `safety-reviewer` is not needed unless a news source adds a key. `quant-auditor` reviews the stamping rules and the as-of API.

## Follow-up: event studies (a separate spec, after T45b)

The first draft of this spec carried an event-study engine. Two reviews on PR #307 (spec-critic and the owner) found the same registry defects, and the engine is deferred to its own spec, to be written once Phase 3 has closed and the strategy-lab's `family_rules` and `FAMILY_PARENTS` exist. That spec must satisfy, at minimum:

- **Migration.** `trials.hypothesis_id` is NOT NULL and `kind` has an inline CHECK, so a study trial needs a staging-table rebuild of `trials` in one transaction (the `corporate_actions` version-4 precedent), at the next free schema version at merge time, with a row-for-row copy test and the owner's store copied before the pull.
- **Counting.** A study's family lives on `studies.family`; `family_sharpes`, `family_holdout_spends`, `list_trials` and the trials page resolve the family through both `hypotheses` and `studies`; N and V come from separate row sets (V reads `in_sample` trials only); `TrialHandle` carries `study_id`; a test proves N moves when a study trial completes.
- **Families.** A study belongs to the family of the hypothesis it could inform (`earnings`, `insider`, never `momentum` for those two), each added to `HypothesisFamily` and `FAMILY_PARENTS` in code. A study registration may be a family's **first** registration and then fixes its `family_rules` from the study file, as a sweep does; otherwise it reads the family's existing rules and is refused when they disagree. The holdout is never read from live `Settings`.
- **Holdout.** Events whose widest window would touch the family's frozen holdout are **dropped and counted**, not a reason to refuse the run; windows before `in_sample_start` likewise. A study never spends a holdout.
- **Diagnostic studies** (fills timing, Form 25 pre-delisting paths) are limited at registration to a closed list of non-event selection sources, exempt from `FAMILY_PARENTS` and counting as `oracle` is, and the fills timing study belongs with T70 in the paper-trading spec.
- **Engine semantics.** Window [a, b] return defined as close(E+a−1) to close(E+b); the post-exit abnormal return and the missing-bar rule defined; one measurement clock (`data_cutoff` = the close of the last session of the widest window over the selected events) and one selection clock (each event's `known_at`); the look-ahead harness gains truncation of the events file; market-wide events excluded from v1 studies; standard errors clustered by event date with the overlap of long windows either clustered by month or stated as a limitation on the page; `run_study(store_path=...)` refuses a store without the fixture marker; quiet intervals apply.
