## Problem & why now

Phase 3 registers and runs one hypothesis at a time: a file, a registration, a trial. That is right for H1 and wrong for what comes next. While H1's six-month paper run proceeds (Phase 4), the owner wants to develop strategies in volume: hundreds to thousands of registered runs and rebalance cadences faster than monthly. Done carelessly, volume is exactly what the charter warns against (principle 2: count every trial, keep a locked holdout; principle 6: around 100 trades is noise, tune on process, not on P&L). Done as this spec says, every variant of every sweep is a registered trial in the family's N, no sweep can reach the holdout, a variant leaves a sweep only through a selection rule declared before the sweep ran, at most once, with a logged owner decision, and the same parameter set can never be registered twice to reset the count.

**Charter principle 6, reconciled.** A sweep compares in-sample P&L across variants, which is a search over P&L. Principle 6 forbids *tuning* on it: adjusting a rule after looking at its return and calling the result the strategy. The lab turns the search into a counted experiment: the grid, the statistic that ranks it, the floor it must clear and the condition that retires it are all written down before the first variant runs; every variant is a trial in N whether it wins or loses; the report always shows the whole distribution beside the winner; and nothing promoted is ever judged by the statistic that selected it. The promoted hypothesis is judged the Phase 3 way (one holdout spend, by the owner) and the Phase 4 way (paper tracks backtest). What the lab tunes is the process: what a grid of null variants looks like, how many it takes to make a Sharpe of 0.25 look ordinary, which cadence pays for its turnover.

Two things change. **Sweeps:** one file registers a grid of variants, each its own hypothesis with its own trials, with the expected magnitude and retirement condition pre-registered per sweep. **Cadence:** the rebalance schedule becomes a frozen hypothesis parameter (`month_end`, `week_end`, `daily`), which resolves the pending Phase 3 amendment in STATUS "Decisions needed" and supersedes ADR 0006's cadence sentence. The shadow-paper replay that issue #281 asked for was, in this draft, deferred to a follow-up spec (Out of scope; superseded by the amendment at the end of this paragraph: it is dropped): the Phase 4 tracking run as specified cannot host a historical clock, an in-sample window, a programmatic resume or a second store path without relaxing rules that exist to protect the paper account, and those relaxations have to be specified against the merged wrapper and run tasks, not against a draft. *Amendment 2026-10-04 (#809):* the owner dropped the replay (open question 8, option (b)): the paper account and the paper spec's `FakeBroker` suites exercise the execution layer, and no follow-up spec issue is opened unless the first paper window shows a fill-timing term he wants to reproduce offline.

The registry's four guarantees stay intact and are restated where each requirement touches them: the holdout is locked; the holdout is spent at most once per hypothesis, by the owner; every run is a registered trial; the deflated Sharpe is computed over the whole family.

## Users & usage

- **The owner** writes a sweep file, merges it, runs `tradepartner sweep register`, then `tradepartner sweep run` on the real store (only the owner runs anything against the real store, CLAUDE.md rule 9), reads the sweep report and the family view, and promotes at most one variant per sweep with `tradepartner sweep promote --reason`. Nothing here places an order.
- **The `backtest-runner` agent** (Phase 3 T45) runs sweeps **only on a fixture store** (`run_sweep(..., store_path=...)`, which opens every trial `synthetic=True` as `run_hypothesis` does and refuses a store without the fixture marker, req 2), never on the real store or a copy of it. The sweep runner has no holdout, gap, window or promotion flag to pass.
- **Phase 4** is untouched: H1's paper window is monthly, `paper start` refuses a hypothesis whose cadence is not `month_end` (req 11), and no lab table is read by `paper report` or `paper check`.
- **Tests**, on every PR, register and run two fixture sweeps on the fixture store (one for the accounting rules, one for the read-group cases) and run the cadence suites at all three cadences with the network disabled.

## Definitions

- **Family**: as in the backtest spec: one of the enumerated `hypotheses.families`, the unit for N, V and holdout spends. This spec does not change what a family is. Issue #281's "family of hypotheses from one file" is a **sweep** below.
- **Family rules**: fixed by the family's **first registration** and stored in `family_rules`: the holdout (`holdout.start`, `holdout.end`), `in_sample_start`, the frozen value of every key under `FORBIDDEN_AXIS_PREFIXES` (`costs.*`, `universe.*`, `gap.*`, `adjust.*`, `master.*`, `metrics.*`, `backtest.*`, `benchmarks`, `alpaca.*`, `execution.fill_price`), the caps and floors in force (`lab.max_family_holdout_spends`, `lab.max_family_promotions`, `lab.min_sharpe_variance_annual`, `lab.axis_lattice`) and its `parent_family` from the code table `FAMILY_PARENTS` (every family in the `HypothesisFamily` literal except `oracle` has an entry there, either a parent or `None` for a **root** family with an unrelated signal; a family is added by one reviewed code change to the literal and the table together, whose PR says why it is a child or a root, open question 11; a file may name `parent_family`, and it must then equal the table's entry). Every later registration in the family, sweep or standalone, must equal them, except that `costs.per_side_bps` may be **higher** (never lower). A family is therefore one data-and-cost world in which hypotheses vary the strategy and the schedule only; a different world is a new family whose holdout may not overlap any existing non-oracle family's holdout and which, when it has a parent, holds out only months after the parent's `holdout.end`, registers only once the parent has spent its holdout or reached its spend cap, and starts its SR* high-water mark at the parent's (a root family starts its own). For `momentum` the rules are H1's frozen values as registered ([2024-01-01, 2026-09-30], `in_sample_start = 2020-08-31` since H1's #975 re-pin (2019-11-29 under the #842 amendment before it, 2017-01-31 before that; if H1's pre-registration coverage check re-pins the date again, the family rule is whatever H1 registers with), `execution.fill_price = close`, 15 bp, the ADR 0006 universe).
- **Sweep**: a file under `docs/sweeps/<slug>.md` (template `docs/templates/sweep.md`) that names one family, its family rules' holdout and `in_sample_start`, a fixed parameter block and a **grid**, plus the prose a hypothesis file carries (rationale, expected magnitude, prior-evidence disclosure, retirement condition), written once **per sweep, not per variant**. Registered as a `sweeps` row plus one `hypotheses` row per variant. A sweep **registration** is one `sweeps` row; a changed file is a new registration with new variants.
- **Grid**: a table of dotted config keys, each with a list of values, restricted to `lab.sweepable_keys`. The **variants** are the Cartesian product of the lists, at most `lab.max_variants_per_sweep`. The **canonical order** of variants is ascending `params_sha256` of each variant's frozen set; the owner never chooses it. A variant's slug is `<sweep-slug>--r<sweep_id>-v<NNN>` (the sweep registration id, then the 1-based canonical index zero-padded to the digit count of the sweep's own `n_variants`).
- **Fingerprint**: the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON of a hypothesis's frozen values for `strategy.*`, `schedule.*`, `universe.*`, `execution.fill_price`, `costs.per_side_bps`, `in_sample_start`, `holdout.start` and `holdout.end`, read through the frozen-key accessor below and **canonicalised with every key whose value equals its `FROZEN_KEY_DEFAULTS` default left out**, so a table entry added later changes no fingerprint: the keys that decide what a run computes. Two registrations with one fingerprint are the same test whatever else differs. A fingerprint is registered **at most once** in the registry, across sweeps, standalone files and families, except by promotion (req 4). The **canonical frozen set** of a registration is its stored `params` read through the accessor with the same default-valued keys left out; every equality test on frozen sets in this spec (promotion, "returns the existing record") compares canonical sets, never raw `params_sha256` values across schema versions. *Amendment 2026-10-05 (#951, T91; records the [backtest spec](backtest.md)'s decision 13, owner, 2026-10-03, B3-2 (a)):* the key list also includes `family` and every key of the family's own signal section (`strategy.*` for `momentum`; `profitability.*` for `profitability`, B3's family), and those keys are **never left out as default-valued** when the fingerprint and the canonical frozen set are built, so B3's fingerprint never equals H1's however their other frozen values agree; the `schedule.*` and other table keys keep the default-left-out rule. The strategy-lab plan's T96 implements it.
- **Frozen-key defaults**: a pinned table `FROZEN_KEY_DEFAULTS` in `backtest/hypothesis.py` of (key, behaviour-preserving default, schema version that introduced it), starting with `schedule.rebalance_cadence = month_end` and `schedule.signal_anchor = month_end`. One accessor, `frozen_values(record)`, returns a registration's stored `params` with the table's defaults overlaid for every key the stored set lacks; **every** reader of frozen parameters (`load_frozen`, `registry.fingerprint`, `family_sharpes`, `holdout.Frozen.from_hypothesis`, `_first_rebalance_on_or_after`, the engine, `paper start`) reads through it and never `record.params` directly. A **pre-lab registration** is, mechanically, a `hypotheses` row listed in `pre_lab_hypotheses`: the lab migration (the schema version that lands the spend gate) inserts one row there for **every** `hypotheses` row that exists at that moment, whatever keys its `params` hold, so H1 qualifies whether it was registered before or after the schedule task, and every grandfathered member qualifies by construction; `hypothesis.is_pre_lab(record)` reads that table and is the one predicate every rule reads (the spend gate in req 5(b) included); a fixture store, created at the current schema version, gets its pre-lab twins from a test helper that inserts the marker row. Reading a registration's `schedule.*` (or any later table key) is a separate matter: `frozen_values` overlays the default for whatever key the stored set lacks, at the stored hash, and no registration is ever re-registered. **Every frozen key added after the lab's baseline must have a table entry** (a test pins `frozen_keys()` minus the baseline set ⊆ the table's keys); a behaviour-changing key enters with the default that reproduces the old behaviour, and the table is **append-only and immutable**: a test pins every existing (key, default, version) entry by value and pins the baseline set ⊆ `frozen_keys()`, so a default can never be edited (which would move every pre-lab run and every stored fingerprint) and a frozen key can never be removed or renamed; `load_frozen`'s Phase 3 drift refusal ("re-register") then fires only on a removed key, which that test forbids, no registration is ever re-registered, and no fingerprint or canonical set changes when a key lands. *Amendment 2026-10-07 (#1199, owner answer to the [ADR 0003 amendment](../decisions/0003-data-adapters-local-first.md#amendment-2026-10-07-1199-the-stale-listing-rule)'s open question 1):* a `gap.*` key that measures the store's coverage rather than a choice of the hypothesis enters the table at its real default, not the one that reproduces the old behaviour: `gap.stale_listing_sessions = 63`, so every registration, H1 included, reads 63.
- **Variant**: one registered hypothesis whose `hypotheses` row is linked to a sweep through `sweep_variants`. It carries the family rules. A variant is never run by `backtest`, never spends the holdout and is never promoted in place (reqs 4 and 5).
- **Read group**: the variants of one sweep registration that agree on every frozen key except `strategy.formation_months`, `strategy.skip_months`, `strategy.top_fraction`, `strategy.weighting` and `schedule.signal_anchor` (the keys that change the computation but not the provider reads), ordered by the lowest canonical index of their members. One read set per step serves the whole group (req 2).
- **Vintage**: the **data vintage** of a trial is the maximum `ingested_at` over fact-table rows with `known_at` ≤ that trial's data cutoff (close of its last rebalance session; cutoffs differ across cadences in one sweep), so a nightly ingest that adds new sessions leaves it unchanged and a late-arriving fact for an in-window session (stamped at that session's close, ADR 0003 rule 2) changes it; the **code vintage** is the SHA-256 over the git-tracked `*.py` files under `src/tradepartner/` plus `uv.lock` (`code_tree_sha256`), so a merge that touches only docs or tests, or a `__pycache__` write, leaves it unchanged while a dependency bump or any source change under the package changes it (a change to `dashboard/` or `execution/` therefore restarts open sweeps too, and every `--rerun` adds the variant count to N; narrowing the hash to the backtest import closure is a plan option once the measurement task shows how often it bites). Both are recorded on every trial (additive columns) beside the Phase 3 `store_max_ingested_at`, `code_version` and `code_dirty`. A trial is **current** when its data vintage equals the data vintage computed now at its own cutoff and its code vintage equals the checkout's.
- **Selection statistic**: the sweep file's `selection_statistic`, one of `dsr_excess`, `sharpe_annual_excess_spy`, `excess_cagr_spy`, always read at the **base** cost level from each variant's **counted trial**: its latest `ok`, non-synthetic, in-sample trial over the variant's default window under the sweep's registration **that is current** (Definitions, Vintage); an `ok` trial that is no longer current is **stale** and counts in N but never selects. `dsr_excess` is always recomputed for every variant in one call with today's N and V, never read from stored rows written at different N.
- **Complete sweep**: every variant has a counted (current) trial or is **terminal-failed** (req 2); a sweep with a stale trial and no current one for some variant is **incomplete (stale)** and the runner's next plain run reruns exactly those variants, so a vintage change, a budget stop, a crash or a reboot never strands a sweep. Every `sweep_runs` row, completing or not, records the family's N and SR*_annual (excess basis) at its end; the family's **SR* high-water mark** is the maximum over every `sweep_runs` row of every sweep in the family, its parent's mark at the family's first registration, and today's value, each computed with V floored at the family rules' `min_sharpe_variance_annual` (so a family's first or only sweep, whose own clustered grid could make V ≈ 0 and SR* ≈ 0, still faces a floor above "Sharpe more likely positive than negative").
- **Declared count** n of a family: the number of distinct fingerprints declared by every sweep registration in the family, run or not; the report's "SR* at declared count" uses N_declared = N + the number of those fingerprints that have no counted trial yet, with today's V.
- **Promotion**: an owner decision (`owner_decisions.kind = promotion`) that registers a standalone hypothesis file whose canonical frozen set equals one variant's with a sweep-provenance section, so the holdout can be spent on it under the Phase 3 rules (req 4). A **promotion identity** (the fingerprint computed without `costs.per_side_bps`) is promoted at most once, under the selection statistic of the sweep that first declared it, so re-gridding a winner at a higher cost base cannot buy a second promotion.
- **Cadence**: the frozen `schedule.rebalance_cadence` ∈ {`month_end`, `week_end`, `daily`}. **Rebalance session** T_i: under `month_end` the last XNYS session of each calendar month (the Phase 3 definition, unchanged); under `week_end` the last XNYS session of each ISO week (Monday to Sunday); under `daily` every XNYS session. All three come from `tradepartner.calendar` over `all_sessions()`, never from weekday arithmetic. **Fill session** F_i = `calendar.next_session(T_i)` and **read time** close(T_i) are unchanged at every cadence; at `daily`, F_i = T_{i+1}. **Hold**: one period, from F_i to F_{i+1}. A window whose end is not a rebalance session rebalances last at the last rebalance session ≤ end, at every cadence (the Phase 3 month rule). **Implementation lag**: with `execution.fill_price = close` a trade decided at close(T_i) fills at close(F_i), one session later, at every cadence (H1 included); the position is then priced forward from F_i by the Phase 3 step mechanics, so exposure is full and the lag is one session. `daily` is the floor: the store holds daily bars, so the finest schedule is one rebalance per session, read at close(T_i), filled at F_i's `execution.fill_price`; nothing intraday exists or is specified (out of scope without a data ADR).
- **Period** i: the interval (T_i, T_{i+1}] between consecutive rebalance sessions. **Period i return**: equity at close(T_{i+1}) over equity at close(T_i), minus one. Period returns are the unit for Sharpe, skew, kurtosis, the DSR's T and (monthly, since paper stays monthly) the ADR 0005 tracking check. **`PERIODS_PER_YEAR`** = {`month_end`: 12, `week_end`: 52, `daily`: 252} is a constant table in `backtest/schedule.py` derived from the cadence, not config; `MONTHS_PER_YEAR = 12` is its `month_end` entry.
- **Signal anchor**: the frozen `schedule.signal_anchor` ∈ {`month_end`, `offset`}. It fixes the two bars the momentum score reads at T (req 7). `month_end` is the Phase 3 rule and H1's.
- **Fixture marker**: a `store_markers` row (`kind = fixture`) that `load_universe_fixtures` and the test conftest write and the real store never has. Every `store_path` entry point (`run_sweep`, `run_hypothesis`) refuses a store without it, so a copy of the real store cannot host uncounted runs: such a run would be an untracked backtest under ADR 0005's integrity criterion. *Amendment 2026-10-05 (#951, T91; the plan's T110):* for `run_hypothesis` only, a `store_path` equal to the configured real store's path (`settings.store.path`) is the real store, not a copy: it needs no marker and keeps the Phase 3 rules for its kind (non-synthetic; a tracking trial never counts, req 5(e)), which the paper window's tracking trial relies on; `run_hypothesis` refuses every other unmarked `store_path`, and `run_sweep` refuses every unmarked one (req 2).
- **Quiet interval**: a time interval inside which the sweep runner opens no trial, holds no connection and writes nothing; a group that is running when one begins **pauses between steps** with its connections closed and resumes when it ends. Two sources: `lab.quiet_intervals`, local-time intervals in `lab.quiet_timezone` (the plists' zone) applied on **each day in `lab.quiet_weekdays`**, the days launchd fires whether or not XNYS is open, covering the scheduled `ingest` and any other job the plan's runbook names; and the **paper interval**, derived on each day in `lab.quiet_weekdays` while a paper window is open (the paper plist fires on weekday holidays too and writes a `no_session` row) from the `paper.*` keys as [open − `paper.submit_window_before_open_minutes` − `lab.paper_run_lead_minutes`, open + `paper.submit_window_after_open_minutes` + `paper.sell_wait_seconds`], with "open" the session open on a session and the regular open time on a holiday, so the T70 timing keys move it without a config edit here. `lab.quiet_timezone` must equal the machine's system time zone, the one launchd fires in; `lab status` warns when it does not.
- **Detail level**: what a trial stores beyond metrics and rebalance rows: `full` (the Phase 3 tables as written: daily equity per series per cost level, base-level weights) or `summary` (daily equity for every series at the base level only, equity at rebalance sessions for the other levels, no `trial_weights`). Metrics are computed from the full in-memory result at both levels (req 12).

## Requirements

1. **Sweep file and registration.** `tradepartner sweep register <file>` parses the single ```` ```toml sweep ```` block: `slug` (= file stem), `family` (from `hypotheses.families`; `oracle` refused on the real store as for a hypothesis), `title`, `in_sample_start`, `[holdout]` `start`/`end`, a fixed block naming **every** `strategy.*` and `costs.*` key and `schedule.rebalance_cadence` (either as a fixed value or as a grid axis; a key in both is refused), any other frozen key it pins, `[grid]` (dotted key → non-empty list of values distinct **after** `Settings` validation, so `12` and `12.0` are one value, every key in `lab.sweepable_keys`; two variants of one grid with one fingerprint are refused), and `[lab]` (`selection_statistic`; `expected_excess_cagr_spy_pp` and `expected_range_pp = [lo, hi]`; `promote_at_least`, a `dsr_excess` floor ≥ `lab.promotion_min_dsr_excess`; `retire_below`, a floor on the selection statistic, which when the statistic is `dsr_excess` must be below `promote_at_least`, so no argmax can both retire and promote). **Forbidden axes** are a code constant `FORBIDDEN_AXIS_PREFIXES = ("costs.", "universe.", "holdout.", "gap.", "adjust.", "master.", "metrics.", "backtest.", "benchmarks", "alpaca.", "execution.")` pinned by a test, and a `Settings` validator refuses a `lab.sweepable_keys` entry under any of them: costs are sensitivities inside a trial (backtest spec req 6), the universe is not tuned on P&L (ADR 0006 guard a), the fill convention is a convention (ADR 0006), and the rest are data or accounting rules. When `schedule.rebalance_cadence` is an axis, `selection_statistic` must be `sharpe_annual_excess_spy` or `excess_cagr_spy` (`dsr_excess` is refused: its T differs by cadence). The Cartesian product is expanded, refused above `lab.max_variants_per_sweep`, and each variant's frozen set is built exactly as `backtest/hypothesis.py` builds a hypothesis's (file values over live `Settings`, validated through `Settings`, hashed). **Refusals before any row is written:** (a) the file's holdout, `in_sample_start` or any forbidden-prefix key differs from the family rules (`costs.per_side_bps` higher is allowed), so one-sweep-per-value can never become the universe, cost, window or fill-convention sweep the grid refuses (req 5); (b) any variant's fingerprint is already registered anywhere in the registry (a standalone hypothesis, a variant of any sweep in any family), named in the message: the existing registration is the record and N is not reset by a new grid, a one-value grid or a changed selection statistic; (c) a standalone hypothesis of the family (H1 for `momentum`, or any promoted hypothesis) has no `ok`, non-synthetic, in-sample trial over its default window yet, so a sweep's trials cannot move N before the pre-registered reading of that hypothesis's first trial (H1's retirement condition reads `dsr_excess` at that trial's N; T45b's first `ok` real-store trial comes before any sweep registration; open question 2, decided (b) on 2026-10-04 (#809): this refusal, not the plan's merge order, does the sequencing, so lab tasks may merge before T45b, the lab migration excepted, see the decision under open question 2); (d) a variant whose signal anchor can fall after T (`schedule.signal_anchor = month_end` with `strategy.skip_months = 0` at a non-`month_end` cadence, where A_0 is the month's last session) or whose formation anchor at the window's first rebalance precedes the store's earliest bar session (`registry.first_session(conn)`, the `MIN(session)` of the bars table; 2016-01-04 under ADR 0009), because either would hold cash silently and skew the comparison; (e) a grid value on a continuous axis that is not a multiple of the axis's step in the family rules' `axis_lattice_json` (copied from `lab.axis_lattice` at the family's first registration; the sweep row's copy is for the report), for example `strategy.top_fraction` on a 0.01 step, so near-duplicate variants cannot be gridded to shrink V. The command then stores the sweep (`sweeps` row: file hash, grid JSON and hash, declared variant count, the `[lab]` values, and copies of `lab.max_promotions_per_sweep`, `lab.promotion_min_dsr_excess`, `lab.max_failures_per_variant` and the `lab.axis_lattice` steps in force at registration, so a later config change never loosens a registered sweep), registers each variant through `registry.register_hypothesis` with the sweep file as `doc_path`, the variant slug, the sweep's family, window and holdout, one `sweep_variants` row (canonical index, the varied values, the fingerprint) and one `hypothesis_fingerprints` row. Registration is idempotent for an unchanged file and frozen sets; a changed file is a new sweep registration, and its variants pass the fingerprint check like any other (an unchanged variant of a changed sweep is refused as already registered: a sweep that must change re-declares only what is new). **Standalone files after the lab:** `hypothesis register` accepts exactly two kinds of file once the lab migration has run: a file whose slug, doc hash and canonical frozen set equal a stored registration's (it returns that record, below), and a promoted file named by an `owner_decisions` row of kind `promotion` (req 4); **any other standalone file is refused** with the message "write it as a one-value sweep", so a new hypothesis (an H2) always enters as a sweep, its fingerprint is never taken by a file that could not be promoted, and refusal 1(c) is never tripped by an unrun standalone file. A promoted file is also refused for a holdout, `in_sample_start` or forbidden-prefix value other than the family rules, and by refusal (d). `hypothesis register` **returns the existing record** when the file's slug, doc hash and canonical frozen set equal a stored registration's (so re-registering H1's unchanged file after this lands returns H1's pre-lab row and writes nothing; a different slug with H1's set is refused by the fingerprint rule, never handed H1's record). A **prose-only edit** of a registered file (same slug, same fingerprint, new doc hash) is refused: it is neither a new hypothesis nor a re-registration, and a correction lives in the PR or an owner decision note (the Phase 3 sentence "a changed file yields a new hypothesis" is amended below). **A new family's first registration** (a sweep file, since no other standalone file is accepted after the lab) fixes its family rules, takes its `parent_family` from `FAMILY_PARENTS` (a file naming a different parent is refused), is refused unless its holdout overlaps no existing non-oracle family's holdout (so no family ever holds out months another real family's sweeps selected on) and, for a child, unless its `holdout.start` is after the parent's `holdout.end` **and the parent has at least one holdout spend or has reached its spend cap** (a child's in-sample window covers the parent's holdout, so the parent's single owner spend must come first, or that holdout is read in sample before it is spent), seeds a child's SR* high-water mark with the parent's, and is otherwise the Phase 3 registration. A root family names no parent, starts its own N and mark, and its holdout obeys the overlap rule only.
2. **Sweep run: every variant a trial, one read set per group, no flags.** `tradepartner sweep run <sweep-slug> [--time-budget-minutes N] [--rerun] [--note]` runs, group by group in read-group order and within a group in canonical order, every variant of the sweep's latest registration that has no **current** counted trial and is not terminal-failed (a variant whose latest `ok` trial went stale is rerun by the next plain run, so a rerun stopped by the budget, a crash or a reboot is finished by ordinary runs). The window is always the variant's default in-sample window (`holdout.default_in_sample_window`); the command has no `--start`, `--end`, `--spend-holdout`, `--holdout-repeat`, `--override-gap`, `--synthetic` or store-path option (parser test), so a sweep cannot reach the holdout, the gap override, another window or another store. `engine.run_many` runs a read group from **one read set per step** (the marking and signal frames read for the union of the group's ids; each variant restricted to its own) and returns per-variant results identical to separate `engine.run` calls. Every variant of a group has its own `TrialHandle` from `registry.open_trial` **before** the group's first provider call, and `run_many` raises before any read if any variant lacks one (no id, no run; ADR 0005). A failure inside one variant's own computation (its signal, targets, fills or valuation) closes that variant `failed` and does not fail the others; a failure of the **shared read** fails every open variant of the group with `shared read failed`; and the **store-changed rule** applies to the group by data vintage: every open variant of the group fails with `store changed during run` when its data vintage (at its own cutoff) differs between trial open and result write (backtest spec req 9 as amended; a nightly ingest that only adds sessions after the cutoff does not trip it). A variant is **terminal-failed** when it has the sweep row's copied `max_failures_per_variant` `failed` trials carrying the same message under the current registration, counting only failures written with `code_dirty = false` at the checkout's current code vintage and **excluding** `store changed during run` and `shared read failed` (a derived state, listed in the report, excluded from any argmax, never counted in N since only `ok` trials count; a patched or dirty checkout can therefore not engineer a variant out of the argmax); a **current** `ok` trial for the variant, before or after those failures, makes it counted, not terminal-failed, while a stale `ok` trial neither counts nor blocks. Each run appends a `sweep_runs` row (declared, planned, `ok`, failed and terminal-failed counts, seconds, code vintage, `code_version`, `code_dirty`, `run_by`, the family's `n_trials_at_end` and `sr_star_annual_at_end` on every row, and `completed` when the run leaves the sweep complete) and one `sweep_trials` row per trial (read group index, seconds). The run stops cleanly at a group boundary when the time budget (`--time-budget-minutes`, default `lab.sweep_time_budget_minutes`) is exhausted; `--rerun` is accepted only on a complete current sweep and reruns every variant, terminal-failed ones included (they get a fresh count under the new run). **Quiet intervals:** the runner starts a group when its predicted duration ends before the next quiet interval, or when that duration exceeds the longest gap between intervals (such a group starts at once and pauses through each interval, so a long `daily` group never waits for a weekend that may never be long enough), the prediction being the group's variant count × the measured seconds per variant **at that group's cadence** (from `sweep_trials` joined to the variants' cadence, or the plan's measured figure per cadence when none exists); a group that is running when an interval begins pauses between steps with its connections closed and resumes after it; inside an interval it opens no trial, holds no connection and writes nothing, so the sweep never contends for the store with `ingest` or `paper run` (a `paper run` that fails on "store busy" engages the kill switch, paper spec req 5). `--rerun` on a complete sweep runs every variant again: every rerun is a new trial, N rises by the variant count, V takes the latest per pair (backtest spec req 8). Trials are written at the sweep's detail level (req 12). Sensitivities per cost level are computed for every variant as in Phase 3 and are never new trials. `run_sweep(..., store_path=...)` and `run_hypothesis(..., store_path=...)` refuse a store without the fixture marker and open every trial `synthetic=True`; the CLI never sets `store_path`.
3. **Sweep accounting and report.** N and V are the family's, unchanged: N counts every `ok`, non-synthetic, in-sample trial of the family including every variant's; V is over the latest `ok` trial per (canonical frozen set hash, window) pair (req 9 fixes the unit; the pair key is the canonical set so a promoted file never doubles its variant's pair). A sweep has no N of its own. `tradepartner sweep report <sweep-slug>` prints, for the sweep's latest registration: the declared and run variant counts, the terminal-failed variants, the stale variants awaiting a rerun, and whether the sweep is complete; the family's N, V and SR*_annual today, the family's declared count n and "SR* at declared count" (SR*_annual from N_declared and today's V), and for a family with a `parent_family` the parent's N beside its own; per variant, one row in canonical order with its slug, the varied values, the base-level `excess_cagr_spy`, `sharpe_annual_excess_spy`, `dsr_excess` recomputed with today's N and V (one `family_sharpes` call for the whole report, itself one set-based query per basis, never one query per trial), `cost_drag`, `turnover_annual`, `max_drawdown`, `red_flag` and status; the distribution of the selection statistic (minimum, quartiles, median, maximum), the share of variants with recomputed `dsr_excess` > 0.5, and the share of variants whose base-level `excess_cagr_spy` lies inside `expected_range_pp` (always over `excess_cagr_spy`, whatever the selection statistic); and, **only for a complete sweep**, the argmax variant and whether `promote_at_least` (evaluated as req 4 says) and `retire_below` are met. An incomplete sweep's report prints no argmax and no verdict line. A variant whose base-level `excess_cagr_spy` exceeds `metrics.red_flag_excess_cagr_pp` is `red_flag` like any trial (backtest spec req 15); the report counts them. The report never prints a holdout value, because no variant has one.
4. **Promotion, once per fingerprint, capped and pre-declared.** `tradepartner sweep promote <sweep-slug> --file docs/hypotheses/<slug>.md --reason` is refused unless: the sweep is complete and every counted trial has `code_dirty = false`; the sweep has no `sweep_retired` decision and its argmax does **not** meet `retire_below` (a retired sweep, or one whose pre-registered retirement condition holds, promotes nothing); fewer than the sweep row's copied `max_promotions` promotions exist for it and fewer than the family rules' `max_family_promotions` for the family; the file's canonical frozen set equals the argmax variant's (so no key inside or outside the fingerprint, `gap.count_share_threshold` or `backtest.initial_capital` included, can differ from what won, and a `FROZEN_KEY_DEFAULTS` entry added between the sweep and the promotion changes nothing; the argmax is the variant that maximises the sweep's selection statistic at the base cost level, ties by canonical index; a non-argmax file is refused, however good the reason: a different rule is a new sweep, which the fingerprint rule then makes declare only new variants); that promotion identity has never been promoted (from this or any sweep, at any cost base); the argmax's `dsr_excess` ≥ the sweep row's `promote_at_least` when computed with **the family's SR* high-water mark** (Definitions: the maximum over every `sweep_runs` row of every sweep in the family, the parent's mark, and today's value, each with V floored at `min_sharpe_variance_annual`), so neither a later sweep, nor this sweep's own clustered variants, nor an incomplete sweep, nor a family's first sweep, nor a rerun can shrink V to turn a refusal into a promotion; and the file carries a `## Sweep provenance` section naming the sweep slug and registration id, the declared count n, the variant slug and rank, and its base-level in-sample statistics, plus the usual hypothesis sections (its prior-evidence disclosure lists the sweep's results as evidence already seen). The command registers the file (a standalone hypothesis in the same family, its `hypothesis_fingerprints` row pointing at the variant's fingerprint), appends an `owner_decisions` row of kind `promotion` (values: sweep id, variant hypothesis id, promoted hypothesis id, declared count, family N, the high-water mark and today's SR*, and the statistic at promotion) and prints both ids. The promoted hypothesis then follows the Phase 3 rules unchanged: `backtest <slug>` runs it (each run a trial), and only the owner may spend its holdout, once, with `--spend-holdout --holdout-reason`, marked `holdout_repeat` when any hypothesis in the family already spent it (backtest spec req 11), subject to the family cap in req 5. The page and the report show "promoted from <sweep>, 1 of <n>" beside it. `sweep retire <sweep-slug> --reason` appends an `owner_decisions` row of kind `sweep_retired`; the report shows it. Neither promotion nor retirement changes N.
5. **Holdout rules restated for volume.** (a) A sweep variant is refused by `backtest <slug>` (`refused_variant`, exit 2, a trial with that status like any refusal, no provider call): variants run only through `sweep run`, which cannot spend the holdout (req 2). (b) A holdout spend is one per hypothesis by the owner, exactly as in Phase 3; the lab adds no spend path. It removes one: **`holdout.decide` refuses a spend (`refused_holdout`) by any hypothesis that is not pre-lab (Definitions, `is_pre_lab`) unless a `promotion` decision names it**, so neither a standalone near-neighbour of an argmax (a nudged `top_fraction`, a new fingerprint) nor a grid written as standalone files before any sweep can skip the statistic, the floor, the argmax rule and the caps by taking the Phase 3 path; pre-lab registrations (H1) spend as before, and a grandfathered member does too. The consequence is stated plainly: **after the lab lands, a standalone file is never the road to the holdout, and `hypothesis register` refuses one that is not a promoted file** (req 1); a new hypothesis (an H2) is written as a one-value sweep, run, and promoted, which costs one `[lab]` block and one owner decision. (c) **The family rules are fixed at first registration** (Definitions): `hypothesis register` and `sweep register` refuse a holdout, an `in_sample_start` or a forbidden-prefix value that differs from the family's first registration's (`costs.per_side_bps` higher allowed), so no later registration can move a holdout over months an earlier member used in sample or over its paper months, widen the window, cheapen the cost base, change the universe or the fill convention; a different world is a new family (open question 11). (d) **Family spend cap:** `holdout.decide` refuses a spend (`refused_holdout`, naming the prior spends) when the family already has the family rules' `max_family_holdout_spends` holdout trials of any outcome; below the cap each family repeat still needs its own `--holdout-reason` and is marked `holdout_repeat` (backtest spec req 11 as amended). (e) A `tracking` trial (Phase 4) is unchanged and never counts.
6. **Cadence as a frozen parameter.** A new frozen section `schedule` (`FROZEN_SECTIONS` gains it; `backtest/hypothesis.py` freezes it like the others) holds `schedule.rebalance_cadence` (default `month_end`) and `schedule.signal_anchor` (default `month_end`), both in `FROZEN_KEY_DEFAULTS`. A hypothesis file may pin both; a sweep file must name `schedule.rebalance_cadence`; a file registered after this lands stores both keys. **Pre-lab registrations** are read through `frozen_values`, not rewritten: `load_frozen` checks the stored hash against the stored keys, overlays the table's defaults and refuses only a key drift the table does not cover, so H1's `hypotheses` row, its `params_sha256`, its trials, its `gap_signoff` and its paper window are untouched whether H1 was registered before or after this lands. **Fill convention and lag:** the fill convention is a family rule (`close` for `momentum`, H1's); at every cadence a `close` fill is one session after the decision (Definitions, implementation lag) with full exposure, and the report states each family's convention once in its header rather than flagging rows. In every step, at every cadence, the fill at F_i is applied and the period valued before the plan at close(T_{i+1}) reads the drifted weights (the Phase 3 step order; at `daily` the fill at F_i and the read at close(T_{i+1}) fall on the same session, the fill first). `schedule.rebalance_sessions(start, end, cadence)`, `fill_session`, `read_time`, `holdout.gap_sessions(window, cadence)` and `holdout.default_in_sample_window(frozen, cadence)` (its end is the last rebalance session at the hypothesis's cadence strictly before `holdout.start`) take the cadence; the engine, the results writer, the holdout decision (its "touches the holdout but reaches none of its rebalance sessions" rule and the gap gate's sessions) and the registry's window key (`_first_rebalance_on_or_after`) read it through `frozen_values`. `calendar.last_session_of_week(iso_year, iso_week)` and `calendar.rebalance_sessions_between(start, end, cadence)` come from `all_sessions()`. At `week_end` the rebalance is the last session of the ISO week, so the Good Friday week of 2024 rebalances on Thursday 2024-03-28 and the Independence Day week of 2025 on the half day Thursday 2025-07-03; at `daily` every session is a rebalance. Forced exits, `stale_exit_sessions`, `missing_fill`, late dividends, the marking frame and one-frame-per-step valuation are per step exactly as in Phase 3, with "month" read as "period". **Phase 4 stays monthly** (req 11).
7. **Signal anchor.** `signals.momentum(frame, t_session, formation_months, skip_months, anchor, cadence)` scores a name as close(A_skip)/close(A_form) − 1 where, under `anchor = month_end`, A_k is the last XNYS session of the calendar month k months before T's month (the Phase 3 rule; at `month_end` cadence identical to `momentum_12_1`), and under `anchor = offset`, A_k is the last session on or before the calendar date T − k months (`dateutil.relativedelta`). Under `month_end` the score changes only when a month ends, so a weekly or daily rebalance re-trades drift and universe changes between month ends; under `offset` it refreshes every rebalance. Both read only sessions ≤ T, and registration refusal 1(d) guarantees an anchor never falls after T and the formation anchor never precedes the store's first session. A name lacking either bar is excluded and counted, as before. Cadence and anchor are two named keys so a sweep can vary them separately; the combination is stated in every report row.
8. **Metrics annualised from the schedule.** `metrics.series_metrics(period_returns, periods_per_year, ...)` computes, per series and cost level, the Phase 3 keys with the period keys renamed and three added: `cagr` (years = `n_periods` / `periods_per_year`), `vol_annual` (period std × √ppy), `sharpe_period` (non-annualised, `metrics.risk_free_rate` converted to the period), `sharpe_annual` (= `sharpe_period` × √ppy), `sharpe_period_excess_spy`, `sharpe_annual_excess_spy` (new), `max_drawdown` (daily equity, cadence-independent), `turnover_period` (one-sided average per rebalance), `turnover_annual` (new; = `turnover_period` × ppy), `cost_drag`, `excess_cagr_spy`, `excess_cagr_mtum`, `tracking_error_spy`, `tracking_error_mtum` (× √ppy), `skew_period`, `kurtosis_period`, `skew_period_excess_spy`, `kurtosis_period_excess_spy`, `n_periods`, `periods_per_year` (new; the constant, stored so a reader never guesses). The `*_monthly` and `n_months` keys are no longer written. The additive migration (the next free schema version) inserts, for every existing trial, the `*_period` rows copied from its `*_monthly` rows, `n_periods` from `n_months`, `periods_per_year = 12`, `turnover_annual = 12 × turnover_monthly` and `sharpe_annual_excess_spy = √12 × sharpe_monthly_excess_spy`; the old rows stay (append-only) and nothing reads them afterwards. The cost model is unchanged at every cadence: cost per trade = notional × `costs.per_side_bps`/10⁴ + commissions, on every fill including exits; the ladder applies; what changes with cadence is how often trades happen, which `turnover_annual` and `cost_drag` show. The AST literal check on `backtest/{metrics,costs,fills,valuation,engine,schedule}.py` allows 0, 1, −1, 2, 4, 10 000 and the three `PERIODS_PER_YEAR` entries in `schedule.py` only.
9. **Deflated Sharpe across cadences.** The DSR is computed at each trial's own period: SR = `sharpe_period` (or `sharpe_period_excess_spy`), T = `n_periods`, γ₃ and γ₄ the period skew and kurtosis. **V is the sample variance of annualised Sharpes**: `family_sharpes` reads `sharpe_period`, `sharpe_period_excess_spy` and `periods_per_year` and returns each counted trial's Sharpe × √ppy per basis; SR*_annual = √V · ((1−γ)Φ⁻¹(1−1/N) + γΦ⁻¹(1−1/(N·e))) as before, and the trial's SR* = SR*_annual / √(its ppy). For a family whose trials are all `month_end` this equals the Phase 3 computation exactly: V_annual = 12·V_monthly, so √V_annual = √12·√V_monthly and SR*_annual/√12 = SR*_monthly, and the Phase 3 reference values hold. `trial_results` gains `sharpe_unit ∈ {monthly, annual}` (additive column; null on pre-lab rows means `monthly`; new rows `annual`), so `sharpe_variance` and `sr_star` are never read without their unit; the page converts pre-lab rows to annual when it shows them beside today's values. The page and the report recompute DSR with today's N and V as before; only the promotion floor uses the larger SR* (req 4). This is the one arithmetic change to backtest spec req 8 (amendment 6 below). The formula assumes Sharpe scales with √k under aggregation and ignores period autocorrelation; the risk section says what that does at faster cadences.
10. **Engine, checks and oracle per cadence.** `engine.run` and `run_many` iterate `rebalance_sessions(start, end, cadence)`; `results.write_results` derives period ends from the same function. The Phase 3 look-ahead suites (truncation invariance, prefix invariance, the revision case, the plan-read timing check) and the `bt` oracle are parametrised over the three cadences on the fixture store; `run_many` on a group passes truncation invariance per variant. The gap gate reads `survivorship_gap` at every rebalance close of a holdout window at the hypothesis's cadence (never a sweep's: sweeps have no holdout runs). At `daily` cadence every step re-reads each id's full adjusted history through `adjusted_prices_as_of`, which has no session-range parameter; the Phase 3 spec's follow-up (a session-range filter, opened if a run exceeds one hour) is a **dependency of the daily-cadence task** in the plan, not an option.
11. **Phase 4 stays monthly; ADR 0005 unchanged.** `paper start --hypothesis` refuses (`refused_cadence`) a hypothesis whose `frozen_values` cadence is not `month_end` (a pre-lab registration reads as `month_end`); the paper spec's T_i, `paper.min_rebalances = 6` and the monthly tracking comparison are as written. Paper trading at another cadence is a Phase 4 spec amendment made when, and only when, a non-monthly hypothesis is promoted toward paper (out of scope here). ADR 0005's six-rebalance criterion, its integrity stop criterion ("any untracked backtest run halts the phase": the lab keeps every run registered and refuses unmarked stores) and its budget line are untouched.
12. **Storage per trial.** `trials` gains a `detail_level` column (additive migration; `full` for every existing row). Standalone hypotheses store `full` (the Phase 3 tables as written). Sweep variants store `lab.sweep_detail_level` (default `summary`): `trial_metrics` per series and level, `trial_results`, `trial_rebalances` per level, `trial_equity` daily for every series at the base level and at rebalance sessions only for the other levels, and no `trial_weights`. Metrics, including `max_drawdown` per level, are computed from the full in-memory result before the rows are chosen, so a summary trial's metrics equal a full trial's. Anything not stored is recomputed by a rerun, which is a new trial. At `daily` cadence every session is a rebalance session, so `summary` saves no equity rows against `full` and only the weights; the row arithmetic in req 13 says so. The page shows a summary trial's base-level equity with the detail level stated and no weights table.
13. **Compute and registry growth.** Row counts per trial are stated so the budget is arithmetic, not hope. Cost levels are five (the base plus the four-rung ladder). At `full` and `month_end` over H1's window: about 3 series × 5 levels × 1,750 sessions ≈ 26,000 equity rows plus about 100 names × 84 rebalances ≈ 8,400 weight rows; at `summary`, about 3 × 1,750 + 3 × 4 × 84 ≈ 6,300 equity rows and no weights; at `daily` cadence `trial_rebalances` grows to about 1,750 rows per level (8,750), `summary` equity equals `full` (about 26,000 rows, every session being a rebalance) and `full` weights reach about 175,000 rows, which is why sweeps default to `summary`. `tradepartner lab status` prints the store file size, the row count of every registry table, the family N, V and declared count, the family rules, grandfathered duplicate fingerprints (below), open sweeps with their completion and stale counts, and the last ten `sweep_runs` with seconds per variant. `sweep run` refuses to start when the store file exceeds `lab.registry_size_refuse_gb` and warns above `lab.registry_size_warn_gb`; **the guard only refuses new runs: nothing in the lab deletes, prunes, compacts or recreates the registry** (text check: no `DROP`, `DELETE`, `TRUNCATE` or `VACUUM` under `backtest/lab.py` or `store/registry.py`). Per-step cost at `daily` cadence is a universe rebuild, a gap read and a full-history price read per session, about 21 times the monthly count; the plan's first task measures seconds per step per cadence and per variant in a read group on the owner's store (owner task) and records it in the plan's verification section, and `sweep_runs` records seconds per variant from then on, so the time budget and the quiet-interval prediction are set from measurements. Read groups are the only sharing: one process, no worker pool in this spec (open question 7). **Grandfathered fingerprints:** a Phase 3 store may already hold two registrations with one fingerprint (a prose-only re-registration); the migration inserts a `hypothesis_fingerprints` row for each, `fingerprint_registered` returns the earliest, `lab status` lists the duplicates, and the once-only rule applies to registrations made after the migration. The migration builds each existing family's `family_rules` row from its earliest hypothesis; an existing member whose frozen values differ from those rules is grandfathered too, listed by `lab status`, and its holdout spend and runs follow the Phase 3 rules it was registered under.
14. **Family and sweep view on the trials page.** `dashboard/trials_page.py` gains, above the trial table, a **families** card per family (N today, V today per basis in annual units, SR*_annual, declared count and SR* at declared count, the family rules in one line, `parent_family` with the parent's N, holdout spends with the spending hypothesis and the cap, promotions and the cap, the SR* high-water mark) and a **sweeps** table (slug, registration id, declared and run counts, terminal-failed count, complete or incomplete with its stale and terminal-failed counts, the selection-statistic quartiles, the recomputed `dsr_excess` > 0.5 share, red-flag count, argmax only when complete, promotion and retirement rows). Read-only, inside the shell's single read-only connection, per the design standard; no run button. The card's and the report's recompute of `dsr_excess` for every trial read `trial_metrics` through one set-based query per basis (no per-trial query), so the shell's read connection is held for one query, not thousands, and a render-time bound (a constant in the test file) holds on a generated registry of 10,000 trials.
15. **CLI.** `tradepartner sweep register <file> | run <slug> [--time-budget-minutes] [--rerun] [--note] | status [<slug>] | report <slug> | promote <slug> --file <path> --reason | retire <slug> --reason`; `tradepartner lab status`. `backtest <variant-slug>` exits 2 `refused_variant`. No command takes a store path, a window, a holdout, gap or synthetic flag (parser test); output contains no secret-shaped string.

## Acceptance criteria (testable)

Sweep registration and guardrails
- [ ] `sweep register` on the fixture sweep file (two axes, 2 × 2 = 4 variants) writes one `sweeps` row (with the copied caps and floor), four `hypotheses` rows with slugs `<slug>--r<id>-v1` to `-v4` in ascending `params_sha256` (width from the sweep's `n_variants`), four `sweep_variants` and four `hypothesis_fingerprints` rows, the family rules' holdout and `in_sample_start` on every variant, and `doc_path` = the sweep file; re-running it writes nothing; a grid axis outside `lab.sweepable_keys` is refused; `Settings` refuses a `lab.sweepable_keys` entry under any `FORBIDDEN_AXIS_PREFIXES` entry and the constant's contents are pinned; a key both fixed and gridded is refused; a product above `lab.max_variants_per_sweep` (set to 3 in the test) is refused with no row written; a file missing any `strategy.*` or `costs.*` key or `schedule.rebalance_cadence` is refused; a `[lab]` block missing `selection_statistic`, `expected_range_pp`, `promote_at_least` or `retire_below`, or with `promote_at_least` below `lab.promotion_min_dsr_excess`, is refused; `dsr_excess` as the statistic with `schedule.rebalance_cadence` as an axis is refused; a variant with `signal_anchor = month_end`, `skip_months = 0` and `week_end` cadence is refused, and so is one whose formation anchor at the first rebalance precedes the fixture store's `MIN(session)`; a `strategy.top_fraction` value off the `lab.axis_lattice` step (0.105 on a 0.01 step) is refused.
- [ ] Family rules: with H1's fixture twin registered first, a sweep or standalone file that changes `holdout.start`, `holdout.end`, `in_sample_start`, `universe.top_n_by_cap`, `execution.fill_price`, `gap.count_share_threshold`, `backtest.initial_capital`, `costs.sensitivity_per_side_bps` or lowers `costs.per_side_bps` is refused naming the family rule; raising `costs.per_side_bps` is accepted; a file in a family with no registered hypothesis fixes the rules, its `family_rules.parent_family` comes from `FAMILY_PARENTS` (a file naming another parent is refused; the test pins that every non-oracle family has an entry, a parent or `None`), it is refused when its `holdout.start` is not after the parent's `holdout.end`, when its holdout overlaps another real family's, or when the parent has no holdout spend and has not reached its spend cap (accepted once the parent's fixture twin spends), and its high-water mark starts at the parent's; a root family in the table (`None`) registers with no parent, its own mark and the overlap rule only; `family_rules` carries the caps, `min_sharpe_variance_annual` and the lattice in force at first registration and a later config change to any of them changes nothing for that family; a prose-only edit of a registered file (new doc hash, same fingerprint) is refused and writes nothing; two grid values that validate to one value, or two variants with one fingerprint, are refused; `retire_below ≥ promote_at_least` under a `dsr_excess` statistic is refused.
- [ ] Fingerprint: a second sweep, in the same or another family with the same rules, whose grid contains a fingerprint of the first is refused naming the existing variant, whatever else differs (a different selection statistic or `[lab]` values); a sweep containing H1's fixture twin's fingerprint is refused; `hypothesis register` on a standalone file with a variant's fingerprint is refused without a `promotion` decision naming it and accepted after; `registry.fingerprint` of a pre-lab registration equals that of the same file registered with `schedule.*` defaults written out; on a store holding two Phase 3 registrations with one fingerprint the migration inserts both rows, `fingerprint_registered` returns the earlier, `lab status` lists the pair, and a third registration of that fingerprint is refused.
- [ ] Family readiness: `sweep register` in a family whose standalone hypothesis has no `ok` non-synthetic in-sample trial over its default window is refused naming that hypothesis; after one `ok` trial it is accepted; a promoted hypothesis without a run blocks the next sweep the same way.

Sweep run and accounting
- [ ] `sweep run` on the fixture store runs the four variants in read-group order then canonical order, each with its own `TrialHandle` opened before the group's first provider call (recording provider, recording registry), writes four `ok` trials over the default in-sample window with `data_vintage` and `code_tree_sha256` set, one `sweep_runs` row (with `n_trials_at_end`, `sr_star_annual_at_end` and `completed = true`, since it completes the sweep) and four `sweep_trials` rows; N rises by four; `family_sharpes` returns four annualised Sharpes; a second `sweep run` without `--rerun` runs nothing; with `--rerun` it runs four more trials, N rises by four and V is over four pairs; under `store_path` every trial is `synthetic = true` and N is unchanged.
- [ ] Read groups (second fixture sweep): two variants that differ only in `strategy.top_fraction` make exactly the provider calls of one `engine.run` per step (recording provider, call count equality), and their results equal separate `engine.run` results row for row (equity, rebalances, metrics); two variants that differ in `schedule.rebalance_cadence` or `strategy.signal_total_return` are separate groups ordered by lowest canonical index; `run_many` with a variant lacking a handle raises `TypeError` before any provider call.
- [ ] Failure isolation and terminal failure: an injected error inside variant 3's own signal (a variant-specific fake) leaves variants 1, 2 and 4 of its read group `ok` and variant 3 `failed`; an injected error in the shared read fails every open variant of the group with `shared read failed`; an in-window fact inserted mid-group (known_at ≤ the cutoff) fails every open variant of that group with `store changed during run` and the next `sweep run` resumes them, while a row inserted for a session after the cutoff fails nothing; after the sweep row's `max_failures_per_variant` (2 in the test) identical variant-specific failures on a clean checkout at the current code vintage variant 3 is terminal-failed, the sweep reads complete, the report lists it and excludes it from the argmax; three `store changed during run` or `shared read failed` failures, or two identical failures written with `code_dirty = true` or at another code vintage, leave it not terminal-failed; an `ok` trial after two identical failures makes it counted; a time budget of one group (fake clock) stops after the first group with `sweep_runs.n_ok` equal to that group's size and the sweep incomplete.
- [ ] Vintage and resumption: an in-window fact inserted between two groups makes the earlier trials stale, the report lists those variants as stale with no argmax, the next plain `sweep run` reruns exactly them and the sweep completes with the stale trials still counted in N; a `--rerun` stopped by the budget after one group is completed by the next plain run without `--rerun`; a variant whose stale `ok` trial is followed by two identical current failures is terminal-failed; a cadence-axis sweep whose `month_end` and `week_end` variants have different cutoffs (holdout starting 2025-01-01 in the fixture: 2024-12-31 and 2024-12-27) completes when each trial's vintage equals the current vintage at its own cutoff; a doc-only or test-only change to the checkout, or a `__pycache__` write, leaves `code_tree_sha256` unchanged and the sweep runnable, while a change to a tracked `*.py` under `src/tradepartner/` or to `uv.lock` changes it; `--rerun` on an incomplete sweep is refused.
- [ ] Quiet intervals: with `lab.quiet_intervals` set and a fake clock, a group whose predicted duration (variants × measured seconds per variant at its cadence; a `daily` group after a `month_end` group predicts from the `daily` figure) would end inside the next interval is not started, while one whose predicted duration exceeds the longest gap between intervals starts at once; a group running when an interval begins pauses between steps with no connection open (recording connection factory) and no row written until the clock leaves it, then resumes and completes; the paper interval is derived from the `paper.*` keys and changes when they do, and applies on a weekday holiday at the regular open time; a holiday on a day in `lab.quiet_weekdays` still carries the configured intervals and a day outside them carries none; `lab status` warns when `lab.quiet_timezone` differs from the system time zone.
- [ ] `sweep report` on an incomplete sweep prints the counts, the per-variant rows for the trials that exist, the family N, declared count n and "SR* at declared count" (equal to `expected_max_sharpe(N_declared, V)` for the fixture's numbers), the parent family's N when `family_rules.parent_family` is set, and neither an argmax nor a verdict line; on the complete sweep it prints the argmax at the base cost level with ties broken by canonical index, `dsr_excess` per variant equal to a fresh `deflated_sharpe` call at today's N and V for every variant (not the stored value: the test changes N between the run and the report), the quartiles, the `dsr_excess` > 0.5 share, the share of `excess_cagr_spy` inside `expected_range_pp` under a `sharpe_annual_excess_spy` selection statistic, the red-flag count (one variant given an `excess_cagr_spy` above the threshold by a synthetic frame) and the `promote_at_least`/`retire_below` verdicts; no holdout value appears in the output (text check).

Promotion

## Risks & domain checks

- **Look-ahead:** every cadence reads at close(T_i) through the same `t`-parameterised provider; the anchor rules read sessions ≤ T only and refusal 1(d) keeps an anchor from falling after T; the Phase 3 suites (truncation, prefix, revision, plan-read timing) run at every cadence and per variant of a read group; a read group's union read is a superset of each variant's, restricted per variant, so no variant sees another's holdings; at `daily` the fill at F_i precedes the plan's read at the same session's close, so a plan never sees a fill it has not paid for.
- **Multiple testing:** every variant is a hypothesis and every run a trial (no handle, no read, per variant); N counts variants, reruns and failures-turned-ok alike; a fingerprint is registered once, so a new grid, a one-value grid, a changed sensitivity ladder or a changed selection statistic never resets the count or buys a second promotion; a new family with a new holdout does start its own N (open question 11), which is why every family has a `FAMILY_PARENTS` entry (a parent or `None`), may not hold out months any real family's sweeps selected on, and a child registers only after its parent's spend, inherits the parent's SR* high-water mark and shows the parent's N beside its own; the family rules fix the holdout, the window, the cost base, the universe and the fill convention at first registration, so neither a sweep nor a sequence of sweeps can fish over any of them; the declared count is shown as the SR* lower bound before a sweep completes; the canonical order and the vintage rule stop stopping-early and vintage-shopping; `backtest` refuses variants; promotion is argmax of a pre-declared statistic with a pre-declared floor copied into the sweep row, evaluated at the family's SR* high-water mark with V floored (so neither a later sweep, nor a sweep's own clustered grid, nor an incomplete sweep, nor a family's first sweep, nor a rerun can shrink V to pass it, and the lattice keeps near-duplicates off the grid), once per promotion identity whatever the cost base, refused on a retired sweep or a met retirement condition, capped per sweep and per family, logged with N; no hypothesis registered after the lab's migration spends the holdout without a promotion, so the Phase 3 path cannot carry a near-neighbour or a grid of standalone files past the gate; fingerprints and canonical sets leave default-valued keys out, so a later `FROZEN_KEY_DEFAULTS` entry never re-opens a fingerprint or blocks a promotion; the report shows the whole distribution beside the argmax; a sweep cannot register before the family's standalone hypothesis has its first counted trial, so H1's pre-registered reading is taken at the N it was written for; sensitivities never select; synthetic and oracle trials stay impossible on the real store; `store_path` refuses a store without the fixture marker, so a copy of the real store cannot host uncounted runs (such a run would be untracked under ADR 0005); agents run only on fixtures.
- **Holdout:** no sweep path reaches it (parser and decision tests); the promoted hypothesis spends it under the Phase 3 rules only, within the family cap; the sweep report and the page print no holdout value for variants.
- **Costs:** per trade, cadence-independent, and the base is a family rule that can only rise; `turnover_annual` and `cost_drag` show what a faster cadence costs; the ladder applies to every variant; no market impact is modelled at any cadence, so `daily` results at the base level are optimistic, which the ladder bounds; `close` fills lag the decision by one session at every cadence with full exposure, stated once per family rather than flagged per row.
- **Statistics at faster cadences:** the DSR formula and the √ppy conversion assume Sharpe scales with √k under aggregation and that period returns are not autocorrelated. With the same annual Sharpe (0.866) and the same annual SR* (0.658), the reference DSR is 0.727 monthly, 0.737 weekly and 0.741 daily: the rise comes only from T in the formula, not from new information, because the extra periods are not independent draws to the extent the formula assumes. The annual-unit V makes cadences comparable in N and V but not identical in power, and the report labels DSR per variant with its cadence. In a cadence-axis sweep the variants' rebalance sessions can diverge from the first rebalance on, not just the second (with H1's `in_sample_start = 2020-08-31` since #975, a Monday and so not the last session of its own ISO week, `month_end` first rebalances on 2020-08-31 itself while `week_end` first rebalances on 2020-09-04, the last session of that ISO week, and then 2020-09-30 at `month_end` against 2020-09-11 at `week_end`; under the pre-#975 start, 2019-11-29, a Friday that was the last session of both its ISO week and its month, the two cadences' first rebalances coincided at 2019-11-29 and only diverged from the second rebalance on, 2019-12-31 at `month_end` against 2019-12-06 at `week_end`; under the pre-#842 start they differed from the first, 2017-01-31 against 2017-02-03) and `cagr`'s years = n/52 overstates elapsed time by about 0.34% against the calendar; open question 3 keeps the constants and records both.
- **Survivorship:** the gap is recorded per rebalance at every cadence and gated on holdout runs as before; at `daily` the gap read per session is the compute cost, not a bias.
- **Compute and the owner's time:** thousands of runs are hours on the owner's machine; the time budget, read groups, `summary` detail and the size guards bound it; the measurement task turns the placeholders into numbers; the data vintage is per trial cutoff, so a nightly ingest that only adds sessions does not restart a sweep and a cadence-axis sweep with differing cutoffs can complete, and the code vintage is the tracked Python under the package plus `uv.lock`, so a doc or test merge does not restart one either, while a merge under the package does, for open and complete sweeps alike (a complete sweep goes stale, its report drops the argmax, and promoting from it again needs a full rerun that adds its size to N: a cost the plan measures before narrowing the hash); the quiet intervals, the between-step pause and the per-cadence duration prediction keep a sweep from colliding with `ingest` and `paper run`; the daily-cadence task depends on the session-range filter follow-up.
- **Registry migration:** renaming the period keys is done by inserting rows, never editing; a Phase 3 trial's DSR is unchanged (fixture comparison); pre-lab registrations are read through `frozen_values` at an unchanged hash and are marked in `pre_lab_hypotheses` by the lab migration, so H1 is never re-registered, its `gap_signoff`, paper window and V pair stand, and it keeps its Phase 3 holdout spend whether it was registered before or after the schedule task (the marker, not the stored keys, decides); `sharpe_unit` makes a pre-lab `sharpe_variance` readable beside an annual one; H1's red-flag text names `turnover_monthly` and `cost_drag ≈ 12 × turnover_monthly × 2 × 15 bp`, which auditors read as `turnover_period` at `periods_per_year = 12` (the file is not edited, by design).
- **Phase 4 coupling:** `paper start` refuses non-monthly cadences, so the six-rebalance criterion stays monthly and ADR 0005 needs no change; the quiet intervals keep sweep reads and writes away from the pre-open `paper run` and the post-close `ingest`; the replay is dropped (#809) so no paper rule is relaxed by this spec.
- **Order path, secrets, LLM inputs:** none. Nothing here constructs a broker, reads a key or emits or consumes model output. `quant-auditor` runs on every PR of the plan; `safety-reviewer` on none unless a task touches `execution/` (none is expected).
- **Terms and budget:** no new data, API or spend (ADR 0009's $0 budget holds).
