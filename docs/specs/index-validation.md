# Spec: Known-answer index validation (the whole universe against SPY and RSP)

**Status:** Draft  ·  **Issue:** #1598  ·  **Related:** [backtest spec](backtest.md) (Definitions "Trial", req 3 benchmarks, req 8 the trial count, "Risks" multiple testing), [research-registry spec](research-registry.md) (Definitions "Research experiment": a computation that reads outcomes only is not a run), [strategy-lab spec](strategy-lab.md) (Definitions "Vintage", "Fixture marker"), [data-foundation spec](data-foundation.md) (amendment 2026-10-11 #1506, the vintage rule; amendment 2026-10-11 #1541, the coverage reference), [ADR 0003](../decisions/0003-data-adapters-local-first.md) (local-first; rule 6 the survivorship gap), [ADR 0005](../decisions/0005-objective-benchmark-stop-criteria.md) (integrity: no id, no run), [ADR 0006](../decisions/0006-universe-and-cadence.md) (the universe and the month-end cadence), [data-releases runbook](../runbooks/data-releases.md), [benchmark-backfill runbook](../runbooks/benchmark-backfill.md), #974 (the 2019-08..2020-07 first-span gap), #1541 and #1542 (the coverage audits), [plan](../plans/index-validation.md)

## Problem & why now

Every trial result rests on one pipeline: the point-in-time universe, the adjusted total-return marking and the valuation arithmetic. Nothing checks that pipeline against a number the world already knows (the engine's own step logic, sizing, exits and cash, is covered by its tests and the `bt` oracle; req 1's parity test ties this index to it). The coverage audits (#1541, #1542) check *membership* against outside lists; nothing checks *returns*. A known-answer test does: hold the whole universe with no signal and no costs, cap-weighted and equal-weighted, rebalanced monthly, and compare it year by year with SPY (cap-weighted S&P 500) and RSP (equal-weighted S&P 500). Our universe is about 1,000 names and not the S&P 500, so a steady, explainable gap is expected; a jump or a drift is a defect (missing names, a split or dividend applied wrongly, a delisting marked late, a timing slip), and the names behind the worst months say which.

The owner decided on #1598 (2026-10-12): build it as a feature (spec, plan, build), not a script; run it over the **full window, 2019-08 to 2026-09, holdout years included**, because the index carries no strategy information and the benchmarks' returns are public, with a guard on what the output states for holdout years; and it must **never be a strategy trial**: no row in `trials`, no change to any family's N, no holdout spend. When it first runs is decided after the build.

## Prior art (what we took)

Index providers and fund managers validate a replicating portfolio by two numbers, the annual active return (portfolio minus index) and the tracking error (the annualised standard deviation of the monthly active return), and they explain the residual by a short list of structural causes before they look for errors: membership differences, float adjustment of the weights, rebalance timing, fees and the treatment of dividends and corporate actions (full replication is expected to track within a few tens of basis points a year; see for example the [S&P Dow Jones index mathematics](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/methodologies/methodology-index-math.pdf), which also fixes the S&P 500's float adjustment and the Equal Weight index's **quarterly** rebalance). Academic data work does the same against a known series: a value-weighted market return rebuilt from one's own merged data is compared with CRSP's `VWRETD` or Ken French's market factor, and the differences are traced to delisting returns (Shumway, *Journal of Finance* 1997: the missing delisting return biases a survivors-at-last-close series upward), share-count timing and security-type filters. From that we take: (1) the two summary numbers, per year, not a single correlation; (2) the expected-gap list written down *before* the run, so a residual is judged against it; (3) attribution of the worst months to names by contribution (weight × return), the simplest form of return attribution, rather than a sector or factor decomposition we cannot verify; (4) a check that the tool sees a *known* defect (the #974 window) before its clean months are trusted. Backtesting frameworks (zipline, QuantConnect, `bt`) ship no such check; it is the user's.

## Users & usage

- **The owner** runs `tradepartner validation fetch-reference <SYMBOL>` once per reference symbol (an Alpaca read through the existing price source, written to a table no trial can read) and `tradepartner validation index [--release <name>]` after each named data release and when he wants a check; he reads the printed report and the report file, and decides the pass bars after the first run (open question 6). Only he runs it on the real store: a process rule (the owner's store is his; agents run on fixture stores, CLAUDE.md rule 9 and the common brief), not something the code enforces.
- **Agents** run it on fixture or temp-file stores only (the run is then `synthetic`, excluded from every listing by default), to test the tool and to prove the pipeline on a planted defect; never on the owner's store.
- **The registry and the backtest** are unchanged: `trials`, `trial_results`, `family_sharpes` (N and V), `family_holdout_spends` and `owner_decisions` never see a validation run.

## Definitions

- **Validation window**: `[validation.window_start, validation.window_end]`; the **rebalance sessions** T_0..T_n are `schedule.rebalance_sessions(start, end, "month_end")` inside it (the backtest spec's definitions at the ADR 0006 cadence); **fill session** F_i and **read time** close(T_i) as there. A **month i** is (T_i, T_{i+1}], its return is equity at close(T_{i+1}) over equity at close(T_i) minus one; a **year** is the calendar year of T_{i+1}.
- **Whole-universe index**: at each T_i the members of `universe_as_of(close(T_i))` (every admitted class row), weighted **equal** (1/k each) or **cap** (each row's `market_cap` over the members' sum; `market_cap` is the cover-page share count times the close, so it is total shares, not float), bought at F_i's fill price (`execution.fill_price`), marked through the month by the same adjusted-as-of-close(T_{i+1}) frame the engine uses (dividends reinvested in the paying name on the ex-date, splits cancel), with the engine's two exits (a delisted name sold at its last close on its listing's end session, a name with no bar on the last `backtest.stale_exit_sessions` sessions sold at its last bar), zero costs, no signal. Cash is held only by a missed fill or an exit, and is re-deployed at the next rebalance.
- **Benchmark return** for month i: for a symbol in the store as a benchmark security (SPY today, `store.benchmarks.benchmark_security_ids`, by symbol as #840 fixed it), the buy-and-hold series the engine would compute (bought at F_0, marked through the same frames). For a symbol that is **not** a stored benchmark (RSP), the **reference series**: the vendor's daily closes, dividends and splits for the symbol, stored in `reference_series` (req 2), turned into a total-return index by `total_return_index`, a pure function with the marking's own dividend convention (`store.asof.adjusted_prices_as_of`'s backward factor `1 - amount / prior_close` applied to every close before the ex-date, so the return across the ex-date is `P_ex / (P_prior - amount)`; a dividend whose prior bar is more than `adjust.max_prior_close_gap_sessions` sessions before the ex-date, or whose amount is at or above `adjust.max_dividend_to_prior_close` times the prior close, is dropped exactly as there and counted), and read at the same sessions.
- **Gap** for a (weighting, benchmark, month): index return minus benchmark return. **Year gap**: the compounded index return over the year's months minus the compounded benchmark return. **Tracking error** for a year: the standard deviation of the year's monthly gaps times √12.
- **Worst months** for a (weighting, benchmark): the `validation.worst_months` months with the largest |gap| in the window. **Attribution** of a worst month: every name held in it with its weight at F_i, its month return, its **contribution** (weight × return) and its **flags**: `missing_fill` (a member at T_i with no bar at F_i), `delisting_exit`, `stale_exit`, `large_move` (|month return| above `validation.suspect_abs_return`), `dividend` and `split` (an action of that type on the name with an ex-date inside the month, from `live_actions_as_of`), the top `validation.attribution_names` by |contribution| plus every flagged name; and, when T_i is a session the coverage reference (#1541) covers, the reference names (`coverage.reference_names`, resolved as `health.coverage_reference` resolves them) missing from the universe already read at T_i, as the names that *should* have contributed. The driver filters the reference against the universe it holds; it never calls `health.coverage_reference(conn, t)` per step, which rebuilds one universe per sample session up to `t`.
- **Guarded month**: a month whose T_{i+1} lies inside `[holdout_start, holdout_end]` of any row of `hypotheses` whose family is not `oracle`, read from the store at the run, never from config. A **guarded year** is a year holding a guarded month. On the owner's store today every month from 2024-01 to 2026-09 is guarded (H1, B3, B10 and the daily shakedown all hold out `[2024-01-01, 2026-09-30]`; B9's `[2027-01-04, 2027-06-30]` lies after the window).
- **Validation run**: one execution of the index computation under a `ValidationHandle`, issued by `store.validation.open_validation` before any data read, with its own result row (req 5). It is **not a trial**: the backtest spec's definition (one engine run under a hypothesis, from a `TrialHandle`) is unchanged, `engine.run` still refuses anything but a `TrialHandle`, and the driver here never calls it. It is not a research run either (research-registry spec, Definitions): the index has no treatment under study, since `market_cap` and the equal weight define the index being checked (as a benchmark's own weights do) rather than a feature whose relation to returns is measured; that spec's Definitions carry the same dated pointer this PR adds to the backtest spec. A run on a store whose file is not `settings.store.path` is **synthetic**, by the registry's file-identity check.
- **Reference series**: one vendor's raw daily closes, dividend amounts and split ratios for one symbol, written by the owner's `fetch-reference` command, with `known_at` = the fetch time (`snapshot` provenance), in a table outside `TABLE_NAMES`, outside `_TRIAL_FACT_TABLES` (so writing it moves no trial's `data_vintage`), outside `TABLE_PROVENANCE_VALUES` (so writing it moves no `store_max_ingested_at`, which `open_trial` records and `_store_changed` falls back to, and health's per-fact-table scans never see it) and outside every as-of function (no trial can read it, tested). A refetch of a symbol replaces its rows in one transaction, with the new fetch time as `known_at`; it is a copy of vendor data, not a point-in-time fact.

## Requirements

Each requirement is a property with its done-test; the plan names the test files.

1. **What it computes.** For every weighting in `validation.weightings` (`equal`, `cap`) and every rebalance session of the window, the index of the Definitions, from one read set per step as the engine reads (the universe at close(T_i); the marking frame, the listing ends and the actions at close(T_{i+1})), zero costs, no signal, at the live `execution.fill_price`, `backtest.stale_exit_sessions`, `universe.*`, `adjust.*`, `master.*` and `gap.*` values, every one of them recorded on the run (req 5). *Done-test:* on the fixture store the equal-weight index over the fixture window reproduces a hand-computed equity path on a three-name fixture (one split, one dividend, one delisting inside the window), and the cap-weight index weights at every T_i equal `market_cap / sum(market_cap)` over `universe_as_of(close(T_i)).members` to 1e-12; with no exits and no missed fills, equity at close(T_{i+1}) equals the weight-sum of the members' fill-to-close ratios times equity at F_i. A universe that holds a benchmark security id at any T_i is refused, as the engine refuses it (an ETF classified `common` is a defect to see, not to weight). **Parity with the engine:** on the fixture store, a `synthetic` fixture trial of `engine.run` at zero cost with `strategy.top_fraction = 1.0`, equal weight, over the same window has an equity path equal to the equal-weight index's to 1e-9 at every close where the engine's scored set equals the universe's member set (the momentum signal scores only names with its formation history; the test picks a window where the two sets coincide and asserts they do), so the index's step is tied to the engine's loop rather than a free re-statement of it.
2. **Benchmarks and the reference series.** `validation.benchmarks` (default `["SPY", "RSP"]`) names the comparison series. A symbol that is a stored benchmark security is marked from the store as the engine would mark it (Definitions). Any other symbol is read from `reference_series`, written only by `tradepartner validation fetch-reference <SYMBOL> --since <date>`: the symbol-keyed raw reads `adapters.alpaca_raw.daily_bars` and `adapters.alpaca_raw.corporate_actions`, parsed by `adapters.alpaca_prices.parse_bars` and `parse_corporate_actions` with a one-symbol resolver (`PriceSource.bars` is keyed by security id and resolves through the master's listings, which RSP must never enter; neither adapter file is edited), month by month as `backfill-benchmark` fetches. It refuses a symbol the store holds as a **tradable** security's ticker (a `benchmark`-flagged security does not refuse: open question 3 (b) may want a stored benchmark's reference series), writes nothing for a month with a missing session, and prints the dividend count per calendar year, flagging any year whose count differs from the window's most common count (a missing quarterly dividend would bias the reference down by about 0.4 pp and read as a pipeline gap). The frozen `benchmarks` key, the master, `prices_daily` and `corporate_actions` are untouched: the owner's store gains nothing a trial can read, and `data_vintage` at any cutoff is the same before and after a reference write. A run whose window has a session with no reference value for a named symbol is `refused` with the first such session named. *Done-tests:* `reference_series` is not in `TABLE_NAMES`, `_TRIAL_FACT_TABLES` or `REGISTRY_TABLE_NAMES` (pinned); `registry.data_vintage(conn, cutoff)` and `registry.store_max_ingested_at(conn)` on the fixture store are byte-equal before and after `write_reference_series`; `reference_series` is not in `TABLE_PROVENANCE_VALUES` (pinned); no module under `backtest/` other than `validation_run.py` and no as-of function imports or names the table (a grep test, like the "nothing under `backtest/` imports adapters" test); `total_return_index` on a hand-made series with one dividend and one split equals the hand-computed values, and over a stored benchmark's own bars and actions equals the engine's buy-and-hold series for that benchmark to 1e-9 (the two conventions are one convention).
3. **Outputs.** The run prints and writes (the report file, `validation.report_dir/<validation_id>.md`, its SHA-256 on the run row) for every (weighting, benchmark): a per-year table of year gap and tracking error; the worst months with their gaps; and each worst month's attribution (Definitions). The **expected-gap list** (req 7) is printed above the tables, so the reader judges the residual against it. The detail rows go to `validation_months` and `validation_attribution` (Data / interfaces). *Done-test:* on the fixture store with a planted defect (a copy with one top-cap member's bars deleted for three consecutive months after a rebalance), the months show a gap against the fixture's SPY that the clean copy does not, the month with the deletion is among the worst months, and the attribution for it lists that name flagged `missing_fill` for the month it had no fill and `stale_exit` where the exit rule fires.
4. **The holdout guard.** For a guarded year (Definitions), the printed report, the report file, `validation_months` and `validation_attribution` carry only the gaps, the tracking error, the worst months by gap and their attribution: the index's own month and year returns are **not stated** (`universe_return` is NULL on guarded months and the per-year table shows the gap column only). The benchmark's returns are public and are shown. The guard is **presentational**: the index's guarded month and year returns are arithmetically recoverable as benchmark return plus gap, and equal-minus-cap over the guarded years (from the two gaps and the two public benchmark returns) is a size-and-concentration-tilt return on the shared holdout; the owner accepted on #1598 that these carry no strategy information, and the Risks section names the hypotheses that must disclose them. The run row records the guarded years it applied (`guarded_years_json`). *Done-tests:* on a fixture store with a registered fixture hypothesis whose holdout covers the last year of the window, every row of that year has `universe_return` NULL and `gap` filled, the report text for that year contains no index return figure (the test greps the year's section for the index's level, which it knows from the unguarded run on the same store without the registration), and the unguarded years are identical between the two runs; a store with no `hypotheses` row guards nothing.
5. **Logging: the validation record, outside the trial count.** `open_validation(conn, ...)` writes the `validation_runs` row and returns the only `ValidationHandle` there is, committed in its own write chunk before any data read; the driver raises `TypeError` without one, before any provider read (ADR 0005, no id, no run, as `engine.run` does). The row carries: `started_at`, `code_version`, `code_dirty`, `store_max_ingested_at`, `data_vintage` at close(T_n) (the same `registry.data_vintage`), `release_name` (the newest closed data release at the start, NULL when none; `--release <name>` must match it or the run is refused), `config_sha256` and `config_json` (the canonical JSON of `validation.*` plus every live key req 1 lists), `window_start`, `window_end`, `benchmarks_json`, `reference_sha256_json` (per reference symbol, the SHA-256 of its rows as read), `owner_ok_ref` (required, non-blank: the issue and date of the owner's full-window OK, `#1598 2026-10-12` for the first run and every rerun under it; this is CLAUDE.md non-negotiable 7's explicit flag for this run kind, since the run reads holdout sessions through the as-of API outside the registry's holdout lock, and the row is the log rule 7 asks for), `guarded_years_json`, `synthetic`, `run_by`, `note`; `close_validation` writes `finished_at`, `status ∈ {ok, failed, refused}`, `message`, `report_path`, `report_sha256`, `verdict ∈ {report-only, pass, fail}` (req 7). A failure or a refusal is a run with a row, like a trial. Nothing is written to `trials`, `trial_results`, `owner_decisions`, `research_runs` or any journal table; `validation_runs` is in `VALIDATION_TABLE_NAMES`, disjoint from `TABLE_NAMES`, `REGISTRY_TABLE_NAMES`, `RESEARCH_TABLE_NAMES` and the journal tables, so no count query can reach it. *Done-tests:* after a validation run on a fixture store holding a registered hypothesis and one closed trial, `family_sharpes(family)` (N and V), `family_holdout_spends(family)`, `list_trials` and `research.family_run_count` are byte-equal to before the run and the `trials`, `owner_decisions` and `research_runs` row counts are unchanged; `validation_run.run` without a `ValidationHandle` raises `TypeError` before any read (a provider that records calls sees none); a run with an empty `--owner-ok` is a usage error with no row; a run on a temp-file copy of the fixture store is `synthetic` and refused on a store that is neither `settings.store.path` nor `fixture`-marked (the strategy-lab spec's marker rule, as `UnmarkedStoreRefused` applies it).
6. **Point-in-time.** Every read uses a rebalance close as `t`: membership and caps from `universe_as_of(close(T_i), settings)`, the marking frame from `adjusted_prices_as_of(close(T_{i+1}), ids, include_dividends=True, traded_only=True, sessions_from=T_i)`, exits from `listing_ends_as_of(close(T_{i+1}))`, actions from `live_actions_as_of(close(T_{i+1}))`; ids restricted to members and holdings; one short-lived read-only connection per step from `connect` (the fact reads only), and a separate `registry_connect` (default `connect`) for the handle and its close, `registered_holdouts`, the benchmark identity (`benchmark_security_ids`: SPY's master rows are `snapshot_static`, invisible on a store truncated at an in-sample close), the vintage and `store changed` checks and the coverage reference, exactly as `engine.run` and `StoreProvider` split them. The reference series is the answer key, not an input, and is read whole through `registry_connect`. *Done-tests* (`tests/lookahead/test_index_validation.py`, on the harness's `TruncatedStore`): **truncation invariance**, the run to T_n on the full fixture store equals the run on the store truncated at close(T_n), for every T_n of the fixture window; **prefix invariance**, the run to T_k is the prefix of the run to T_n in every month row and weight; **revisions known after close(T_i)**, each seeded and checked alone as the engine suite does: a later shares fact for a member leaves the weights at T_i unchanged and changes a later T_j's; a bar revision at T_i on a member, known after close(T_i), leaves the membership and the cap weights at T_i unchanged (`market_cap` is shares times the T_i close) and moves them if the code reads at the wrong time; a dividend first known after close(T_{i+1}) with its ex-date inside month i leaves month i's return unchanged in the run to T_{i+1} and enters the run to T_{i+2} as a late dividend; **non-vacuity** as `test_the_run_is_not_vacuous` states it: the truncation walk crosses the fixture's seeded revision facts (a bar, a dividend, a shares fact; `SEC_DIV_REVISED`, `SEC_SPLIT_BACKFILLED` among them) on names the index holds, which in a whole-universe index is every member, and the membership changes at least once.
7. **Expected gap and pass bars.** The report states, before any number, the structural reasons the index is not the benchmark, with their signs where known: (a) membership, about 1,000 names on NYSE, Nasdaq and NYSE American against the 500 committee-selected S&P members (which also pass S&P's positive-earnings entry rule; the top-1,000 admits unprofitable names), so a mid-cap tilt in both weightings and a stronger one in equal weight; (a') multi-class companies: the index weights every admitted class row (two equal-weight slots and class-level caps for GOOGL/GOOG, BRK and the like) where S&P and RSP treat the company once and split its weight across classes; and the universe's own exclusions beside (i) below, rule 6 (price jumps, the twelve-month history) and the stale or out-of-line shares rules, each counted per rebalance in `universe_as_of`'s exclusions; (b) weights from total cover-page shares, not float-adjusted, so closely held names are overweighted against SPY; (c) RSP rebalances quarterly, the index here monthly; (d) the ETFs carry their expense ratios (SPY about 0.09%, RSP about 0.20% a year) and the index pays none, so the index is favoured by about that much; (e) no delisting return: a delisted name is sold at its last close (ADR 0004), the optimistic convention, so the index is favoured against a benchmark whose constituents are sold at the real price; (f) dividends reinvested in the paying name on the ex-date, against an ETF's quarterly cash distribution; (g) the fill at F_i's close against the index's T_i close, one session of timing; (h) **the #974 first-span gap**: 192 names (about 19% of member-months 2019-11 to 2020-06, META and Elevance among them) are outside the universe until 2020-07-31 or 2020-08-31 because their bars start at their first ticker-bearing cover page, so the cap-weight index 2019-08 to 2020-07 is expected to differ from SPY through the COVID crash and recovery, with the sign set by the missing names' own returns (META outperformed in that span), which the attribution's coverage-reference names must show; (i) rules 4 and 5 (the $5 price and the liquidity floor) drop a few S&P names in a stressed month. **Pass bars** are config (`validation.bars.*`, per weighting: `max_abs_year_gap`, `max_tracking_error`, `max_abs_month_gap`, **all plain returns** (0.04 is four points), the unit `gap` and the tracking error are stored in; and `validation.exempt_before`, a date: months whose T_{i+1} is on or before it are reported, not judged), **default unset**, under which the verdict is `report-only`; set, the verdict is `pass` when every judged year and month is inside the bars and `fail` otherwise, printed as a verdict beside each number. A partial year (2019 has four months in the window, 2026 nine) is marked `partial` in the year table, and its tracking error is judged only when it holds at least six months. The numbers are the owner's (open question 6); this spec proposes them and decides none. *Done-test:* with the bars unset the verdict is `report-only` whatever the numbers; with bars set on the fixture, a year gap over the bar gives `fail` and names the year, and a month on or before `exempt_before` is listed as `exempt` and never fails.
8. **Reruns: the standing suite.** The run is cheap to repeat and is meant to be: the runbook adds one step to the data-releases runbook's close ("after `decision data-release close`, run `validation index --release <name> --owner-ok <the standing OK's reference>`") and a monthly step after the month-end ingest. `tradepartner validation list` shows every non-synthetic run with its release name, its config hash, the verdict and the per-year cap-vs-SPY and equal-vs-RSP gaps in one row each, and marks a run **stale** when `registry.data_vintage` at its cutoff no longer equals the recorded one (the trials' own rule), so two rows a release apart are compared by eye and a stale clean run is not trusted. The coverage reference (#1541) is the membership half of the same suite; the attribution joins it (Definitions) and the runbook pairs the two commands; nothing of #1542 is built here. *Done-test:* a reference write leaves a prior run current (req 2) and a `prices_daily` row inserted with `known_at` inside the window marks it stale.
9. **Config.** A `validation` section on `Settings`, last in the list after `health`, none of it frozen, none of it in any fingerprint, canonical set or `FROZEN_KEY_DEFAULTS` (so no registration moves): `window_start = 2019-08-01`, `window_end = 2026-09-30`, `benchmarks = ["SPY", "RSP"]`, `weightings = ["equal", "cap"]`, `worst_months = 6`, `attribution_names = 10`, `suspect_abs_return = 0.5`, `exempt_before = None`, `bars = {}` (per weighting, req 7, plain returns), `report_dir = "data/validation"`; every key listed in `.env.example`; the section goes last in `Settings` and the comment on `health` that says it is last is updated in the same change. *Done-test:* `frozen_keys()`, H1's and the TWIN's canonical sets and fingerprints are unchanged after the section lands (the existing pins); the defaults are pinned by value and an unknown key is refused.

## Out of scope

A dashboard page (a follow-up once the owner has read two reports); a sector or factor decomposition of the gap; S&P 500 constituent lists (the attribution uses the coverage reference's names, not the index's); a delisting-return convention (backtest spec open question 4); any change to the engine, `StoreProvider`, the trial registry's tables, the frozen key set, the `benchmarks` key or the master; running on the owner's store by an agent; the #1542 audit itself.

## Data / interfaces

**Tables** (`VALIDATION_TABLE_NAMES`, additive at the next schema version, 25 today; if another migration lands first with 25, this one takes the next number and says so in its docstring, as the paper plan's T132 did):

## Risks & domain checks

- **Look-ahead:** every read is at a rebalance close through the as-of API; req 6's invariance suite proves it on the harness. The reference series is read whole on purpose: it is the answer, not an input, and nothing it holds reaches a trial (req 2's pins).
- **Survivorship:** the index inherits the engine's optimistic last-close exit (ADR 0003 rule 6); the per-month exit counts are in `validation_months` and the expected-gap list names the bias and its sign, so a clean cap-vs-SPY year is not read as proof of a clean delisting path.
- **Multiple testing:** by construction the run writes no `trials` row and nothing reads `validation_*` for N, V or spends (req 5's pins). The holdout months are *read* by the run (the owner's decision on #1598); the guard limits what is stated, not what is computed, and the run row records the guarded years and the owner's OK. The guard is presentational (req 4), so what was seen is the whole-universe cap-weight and equal-weight returns over 2024-01 to 2026-09 and their difference, a size-and-concentration-tilt return. Every later hypothesis file in any family, and in particular a size, equal-weight, low-concentration or whole-universe one, must disclose that in its prior-evidence section (backtest spec, "prior-evidence disclosure"), naming the validation run id; `spec-critic` checks that on the hypothesis PR. The four files already registered with that holdout (H1, B3, B10, the daily shakedown) are frozen and hashed (`doc_sha256`), so they are not edited; open question 9 asks the owner how their disclosure is recorded.
- **The owner's store:** nothing writes to a trial-readable table; the reference table is outside the vintage (req 2's byte-equal test), so no sweep goes stale and no rerun enters N because of this feature. The fetch is owner-run and explicit; an agent runs only on fixture or temp-file stores, where the run is `synthetic` by the file-identity rule (the rule labels, it does not block: keeping agents off the real store is the process rule above).
- **Costs, fill realism:** none by design (zero costs, the fill convention recorded); the comparison is of data and arithmetic, not of a strategy.
- **Secrets, order path, LLM inputs:** none. The fetch uses the Alpaca data keys through the existing adapter; no new variable.
- **Store consistency and locks:** as the engine: the handle is written in its own chunk, steps read on short-lived read-only connections, and a store that changed between the handle and the last step (`data_vintage` at close(T_n) differs from the handle's) fails the run (`store changed during run`).
- **Why no ADR:** nothing here is hard to reverse (additive tables, dropped to roll back; no vendor, no risk rule, no change to what a trial is: the engine's `TrialHandle` refusal and the fingerprint pins stay green) and the one semantic addition, a logged run kind that is neither a trial nor a research run, is recorded here and by a dated pointer in each registry spec's Definitions. CLAUDE.md non-negotiable 7 ("every backtest run is logged; never touch the holdout without an explicit flag") and charter principle 2 (keep a locked holdout) are kept as written: the run is logged in its own table under its own handle, and `--owner-ok` is the explicit flag, recorded on the row. Open question 1 asks the owner to confirm.

## Open questions

1. **No ADR.** The reasoning is above ("Why no ADR": additive, reversible, rule 7 and charter principle 2 kept, the flag recorded). Options: (a) none, this spec and the two registry-spec pointers record it; (b) a short ADR "validation runs are logged outside the trial count". Recommendation: (a).
2. **Where RSP lives.** Options: (a) as specified: a `reference_series` table outside the vintage, filled by an owner-run fetch, no change to the frozen `benchmarks` key; (b) add RSP to `benchmarks` and run `backfill-benchmark RSP`: every registered hypothesis then fails `load_frozen`'s key-set check or re-registers, every trial buys a third benchmark, and the new `prices_daily` rows move `data_vintage` at every cutoff in the window, so every sweep variant goes stale and reruns count in N; (c) a file under `data/validation/`, read by the run: no schema change, but outside the store and its locks, and a second place to back up. Recommendation: (a); (b) is refused by its cost to N, (c) is a file nobody lists.
3. **SPY through the store or through the reference series too.** Options: (a) as specified: SPY from the store, as the engine marks it, so the comparison also exercises the benchmark path trials use; (b) both SPY series, with the store-marked minus vendor-adjusted difference as a check on the dividend arithmetic. Recommendation: (a) now; (b) is one extra row the owner can ask for after the first report, at the cost of one more fetch.
4. **The guard's unit.** The owner said "holdout years". Options: (a) as specified: a year with any guarded month is guarded whole; (b) month by month. Recommendation: (a); today the guarded months are whole years anyway (2024-01 to 2026-09).
5. **The first-span exemption.** `validation.exempt_before` for the #974 window. Options: (a) `2020-08-31` (the last entry date #974 names), months on or before it reported but never judged; (b) none, and the bars are set wide enough to pass through it, which hides a defect elsewhere in the window. Recommendation: (a), set together with the bars.
6. **The pass bars** (the owner decides after the first report; none is set until then). Proposed, as plain returns, per year outside the exemption: cap vs SPY `max_abs_year_gap = 0.04`, `max_tracking_error = 0.04`; equal vs RSP `max_abs_year_gap = 0.06`, `max_tracking_error = 0.06`; any month `max_abs_month_gap = 0.05` for both. Recommendation: (c), because a bar set before any number is seen is a guess, and the first two reports (before and after the next release) show the spread.
