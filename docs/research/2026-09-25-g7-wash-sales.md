# Research Report: G7, IRS primary sources on wash sales and holding periods

**Brief:** #50  ·  **Date:** 2026-09-25  ·  **Status:** COMPLETE  ·  **Agent/model:** researcher, claude-opus-5-5 (orchestrated by team macedonia on Fable 5.1)

## Answer
**Verdict:** SUPPORTED (the handoff §7 D5 wash-sale note, checked against primary sources)  ·  **Confidence:** high

Under IRC §1091(a) and Pub 550, you cannot deduct a loss on stock or securities if you buy substantially identical stock or securities within 30 days before or after the sale. The rule also applies if you acquire them in a fully taxable trade, acquire a contract or option to buy them, or acquire them for your IRA or Roth IRA. Pub 550 also counts a purchase by your spouse or by a corporation you control. The disallowed loss is added to the basis of the replacement shares (§1091(d)), except when the IRA or Roth IRA made the purchase. In that case the IRA's basis is not increased (Rev. Rul. 2008-5; Pub 550, "except in (4) above"). The replacement's holding period includes the old shares' holding period (§1223(3)). Holding more than 1 year makes a gain or loss long-term. You count from the day after the trade date and include the sale's trade date (§1222; Pub 550). Brokers must report wash sales only when both trades are in the same account and have the same CUSIP. A loss is still disallowed even if the broker does not report it, and the taxpayer reports it on Form 8949 with code W. None of the sources define "substantially identical" for funds. They say nothing about two ETFs or index funds on the same index, about trades made wholly inside an IRA, about DRIP purchases as replacements, or about fractional shares for wash-sale matching.

## Plain-language rules, each with a citation

Pub 550 quotes are from Pub 550 (2025), chapter 4, the "Wash Sales" or "Holding Period" section, as shown.

| # | Rule (plain language) | Operative text (quoted) | Citation |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **The 61-day window.** A loss is disallowed if substantially identical stock or securities are acquired in the period from 30 days before to 30 days after the loss sale. | "within a period beginning 30 days before the date of such sale or disposition and ending 30 days after such date, the taxpayer has acquired (by purchase or by an exchange on which the entire amount of gain or loss was recognized by law), or has entered into a contract or option so to acquire, substantially identical stock or securities, then no deduction shall be allowed under section 165" | 26 U.S.C. §1091(a). 26 CFR 1.1091-1(a) calls this the "61-day period" |
| R2 | **The four triggers, in the IRS's own words.** | "A wash sale occurs when you sell or trade stock or securities at a loss and within 30 days before or after the sale you: Buy substantially identical stock or securities, Acquire substantially identical stock or securities in a fully taxable trade, Acquire a contract or option to buy substantially identical stock or securities, or Acquire substantially identical stock for your individual retirement arrangement (IRA) or Roth IRA." | Pub 550, "Wash Sales" |
| R3 | **Spouse or controlled corporation.** A purchase by either one triggers a wash sale on your loss. | "If you sell stock and your spouse or a corporation you control buys substantially identical stock, you also have a wash sale." | Pub 550, "Wash Sales" (this is not in the §1091 or Reg. 1.1091-1 text I retrieved; Pub 550 is the source) |
| R4 | **Only losses are affected, and dealers are exempt.** | §1091(a) begins "In the case of any loss claimed…". Pub 550: "You cannot deduct losses from sales or trades of stock or securities in a wash sale unless the loss was incurred in the ordinary course of your business as a dealer in stock or securities." | §1091(a); Pub 550, "Wash Sales" and "Dealers" |
| R5 | **Contracts and options, including cash-settled ones.** Commodity futures and foreign currency are excluded. | §1091(f): "This section shall not fail to apply to a contract or option … solely by reason of the fact that the contract or option settles in (or could be settled in) cash…". Pub 550: "The wash sale rules apply to losses from sales or trades of contracts and options to acquire or sell stock or securities. They do not apply to losses from sales or trades of commodity futures contracts and foreign currencies." | §1091(f); Pub 550, "Options and futures contracts" |
| R6 | **Warrants.** The rule is asymmetric. | "The wash sale rules apply if you sell common stock at a loss and, at the same time, buy warrants for common stock of the same corporation. But if you sell warrants at a loss and, at the same time, buy common stock in the same corporation, the wash sale rules apply only if the warrants and stock are considered substantially identical" | Pub 550, "Warrants" |
| R7 | **"Substantially identical" is a facts-and-circumstances test.** Neither the statute nor the regulation defines it. | "In determining whether stock or securities are substantially identical, you must consider all the facts and circumstances in your particular case. Ordinarily, stocks or securities of one corporation are not considered substantially identical to stocks or securities of another corporation. However, they may be substantially identical in some cases. For example, in a reorganization, the stocks and securities of the predecessor and successor corporations may be substantially identical." | Pub 550, "Substantially identical". Not defined in §1091 or 26 CFR 1.1091-1 |
| R8 | **Bonds and preferred stock versus common stock.** Convertible preferred can be substantially identical to common if it meets five conditions. | "bonds or preferred stock of a corporation are not ordinarily considered substantially identical to the common stock of the same corporation. However, where the bonds or preferred stock are convertible into common stock of the same corporation, the relative values, price changes, and other circumstances may make these bonds or preferred stock and the common stock substantially identical." The five conditions: convertible into common, same voting rights, same dividend restrictions, trades at prices that "do not vary significantly from the conversion ratio", and "unrestricted as to convertibility". | Pub 550, "Substantially identical" |
| R9 | **Basis adjustment, with the IRA exception.** | §1091(d): "the basis shall be the basis of the stock or securities so sold or disposed of, increased or decreased … by the difference … between the price at which the property was acquired and the price at which such substantially identical stock or securities were sold". Pub 550: "If your loss was disallowed because of the wash sale rules, add the disallowed loss to the cost of the new stock or securities (except in (4) above). The result is your basis in the new stock or securities. This adjustment postpones the loss deduction until the disposition of the new stock or securities." | §1091(d); Pub 550, "Wash Sales"; 1099-B Instructions, Box 1g: "Increase the adjusted basis of the acquired securities by the amount of the disallowed loss reported in box 1g." |
| R10 | **Holding period carries over to the replacement shares.** | §1223(3): "there shall be included the period for which he held the stock or securities the loss from the sale or other disposition of which was not deductible." Pub 550: "Your holding period for the new stock or securities includes the holding period of the stock or securities sold." | 26 U.S.C. §1223(3) (not §1223(4), which covers §307 stock distributions); Pub 550, "Wash Sales" and "Holding Period: Wash sales" |
| R11 | **More or fewer shares bought than sold.** Match the replacement shares in the order they were bought. | Reg.: "The stock or securities acquired will be matched in accordance with the order of their acquisition (beginning with the earliest acquisition)". Pub 550: "You do this by matching the shares bought with an equal number of the shares sold. Match the shares bought in the same order that you bought them, beginning with the first shares bought. The shares or securities so matched are subject to the wash sale rules." | §1091(b)-(c); 26 CFR 1.1091-1(c)-(d); Pub 550, "More or less stock bought than sold" |
| R12 | **Worked example: partial disallowance spread across lots.** Pub 550 Example 1: 100 shares bought Sept 20, 2024 for $5,000. 50 shares bought Dec 13, 2024 for $2,750 and 25 shares bought Dec 20, 2024 for $1,125. The 100 shares sold Jan 3, 2025 for $4,000. The $750 loss on 75 shares is disallowed and $250 is deductible. The 50-share lot's basis becomes "$3,250 ($2,750 + $500)" and the 25-share lot's basis becomes "$1,375 ($1,125 + $250)". Reg. 1.1091-1(h) Example (2) uses the same figures with 1954-55 dates. | Quoted figures | Pub 550, "More or less stock bought than sold", Example 1; 26 CFR 1.1091-1(h) Ex. (2) |
| R13 | **Each replacement share absorbs only one loss, and multiple losses are processed in order of sale.** | Reg. (e): "The acquisition of any share of stock or any security which results in the nondeductibility of a loss under the provisions of this section shall be disregarded in determining the deductibility of any other loss." Reg. (b): "losses in the order in which the stock or securities the disposition of which resulted in the respective losses were disposed of" | 26 CFR 1.1091-1(b), (e) |
| R14 | **Loss and gain on the same day.** A wash-sale loss on one block cannot offset gains on identical blocks sold that day. | "Loss from a wash sale of one block of stock or securities cannot be used to reduce any gains on identical blocks sold the same day." | Pub 550, "Loss and gain on same day" |
| R15 | **Tax-year boundary.** A December sale bought back in January is still a wash sale, and the IRS gives this exact case as an example. | "On December 27, 2024, you sold 300 shares of X stock for $125 per share. On January 10, 2025, you bought 250 shares of identical X stock. You cannot deduct the loss of $33 per share on the first block because within 30 days after the date of sale, you bought 250 identical shares of X stock." | Pub 550, "Loss and gain on same day", Example. The reverse case (December buy, January sale) is Pub 550 Example 1 above and Reg. 1.1091-1(h) Ex. (1)-(2) |
| R16 | **IRA purchase.** The loss is disallowed and the IRA's basis is not increased. | Rev. Rul. 2008-5 holding: "The loss on the Sale of stock is disallowed under § 1091. A's basis in the individual retirement account or Roth IRA is not increased by virtue of § 1091(d)." It reasons from *Security First National Bank* (28 BTA 289, 1933) that the IRA title "amounts only to a refinement of title and may be disregarded." Pub 550's trigger (4) and "(except in (4) above)" say the same. | Rev. Rul. 2008-5, IRB 2008-3; Pub 550, "Wash Sales" |
| R17 | **Trades made wholly inside an IRA.** None of the sources addresses this. | No text found | See Disconfirmation |
| R18 | **Short-term versus long-term.** | §1222: short-term is "held for not more than 1 year" and long-term is "held for more than 1 year". Pub 550: "If you hold investment property more than 1 year, any capital gain or loss is a long-term capital gain or loss. If you hold the property 1 year or less, any capital gain or loss is a short-term capital gain or loss." | 26 U.S.C. §1222(1)-(4); Pub 550, "Holding Period: Long-term or Short-term" |
| R19 | **How to count the holding period.** Start the day after acquisition, include the day of sale, and use trade dates rather than settlement dates. | Pub 550: "begin counting on the date after the day you acquired the property. The day you disposed of the property is part of your holding period." "For securities traded on an established securities market, your holding period begins the day after the trade date you bought the securities, and ends on the trade date you sold them." "Do not confuse the trade date with the settlement date." Pub 550 example: bought Jan 31, 2024 and sold Jan 29, 2025 is short-term; sold Feb 6, 2025 is long-term. Form 8949 Instr.: "begin counting on the day after you received the property and include the day you disposed of it." | Pub 550, "Holding Period"; Form 8949 Instructions (2025), "Short-Term or Long-Term" |
| R20 | **Year of sale follows the trade date.** | "You sold stock on December 31, 2025. … the sale was closed by delivery … in January 2026. … Report your gain or loss on your 2025 return … Your holding period ended on December 31." | Pub 550, "Securities traded on an established market", Example |
| R21 | **Fractional shares bought through an automatic investment service.** This is a holding-period rule only, not a wash-sale rule. | "full shares are considered bought first and any fractional shares are considered bought last. Your holding period starts on the day after the bank's purchase date. If a share was bought over more than one purchase date, your holding period for that share is a split holding period." | Pub 550, "Holding Period: Automatic investment service" |
| R22 | **What brokers report is narrow.** | 1099-B Instr.: "You must report any loss disallowed under section 1091 if both the sale and purchase transactions occur in the same account with respect to covered securities with the same CUSIP number." "You are permitted but are not required to report in box 1g all loss disallowed under section 1091." Pub 550 gives the same conditions: covered securities, "the same CUSIP numbers", and "bought in the same account". | Instructions for Form 1099-B (2026), Box 1g; Pub 550, "Nondeductible wash sale loss" |
| R23 | **The taxpayer's own duty.** The loss is disallowed whether or not the broker reports it. Report it on Form 8949 with code W and correct box 1g if it is wrong. | Pub 550: "However, you cannot deduct a loss from a wash sale even if it is not reported on Form 1099-B." "Report a wash sale transaction in Part I or Part II of Form 8949 with the appropriate box checked. Complete all columns. Enter "W" in column (f). Enter as a positive number in column (g) the amount of the loss not allowed." 8949 Instr.: "…the amount of nondeductible wash sale loss shown in box 1g on Form 1099-B … is incorrect, enter the correct amount of the nondeductible loss as a positive number in column (g)." | Pub 550, "Nondeductible wash sale loss" and "How to report"; Form 8949 Instructions (2025), columns (f) and (g) |
| R24 | **Covered-security scope.** This defines which lots the broker tracks basis for. | "Stock acquired for cash in an account after 2010 except stock for which the average basis method is available." | Instructions for Form 1099-B (2026) |

## What a monthly-rebalanced system must log to handle wash sales

Each item comes from a rule above. Brokers only report wash sales within one account and one CUSIP (R22), but the loss is disallowed across accounts, IRAs, and a spouse's trades whether or not it is reported (R2, R3, R16, R23). The system therefore needs its own lot ledger across every related account.

1. **A lot record for each acquisition:** lot id, account id, account type (taxable, traditional IRA, Roth IRA, other), account owner (self, spouse, or controlled corporation, per R3), security identifier (**CUSIP as well as ticker**, R22), **trade date** (not settlement date; R19) stored as a UTC timestamp together with the exchange-local trade date, quantity including fractions, and cost basis.
2. **A disposal record for each sale:** lots closed, trade date, quantity, proceeds, and realized gain or loss before any wash-sale adjustment.
3. **A 61-day window scan for each loss sale (R1, R2):** every acquisition of the same or a candidate substantially identical security from 30 days before to 30 days after the sale. The scan covers **all accounts of the owner, the owner's IRA and Roth IRA, the spouse's accounts, and accounts of any controlled corporation** (R2, R3, R16), and includes contracts and options to buy (R5) and warrants (R6). The window extends past the sale date and across December 31 (R15), so the scan must run again as later purchases arrive, including purchases in the next tax year.
4. **Replacement-share matching:** each disallowed loss is matched to specific replacement lots and quantities, taking the first shares bought first (R11). Record which replacement shares have already absorbed a loss (R13), the order of multiple losses (R13), and same-day blocks so that a wash loss is not netted against gains on those blocks (R14).
5. **Disallowed loss** for each sale and each matched replacement lot, including partial disallowance allocated across lots (R12).
6. **Carried-forward basis adjustment** on each replacement lot (R9). If the replacement lot is in an IRA or Roth IRA, record **no basis increase** and mark the loss as disallowed with no carry-forward (R16).
7. **Carried-forward holding-period adjustment:** the replacement lot's adjusted holding-period start, which includes the holding period of the lot sold (R10). This moves the long-term date that R18 and R19 use.
8. **Split holding periods for fractional or multi-date shares** when purchases are made through an automatic investment service (R21).
9. **Broker-reported figures** (1099-B box 1g and basis) next to the system's own figures, so any difference can be reported on Form 8949 with code W (R22, R23).
10. **Tax-year tags:** the tax year of the sale, set by trade date (R20), and a flag when the window crosses December 31 (R15).
11. **Substantially-identical decisions:** for each pair of securities (for example, two ETFs on the same index), record the decision and the reason. Pub 550 offers only a facts-and-circumstances test with no fund-level guidance (R7), so the owner's policy has to be written down and applied the same way every time.

- **Evidence against:** nothing contradicts the D5 note. The primary sources are **silent or ambiguous** on the following:
  - **Two ETFs or index funds on the same index:** not addressed. "Exchange-traded" appears in Pub 550 only in the commodity futures definitions. Pub 550's only fund-related rule mentioning "mutual fund" is a separate holding-period loss rule ("Loss on mutual fund or REIT stock held 6 months or less"), not wash-sale guidance. The test is facts and circumstances (R7), and the one default it states ("stocks or securities of one corporation are not considered substantially identical to stocks or securities of another corporation") is written for corporate stock. Whether it carries over to two funds from different sponsors tracking the same index is **not answered**, and I have not filled the gap.
  - **Trades made wholly inside an IRA:** not addressed. Pub 550 and Rev. Rul. 2008-5 cover only a loss in a taxable account with the replacement bought in an IRA. I infer that IRA-internal losses give no §165 deduction to disallow, but that is my inference and **UNVERIFIED**.
  - **IRA sale followed by a taxable-account repurchase:** not addressed.
  - **Automated or systematic rebalancing:** no carve-out and no mention. §1091(a) and Pub 550 contain no intent test. The only exemption is for dealers (R4).
  - **Fractional shares in wash-sale matching:** not addressed. Pub 550 gives a fractional-share holding-period rule only for automatic investment services (R21). The matching rules (R11) are written in terms of "shares".
  - **Dividend reinvestment as a replacement purchase:** not addressed in the wash-sale section. "Reinvest" appears elsewhere in Pub 550, but not in the wash-sale text. Pub 550's "incentive pay plan" example shows that non-purchase acquisitions can trigger the rule ("If you receive another bonus award of substantially identical stock within 30 days of the sale, you cannot deduct your loss"). Applying that to DRIP is an inference.
  - **The meaning of "a corporation you control":** Pub 550 does not define "control" in the wash-sale section.
  - **Corrections to the brief:** the holding-period tacking rule is §1223(3), not §1223(4). Also, the Form 8949 instructions refer to Pub 550 "for more information on wash sales involving substantially similar stock or securities". "Similar" is the IRS's actual wording, as the orchestrator confirmed against the raw text. The statute and Pub 550 both use "substantially identical". I treat the 8949 wording as a drafting inconsistency, not as a different standard.
