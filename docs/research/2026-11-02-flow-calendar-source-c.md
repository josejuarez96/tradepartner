## 1. The rule, written before the reading

**Body mined.** The primary rule documents for scheduled or rule-bound trades in US equities, listed in section 2 with each one's version. The literature was read only for questions 3 and 5 (spec source A, "context only").

**Rule (anti-fishing rule 2 of the spec).** A flow gets a card when all three of these hold. Otherwise it gets a one-line reject (section 4):

1. A **named holder must trade** because a written rule tells it to (an index methodology, a fund's mandate or prospectus, a statute, a settlement rule, a contract), whatever the price;
2. the **date of the trade follows from the document**: a calendar date, or a rank formula on public data known before that date. It is not a committee's discretion and not an event nobody can foresee;
3. it trades **US-listed equities**, the only instrument the charter holds.

For each flow, the date rule was written from the document **before** any literature on its returns was read. The card's test would be one declared event window on that date rule. **Reads:** web pages and PDFs of the documents and papers cited below, nothing else; and one local read of the held Chen-Zimmermann `SignalDoc.csv`, limited to the `Acronym`, `LongDescription`, `Authors` and `Year` columns, to find related published signals for question 5. The `Return` and `T-Stat` columns were not read (spec anti-fishing rule 3). No `data/*.duckdb` file, no `.env`, no store row.

## 5. What the pass found

- **Seventeen flows were reviewed: six cards and eleven rejects.** Of the six cards, two are admitted as `blocked` (FC-1 and FC-6), two are `deferred: power` (FC-2 and FC-5) and two are `rejected: question 3` (FC-3 and FC-4). None is `admit`: every admitted card waits on a missing capability.
- **Calendar flows run into the clustering problem.** A flow fixed by a calendar puts every event of a year on one or two dates. Cross-sectional events on one date share the market's move, so the effective count is close to the number of dates times a small factor, not the number of names. Over the development window (2016 to 2023), the Russell gives 8 dates, the Nasdaq-100 8, and tax-loss January 8. This, not the story, is what defers FC-2 and FC-5, and it bounds FC-1.
- **Market-level month-end flows fail on cost at the rule's figure.** The two month-end mechanisms (FC-3 and FC-4) are well documented and recent. But a long-only book can use them only by sitting out of the market for a few days and paying two sides each month. At the rung-1 figure of +8.8 bp per side, break-even is 17.6 bp a month. After the publication haircut, the plausible gain is 7 to 12 bp. The rule's band figure was measured on single stocks; SPY probably costs less, but nothing has measured that, and the card does not assume it. If an ETF cost were ever measured under the rung-1 rule, both cards would be re-read; that is a reviewer's note, not a block.
- **The two survivors are the two whose trade is not on the market's own calendar.** FC-1, the reversal after Russell migrations, has a post-publication 2014-2023 figure of about +2% over 20 sessions, against a 17.6 bp round trip. It is blocked on the cost of names below $200M dollar volume, which nothing has measured, and on Russell membership history, which is not held but can be rebuilt approximately from held rank-day prices and share counts. FC-6, lock-up expiry, needs a short: paper shorts are allowed (ADR 0024) but no book exists, borrow costs on recent IPOs are unmeasured, and lock-up terms are in prospectus text the store does not parse.
- **Both survivors can be killed without a return read.** The published volume spike (about 12× at the Russell implementation close; +40% at lock-up expiry) gives a rung 2-O check on held bars: if our rebuilt Russell migrants, or our day-180 dates, show no volume spike on the rule's date, our date rule or our membership rebuild is wrong, and the card dies before any return is read.
- **An honest word.** Every effect here is published, and most have shrunk. Source C did not find an unpublished flow. What it found is the date rules written down, so that a test is one declared window, and a cost and power arithmetic that settles four of the six cards with no look at all.
