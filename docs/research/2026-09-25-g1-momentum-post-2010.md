## Answer
**Verdict:** MIXED  ·  **Confidence:** medium (grade); medium (magnitude)

The claim graded is: *a long-only, monthly 12-1 momentum portfolio of liquid US large/mid caps has earned a positive return over the market, net of costs, since 2010.*

- **Live evidence (Tier 1):** two long-only large/mid-cap momentum products report returns against a broad index in their SEC filings.
  - Across 10 windows of five years or more between 2009 and 2025, their excess returns, net of fees and trading costs, range from **−2.79 to +1.09 pp/yr**.
  - The four 10-year windows are **+0.04, −0.27, −0.65 and +0.08 pp/yr**.
  - MTUM's own index beat MSCI USA by about **+0.4 pp/yr gross** over 2013–2025 (agent-computed from MSCI annual returns). That margin is smaller than an individual investor's likely trading costs, and MTUM's own fees and trading costs already leave it at about the market.
- **Academic evidence of weakening (Tier 1):**
  - The US long-short momentum factor fell from **0.92%/month (1987 – mid-2002) to 0.16%/month (mid-2002 – 2018)**, gross.
  - Momentum has been insignificant since the late 1990s (to 2012).
  - Modern-era long-short anomalies average about **4 bp/month** net.
  - Mutual funds capture no momentum after implementation costs.
  - Returns are 58% lower after publication across 97 predictors.
- **Supporting evidence, all pre-2013 or not net of costs:**
  - 12-1 momentum was one of the few monthly anomalies to survive costs over 1963–2012: **0.68%/month** net long-short.
  - Long positions carry half of momentum profits.
  - Institutional costs are low.

The sources conflict, and costs plausibly consume what premium remains. That meets the handoff §6.1 MIXED definition. It is not NOT SUPPORTED: live products matched the market rather than falling clearly below it, and no replication shows the long-only large-cap effect is gone.

- 12-1 momentum survived costs over 1963–2012 (0.68%/month net long-short).
- Buy/hold buffering raised net returns (0.85%/month).
- The long leg carries half of momentum profits, with no reliable size relation.

## Implications for the Phase 3 spec (inputs for the owner, not decisions)
Candidate numbers from the sources above, for the owner to accept, change or reject in the Phase 3 pre-registration.

1. **Expected net excess return over the benchmark (prior):** centred near **0 pp/yr**, with a plausible 10-year range of **−1 to +1 pp/yr**. The four live 10-year windows fall between −0.65 and +0.08. Five-year windows between **−2.8 and +1.1 pp/yr** and single-year gaps of **±13 to 18 pp** occurred live, so the owner may want the pre-registration to call these within normal experience.
2. **Backtest red flag (candidate):** a 2010+ backtest showing net excess over SPY above **~+3 pp/yr** exceeds every live window of five years or more and every gross index window found (the maximum was +1.10). It could trigger a look-ahead or cost-model audit. The +3 is the agent's framing.
3. **Absolute drawdown inputs:**
   - Plan for losses of **~−18% to −19%** in a single quarter or calendar year: three separate post-2010 live episodes, plus 2022.
   - Treat a full-crisis drawdown of **~−55%**, about the same as the market's, as possible: MSCI back-tested 2007–09.
   - Momentum's worst case was about the parent index's, not better.
   - An absolute stop tighter than about −20% would have been at or near its trigger at least three times since 2010.
   - The exact post-2010 peak-to-trough should be computed in code from MTUM/SPY prices within the system.
4. **Relative drawdown inputs:**
   - Tracking error against the parent is about **8.3–8.4%/yr** (MSCI).
   - Calendar-year shortfalls of **−13.5 and −18.0 pp** occurred in 2021 and 2023.
   - A relative-underperformance stop needs to sit well outside these to avoid firing on ordinary momentum behaviour. Where to set it is the owner's decision.
5. **Turnover and costs:**
   - A plain monthly 12-1 decile portfolio turns over about **34.5% per side per month** (Novy-Marx & Velikov, 1963–2012). Live products turned over **53–66%/yr** (AQR) and **~101–118%/yr** (MSCI indices, 116% for MTUM).
   - Costs then consumed about **half the gross spread** (0.65 of 1.33%/month).
   - A 10%/20% buy/hold buffer cut turnover to 18.8% and raised net returns (0.68 to 0.85%/month). Whether to include a buffer is a design decision for the owner.
   - Cost range for sensitivity runs: institutional lower bound (Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz) up to Patton & Weller's "no momentum after costs".
6. **Taxes:** AQR's 10-year return to 2025 fell from **13.94%** before taxes to **10.57%** after taxes on distributions, despite tax management. This bears on the ADR 0005 account-type question.
7. **Detectability (agent computation):** a +1 pp/yr excess with 8.4% tracking error is an information ratio of about 0.12. Over 14 years that gives t ≈ 0.45, so neither the backtest nor paper trading can separate this strategy from its benchmark. This is consistent with ADR 0005.

## Caveats & gaps
- **No Tier 1/2 post-2010 peak-to-trough maximum** (see the Status line). Quarter-end and calendar-year losses understate intra-period drawdowns.
- **Design mismatch:** AQR used multi-signal, cap-weighted, tax-managed portfolios; MSCI and MTUM use risk-adjusted 6- and 12-month momentum, 125 names, quarterly rebalancing and a turnover cap. None is monthly 12-1, equal-weight or top-N.
- **Back-testing inside the index data:** the SR Variant index is back-tested before May 2020 and the Momentum index before Feb 2013. The 2007–09 drawdowns are therefore back-tested.
- **Gross vs net:** index figures are gross. MTUM trailed its own index by about 0.2 pp/yr (10 yr to Jun 2026: 17.58 vs 17.79).
- **Academic sample periods:** Novy-Marx & Velikov end in 2012–13, and their long-short costs use historical effective spreads, which are higher than today's for large caps. Chen & Velikov remains abstract-level (full text returned 404).
- **Survivorship of live products:** AQR's momentum fund became a multi-style fund in May 2026; MTUM is the largest survivor.
- **Exposures:** MTUM carries high beta and IT concentration. Beta is 1.22 vs the S&P 500 on the fact sheet and 1.28 on the product page in Sep 2026; IT is 53–54%.
- **Dropped:** the Schwab MTUM figure (unreconcilable extract) and the Advisor Perspectives extract (superseded).
