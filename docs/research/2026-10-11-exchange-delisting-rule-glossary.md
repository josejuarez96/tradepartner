# Research Report: Exchange delisting-rule glossary for labelling Form 25s (2026-10-11)

**Brief:** #1490 (labelling aid for #963)  ·  **Date:** 2026-10-11  ·  **Status:** COMPLETE  ·  **Agent/model:** team window rulegloss, claude-opus-5-5

**Question.** The exchange notices on Form 25 cite listing rules by number (Nasdaq 5110(b), NYSE 802.01D, NYSE American 1003(a)(i) and so on). Which rules come up, what does each one say in the exchange's own rulebook, and which departure-reason label does each imply under the owner's labelling rules on #963?

**Decision it feeds.** The owner's gold labelling session for `departure-reason-gold` (#963), and later a deterministic "rule number to option" arm (a separate issue plus a spec amendment, not this report).

## Answer
**Verdict:** SUPPORTED (a lookup, built from official rulebooks only)  ·  **Confidence:** high for the 54 verified rules, low for the one unverified rule

- **55 rules cover 95.2% of the citations** (2,306 of 2,423 rule citations in 1,305 notices that cite any rule). The full list is in the table below and in the TOML file next to this one.
- **One rule alone names bankruptcy:** Nasdaq **5110(b)**. Owner rule 3 already says so. Nasdaq 5101, IM-5101-1 and 5100 appear beside it almost every time (5101 in 167 of 210 notices, IM-5101-1 in 166 of 168), and they decide nothing on their own.
- **Most other substantive rules are failed listing standards** and imply `compliance_delisting`: bid price, equity, market value, holders, late filings, unpaid fees, annual meeting and board or committee rules.
- **Procedural rules decide nothing:** Nasdaq 5815, 5820(a) and 5825(a), NYSE 802.02, and NYSE American 1009, 1009(a), 1009(d) and 1203. They only say who reviewed the case. Read the other rule the notice cites.
- **Seven rules depend on the notice text:** NYSE 802.01D, the SPAC deadline rules (Nasdaq IM-5101-2, NYSE 102.06e, NYSE American 119(b) and 119(f)), and NYSE American 1003, 1003(c)(iii) and 1003(a)(iv). They are explained under "Mixed rules" below. The SPAC rules raise **one open question for the owner**.
- **One rule is unverified:** NYSE Arca 5.2(j)(6)(B)(II) (5 notices, 0.2%). Its official text could not be retrieved. Its label comes from the notice wording only.

## How to use this while labelling
1. Find every rule number in the exchange notice. Ignore the procedural ones (label "decides nothing alone").
2. If what remains is a single-meaning rule, use its label. Owner rule 3 says a rule that names the reason counts as stated.
3. If what remains is "depends on the notice text", read the notice and the 8-K for the ground they name. The notes below say what to look for.
4. If only discretion or procedural rules remain and no text names a ground, the rule decides nothing. Fall back on the `12d2-2` provision (owner rule 2) and the 8-K (owner rule 1).

## Mixed rules: what in the notice decides them

**NYSE 802.01D "Other Criteria" (262 notices, the most cited NYSE rule).** The section lists separate grounds, and the notice names the one used. Read it and label:
- "bankruptcy", "Chapter 11", "filed voluntary petitions", or liquidation authorised: `bankruptcy`. 97 of 262 notices or their 8-Ks mention bankruptcy.
- "abnormally low" price or volume, or a specialised-security threshold (fewer than 100 holders, under $1,000,000 market value): `compliance_delisting`. 155 notices say "abnormally low", mostly warrants. Many notices name their class "Redeemable Warrants" or "Redeemable Preferred"; the word "redeemable" in a class name is not a redemption.
- "Payment, Redemption or Retirement of Entire Class": the whole class was redeemed or matured, so `instrument_retirement`.
- **SPAC warrants when the SPAC winds up.** 13 notices say "there will be no redemption rights or liquidating distributions with respect to" the warrants, and 4 more name the "liquidation of the trust account". The warrants expire worthless because the SPAC is liquidating its trust. The stated mechanism is the trust liquidation, so owner rules 1 and 5 give `instrument_retirement`. This is the same SPAC question as below.
- **Warrants after a partial redemption** (2 notices). The ground stated is a failed distribution standard after a partial redemption. The class was not retired, so `compliance_delisting`. This is a judgment call; the owner should confirm it.
- Public interest, accounting, or "any other event": no option is named. Use the 8-K, and label `unresolved` if it states nothing.

**NYSE 802.01B (122 notices).** Usually market capitalisation or equity: `compliance_delisting`. The same section holds the SPAC ("Acquisition Company") criteria. When the notice cites it with 102.06e, or says the company "failed to consummate a business combination", treat it like the SPAC rules below.

**SPAC deadline rules: Nasdaq IM-5101-2 (98), NYSE 102.06e (17), NYSE American 119(b) (7) and 119(f) (9). Open question for the owner.** Each rule names a failed listing requirement: the business combination was not completed within 36 months, or three years on NYSE, or the SPAC's own shorter deadline. Read alone under owner rules 3 and 4, that is `compliance_delisting`. When a missed deadline is the cause, the SPAC normally redeems its public shares from the trust (NYSE 102.06e: "the AC will be liquidated if no Business Combination has been consummated"), and owner rules 1 and 5 label a stated redemption or trust liquidation `instrument_retirement`. The two readings collide when a notice cites the SPAC rule and the 8-K states the redemption. The second-labeller test on #963 found exactly this gap: IM-5101-2 was labelled `unresolved` in one batch and `compliance_delisting` in another. **Proposed reading, for the owner to accept or change:** if the notice or 8-K states the redemption or trust liquidation, use `instrument_retirement` (rule 1, the stated mechanism). If only the SPAC rule is cited, use `compliance_delisting` (rule 3, the rule names the ground). A post-merger company that fails initial listing (the second branch of IM-5101-2 and 119(f)) is `compliance_delisting`. In this corpus, 20 of the 98 IM-5101-2 notices or their 8-Ks mention redemption, the trust or liquidation, and 68 mention none of the themes searched. On NYSE (102.06e) it is 15 of 17, and on NYSE American (119(f)) 8 of 9.

**Nasdaq 5110(b) (176).** The rule covers bankruptcy filings **and** a board-authorised liquidation the company is committed to. Owner rule 3 calls it the bankruptcy rule. In this corpus 140 of the 176 have an 8-K. In 125 of those the 8-K mentions bankruptcy, and in 1 it mentions only a plan of liquidation or dissolution. Label `bankruptcy` unless the 8-K states a voluntary liquidation and no bankruptcy filing. That rare case has no single option; owner rule 1 applies to what the text states.

**NYSE American 1003(c)(iii) (18) and 1003(a)(iv) (5).** By their words these are "security without value" and "financial condition so impaired". Every notice here bases the decision on a bankruptcy or restructuring filing, which the notice names. Owner rule 4 (the stated ground wins) gives `bankruptcy` when the notice names the filing, and `compliance_delisting` when it names only the financial standard.

**NYSE American 1001 (18) and 1003 without a paragraph (15).** These are general sections. All 18 notices citing 1001 are warrants, 16 of them removed for a "low trading price" or "low selling price", and 11 of the 15 citing bare 1003 say "abnormally low". The notice wording gives `compliance_delisting`, not the section number.

**Nasdaq 5110(a) (7).** A change of control with a non-Nasdaq company whose new listing was not approved before closing. The issuer survives the deal, so it is not `merger_or_acquisition`. The stated ground is a failed listing requirement: `compliance_delisting`.

## Historical changes, 2016 to 2026

Each of these was checked against the amendment history printed under the rule on the official page, or against the SEC rule-filing notice:
- **Nasdaq IM-5101-2.** The text is unchanged since Dec. 23, 2010 (SR-NASDAQ-2010-137): 36 months throughout 2016-2026. The procedure changed with **SR-NASDAQ-2024-038** (SEC notice of filing and immediate effectiveness, Federal Register July 19, 2024; rule history "amended Jul. 8, 2024 ... operative Aug. 7, 2024"). Before it, "a timely request for a hearing for noncompliance with the provisions of Listing Rule IM-5101-2 currently stays the suspension and delisting action pending the issuance of a written panel decision". After it, a SPAC past 36 months is suspended at once, and "Nasdaq also proposes to limit the Hearings Panels authority to review the Nasdaq Staff's decision in these instances to a review for factual error only." This changes when delisting happens, not the label. Source: https://listingcenter.nasdaq.com/assets/rulebook/nasdaq/filings/SR-NASDAQ-2024-038_Notice_Approval.pdf. Nasdaq 5452, the alternative SPAC standards on the Global Market, was adopted Nov. 12, 2021 (SR-NASDAQ-2021-092), so 5452 citations date from late 2021 onward.
- **Nasdaq 5110(b).** The rule's last amendment is May 25, 2017 (SR-NASDAQ-2017-053). Nasdaq's rule-filing list describes that filing as reverse-merger seasoning, which is 5110(c). Paragraph (b) kept the same meaning throughout.
- **Nasdaq 5450 and 5550.** Adopted in 2009, amended Jul. 22, 2026 (**SR-NASDAQ-2026-004**, "a new Market Value of Listed Securities continued listing requirement of at least $5 million", listed by Nasdaq as **Stayed** by the SEC) and Sep. 25, 2026 (SR-NASDAQ-2026-081, which changes the operative date). The new requirement is the last item of each list: 5550(a)(6) and 5450(a)(3). The paragraphs cited in 2016-2026 notices kept their numbers and meaning. That is an inference from the amendment history and the current text; the 2009 text itself was not fetched.
- **NYSE 802.01C.** Amended Jan. 15, 2025 (NYSE-2024-48), which limits reverse splits, and Aug. 14, 2026 (NYSE-2025-43), which adds a $0.25 "Minimum Trading Price" effective July 1, 2027. The $1.00, 30-day test is unchanged. The label is unchanged.
- **NYSE 802.01B.** Amended several times in 2016-2017 (NYSE-2016-22, NYSE-2016-72, NYSE-2017-11, NYSE-2017-08, NYSE-2017-31). The Acquisition Company criteria and the $15,000,000 floor sit in this section. The label is unchanged.
- **NYSE American.** Notices before mid-2017 call it the "NYSE MKT Company Guide" (seen in the corpus). The section numbers are the same. Sec. 119 was adopted Nov. 23, 2010 and amended June 4, 2025 (NYSEAmer-2025-30). Sec. 1009 was amended Aug. 28, 2025 (NYSEAmer-2025-54).
- **Display note.** The NYSE American pages render paragraph letters and roman numerals as a numbered list. For example, 1003(a)(i) to (iv) appear as items 1 to 4 under (a), and 119(a) to (f) as items 1 to 6. The quotes above are the text of the item each citation points to.

## Method

- **Corpus.** `data/edgar_cache/corpus/departure-reason/corpus.jsonl` (9,694 Form 25 rows, read-only). Only the exchange notice exhibit text (`exhibit.text`) was searched: 6,851 with text and 2,843 stubs. The 8-K text was used only for the theme counts in the notes, not for the citation counts.
- **Namespace by exchange.** A notice's rule numbers are read in its own exchange's rulebook: Nasdaq Listing Rules on NASDAQ (3,638 notices), the NYSE Listed Company Manual on NYSE (4,320), the NYSE American Company Guide on NYSE_AMERICAN (302), NYSE Arca rules on NYSE_ARCA (1,214), and Cboe BZX rules on CBOE_BZX (210). The 10 notices of other exchanges cite no rules.
- **Patterns (Python `re`).** `PAR = (?:\s?\((?:[a-z]{1,4}|\d{1,2}|[A-Z]{1,3})\))*`.
  - Nasdaq: `\bIM\s?[-–‐]?\s?(5\d{3})\s?[-–‐]\s?(\d{1,2})\b` and `(?<![\d.,$/-])\b(5\d{3})(PAR)(?!\.?\d|%|\s?\()`.
  - NYSE: `(?<![\d.$])\b([1-9]0\d\.\d\d)\s?([A-Za-z])?(?=[\s.,;:)'"]|$)`. Only 102.xx keeps a lower-case suffix (102.06e).
  - NYSE American: `(?<![\d.,$])\b(1[0-2]\d{2}|1[0-4]\d)(PAR)(?!\.?\d|%|\s?\()`, plus list continuations such as "1003(a)(i), (ii) and (iii)".
  - NYSE Arca: `Rule\s+(5\.\d+)(?:-E)?(PAR)`. Cboe BZX: `Rule\s+(14\.\d+)(PAR)`.
  - A number without parentheses counts only when "Rule(s)", "Section(s)", "Company Guide" or "Manual" appears earlier in the same sentence (within 80 characters). This drops exchange ratios such as "0.5252 of a share" in NYSE merger notices.
  - Within one notice, each rule counts once. A bare citation is dropped when a more specific citation of the same rule is present.
- **Result.** 1,305 notices cite at least one rule: NASDAQ 788, NYSE 412, NYSE_AMERICAN 97, NYSE_ARCA 6, CBOE_BZX 2. They hold 2,423 citations of 127 distinct rules. The rules were sorted by count and kept until the running total passed 95%. The cut fell inside a tie at count 4, so the whole tie was kept: 55 rules, 2,306 citations, 95.17%. The orchestrator's rough counts on #1490 (802.01D 275, 5550(a)(2) 183, 5110(b) 173 ...) differ slightly because of de-duplication per notice and stricter context.
- **Misses.** A scan for "Rule/Section + number" that the patterns did not capture found 13 hits in odd formats: 4 truncated "5450(b)(2", 1 "M-5101-2", 1 each of "5550(b)(1)" and "5550(a)(2)", 2 Cboe "Rule 14.2", 2 NYSE Arca "5.2-", and 2 NYSE "Section 12" (not listing rules). That is under 0.6%, and none of them would change which rules are kept.
- **The tail below the cut** (72 rules, 117 citations) is mostly further Nasdaq 5450, 5550 and 5605 sub-paragraphs (failed standards), NYSE American 134, 1101, 1002(c) and 1205, NYSE 804.00 (the delisting procedure), and Cboe BZX 14.2 and 14.12.
