## Context

In plain words: the lab decides where a strategy's practice data ends by looking at where that strategy's own exam begins. That worked with one family. It breaks with the second.

**How windows work today.** A family's first registration fixes its rules: the holdout (`holdout.start`, `holdout.end`), `in_sample_start`, costs, universe and data keys (strategy-lab spec, Definitions). A run's default in-sample window is `[in_sample_start, the last rebalance strictly before holdout.start]` (`backtest/holdout.py`, `default_in_sample_window`), and a window that reaches past `holdout.end` is refused. A child family (ADR 0014, `FAMILY_PARENTS`) may hold out only months after its parent's `holdout.end`, and only once the parent has spent its holdout or reached its spend cap. No family's holdout may overlap another non-oracle family's (strategy-lab spec req 1).

**The B4 case.** `momentum` (H1) holds out [2024-01-01, 2026-09-30] and spent it (trial 4, #1301). `profitability` (B3) holds out the same months, as a pre-lab registration (plan task T85f, 2026-10-07), unspent and on hold until a profitability finalist exists (#1301 decision 3). `combined` (B4, #1292) is a child of `momentum`, so its holdout must start after 2026-09-30. Under today's rule its practice window would then run through 2026-09: through momentum's spent exam and through profitability's unspent one. The owner withdrew the proposed fix, a per-strategy practice-end setting, because choosing windows per strategy is a degree of freedom the lab removed on purpose (#1301 decision 1), and parked B4 (decision 4).

**The standing state.** On history that starts 2016-01-04 (ADR 0009), the months momentum and profitability hold out are the only historical exam months there are. The overlap rule means no new root family can be given a historical exam, and every child's exam starts after 2026-09. "B4 waits for forward data" is therefore not special to B4. It is the state of every new idea until history is extended (#1303 decision record, item 7).

**What the owner asked for.** His governance brief (#1301, comment 6065810154) separates two questions the lab couples today: "what data may be used for development?" and "what untouched data is a family's final exam?". The data-architecture evaluation, accepted as direction on 2026-10-08 (#1303, newest comment, item 3), recommends one global development boundary, recommended 2023-12-29, frozen in the store, with forward exams judged by the paper book. The orchestrator's notes on the brief hold: a global boundary set once is not a per-strategy knob; refusals around sealed data stay hard, because agents run backtests; the boundary itself must be frozen, and moving it is a recorded owner decision.

**Two things this record does not do.** It does not extend history (that is the Sharadar overlap test and the vendor ADR, #1303 items 4 and 5). It does not change how trials are counted: N, V, the deflated Sharpe and the family's SR* high-water mark are the strategy-lab spec's, unchanged.

## Options considered

1. **A per-strategy practice end** (a sweep-file or hypothesis key). Pro: fixes B4 in one line. Con: window selection becomes something a researcher can tune after seeing results, the exact degree of freedom the lab removed. Withdrawn by the owner (#1301 decision 1).
2. **Keep deriving the practice end from each family's holdout start** (today). Pro: nothing to build. Con: the lab cannot express "practise on the declared development years, with a later exam"; every new family is parked until history grows; the rule depends on no one running a child family's default window, which is a rule that depends on remembering.
3. **One global development boundary, frozen in the store; exams for frozen finalists only; a holdout that lies in the future at registration is judged by the paper book** (this record). Pro: one value the owner sets once, read by every run and every registration; B4 practises on the same months as H1 and B3 and reads nothing after 2023-12; the exam rules stay one-spend and hard; the forward exam already is the end state for every new family on today's history, so writing it down costs nothing. Con: a small code change in the window rule and the refusal; a second owner value to keep (the boundary beside the family rules); one more thing to re-set when history is extended.
4. **The brief's full partition model**: development, protected validation and forward eras as first-class store objects, with warnings and evidence penalties instead of refusals inside development. Pro: the general shape for many families and ML searches. Con: the research registry already has sealed periods for research runs, and the backtest side needs only the boundary; warnings in place of refusals must not reach sealed data, since agents open trials daily; the rest is what option 3 gives, built larger before a second exam slice exists. Deferred, with the re-split on longer history (#1303 item 7) as its trigger.

## Decision

We will take option 3.

1. **One boundary.** A single date, the **development boundary**, is an `owner_decisions` row of kind `development_boundary` carrying the date and a reason, written by the owner (`tradepartner decision development-boundary --date --reason`). The recommended value is **2023-12-29**, the last practice rebalance of H1 and B3 (open question 1). The boundary is read from the store at every run and every registration, never from live config, and it is not a frozen key of any hypothesis: it enters no fingerprint and no family rule, so no registered hash moves. Until the owner writes the row, nothing changes: with no boundary, today's window rule applies as written.

2. **What the boundary does.** No in-sample run reads a session after it. The default in-sample window becomes `[in_sample_start, the earlier of the last rebalance strictly before holdout.start and the last rebalance on or before the boundary]`. A requested in-sample window (neither a holdout spend nor a tracking window) that reaches past the boundary is refused (`refused_window`, before the holdout and gap rules of `holdout.decide` are read; the tracking branch is unchanged). The refusal binds every path that builds a window: `backtest <slug>`, `sweep run`, and a research-registry run whose window reads a backtest family's months (`store/research.py`; its sealed periods already protect the holdouts, and the boundary is read there as one more protected edge). A holdout spend covers only `[holdout.start, holdout.end]` (a spend window starting before `holdout.start` or ending after `holdout.end` is refused, which today's rule does not do), never a session between the boundary and `holdout.start`: those are **dead months** for that family, read by no run of its own (for `momentum` and `profitability` there are none, since their holdouts start the day after the boundary). Refusals stay refusals: no warning, no override flag, because agents run backtests as well as the owner (the orchestrator's note 2 on #1301). A registration whose `in_sample_start` is on or after the boundary is refused at `sweep register`, so no default window is ever empty. Tracking runs of a historical holdout are untouched: they start after `holdout.end` as today; a forward holdout's are point 4's.

3. **Exams are for frozen finalists.** Compare and select on development data; send only the chosen, frozen candidate to its exam; never pick among several strategies on one exam (#1301 decision 2). The one-spend, promotion, family-cap and parent-spent-first rules of the strategy-lab spec stand unchanged. With the boundary in place, the parent-spent-first rule and the overlap rule keep protecting the exam months, but the case they existed for, a child's practice window reading its parent's exam, no longer arises: they are belt and braces, not the fix, while the boundary sits before every holdout, which point 6's refusal keeps true.

4. **A forward exam is judged by the paper book.** A family's holdout is **forward** when its `holdout.start` is after the day the family was first registered, so that no session of it existed in the store at registration. A forward holdout's exam of record is the family's promoted hypothesis in its own paper book (`book_id`, ADR 0015 seam 1), over at least `paper.min_rebalances` rebalances, under the ADR 0005 tracking check. **The paper window sits inside the forward holdout**, which today's code does not allow: `paper start` refuses a hypothesis whose `holdout.end` is not a completed month-end (`execution/window.py`, `holdout_not_complete`) and starts the window after `holdout.end`; `holdout._tracking` refuses a tracking window that starts on or before `holdout.end`. For a forward holdout, and only then: `paper start` accepts the promoted hypothesis once `holdout.start` has passed, the window starts at the first rebalance session, at the hypothesis's cadence, on or after `holdout.start`, the tracking trial's window may start at that session too (cadence-aligned, as `first_tracking_session` is today), and `holdout.end` is the declared last month of the exam (the family sets it at registration, at least `paper.min_rebalances` months after `holdout.start`; a book may run past it as an ordinary Phase 4 window). Everything else about `paper start` stays: the flat account, the one open window, the gap sign-off, month-end cadence, `PAPER_FAMILIES`. These are order-path changes (`safety-reviewer`), listed below. `lab status` says `forward` beside such a holdout; this is derived from the family rules and the registration day, not a stored column. Whether a backtest may also spend a forward holdout once its months have passed is open question 2. *(Amended 2026-10-09, [ADR 0017](0017-fast-paper-and-machine-readiness-gate.md) parts B to D: "the one open window" is one per book, "month-end cadence" is the hypothesis's cadence, `PAPER_FAMILIES` gains `profitability` and `combined`; "at least `paper.min_rebalances` months after `holdout.start`" reads "rebalance periods at the hypothesis's cadence", the per-cadence default 6, 13, 63.)*

5. **Today's families.** `momentum`'s exam [2024-01-01, 2026-09-30] is spent (trial 4). `profitability`'s is the same months, pre-lab, unspent, on hold (#1301 decision 3). Neither family rule moves and nothing in H1's or B3's registration, trials or fingerprint changes. The unspent B3 holdout stays protected exactly as today: the boundary keeps every family's in-sample window out of 2024 to 2026, so no family's practice can read B3's exam months before B3 spends them. `combined` (B4, #1292) registers, when the owner chooses, as a one-value sweep with `holdout.start` after both 2026-09-30 and its own registration day: its practice window is then [2020-08-31, 2023-12-29], the same as H1's and B3's, the months from 2024-01 to its `holdout.start` are its dead months, its holdout is forward, and its exam is its paper book. `combined` is a child of `momentum` in `config.FAMILY_PARENTS` (ADR 0014, owner answer on #1074); B3's file still calls it a root in three places, and the code wins. Which hypothesis B4 registers and when stays the owner's, and so does when a second paper book opens for it (#1301 decision 5: one track now, H1 into paper; `combined` is `paper_ready = False` until ADR 0014 point 5's gate is passed), so B4 can practise now and its exam waits for a second book.

6. **Moving the boundary.** A run reads the newest `development_boundary` row by `made_at`. The boundary moves only by a new row, never an edit, and only by the owner. A new boundary is refused when it lies on or after the `holdout.start` of any registered non-oracle family, spent, unspent or forward, so no family's exam months, momentum's spent ones and B3's unspent ones included, can ever become development months by a boundary move; a boundary before every family's `in_sample_start` is refused too. The row's reason names every family that registered or ran under the old value; their trials stay counted (N never resets) and their family rules do not move. A moved boundary changes the default window of every family it reaches, so each trial records the boundary it ran under (`trials.development_boundary`, an additive nullable column), and a counted trial whose recorded boundary differs from the current one is **stale** under the strategy-lab spec's vintage rule: it counts in N and never selects, and the next plain `sweep run` reruns it. Staleness is applied as the window test (the owner's decision on #1333, 2026-10-09): a counted trial is stale only when its default window under the current boundary differs from the window it ran, so writing the first boundary at 2023-12-29 stales nothing, and a trial with a `NULL` recorded boundary stays current while its window fits. The expected occasion for a move is longer history: the owner decided on #1303 (decision record item 7) that the re-split on longer history registers new families as children of today's with the SR* high-water mark carried; the boundary for that history is set then, by its own row, and the old families' rows are untouched.

7. **Data under an open exam.** A forward exam compares the paper book against a backtest half that is recomputed from the live store (ADR 0005), so a store repair on those sessions would move the exam without any change in the book. Once a paper window is open, no row-changing data release touches sessions inside it; the daily ingest is the only writer there (#1303 accepted policy). Named data releases, and how they relate to the backup H1's holdout trial read, are the plan amendment's (its T140 and T140b, ids as proposed there), not this record's. *(Amended 2026-10-11, #1497: one exception. A filing stamp inside the window may be corrected earlier, to its SGML header time, by at most New York's offset, when a read-only pre-check shows that no paper decision read the row; the data-releases runbook, rule 5, and the data-foundation plan's amendment 2026-10-11, decision (d), define the check, and the repair tools refuse the change without its file. Nothing else in this point changes.)*

8. **A new family's historical exam** *(Amended 2026-10-28, #2444; the owner's decision of 2026-10-28, recorded as such. Points 1 to 7 stand; this point relaxes only the overlap rule for exams, and the sentences above that say "no new root family can be given a historical exam" or that a new family's exam waits for the forward paper book now read through it.)* The owner wants a first out-of-sample verdict sooner than six paper rebalances allow, and the store already holds the months.
   - **Options considered for this point.** (i) Forward exam only (points 1 to 7 as accepted): cleanest evidence, but six rebalances of calendar time per idea. (ii) Shared post-boundary months, one frozen finalist per family (chosen): a verdict in a day, but the era is partly seen and several families read one era. (iii) Disjoint slices of 2024 to 2026 per family: less overlap, but each slice is short and choosing slices is a per-family window choice, the degree of freedom #1301 decision 1 removed. (iv) Wait for longer history (Sharadar, #1303 items 4 and 5): more virgin data, but no date and a vendor spend.
   - **The rule.** A new family's **single frozen finalist** may take **one historical exam** on exactly `[2024-01-01, 2026-09-30]` (the first month after the boundary through momentum's `holdout.end`). The window is fixed: a family registered under this point is refused any other start or end, so it cannot drop a remembered year or start mid-era; a longer span (more history, a later `holdout.end`) needs a new amendment to this record. Unlike the generic rules, whose caps allow more (`lab.max_family_holdout_spends` 3, `lab.max_family_promotions` 2, and strategy-lab spec req 5(d)'s `holdout_repeat`), a historical-exam family has both caps **frozen at 1** in its family rules and `holdout.decide` refuses any second spend of any outcome, so "one exam per family" is enforced here, not assumed. Otherwise the existing rules hold: a promoted hypothesis only, frozen before the read, never picking among several candidates on the exam, N and V unchanged (point 3, strategy-lab spec req 4 and 5). The exam is **recorded as that family's spend** (a counted trial of the family, a `holdout_spend` decision row as today). The choice is one-way: a family registered under this point has a historical holdout, so it can never have a forward paper exam of record (point 4, ADR 0023), spent or not.
   - **The overlap rule bends in one way.** Today a new family's holdout may overlap no other non-oracle family's (strategy-lab spec req 1, "Family rules"). A family registered under this point may hold out the same post-boundary months as `momentum` and `profitability`, and as other point-8 families. Two limits keep it narrow: the holdout must start after the development boundary (point 6 already forbids moving the boundary into any holdout), and no family's in-sample window may reach it (point 2, untouched), so the families' practice stays disjoint from every exam even though their exams share months. A child family (ADR 0014) still holds out only months after its parent's `holdout.end` and registers only once the parent has spent its holdout or reached its spend cap; for `momentum` that is no month before 2026-10, so a `momentum` child cannot use this point on today's history and keeps the forward exam of point 4.
   - **What it does not do.** It does not replace the forward paper exam as the gate to live money: the finalist still opens a paper book, runs it, and passes ADR 0005's tracking check, and a family needs both before a live decision. It does not move the development boundary (2023-12-29, unchanged). It does not touch `profitability`: its unspent hold on [2024-01-01, 2026-09-30] (#1301 decision 3) stays, because a different family spending the same calendar months neither reads nor spends profitability's registered exam, and `profitability` can still spend its own once its finalist exists. It does cost B3 something: a disclosed result on the same months from a related family (quality, filings, earnings) tells whoever still builds B3's finalist something about those months, the leak the overlap rule existed to stop, so point 5's "protected exactly as today" now holds for B3's reading of the sessions, not for what the researchers learn. Mitigation: B3's finalist is frozen before the first point-8 spend, or point-8 results are kept out of B3 finalist work until it is frozen (open question 7).
   - **Contamination, said plainly.** Those months are not virgin. The researchers lived through 2024 to 2026; momentum's exam on the same months is spent and its result is known; event studies of later mechanisms (filings, earnings, insider flows) share the same market moves, so an idea can be shaped, even unconsciously, by what the researchers remember about those years. A pass here is weaker evidence than a pass on never-seen data, and a historical exam's pass cannot be read as a forward result. Mitigations: (a) the finalist is **frozen and registered before any read of those months** (the existing promotion and fingerprint rules enforce the order), and its promoted file carries a `## Post-boundary exposure` section listing every research-registry run and every kill-ladder rung-2 source (ADR 0023) that read post-boundary months for that idea, since a run that names no family is not bound by the boundary (`store/research.py`) and published results can reach 2024 to 2025, plus what the researchers already recalled of the era; `sweep promote` refuses a point-8 family's file without the section; (b) **one exam per family**, so a failed finalist cannot be retried on the same months by tuning; (c) every such result is labelled **"historical exam, post-boundary, partially seen era"** in the registry, `lab status`, the Lab and any report or TP- claim, and never reported as a bare "holdout" or "out of sample", and a check (the claims validator for `docs/research/claims.toml`, a test for the Lab and `lab status`) fails any claim or row that cites a historical-exam trial without the label; (d) a pass promotes only to a paper book, never to live money.
   - **Interaction with ADR 0023.** ADR 0023's rule that a paper book counts as an *exam of record* only when its family's holdout is **unspent** still holds. A family that spends its historical exam therefore has a **tracking window** as its paper book (ADR 0005 criterion 1), not an exam of record. The cost of that is the owner's call and is open question 4; the effect of this point is that the historical exam becomes the family's exam of record and the paper book its operational and tracking gate.

**Order of work.** First a class-B amendment of the strategy-lab spec (req 1's overlap refusal and its acceptance criterion, req 5(d) for the frozen caps), the owner's merge; then a plan amendment in the [strategy-lab plan](../plans/strategy-lab.md), class B (landed on the owner's word), whose build PRs are class A; the build writes the tasks (none is written here).

**Code consequences of point 8:** (1) `store/lab_registry.py` and the strategy-lab spec's req 1 overlap refusal: allow a holdout that overlaps another non-oracle family's only when it equals exactly `[2024-01-01, 2026-09-30]` and the family is registered with a marker `exam_kind = historical` in its family rules, with `lab.max_family_holdout_spends` and `lab.max_family_promotions` frozen at 1 (new frozen family rules, so no fingerprint moves), and refuse any other start or end; (2) the registry and `backtest/holdout.py`: record the exam kind on the spend and the trial, add a "historical exam, post-boundary, partially seen era" label that every report path prints and the label check above, and a refusal to call such a spend a forward exam; (3) the holdout checks: the "dead months" and `holdout.decide` rules need a test that two families' spends of one calendar month do not disturb each other's one-spend count or each other's tracking windows (`profitability` stays unspent after another family spends); (4) `store/research.py` sealed periods: a research run of a new family reads its holdout only through the spend path, and the protected edge stays the boundary; (5) `paper start` (order path, `execution/window.py`, `safety-reviewer`), `lab status` and the Lab UI: show "historical exam spent" beside the family and a paper book of such a family as a tracking window, per ADR 0023; (6) `sweep promote` refuses a point-8 family's file without `## Post-boundary exposure`; (7) tests: a family registered under point 8 with an in-sample read past the boundary is refused, a holdout other than the fixed window is refused, the second exam and a second promotion of the same family are refused, a `profitability` spend after another family's is still accepted, and H1's fingerprint and reference metrics do not move. Nothing touches `FROZEN_KEY_DEFAULTS` for any existing family, and no order shape or risk rule changes.

Nothing touches `FROZEN_KEY_DEFAULTS` or any frozen key, and no order shape or risk rule changes.

## Open questions (the owner's; a recommendation beside each)

1. **The date.** Recommend 2023-12-29. It is the last practice rebalance of H1 and B3, so no registered trial's window changes, and no session after it has been read in sample by any family. The alternative, an earlier date, would shorten H1's and B3's practice windows for every later family without a reason.
2. **A backtest spend on a forward holdout, once its months have passed.** Recommend: allowed, under the same one-spend rules (it is untouched data, and the registry counts it as the family's spend), but it never replaces the paper book as the exam of record, and promotion to live needs the paper book either way. The alternative, paper book only, is simpler and loses a counted check that costs nothing.
3. **Should any family's forward exam be longer than `paper.min_rebalances` (6)?** Recommend: no new key now; the owner can hold a book open longer than the minimum, and the tracking check reads every rebalance it has.

4. **May the historical exam shorten the forward paper book? (point 8; added 2026-10-28.)** Recommend: no. Keep `paper.min_rebalances` and `paper.min_exam_fills` for such a family's paper book as a tracking window. The historical exam says the finalist's rules held on months the researchers half-knew; the paper book is the only test of the machine on live fills, costs and a clean calendar, and shortening it would reward the weakest evidence. Revisit after two families have taken both gates.
5. **How many families may share the post-boundary months?** Recommend: no cap now, since each exam is one frozen finalist and each family is a counted family, but the owner reviews the list of historical exams when a third family asks, because several exams on one era raise the chance that one passes by luck (the family-wise error that strategy-lab req 9's SR* handles inside a family but not across families). A cross-family multiple-testing note belongs in each exam's report until then.
6. **Should a pass on a historical exam alone allow a smaller paper book or a live $100 account sooner?** Recommend: no; point 8 leaves the gate to live as the paper book plus the owner's decision.
7. **B3 and point 8 (added 2026-10-28).** Recommend: freeze B3's finalist before the first point-8 spend, or, if B3 has none by then, keep point-8 results out of B3's finalist work (the owner reads them, the B3 builders do not) until it is frozen. The alternative, no rule, leaks the unspent exam's months to B3 by example.

## Consequences

- Good: B4, and every later family, practises on the declared development years without touching any exam months; the practice end is one owner value in the store, not a per-strategy choice and not something anyone has to remember; the exam rules stay hard and one-spend; the forward exam is written down as the standing state on today's history; nothing in H1's or B3's world moves; the change is small (one kind, one read, two functions, four call sites).
- Bad / accepted risks: a second owner value beside the family rules, to be re-set by a recorded decision when history is extended; a forward exam takes calendar time (at least six paper rebalances) that no build throughput shortens; until the owner writes the row, the lab behaves as today and B4 stays parked; the overlap rule plus a forward exam of at least six rebalances means new families' exams queue one after another in calendar time, each in its own book, so the number of ideas examined per year is bounded by books, not by compute; the `forward` marker is derived, so a family registered the day before its holdout starts is "forward" by one day, which is correct and worth one line in `lab status`.
- Point 8 (2026-10-28): good, a new family gets a first verdict in a day, not six months; bad, the era is partly seen, so the result is weaker than a forward one and must carry its label; more families reading one era raises the cross-family chance of a lucky pass (open question 5). The development boundary, profitability's unspent hold (its registration, not its secrecy; open question 7) and every registered fingerprint are unchanged.
- Reversibility of point 8: a family registered with `exam_kind = historical` can never get a forward exam of record, and its spend cannot be undone.
- Reversibility: cheap for the code (a default argument, a refusal, a nullable column and one `paper start` branch); one-way for anything spent, as always: a window that read sessions past a boundary cannot be unread, which is why the refusal is hard.
- Revisit if: the vendor ADR (#1303 item 5) lands and the re-split (item 7) sets a new boundary for the longer history; the owner registers a family whose signal needs a different development end for a stated reason (that is a new row with the reason, not a per-family key); or a reviewer finds a run path that builds an in-sample window without reading the boundary (a defect).

A researcher who keeps every written rule gets a noise candidate through promotion, rung 4 and rung 5 **19.0%** of the time per idea at H1-like costs, **32.7%** at zero cost and **1.6%** at B10-like fast-book costs (path A). "Across a programme of four independent ideas, the chance that at least one noise finalist reaches the owner's live choice is **about 57%** at H1-like costs and **about 6%** at B10-like costs." A stricter floor does not fix it: "Path A passes 51.0% at threshold 0.5 and 7.6% at 0.95" for a true IR of 0.5, against 0.9% for noise at 0.95. "The in-sample screen cannot discriminate in either direction at these costs and lengths."

Nothing after the screen judges returns:
- point 4's forward exam of record is ADR 0005's tracking check, which compares a book with its own backtest, and every hypothesis file says the tracking check "neither retires nor promotes";
- point 8's historical exam is not built (no `exam_kind` in `src/`), and as written it fixes a window but no pass rule;
- ADR 0023 asks an exam of record for "a kill rule in its file" but does not say what the rule tests;
- momentum's spent exam (H1's trial 4) was read under H1's Q10, "no numeric cut-off".

The [strategy-lab spec](../specs/strategy-lab.md)'s open question 4 keeps the 0.5 promotion floor "because the promoted hypothesis is never judged by the selecting statistic: its single holdout spend and its paper tracking are the judges". Today that premise is false. This amendment makes it true. The floor itself (#3127) and the root/child call (#3126) are not changed here.

**Decision 1: the block.** From 2026-11-06, no hypothesis is named in the owner's live choice ([ADR 0023](0023-break-first-fast-books-paper-exam.md) D6, the Phase 6 live ADR T163, [ADR 0017](0017-fast-paper-and-machine-readiness-gate.md) part F) unless its family's exam of record has **passed a returns-judging exam**: the forward return exam of decision 2, or a historical exam that a later amendment qualifies under decision 3.
- **What does not qualify**, alone or together: the in-sample screen, the kill ladder's rungs, promotion and ADR 0005's tracking check.
- **What stays allowed:** `sweep promote` to a paper book (that is how the exam runs), paper books, tracking windows and the shakedown.
- **Until T381 and T381a are merged, no exam can pass,** so no hypothesis can be chosen for live money.
- **Enforced in code, not only in text.** T163b's live switch refuses (`exam_not_passed`) to name a book whose hypothesis has no `passed` verdict from T381's exam (the paper-trading plan's T163b line, amended in this PR).
- **H1 is covered too, and so is every `momentum` book.** Momentum's holdout is spent without a return rule, and a spent family has no forward exam of record (ADR 0023). H1 therefore cannot qualify under this record (open question 8).
- **Labels.** Until a book's exam passes, every report of its tracking result says **"machine pass, edge unjudged"**, never "exam passed". This covers `paper status`, `lab status` (T381a) and every `TP-` claim (checked at review). An exam's own verdict is reported as "forward return exam passed", "failed", "failed (futility)" or "failed (void)".
- **What this changes in the roadmap and the charter, said plainly.** The [roadmap](../roadmap.md)'s Phase 6 entry gate reads "the machine, not a strategy's months of tracking, is what is gated". Decision 1 adds a strategy gate beside the machine gate. Since a live book needs a strategy, Phase 6's first live order now waits for a passed exam, and that is at least 12 months of paper. The roadmap row carries a dated note saying so (this PR). The [charter](../charter.md)'s line that beating a benchmark is "never a phase-exit criterion" is untouched: no phase's exit criteria change. What changes is which strategy may carry live money, which serves the charter's first objective, "disciplined, evidence-based decisions". Choosing a strategy on gates that pass noise more than half the time across a programme is not an evidence-based decision. The owner's word on this PR is his word on that roadmap change. The other route, a live machine test with an unjudged strategy, is open question 8.

**Decision 2: the forward return exam.** *(Amended 2026-11-07, #3143: the horizon, the pass rule, the futility and void rules and the pinned values below are replaced by the [group-sequential exam](#amendment-2026-11-07-3143-the-forward-return-exam-becomes-group-sequential); the rest of this decision stands.)* It is pre-registered and runs over a fixed horizon of 12 months. It applies to a book that is an exam of record under ADR 0023: a forward, unspent holdout (point 4), frozen parameters, and at least `paper.min_rebalances` and `paper.min_exam_fills`.
- **The kill line is frozen first.** Before the window's first session, the kill line is posted on the finalist's issue. It carries the values below, the benchmark, and the finalist's in-sample monthly net excess skewness. T381a then freezes the same values into the window at `paper start`. Nothing in the line is chosen after a return of the window is seen.
- **The series.** The exam reads H = 12 monthly net excess returns:
  - month m is the book's return from one month-end to the next, net of every fill, fee and dividend the journal books, minus the total return of the benchmark the finalist's file froze over the same dates (SPY under ADR 0005, unless the file names another);
  - the first month starts at the first month-end on or after the window's first rebalance session, and the stub before it is reported but not tested;
  - months with a missed rebalance or an override stay in the series.
- **The pass rule.** Both legs must pass, each one-sided at a nominal α = 0.0025:
  - (i) the t-statistic of the 12 returns' mean (sample standard deviation, 11 degrees of freedom) is at least t₀.₉₉₇₅,₁₁ = **3.497**;
  - (ii) the intercept of the OLS regression of the same 12 returns on the benchmark's monthly return over the T-bill rate ([ADR 0020](0020-fred-risk-free-rate.md)) has a t-statistic of at least t₀.₉₉₇₅,₁₀ = **3.581**. This leg stops a book whose only edge is more market beta than its benchmark in a rising year.
  - If both legs pass, the exam is passed. Otherwise it is failed.
- **Why the nominal α is 0.25%, not 1%.** The t-test is anti-conservative when returns are negatively skewed. In the sizing run below, a nominal 1% test passed a zero-mean skew −0.85 null **2.68%** of the time at 12 months. At 0.25% the same null passes **1.10%**.
- **The skew guard.** `paper start` refuses an exam window (`exam_skew_out_of_range`) when the finalist's in-sample monthly net excess skewness is below −0.85, the most negative skew at which the rate below is stated. Such a finalist cannot take this exam, so it cannot reach the live choice.
- **The futility kill.** At month 6, if the sum of the first six returns is below 0, the exam is failed (futility). The rule can only stop a pass, so it cannot raise the false-pass rate. In the sizing run it ended a noise exam at month 6 in 54.20% of runs at a 1.1%/yr drag. It cost a true IR of 2 nothing: 13.49% with or without it, in the futility block's own draw. The power table's 13.41% is a separate draw of the same cell.
- **A void exam is a failed exam.** A window that ends before month 12, or that has not met ADR 0023's minimums by month 12, is failed (void).
- **One exam per family, no retry** (points 3 and 8 (b)). A changed strategy is a new hypothesis whose trials count (strategy-lab req 4), and its exam counts too.
- **The programme budget: four exams.** `paper start` refuses a fifth exam window under this rule (`exam_budget_spent`, `paper.exam_max_count` = 4). Every started exam counts, whatever its outcome. A fifth exam needs a new dated amendment with a smaller per-exam rate.
- **The values are pinned.** The config validator refuses an `exam_alpha` above 0.0025, a horizon other than 12, a futility month other than 6, a `paper.exam_max_count` above 4 and a skew guard below −0.85. So the exam can be made stricter in config, never looser.

**The arithmetic: why the rate is under 5% for four ideas.**
- **Fresh data breaks the selection link.** A noise finalist's 12 exam months come after its frozen kill line. They also come after every in-sample window (which ends 2023-12-29) and every rung-2 window (which ends 2025-12). So they are independent of every selection that brought the finalist to the exam, and its pass rate is the test's rate under the null, whatever the screen did. A cost drag makes a noise book's true mean negative, which only lowers the rate.
- **The sizing run.** The script is `exam_sizing.py` (sha256 `e202f68217a8e7c64c87951758094a17c22ef05494ea7d11c7a66e2b4cc4b7ef`). It used seed 3125, 300,000 replicates per cell, Audit B's noise model (Student-t df 5, 8%/yr tracking error) and skew-normal nulls. The script and its full output are posted on PR #3129. Leg (i) alone at H = 12 and 0.25% passed the nulls as follows: 0.17% at zero cost, 0.13% at 1.1%/yr, 0.07% at 2.2%/yr, **1.10%** at skew −0.85 and 1.25% at skew −0.96. The two legs together pass no more often than leg (i) alone.
- **Worst case for four ideas, inside the skew guard.** If all four finalists are noise with skew −0.85 and each takes the exam, the rate is 1 − (1 − 0.0110)⁴ = **4.33%**. The union bound gives at most 4 × 1.10% = 4.40%, whatever the dependence between the exams. Both are under 5%. For symmetric noise, it is 1 − (1 − 0.0017)⁴ = 0.68%.
- **Counted from the start of four ideas, as Audit B counts its 57%.** At H1-like costs, path A's 19.0% gives 1 − (1 − 0.190 × 0.0110)⁴ = **0.83%** (against about 57% today). At zero cost, 32.7% gives 1 − (1 − 0.327 × 0.0110)⁴ = **1.43%**.
- **Why not a nominal 1%.** At skew −0.85 it gives 2.68% per exam, and 1 − (1 − 0.0268)⁴ = 10.3% for four.

**Power, said plainly.** The same run measured how often leg (i) at 0.25% passes a real edge (cost 1.1%/yr):

| True IR | 6 months | **12 months** | 18 months | 24 months |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.36% | **0.55%** | 0.73% | 0.95% |
| 1.0 | 0.86% | **1.98%** | 3.49% | 5.26% |
| 2.0 | 3.49% | **13.41%** | 26.21% | 39.33% |

- **What the exam is.** It bounds false passes. It is not a discovery tool: a real IR-0.5 edge almost always fails it, as H1's own file already says of every window this project can afford (a +1 pp/yr excess "would need about 280 years of data to reach t ≈ 2").
- **Why accept that.** The live account is $100 and the objective is learning (ADR 0005). A gate that passes a noise idea more than half the time across a programme is worse than one that rarely passes a weak real one.
- **Why 12 months.** It is the shortest horizon in the table at which a strong edge (IR 2) has a real chance to pass (13.41%). At 6 months it is 3.49%, which is close to the 1.10% at which a skewed null passes, so a pass at 6 months would say almost nothing. A longer horizon keeps the rate under 5% too and buys power: IR 2 passes 26.21% at 18 months and 39.33% at 24, for 6 or 12 more months per finalist. That choice is the owner's (open question 10). The futility kill ends about half of the noise exams at month 6 whatever the horizon.
- **Assumptions behind the rate.** The returns are independent month to month. Their skew is no lower than −0.85, a bound the skew guard checks on the in-sample series, whose own sampling error is real. Paper fills cannot make a zero-gross book positive in expectation. A book that breaks one of these assumptions is outside the stated rate, and the sizing run states nothing about it.

**Decision 3: point 8 is not this gate, and #3128's overlap is disclosed.** A point-8 historical exam, if built, is not a returns-judging exam under decision 1 until a new amendment gives it:
- a pre-registered return rule;
- a false-pass rate stated conditional on the overlap below;
- a place in the same four-exam programme budget as the forward exams.

The rest of point 8 stands. The reasons:
- **The window overlaps rung 2's (#3128, Audit B finding 4).** "The #3100 window (2009-01..2025-12) contains all 40 store in-sample months and 24 of the 33 historical-exam months." `scripts/b3_overlap.py` (400k null signals, seed 3092), given a null rung-2 pass:

  | Link correlation | 0.25 | 0.5 | 1.0 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | P(store in-sample mean > 0) | 72% | 81% | 89% |
  | P(exam-window mean > 0) | 65% | 70% | 77% |

  "An independent check would give 50%." So for a rung-2-sourced idea, the rung-4 store run and a point-8 exam partly re-confirm rung-2 false positives: 65% to 89% instead of 50%. "The end-to-end effect is small today (≈ 5.8% per 20-read programme, because rung 2 is strict)."
- **The overlap moves a nominal test's false-pass rate above 5% for four exams.** This approximation is ours, not the report's, and it assumes a normal null whose median is shifted. If the shift makes P(mean > 0) equal 65%, 70% or 77%, a nominal one-sided 1% test passes noise about 2.6%, 3.6% or 5.6% of the time. Across four exams that is about 10.0%, 13.6% or 20.7%. No point-8 rate can therefore be stated under 5% from Audit B's numbers without a per-finalist conditional-null calibration (#3128's fix: exclude or down-weight the 24 overlapping months, or test against the conditional null). Excluding them would leave 9 months (2026-01 to 2026-09), and those are "not virgin" either (point 8, contamination), which cannot be sized at all.
- **It is not cheaper.** It needs point 8's seven code consequences (one of them on the order path), a return rule it does not have and that calibration. It also does not shorten the road to live money: point 8's finalist "still opens a paper book", and open question 4 recommends no shortening. Idea-pipeline candidates forgo it anyway (that spec's D10).
- **The forward exam has none of this.** Its months start after `paper start` (2026-11 at the earliest), after every rung-2 and in-sample window. The only build it needs is T381 and T381a, in the [paper-trading plan](../plans/paper-trading.md).
- **Rung 4's share of the overlap is unchanged here.** Its fix (a rung-4 report prints the conditional null pass rate) stays on #3128.

**Open questions (the owner's; a recommendation beside each).**

It also delays Phase 6's first live order by at least 12 months. Recommend keeping the block. 10. **A longer horizon for more power?** Recommend 12 months now, the cheapest horizon that is not near-useless. **Consequences of this amendment.**
- **Good:**
  - the 0.5 floor's stated premise becomes true;
  - a noise idea reaches the live choice at most about 4.4% of the time across four exams (inside the skew guard), against about 57% today;
  - the exam reads only months that did not exist when its line was frozen;

- **Bad / accepted:**

  - a strongly negatively skewed finalist cannot take the exam;
  - after four exams the budget is spent until a new amendment.
- **Reversibility:** cheap before any exam starts. After one starts, its frozen line and its place in the budget stand.
- **Revisit if:**
  - a fifth exam is wanted;
  - a sequential rule (SPRT or alpha-spending) is calibrated with Audit B's `paths.py` and the skewed nulls, and shown to keep the same family-wise rate with more power *(taken up 2026-11-07, #3143, at a worst-case 4.9% for four exams rather than the same rate: [the group-sequential exam](#amendment-2026-11-07-3143-the-forward-return-exam-becomes-group-sequential))*;
  - longer history (#1303 items 4 and 5) gives point 8 virgin months;
  - the live account's size grows past the charter's $100.

## Amendment 2026-11-07 (#3143): the forward return exam becomes group-sequential

*The orchestrator's decision on #3143, recorded for the owner's word on this class-B PR (proposals A and B of the strictness check, adopted together; B is recorded in [ADR 0023's amendment of this date](0023-break-first-fast-books-paper-exam.md#amendment-2026-11-07-3143-rung-1-prices-cost-at-the-exams-paper-basis)). It replaces decision 2's horizon, pass rule, futility rule, void rule and pinned values. Decision 1, decision 3, the series definition, the skew guard, one exam per family and the four-exam budget stand. **This is allowed now by open question 10 of the 2026-11-06 amendment: no exam's kill line has been posted.** On 2026-11-07, T381 and T381a have no PR, so no exam window can have started, and an issue search for exam kill lines found only rung kill lines.*

**Why.** The strictness check (2026-11-06/07, read-only, simulated; rules read from origin/main `9354ac7e`, which includes #3100 and #3129; the report is `~/tradepartner-probes/strictness/report.md` on the owner's machine, sha256 `635df286168e6e3cd52b20763750f457d50cd340188069cc9ec1b5e77b27c771`) ran a slow monthly long-only book and an event book through the ladder, promotion and the exam as written, with TP's own gate code. Its findings, quoted:
- End to end, a real edge of net IR 0.5 at the frozen backtest cost reaches a live order in **1.45%** (slow) and **0.18%** (event) of four-exam programmes ("Every idea real at the stated IR"). "In 98.5% (slow) and 99.8% (event) of programmes, the four exams are spent with no pass."
- "The 12-month exam at 0.25% per leg needs a paper-net IR of about 3.5 to pass half the time" (written rule: **3.50**, 3.55 at skew −0.3). "A net IR of 3.5 against SPY is about 3-5 times what the best long-only managers sustain."
- "#3129's power table is optimistic. Its IR is gross IR before a 1.1%/yr drag … At the real 60 bp paper cost, a net IR 0.5 slow book passes 0.29%, not 0.55%."
- "The size budget is not fully spent. At the skew −0.85 null, the full written rule (both legs plus futility) passes 0.76% per exam, against #3129's leg-(i)-only 1.10%. Four noise finalists then pass 3.0%, so about 2 pp of the 5% budget is unused."
- "No exam design that keeps four ideas under 5% can confirm an IR 0.5 edge within 3 years."

**The group-sequential exam (design D4 of the report).**
- **Looks.** The exam reads the monthly net excess series of decision 2 at three looks, after **12, 24 and 36** months.
- **The pass rule at a look.** At look L, both legs are computed on months 1 to L, as decision 2 defines them: (i) the t-statistic of the mean (sample standard deviation, L − 1 degrees of freedom), and (ii) the t-statistic of the OLS intercept on the benchmark's monthly return over the T-bill rate. The exam passes at the first look where **both** legs reach that look's bound. Both legs use the same bound; the calibration applied the same per-look value to both legs.
- **The bounds (O'Brien-Fleming shape, c × √(36 / L)).** t ≥ **4.76 / 3.37 / 2.75** at 12 / 24 / 36 months. The calibrated values in the output are 4.761, 3.367 and 2.749, and those are the pinned values.
- **Total size 1.25%, calibrated to the skew −0.85 null.** The bound scale was found by bisection on 400,000 zero-mean skew-normal nulls at skew −0.85 (the worst case the skew guard admits), 8%/yr tracking error, with both futility rules applied (calibration seed 20261107). "Calibrated variants … were checked again on fat-tailed skew −0.85 nulls, where every one came in at or below its size." On the 200,000-replicate evaluation draw, the design passed **1.252%** of skew-normal −0.85 nulls, **0.986%** of fat-tailed skew −0.85 nulls and **0.343%** of symmetric fat-tailed nulls (`exam_grid.txt`, D4 row).
- **Futility.** At month 6, if the sum of months 1 to 6 is below 0, the exam is failed (futility), as before. At the 12- and 24-month looks, if the exam has not passed and the sum of months 1 to L is below 0, it is failed (futility). Otherwise it continues to the next look. A futility stop can only stop a pass, so it cannot raise the false-pass rate.
- **Void.** A look can pass only if ADR 0023's minimums (`paper.min_rebalances`, `paper.min_exam_fills`) are met by that look. A window that ends before month 36 without a pass or a futility stop, or that has not met the minimums by month 36, is failed (void).
- **The kill line** posted before the window's first session carries the three looks, the three bounds, the futility rules, the benchmark and the finalist's in-sample skew. T381a freezes the same values at `paper start`.
- **The values are pinned.** The config validator refuses looks other than 12, 24 and 36, a bound below 4.761, 3.367 or 2.749 at its look, a futility month other than 6, a `paper.exam_max_count` above 4 and a skew guard below −0.85. So the exam can be made stricter in config, never looser. `paper.exam_alpha` and `paper.exam_horizon_months` are replaced by `paper.exam_looks_months` and `paper.exam_bounds` (T381).
- **The labels are unchanged**, with one addition: an exam that is running reports its next look ("forward return exam running, next look at month 24").

**The arithmetic: four exams.**
- **Worst case, inside the skew guard.** Four noise finalists at skew −0.85 give 1 − (1 − 0.0125)⁴ = **4.9%**. The union bound gives at most 4 × 1.25% = 5.0%, whatever the dependence between the exams. The written rule gave 3.0% (the report's run) and 4.33% (#3129, leg (i) only). The design spends the budget the written rule left unused; it does not raise it.
- **Fresh data still breaks the selection link** (decision 2's arithmetic): every exam month comes after the frozen kill line and after every in-sample and rung-2 window.

In the programme output, the median given a pass is 24.0 / 22.5 / 19.5 months today (slow, 60 bp, IR 0.5 / 0.75 / 1.0) and 46.5 / 43.5 / 40.5 months under A + B.
