# 0014. Strategies are registered objects: one dispatch seam, one family registry, generic exclusions

**Status:** Accepted (owner decisions of 2026-10-06, in chat to the orchestrator, recorded under "Owner decisions" below; by merging PR #1077, a class-B merge by the owner; two open questions for him under "Open questions", neither of which this record pre-empts)  ·  **Date:** 2026-10-06  ·  **Issue:** #1074  ·  **Plan:** [strategy-interface](../plans/strategy-interface.md)  ·  **Related:** [backtest spec](../specs/backtest.md) (amendment #720, the `profitability` family; its Series and data-model lines carry this record's notes), [paper-trading spec](../specs/paper-trading.md) (the `signals` line), [backtest plan](../plans/backtest.md) (T85 to T85f), [strategy-lab spec](../specs/strategy-lab.md), [ADR 0005](0005-objective-benchmark-stop-criteria.md) (benchmarks, not amended), [ADR 0012](0012-cadence-as-a-hypothesis-parameter.md) (cadence)

## Context

In plain words: the code has no idea called "a strategy". It has a family name, `momentum`, that a dozen places each read for themselves. The engine always calls the momentum signal; the paper journal only knows momentum's one reason for leaving a stock out ("no history"); the trial registry has one count column shaped for momentum; and a family that is missing from any of the small tables that list families quietly inherits momentum's settings instead of failing. Adding the second family, B3 gross profitability, therefore costs six plan tasks (T85 to T85f), and the third would cost about the same again.

Two pieces of evidence sit behind this record, both from 2026-10-06 (owner's probes directory, `tradepartner-probes/strategy-interface/`; summarised on #1074). A **design memo** read five open-source frameworks and mapped where family-specific logic lives on `main`. A **planner spike** then pushed a trivial second strategy (equal weight over every member) through the real paths on the fixture store: `engine.run`, `write_results` and the trial registry, `run_hypothesis` end to end, the T40 truncation and prefix invariance suites, and the paper step `plan_rebalance` → `decisions_from` → the `signals`, `decisions` and `paper_plans` journal rows. Thirteen tests passed on +31/−9 lines in four source files (a 22-line planner seam in three of them and nine lines of family tables in `config.py`); the engine's step, fills, valuation, exits, benchmarks, results, registry and holdout never noticed. Where the memo and the spike disagree, the spike wins. What it found, at `d08d116` (line numbers move; the plan re-verifies them per task):

1. **Nothing picks the strategy from the hypothesis.** `engine.run` calls the private `_plan` (`backtest/engine.py:170-193`, called at `:490` and `:520`), which calls `momentum_12_1` outright. `run_hypothesis` (`backtest/run.py:148`) never reads `hypothesis.family` to choose a signal, so its callers, the CLI (`cli.py:823`), the paper run (`execution/run.py:1824` → `execution/planning.py:536` `_plan_at` → `engine.plan`) and the paper report's tracking re-run (`execution/report.py:891`), would all run momentum for any slug. Passing a planner in from a caller, as the spike did, is not enough: the lookup has to happen inside `run_hypothesis` and `plan_rebalance`, keyed on the stored family.
2. **One exclusion reason, in three places.** `Plan` has `n_excluded_no_history` and `excluded_no_history` (`engine.py:97-115`); `trial_rebalances.n_excluded_no_history` is `NOT NULL` (`store/schema.py:844`, `store/registry.py:240`); the paper journal's `_signals` asserts `scores ∪ excluded_no_history == members` (`execution/plan.py:927-934`) and the `signals.reason` CHECK allows exactly `selected`, `below_cut`, `excluded_no_history` (`schema.py:1003`). A strategy with any other exclusion reason is refused by the paper path with a raw `ValueError`. This is the one real schema boundary; T85d's six fixed profitability columns deepen it.
3. **Silent momentum fallbacks.** `frozen.inert_sections` falls back to momentum's section for an unlisted family (`backtest/frozen.py:157`); `hypothesis.required_keys` does the same (`backtest/hypothesis.py:134`), and `frozen_params_of` and `frozen_hash_matches` default `family="momentum"` (`:239`, `:250`). The spike's family froze, hashed and required `strategy.*` keys it never read, so its `params_sha256` changed with `strategy.top_fraction`.
4. **The family list lives in four places** (`config.py:618-645`: the `Literal`, `_HYPOTHESIS_FAMILIES`, `FAMILY_PARENTS`, `ENGINE_FAMILIES`) plus `FAMILY_SIGNAL_SECTIONS` (`frozen.py:102`), `REQUIRED_SECTIONS` (`hypothesis.py:78`) and the sweep prefixes (`config.py:871-873`), and the `ENGINE_FAMILIES` gate is repeated at `run.py:190`, `planning.py:451` and `window.py:590`.
5. **MTUM is wired in by name.** `metrics.Series` and the metric keys `excess_cagr_mtum`, `tracking_error_mtum` (`backtest/metrics.py:41-56`), and `results._check` refuses a trial without MTUM equity (`backtest/results.py:66-67`, `:84`). The charter's constraint ("SPY and MTUM, measured against, never targeted") and ADR 0005 ("a backtest or paper result is reported only alongside both") require MTUM for every family today, so the finding is that a family cannot *add* a comparison benchmark of its own, not that MTUM is wrong; whether MTUM should become momentum's benchmark only is the owner's call (open question 1).
6. **A latent cadence bug, not of this record's making:** the frozen `schedule.rebalance_cadence` and `schedule.signal_anchor` are hashed but read nowhere; a `week_end` registration ran eight month-end rebalances and wrote `ok`. The strategy-lab plan's T98 (engine at cadence) and T100 (`paper start` refuses non-monthly) cover it; this record only sequences its tasks after T98 where they share `engine.run`.

What the spike confirmed is already generic and must stay so: the `DataProvider` protocol with every read as-of `t`, the engine step, fills, valuation, costs and exits, `write_results`, the registry's trial handle and family N, `holdout.decide`, the risk rules over the frozen `risk.*` section, and the shape of the look-ahead suites.

## Options considered

1. **Keep the per-family tables and add each family by hand** (B3's path). Pro: nothing to refactor; the tables are explicit. Con: six tasks and four shared files per family, `engine.py`, `schema.py`, `execution/plan.py` and the CHECK enum edited every time, and the fallbacks stay silent. The memo priced strategy #3 at about a working week of agent time across teams.
2. **A full strategy object model** (the memo's `Strategy` protocol with reader, scorer and constructor, `AsOfView`, `Scores`, a `StrategySpec` registry, a lane-1 diagnostic). Pro: the cleanest layering, qstrader's stages with hikyuu's one object for backtest and paper. Con: the spike showed the engine core needs no change at all and that the real cost sits in two places (the dispatch and the exclusions/counts shape); a protocol with three abstract parts for three families is abstraction ahead of need, and the memo itself names a third method creeping in as the signal to stop.
3. **This record: one seam, one registry, generic exclusions and counts, an additive per-family benchmark**, each the smallest change that makes the next family a registry entry plus its own module. Size M, four build tasks and one split. Pro: everything the spike found blocking or silent is fixed; nothing the spike found generic is touched; H1's identity is pinned byte for byte; the charter's benchmark constraint is untouched. Con: `Plan` keeps its momentum-named fields beside the generic ones for one more plan, and the paper journal takes one additive migration during Phase 4's paper window (kept small, in its own task, `safety-reviewer`).

**Considered, prior art** (the memo's reading; one line each, what we borrow): **qstrader**, pure `(dt, weights) -> weights` stages, each swappable, with the risk stage mandatory (ours already is, in `execution/risk.py`); **hikyuu**, the one strategy object replaying history and then running live, which is why the seam sits in both `run_hypothesis` and `plan_rebalance`; **vectorbt**, a strategy's only output is a weight frame and a separate deterministic simulator turns it into orders, and one variant per column is our "one variant, one counted trial"; **myhhub/stock**, a single self-describing registry list where adding a strategy is appending one entry; **stock-analysis-engine**, the full resolved config saved inside every run artifact, which our trial row already does. None of the five has point-in-time data, delisted names, a counted trial registry or a holdout; those stay ours.

## Decision

We will take option 3. Concretely:

1. **One dispatch seam, keyed on the stored family, inside the engine's two entry points.** `engine.run` and `engine.plan` take the family; `run_hypothesis` passes `hypothesis.family` and the paper planning step passes the window's hypothesis family, so the CLI, the paper run and the paper report's tracking re-run all dispatch without being edited. The lookup is `signal_for(family)` in a leaf module `backtest/strategies.py`, a table keyed by family that returns one record: the as-of reads the signal needs, the pure signal function, the exclusion reasons it may report, the count names it reports, and where its construction parameters (`top_fraction`, `weighting`) live. `_plan` fetches exactly the record's declared reads and constructs from the record's parameters; there is no per-family branch in `engine.py`, which is what lets a new family land without touching it. An unknown family raises before any provider call. The backtest plan's T85e builds this seam (its line is amended on #1074) so that the codebase ends up with one seam, not two.
2. **One family registry, from which the tables derive.** `config.FAMILIES` maps each `HypothesisFamily` to a frozen spec: its **sections** (first its own parameter section, the `Settings` field, then any other family's section its signal reads: `combined` lists `combined`, `strategy`, `profitability`), its parameter model, its parent or `None`, whether the engine and the paper path may run it, its exclusion reasons, its count names, its comparison benchmark or `None`, and its sweepable keys. `FAMILY_PARENTS`, `ENGINE_FAMILIES`, `PAPER_FAMILIES`, `FAMILY_SIGNAL_SECTIONS`, `REQUIRED_SECTIONS`, the family part of `FROZEN_SECTIONS`, `ALLOWED_AXIS_PREFIXES` and the default sweepable keys become derivations with today's values (`oracle`, a test-only entry that reads momentum's `strategy` section, is left out of `FAMILY_PARENTS` and `FAMILY_SIGNAL_SECTIONS` as it is today); a test pins that the `HypothesisFamily` literal equals the registry's keys, and another that `strategies.signal_for`'s table has the same keys. **Every section a family lists is frozen, required and hashed for it, and only the sections it does not list are inert**: `inert_sections`, the fingerprint's own-section rule and the hypothesis file's refusal of other families' keys (`hypothesis.py:186`) all derive from the tuple, so a family that composes other signals freezes their keys in its own registration and never reads them live; for `momentum` and `profitability` the tuple has one entry and nothing changes. The `Literal` stays (mypy strict), the pydantic section per family stays, and `FROZEN_KEY_DEFAULTS` stays append-only: a new family still pays its section's defaults there, which is the lab's integrity rule, not a tax to remove. **The momentum fallbacks go:** an unlisted family fails in `inert_sections`, `fingerprint`, `required_keys`, `frozen_params_of` and `frozen_hash_matches` instead of inheriting momentum's identity.
3. **Generic exclusions and counts, the one schema change.** `Plan` gains `exclusions` (reason → ids, every reason the family declares present, possibly empty) and `counts` (name → integer); `excluded_no_history` and `n_excluded_no_history` stay as constructor fields, filled from `exclusions["no_history"]` by the planner and asserted equal to it, so T40's plan-timing copies and the paper's `decisions_from` tests build a `Plan` as they do today. `momentum` declares `n_excluded_no_history` as its one count, so the fixed column and the generic record always agree. The trial registry gains a long-form `trial_rebalance_counts(trial_id, session, name, value)` table, written **once per rebalance, not per cost level** (a plan's counts are the same at every level, as `trial_weights` already assumes); the fixed count columns (`n_excluded_no_history` and T85d's six) are kept and written from the same mapping by name, their retirement a later size-S issue once the backtest page reads the table. The paper journal's partition check becomes "scores and the union of all exclusions partition the members, pairwise disjoint", and a journal row's reason is `selected`, `below_cut` or `excluded_<reason>`. **The database enforces only the prefix** (`starts_with(reason, 'excluded_')`); that the suffix is a reason the family declares is checked in the paper planner's `_signals`, and this split is what lets a new family land without a `store/` edit. The migration is additive (the count table is new; the `signals` CHECK only widens, so no stored row falls outside it; DuckDB cannot alter a CHECK in place, so `signals` is rebuilt with every row copied, as versions 5 to 8 did for `order_events` and `decisions`, inside one transaction so a failed rebuild leaves the store at its previous version).
4. **A per-family comparison benchmark, additive.** The charter and ADR 0005 require SPY and MTUM for every result, and this record does not amend them: both stay mandatory for every family, `results._check` keeps refusing a trial without them, and momentum's metric keys stay `excess_cagr_mtum` and `tracking_error_mtum`, byte for byte. What changes is that a family may **add** a comparison benchmark of its own as a registry attribute (`FamilySpec.benchmark`): when set to a series other than MTUM, `results` requires its equity too and writes `excess_cagr_<name>` and `tracking_error_<name>` for it; `METRIC_KEYS` stays a tuple and grows only when a family declares a new benchmark. The frozen `benchmarks` key and its default `["SPY", "MTUM"]` do not change (it is in H1's fingerprint). Whether MTUM should become momentum's benchmark alone, which would need an ADR 0005 amendment and a charter edit, is open question 1; a yes is then a one-line registry change plus that amendment.
5. **Paper opens per family, by decision.** `PAPER_FAMILIES` is a separate tuple whose value today is `ENGINE_FAMILIES`' value (`momentum`, `oracle`), read by the `paper start` and planning gates. Adding `profitability` to the engine's runnable families therefore does not open paper to it; `paper start` refuses it, naming the family, until point 3 has landed and the owner says so (a one-line registry change, class A by path, labelled `hold`).
6. **Strategy #3 is B4, momentum + profitability combined by fixed equal ranks** ([hypothesis backlog](../research/hypothesis-backlog.md) B4), and it is the proof of this record: it lands as one pure signal module, one registry entry (its sections `combined`, `strategy`, `profitability`, so both sub-signals' keys are frozen in a B4 registration), one `Settings` section with its `.env.example` lines, its `FROZEN_KEY_DEFAULTS` rows, one binding line in `strategies.py`, a fixture twin and its tests, and touches no file under `execution/` or `store/`, nor `engine.py`, `run.py`, `results.py` or `hypothesis.py`. If it needs more, this record was wrong and the plan says where. Whether B4 is a root family or a child of `momentum` (which decides its holdout order and whose N its trials raise) is the owner's, open question 2, settled before T130 is claimed.
7. **A fast "signal quality" lane is a seam, not a build.** `signal_for` returns per-rebalance scores and exclusions that a later diagnostic can join to next-period returns without the portfolio simulation. On the real store such a run touches returns and so is a **research run counted toward the family's N** (research-registry req 9, `research.family_run_count`); the registry rules do not change. Building it is a later task note in the plan, outside this record's scope.

**What does not change:** point-in-time reads (every read as-of `t` through `DataProvider`; the recording fake still records every read), trial counting and the family N, the holdout and `holdout.decide`, the cost model, the deterministic risk gate before any order, the frozen-defaults integrity rule, the charter's two benchmarks, and H1's identity: `H1_FINGERPRINT` and `TWIN_FINGERPRINT` (`tests/backtest/test_frozen_defaults.py:182-186`) are asserted unchanged by every task of the plan, each of which names that test file.

**Out of scope** (each needs its own decision or spec): the fast-lane diagnostic's build; long/short or leverage (charter scope; **no signed weights in the type** until an ADR allows them); event-driven reads (B6, B7; the event-data spec) and N-session holds; intraday; hysteresis or holdings-aware construction (a known limit: targets are shared across cost levels, so a holdings-dependent target would need an engine change this record does not make).

## Owner decisions (2026-10-06, in chat to the orchestrator; recorded here as decided)

1. Strategy #3 to prove the interface is B4 (combined), not an event-driven family first (point 6).
2. `paper start` refuses `profitability` until the paper path is generic: a separate paper-families tuple, one line in T85e (point 5).
3. A fast signal-quality lane on the real store counts toward the family's N as a research run, registry rules unchanged; it is out of this plan's build scope, a seam and a later task note only (point 7).
4. No signed weights in the type until an ADR allows long/short (out of scope).
5. The refactor is its own small plan plus this ADR, not a backtest-plan amendment, because it touches the paper path and a schema CHECK.
6. B3 is not paused: T85c, T85d (the six fixed profitability columns), T98 and T77b continue as planned; the refactor lands after or alongside them and migrates T85d's columns (point 3).

## Open questions for the owner

1. **MTUM for every family, or momentum's alone?** The charter and ADR 0005 say every result is reported alongside SPY and MTUM. This record keeps that (point 4) and lets a family add its own benchmark. If the owner would rather MTUM compare momentum only, the change is an ADR 0005 amendment and a charter edit (class B) plus `momentum`'s registry entry; T129 is written to need no rework either way.
2. **B4's lineage.** Root, or a child of `momentum` (`FAMILY_PARENTS`)? A child's holdout may start only after its parent has spent or capped its holdout, and the choice decides which family's N the combination's trials raise. T130 carries the answer; it is not claimed before.

## Consequences

- Good: adding a family becomes one module plus one registry entry plus the file-level work that is genuinely new (a read the provider lacks, a hypothesis file); the CLI, the paper run and the tracking re-run agree on which strategy a slug is because there is one lookup; an unlisted family fails instead of becoming momentum; a family that composes other signals freezes their keys too; the paper journal can record any declared exclusion reason; a family can be compared with a benchmark of its own beside the charter's two; H1's registration and fingerprint are untouched.
- Bad / accepted risks: one additive migration on the paper journal's `signals` table during Phase 4 (its own small task, `safety-reviewer`); two writes of the same count for one plan (fixed columns and the table) until the columns retire; `Plan` carries momentum-named fields beside the generic ones a little longer; `engine.py`, `config.py`, `schema.py` and `results.py` are already named by open lab and B3 tasks, so the plan's lanes serialise the refactor behind T98, T85e and T97 and state the shared parts.
- Reversibility: cheap for points 1, 2, 4 and 5 (code tables and signatures; every pin test stays); costly for point 3 only in the sense that a schema version cannot be un-shipped, and it is additive.
- Revisit if: a third family needs state the seam cannot pass (holdings, hysteresis) or a hold longer than one period; a reviewer finds a third abstract method creeping into the strategy shape before B4 exists; the owner wants long/short (an ADR) or an event-driven family (the event-data spec's read seam first); open question 1 is answered yes (then amend ADR 0005 and the charter, not this record).
