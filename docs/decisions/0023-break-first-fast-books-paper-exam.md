# 0023. Break first: fast strategies, paper books as forward exams, open fills, a halt policy and a liquidity-ranked universe for forward books

**Status:** Accepted by the owner's merge of the alignment PR; decisions given 2026-10-18 (#1731)  ·  **Date:** 2026-10-18  ·  **Issue:** #1731 (closes #969; amends [ADR 0005](0005-objective-benchmark-stop-criteria.md), [ADR 0006](0006-universe-and-cadence.md), [ADR 0007](0007-clock-failures-halt-path.md), [ADR 0016](0016-development-boundary-and-forward-exams.md) and [ADR 0017](0017-fast-paper-and-machine-readiness-gate.md) where it says so; the [charter](../charter.md)'s Compliance line)

## Context

Three and a half weeks in, the owner's direction (2026-10-18, in chat) was: faster strategies, because a one-period monthly hold gives one data point a month; paper books as the forward holdouts; paper and then a $100 live account soon, without cutting corners; and a break-first way of working for both building and research. The four-part read-only code review of the same day found what stands between the project and that (the alignment brief in the orchestrator's notes; every finding checked in code on `main` 9c68057):

- **Every paper book halts on anything.** The ticker-to-Alpaca symbol form was never applied on the order path, so every class share (`GEF-B`, `BRK-B`) fails and the daily book halted on its first rebalance (#1729, in flight); any one-name fault, a 30 s acknowledgement timeout or an eleventh skip halts the whole book until a hand `paper resume`; only HTTP 429 and 504 are retried. `FakeBroker` accepts any symbol, which is why the suite missed it.
- **The backtest cannot run a fast strategy honestly or quickly.** It fills at the close while paper fills at the open (immaterial monthly, material for a 20-session hold); its cost is one uncalibrated 15 bp per side, the size of the whole expected edge of a short hold; it has no per-position hold, no deliberate cash, no no-trade band; a cash rate is refused while Sharpe subtracts one; spin-offs are not adjusted while paper credits them; and a 10-year `daily` trial is estimated at 5 to 15 hours because the per-step marking read is unbounded and the valuation loops in Python.
- **The universe misses mega-caps** (IBM, ABBV, ABT, MMM, VZ, ALL, FITB, MMC never; BLK since 2024-10, XOM since 2026-07) because it ranks by the cover-page shares fact keyed on one CIK; the fixes wait on owner releases, and the identity code has grown faster than it has converged (one fix, T192, caused #1728). The "good enough" threshold (#969) had been open since 2026-10-05.
- **Every data fix stales every trial and book**, because `master`, `gap` and `adjust` are frozen into every strategy hash.
- About 16 to 18k source lines are off the trading path (labeling, corpus, index and OpenFIGI checks, repair tooling).

The one observation that reorders the work: **a forward paper exam only needs the universe to be right today.** A fast, liquid strategy can rank by dollar volume from bars and Alpaca's current tradable assets, which puts the mega-caps in at once and decouples a forward book from identity releases; the historical backtest keeps its survivorship limits and becomes a cheap kill filter, not the exam.

## Options considered

1. **Keep the current order:** finish the identity programme, then the shakedown, then fast strategies. Pro: the historical record keeps improving. Con: the owner's sittings go to releases that no running book needs; the first fast forward exam is months away; the review found the identity work regressing.
2. **Build the fast-strategy engine first, paper later.** Pro: research moves. Con: the paper books stay halted, so nothing is learned about the machine, and the exam that matters (the forward book) is not running.
3. **Break first, in this order: paper reliability, the shakedown, the cheapest kill tests, the engine only for survivors, the $100 live book chosen afterwards from what survives (this record).** Pro: every build task answers an observed or provoked break; the owner's decisions are taken in one sitting; the first forward exam and the first live order follow the shakedown and the first kill-ladder verdicts, weeks not months. Con: real work is paused (listed below), and a strategy that dies at a kill rung is never built, which is the point.

## Decision

We take option 3. The owner's decisions of 2026-10-18 on #1731, D1 to D9, and the working rules that go with them:

1. **D1, #969, the accept rule.** A data defect is fixed when it is a **correctness class** (a rule bug, a look-ahead risk, a holdout-integrity fault) or when it touches more than **0.5 % of in-sample universe-months**. Everything else is accepted residue that every backtest report cites: after `rederive-1682`, the FDIC filers (claim TP-4), the 22 SPAC-era CIKs, the unresolved index tickers and the 1:1 chain names (BLK, XOM) until T187d. T212's evidence line (the two top-1,000 sets at 2026-10-16) measures what of it reaches a forward book under D4. #969 is closed by this record.
2. **D2, the halt policy** (amends ADR 0007's "everything halts" for the paper run; the [paper spec amendment](../specs/paper-trading.md) of this date specifies it). A **per-name fault** (one of the three refusals the paper spec's amendment item 2 allowlists, **each on a buy only**: an asset the broker does not know, `not fractionable`, an asset not tradable; a held name's sell, trim or forced exit that the broker does not know is a reconciliation fault, a held untradable name keeps today's rule, and any other refusal halts) is a counted `skipped` decision with its reason and one alert, never a halt. The skip cap becomes a **share of the plan** (`risk.max_skip_share`, frozen at `paper start` for new windows), the absolute `risk.max_skips_per_run` kept as the floor for tiny plans. An **acknowledgement timeout** polls to a longer bound and then leaves that one order `pending_unresolved` for the next run's reconciliation instead of halting: at most one per run; a second timeout, or an order unresolved after the read-back, halts (paper spec item 4). **Halts stay** for a reconciliation mismatch, a limit breach, a clock fault, an engaged kill switch and a collapsed universe (`ReconciliationError`, `LimitBreachError`, `ClockError`, `KillSwitchEngagedError`, `UniverseCollapseError`), and for the rejection and skip caps themselves. 5xx and connection errors are retried with bounded backoff under the same duplicate read-back as 429 and 504 (a planning addition under D2, already built as PR #1735). A refusal of a sell quantity or of a position is never a skip: it is a reconciliation fault.
3. **D3, the fill convention.** `execution.fill_price = "open"` for every registration at `daily` or `week_end` cadence from this date (the [backtest spec amendment](../specs/backtest.md)); `month_end` registrations and every registered hypothesis keep what they froze (H1 stays `close`).
4. **D4, the universe for forward books.** `universe.rank_by ∈ {"cap", "dollar_volume"}`, default `cap` (byte-identical to today); a **new forward book** registers `dollar_volume`: ranked by median dollar volume over the window from bars alone, intersected with Alpaca's tradable assets at `t`, no shares fact required. Registered families keep `cap`. (Amends ADR 0006's "ranked by market cap" for forward books only.)
5. **D5, pause and defer.** The off-path work in the pause list below stops (lines kept, dated "paused" notes, no claims); B3's paper book is deferred so the third paper-account slot goes to a fast survivor. New health rules and identity censuses stop unless a running book or a kill test needs one.

The live book's strategy, name count, cadence and sizing are **chosen after the shakedown passes, from what survives the kill ladder, by the owner, in the Phase 6 live ADR (T163)**, and nowhere else. ADR 0010's limits are unchanged. *(Amended 2026-11-06, #3125: "what survives the kill ladder" is not enough. The live choice names only a hypothesis whose family's exam of record has passed a returns-judging exam, the forward return exam of [ADR 0016's amendment of that date](0016-development-boundary-and-forward-exams.md#amendment-2026-11-06-3125-3128-no-live-choice-until-an-exam-judges-returns-on-unseen-data-the-forward-return-exam). **The break-first working rule** (build and research, from this date):

- **Build.** A build task exists only for a break observed on a scheduled run or provoked in a drill, and it starts with the failing test that reproduces it. The thinnest end-to-end slice goes under real conditions first (paper, the real store) and reality is allowed to break it. Faults are provoked on purpose before they happen by surprise: a stricter `FakeBroker` that refuses what Alpaca refuses, and one drill per fault (a bad symbol, a 5xx, a timeout, the skip cap, a kill-switch drill, a sleeping machine). No speculative edge-case programmes. A reviewer's finding on an unbuilt consumer goes to that task's line or one size-S issue per module (plan-shape rule 6), never a new programme. Class-B docs changes are batched into one alignment PR per sitting; everything else is class A.
- **Research: the kill ladder.** Every idea starts with the cheapest test that could kill it, the kill criterion written before the run, and stops at the first failed rung. The rungs, in cost order: **1** cost break-even (needed edge = 2 × one-sided turnover × measured cost per side, against the published long-leg proxy; the measured cost is the paper books' per-fill slippage plus half-spread *(amended 2026-11-07, #3143: the measured cost is the exam's paper basis, [amendment below](#amendment-2026-11-07-3143-rung-1-prices-cost-at-the-exams-paper-basis))*); **2** the long-only leg's sign and size in published or open data (Chen–Zimmermann or an open dataset), post-2010 *(amended 2026-11-06, #3092: the deciding leg's average t across the two pinned Chen–Zimmermann files over 2009-2025 must be ≥ 2.22, the long leg for a long-only book, the deciding leg per item 6, [amendment below](#amendment-2026-11-06-3092-a-calibrated-rung-2))*; **3** capacity and implementability at our scale ($100 live, whole against fractional shares, orders per session); **4** a harsh backtest on the current store at the harsh settings (cost doubled, one-session delay, open fills), **killed if net excess CAGR over SPY < 0 or `dsr_excess` < 0.5 under those settings** (the hypothesis file's own retirement rule is its rule at the base settings, not this rung's); **5** placebo (shuffled signal, random-entry book, one extra lag). Rungs 1 to 3 are arithmetic and read-only data, logged as a note on the item's issue; **every rung-4 and rung-5 run, placebos included, is a `trials` row** (counted in its family's N as req 7 counts it; a placebo is labelled as such in its note); registered research runs are for rungs 1 to 3 only when a read needs a record. A good number is treated as a bug until the shuffle, cost-shock, delay-shock and top-names-removed tests pass. Only a survivor gets a hypothesis file, engine work and a paper book. The first ladder runs **B10 → B9 → IRRX**, in that order, with the rung-by-rung kill criteria recorded in the [hypothesis backlog](../research/hypothesis-backlog.md).
- **Forward exams and tracking windows.** ADR 0016 point 4 is unchanged: only a family whose holdout is **unspent** can have a forward exam, and a book of a family whose holdout is spent (every `momentum` book: H1's `main`, the daily operations book, **B10's book**) is a **tracking window**, whose evidence is ADR 0005 criterion 1 as reported, never an exam of record. A paper book counts as an exam of record only if its family's holdout is unspent, it is pre-registered with frozen parameters and a kill rule in its file, runs at least `paper.min_rebalances` at its cadence (ADR 0017 part C) **and** has at least the minimum number of live fills for its cadence, `paper.min_exam_fills`: **60 at `month_end`, 150 at `week_end`, 400 at `daily`** (the planning team's proposal, frozen into the window at `paper start`). Every paper book, exam or operations book, is a trial: its window and its tracking trials are listed in the registry beside the family's trials, and an exam's outcome, pass or kill, is recorded as a `TP-` claim. The daily `main`-sibling book stays an operations book, never an exam (the 2026-10-10 plan amendment). Which strategy goes live is never a paper-book contest (ADR 0017 part F).
- **The owner's time.** Owner decisions are batched into one weekly sitting with a recommendation per item; owner-run steps are kept to the minimum, and each is named by what it unblocks (O1 the books back up and the machine awake; O2 the shakedown span; O3 the Phase 6 gates, settled with this record; O4 the first live start, of the book T163 names).

**Pause list** (D5; a dated note on each line, no deletion): T189c (OpenFIGI check), #1598 (index known-answer validation), T187 to T187c (successor chains in history: D4 puts BLK and XOM into forward books without a release), T184h (pre-2019 listings), T174c and the labeling and corpus track, T171d (borrow status), T23, T116c, T141, and the conditional T204 until its trigger fires. **Supersede and fold:** T160 is done in substance (window 4 exists) and ticks when the plan's evidence for it is recorded; T161 item 3 (B3's book) is deferred and item 4 reads "after B10 survives rungs 1 and 4"; T162 is O2 with T22's five scheduled `ok` runs taken from the same span (T23 paused); T163, T163b and T163c are the live tasks A14 and A15 as the plans note; T165e is B10's rung 4; T164e is also gated on B9's rung 1; #1290 is closed (T183 was the fix), only the resume remains. **One release, then freeze:** `rederive-1682` (T198) runs as planned in one sitting with the #1382 restamp store step and `repair-dividends` (T186c); after it the security master, the schema (unless a trading need) and the process scripts are frozen, and the staled trials are replayed only for H1 and for whatever survived rung 4.

## Consequences

- Good: the owner's nine decisions close in one PR instead of nine; every build task in the [targeted plan](../plans/paper-trading.md) names its break and its failing test; the first fast paper book (B10's `month_end` tracking window, if it survives) is weeks away; the live book is not pre-judged: it is chosen in T163 from what survives, under a feasibility rule written now; the backtest's cost, fill and cash conventions stop flattering short holds; the universe a forward book trades is right today without a release.
- Bad / accepted risks: history keeps the identity residue D1 names, cited on every report; a strategy that dies at a cheap rung is never built, and a false kill (a noisy rung-1 slippage number) is possible, which is why rung 1 reads two weeks of fills before it decides; the fill-convention split (H1 `close`, fast books `open`) means two conventions in one registry, each frozen per hypothesis; the per-name skip rule can hide a systematic broker fault behind many small skips, bounded by the share cap and the alert; the paused work loses context while it waits.
- Reversibility: cheap for everything but D3 and D4, which are frozen per registration and therefore never retroactive: a later record can change the default for new registrations and nothing else moves. The pause list is a note per line; un-pausing is a claim.
- Revisit if: the shakedown span restarts twice (the paper policy is wrong, not the machine); B10, B9 and IRRX all die at rung 1 or 2 (the ladder is too harsh, or the measured cost is); the per-fill slippage median on any book is above 20 bp per side (the live book's sizing, not its strategy, is the problem); a forward book under `dollar_volume` holds a name the historical universe never had for a reason other than the shares fact (the two universes have diverged beyond D4's intent); or the owner's circumstances change (D9's compliance statement is his and dated).

## Amendment 2026-10-30 (#2667): one rung-1 cost rule

Owner decision 4 of 2026-10-30 ([#836 comment](https://github.com/josejuarez96/tradepartner/issues/836#issuecomment-6537997869)). The "measured cost per side" in rung 1 is defined once, here:

- **Per side, against arrival:** buy-adverse cost of the order's fill VWAP against the arrival price (the NBBO midpoint when the order could first trade; the session open for a pre-open order), as in the [arrival study](../research/2026-10-30-paper-fill-cost-arrival.md), not against the open.
- **Equal-weighted** across orders (not notional-weighted), the mean of the buy mean and the sell mean.
- **In the book's own liquidity band:** only orders in names inside the universe the book registers (for a daily book, over the $200M floor in the [research program](../ways-of-working/research-program.md#registration-defaults)).
- **Rebalance days only:** build-day and other non-rebalance sessions are excluded.

## Amendment 2026-11-06 (#3092): a calibrated rung 2

Decided by the orchestrator under the owner's delegation of 2026-11-06 (the trigger on #3092 names an owner call; he delegated it). As a class-B change it lands only on the owner's merge of its PR, which is his word on it. The evidence is the [calibration report on #3092](https://github.com/josejuarez96/tradepartner/issues/3092#issuecomment-6700453288) (team `gatecal`; scripts and outputs kept outside git with their SHA-256s, synthetic series only, no store, no holdout, no exam month).

**The trigger fired.** #3092 restated R4 §4.3's pre-registered gate-only consequence before computing: at a planted IR of 0.5, if the current rung-2 rule's true-positive rate is below 25% while the single-window t ≥ 2 rule's exceeds 50% at the same false-discovery rate, rung 2 is amended. In the pooled run (200k nulls, 60k planted, 20% planted prior), both legs live, IR 0.5:

| Matched FDR | Current rung-2 TPR | Single-window t ≥ 2 TPR | Trigger |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 5% | 12.5% | 56.9% | fires |
| 6.8% (current rule as written) | 17.75% | 63.1% | fires |
| **10%** | **24.3%** | **70.2%** | **fires** |
| 20% | 32.7% | 82.9% | does not fire |

When the short leg is dead, the current rule "has 0.0% TPR at any FDR ≤ 20% (1.5% as written, at FDR 46%)".

**The rule "as practised"** was the one the desk reads applied (#2675, #2828, #2946): L/S, long and short legs each with a mean floor and t ≥ 1, in two Chen–Zimmermann files × three windows (2009+, 2016+, 2020+), 18 checks. Line 40's own words ("the long-only leg's sign and size ... post-2010") and the earlier B9, Q1 and I1 reads (long leg ≥ 0.10 %/month and t ≥ 1.0) are also replaced.

**What was fixed before and after computing.** #3092 pre-registered the trigger, rules (a) and (b), and "(c) one justified alternative (in the report)" without defining it. The definitions of (c) and of the sensitivity rule (s) were written in the report after computing; (s) was "added after the first run". Only the thresholds come from matched FDR. The trigger compares (a) with (b). The report leaves "choosing between (b) and (c), and the threshold" to this amendment, and this record chooses (c).

**Rung 2, from this amendment** (the report's rule (c)):

1. **Series.** A leg is read gross and monthly in each of the two pinned Chen–Zimmermann files: value-weighted (`95409cb1…79a8`) and size-screened (`fcd2ae40…3734`), or their successors under the re-calibration rule below. The **long leg** is the favourable portfolio minus the equal mean of all the signal's portfolios (the desk reads' definition). The **short leg** is that equal mean minus the unfavourable portfolio. "Favourable" follows the published sign: when it favours port `01`, the long leg is `01` minus the mean, and the short leg is the mean minus the top port.
2. **Window.** One fixed calendar window: **2009-01 to 2025-12**, n = 204. A later file release does not move it; a change of window is a re-calibration trigger. There are no nested 2016+ or 2020+ windows: they are subsets of the 204 and add conjunction penalties, not information. A leg is read in a file only if all 204 of its months are non-null there (the calibration used only signals with no missing month). A signal whose `SampleEndYear` is 2009 or later is not read under this threshold: its window would include in-sample months, which the demeaned nulls do not model, so it needs a re-calibration first.
3. **Statistic.** In each file, t = mean / (sd / √n), sample sd (ddof 1). The score is the **average of the two files' t**.
4. **Threshold: 2.22.** This is the value the pooled run calibrated to FDR 10% at a 20% planted prior (≈ 2.2 in the report; ≈ 1.7 would be FDR 20%). There is **no mean floor**: cost is rung 1's job. A score below the threshold is a rung-2 kill.
5. **One file only.** If a leg is complete in the value-weighted file only, that file's t alone is read at **t ≥ 2.55**, the report's FDR-10% threshold for rule (s). If it is complete only in the size-screened file, no threshold is calibrated, and rung 2 is not decided until the calibration is re-run for that case; nothing is built in the meantime. A signal with no published long leg goes to rung 2-O as before.
6. **Which legs decide, by the book the idea would run.** ADR 0024 keeps the ladder for both legs:
   - **Long-only** (every live book, and any long-only paper book): the long leg's score decides.
   - **Long/short paper book:** each leg's score must reach the threshold. One leg below it kills the long/short idea. A long-only version of the same signal is a separate idea with its own kill line, posted before its read.
   - **Short-only paper book:** the short leg's score alone decides.
   The calibration scored (c) on the long leg only. So the short-leg threshold is **provisional**: before the first rung-2 read that a short leg decides, the calibration is re-run on the short leg, and that leg's threshold is set from the run (the scripts already draw the short-live configuration). For a long/short book, the two-leg requirement is a conjunction that was not calibrated. It is accepted as stricter than either leg alone, and the same re-run calibrates the joint rule.
7. **Unchanged.** The kill line, naming the signal, the files, the favourable side and the book type, is posted on the item's issue before any return is read. Rungs 1, 3, 4 and 5 and rung 2-O are as before. Every read is a ladder-ledger row.

**Why (c), not the single-window L/S rule (b).** The live book is long-only (ADR 0024 decision 1, until the live account type is decided), and line 40's own rung-2 text is a long-leg test. (b) fires the trigger on its own wording, but "with one leg dead it is weak (4-14% at FDR 10-20%), so (b) alone is a poor rung for a long-only book". With the short leg dead, (c) "holds at 45% (FDR 10%) and 65% (FDR 20%)", and it "beats the current rule by at least 20 pp at both FDRs at IR 0.5, whether both legs are live or only the long leg is". Pooled at FDR 10% and IR 0.5, (c)'s TPR is 45.7% with both legs live and 45.4% with the short leg dead (base seed: 48.6% and 49.2%). The two files' long legs correlate at +0.56, so averaging their t uses both files as corroboration without a conjunction. The long-leg score cannot see a short-only signal; item 6 reads the short leg for the paper books that would trade one.

**Caveat: the margin is not robust to modelling choices.** In the report's words, "24.3% < 25% holds beyond sampling noise. It does not hold beyond modelling choices (prior, block length, floors)". Across six 5k-rep seeds, the current rule's FDR-10% TPR was 21.0-26.3% (mean 23.6%). R4 §4.2 asked for 50 seeds; the run used six seeds plus one pooled run. Three choices cut against the result:
- A lower planted prior makes every rule stricter at a given FDR.
- Planted signals are equally strong in both files with no costs. That flatters (c) against a real signal that is weaker in the screened file.
- The FDR is per signal, not per family.

At IR 0.5, (c)'s gain over the current rule of at least 20 pp does not rest on the 25% line. The **threshold** 2.22 does rest on these choices.

**Re-calibration trigger.** The calibration is re-run, and the threshold re-set by a new dated amendment, before the next rung-2 read after any of the events below. The scripts are `calibrate.py` (`dcf7b0de296c4c8563c857e2f66993e7c4aaaa480f9c9a78369a1bce1443fe9d`) and `pooled.py` (`54a8245d1e88c4d11d7d439c10eafcd1fe8e20a7b7f78efbd9556fdced029f35`). They are kept outside git in the owner's `~/tradepartner-probes/arch/gatecal/`, with their outputs.
- **A sensitivity run is required before the first rung-2 read under this amendment:** the planted prior at 10% and 33%, and the block length at 6 and 24 months. If it moves the FDR-10% threshold by more than 0.2, the threshold is re-set first.
- A pinned Chen–Zimmermann file is replaced, or the window is changed.
- A rung-2 read uses an open dataset other than the two pinned files.
- The case is one item 2, 5 or 6 sends to a re-calibration: a `SampleEndYear` of 2009 or later, a screened-only leg, or the first short-leg or long/short read.
- The pool rule (R4 §4.2: `Cat.Data = Accounting`, `SampleEndYear ≤ 2005`, plus `CBOperProf`) or the leg definitions change, or a read is of a signal outside that pool's category (for example a price-based signal, with different autocorrelation and tails). Until it is re-calibrated, such a signal's rung 2 is not decided.
- The live account type is decided (ADR 0024's revisit trigger). Item 6's live row is then re-read.

## Amendment 2026-11-07 (#3143): rung 1 prices cost at the exam's paper basis

*The orchestrator's decision on #3143, recorded for the owner's word on this class-B PR (proposal B of the strictness check, adopted together with proposal A, the group-sequential exam of [ADR 0016's amendment of this date](0016-development-boundary-and-forward-exams.md#amendment-2026-11-07-3143-the-forward-return-exam-becomes-group-sequential)). It amends rung 1 of the kill ladder (line 40) and the [2026-10-30 amendment (#2667)](#amendment-2026-10-30-2667-one-rung-1-cost-rule). Rungs 2 to 5 are unchanged.*

**Why.** The strictness check (`~/tradepartner-probes/strictness/report.md` on the owner's machine, sha256 `635df286168e6e3cd52b20763750f457d50cd340188069cc9ec1b5e77b27c771`; its scripts and outputs are pinned by sha256 in ADR 0016's amendment of this date) found, quoted:
- "Rung 1 prices cost at 8.8 bp against arrival (#2667), a 2.1%/yr break-even for this book. The exam charges 14.4%/yr. So the ladder sends the exam books that cannot pass it, and each one spends one of four slots." (The event book: one-sided turnover 12 per year, paper cost 60 bp per side.)
- "A net IR 0.5 event book has a paper-net IR of −0.58. It passes the exam 0.04% of the time, below the 0.11% a symmetric zero-mean book passes."
- "The slow book is hit too. At 3.6 per year, the extra paper drag is 3.24%/yr (0.41 IR), so a net IR 0.5 slow book is paper-net 0.10."
- "B first. It is not a loosening: it makes the ladder and the exam price the same cost, and it is the largest single lever. At 60 bp an event book cannot pass any exam."

**The rule.**
1. **Rung 1's cost is the exam's paper basis, for an idea that can take an exam.** From this date, for every idea whose family's holdout is unspent (so it can reach a forward return exam), the "measured cost per side" in rung 1 is the per-side cost the paper exam charges: the `costs.per_side_bps` frozen into the paper books under the [paper-trading spec](../specs/paper-trading.md)'s amendment of 2026-11-07 (#3118, PR #3134, item 3). That figure is measured per order against the bar open of the order's session, weighted by notional, pooled over both sides and rounded to 5 bp, by `paper cost` (T383). Today it is **60 bp**, the owner's data call. If he overrides it, rung 1 uses his figure. When a later window freezes a re-measured figure, rung 1 uses the latest figure frozen into a paper book on the day of the read. **#3134 must merge first:** this amendment cites its item 3, T383 and T385, which exist only once it lands. If the owner rejects #3134, the basis is the 60 bp it proposed, unless he names another figure.
   - **Out of scope:** a book of a family whose holdout is spent (every `momentum` book, B10's included) can never take a forward exam (the forward-exams bullet above), so it spends no exam slot. Its rung 1 stays under the #2667 rule, and #2667's open owner question on B10's rung 1 stays open. This amendment neither kills B10 nor settles that question.
2. **The arithmetic is line 40's.** Needed edge = 2 × one-sided turnover × that cost per side, against the published long-leg proxy. An idea whose proxy's gross edge is below the needed edge has a negative paper-net edge and is killed at rung 1.
3. **What the #2667 rule becomes.** Its four-part arrival measure (against arrival, equal-weighted, in the book's band, rebalance days only) no longer decides rung 1. Every rung-1 note still prints it beside the paper basis, because it measures what the cost work can reach (the paper spec's cost goal of this date). **The 20 bp revisit trigger** in the Consequences above keeps reading the #2667 arrival measure, not the paper basis, so it does not fire merely because the paper basis is 60 bp. The figures quoted elsewhere under the #2667 rule (the [idea-pipeline spec](../specs/idea-pipeline.md)'s cost row, the [idea cards](../research/idea-cards.md)) stay as measurements.
4. **Before an exam slot is spent.** A finalist whose rung 1 was read on an older basis is read again at the paper basis before its exam's kill line is posted, and it does not take the exam if it fails. A book that entered the ladder at rung 4 without a published proxy (an event book) gets the same read at the same point, against the gross edge of its promoted in-sample trial (its net excess plus its frozen cost drag). Both reads are arithmetic, logged as a note on the item's issue.
5. **Past records stand.** The kill lines already written (B10 12 bp, B9 6 bp) and the rung-1 notes already posted are not re-read, except under item 4. A rung-1 read not yet made, for an idea in scope of item 1, is decided at the paper basis. B10's pending read is out of scope (item 1) and is read as the [paper spec](../specs/paper-trading.md)'s 2026-11-03 amendment, item 11.3, already pins.

**What it buys, quoted from the report (proposal B).** False pass: "unchanged (≤ 3.0% with D0, 4.9% with A)". Exam pass at net IR 0.5 once paper cost reaches 24 bp: slow "written 0.29 → **0.67%**; with A 0.84 → **3.13%**", event "written 0.04 → **0.62%**; with A 0.03 → **2.17%**". Time: "none, and books that could never pass stop spending exam slots". The rung-1 change alone moves no exam pass rate: it removes finalists that cannot pass. The pass-rate gains need the cost work to reach the 24 bp goal. Programme rates under A + B are in ADR 0016's amendment of this date (slow 12.1% / 25.5% / 45.8% at IR 0.5 / 0.75 / 1.0).

**Consequences.**
- **Good:** the ladder and the exam price one cost; a book that cannot pass the exam at today's paper cost stops at rung 1, before it spends one of four exam slots.
- **Bad / accepted:** at 60 bp most fast ideas that could take an exam now die at rung 1 (an event book with 12 turns a year needs 14.4%/yr of gross edge); a noisy paper-cost figure can kill a real idea, and the figure is fragile ("48 bp without the top 5 names", the report's Limits); rung 1 moves with each re-measured paper figure.
- **Reversibility:** cheap. Rung 1 is arithmetic, and a later figure re-reads it.
