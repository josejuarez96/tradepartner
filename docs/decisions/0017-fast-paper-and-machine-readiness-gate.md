# 0017. Fast paper: several books, every cadence, and a machine-readiness gate for Phase 6

**Status:** Proposed (the owner accepts by merging; every open question below is his)  ·  **Date:** 2026-10-09  ·  **Issue:** #1352  ·  **Amends:** the [charter](../charter.md) (the "Success looks like" line: criterion 1 leaves the Phase 4 exit; the Scope "Cadence" line: the MVP's paper cadence is no longer monthly; both rewritten in this PR, as ADR 0012 did), [ADR 0005](0005-objective-benchmark-stop-criteria.md) (Phase 4 success criterion 1 becomes a per-book report in rebalance periods, not an exit criterion; the Phase 4 exit), [ADR 0012](0012-cadence-as-a-hypothesis-parameter.md) sentence 1 ("paper and live stay `month_end`"), [ADR 0014](0014-strategies-as-registered-objects.md) point 5 (paper opens per family: the owner's "says so" is this record), [ADR 0015](0015-expansion-seams.md) seam 1 ("the rules do not relax") and its deferred "multi-book aggregation" row, [ADR 0016](0016-development-boundary-and-forward-exams.md) point 4 ("everything else about `paper start` stays: ... the one open window, month-end cadence, `PAPER_FAMILIES`"; and "at least `paper.min_rebalances` months after `holdout.start`", which becomes rebalance periods at the hypothesis's cadence)  ·  **Related:** [roadmap](../roadmap.md) Phase 4 and 6 rows, [paper-trading spec](../specs/paper-trading.md), [strategy-lab spec](../specs/strategy-lab.md) req 11, #878 (live stop criteria), #813 (cash account, compliance; closed), #1286 (month-end fallbacks after T136), #1301 (one track now)

## Context

The owner's direction of 2026-10-09 (#1352), in plain words:

- Paper trading stays a required step. But it must give fast feedback. Daily and weekly strategies are allowed on paper. Several strategies run on paper at once (H1, B3, B4, faster candidates), not one monthly strategy at a time.
- The ~$100 live account (Phase 6) is gated on **the machine being ready**: a fixed shakedown on paper with real orders every session, clean reconciliation, no duplicate or mis-sized orders, a kill-switch drill and complete journal chains. It is not gated on one long-hold strategy's months of paper tracking. Strategy evidence keeps accruing on paper and live in parallel.
- The existing live gates stay: the employer compliance check (charter line 27, unticked), the written stop criteria (#878, memo posted, undecided), the taxable cash account (#813, decided 2026-10-04) and a safety review of the live path.

Why this needs a record: several documents say the opposite today. The charter's "Success looks like" line, ADR 0005 and the roadmap's Phase 4 row make the exit "paper tracks backtest within the configured tolerance" after "at least 6 monthly rebalances of unattended paper runs" of one strategy. The charter's Scope line, ADR 0012 and strategy-lab req 11 say paper stays `month_end` "until a Phase 4 spec amendment names another cadence". ADR 0015 seam 1 kept "one open window, whole account flat" on purpose and deferred multi-book to "a second paper book the owner wants on the same account". ADR 0016 point 4 restated all three. The code enforces them in four places, listed below.

Why now: `paper start` has not run on the real store (T71 is open). The journal holds fixture rows only, so no paper result exists that this change could be fitted to, which is what ADR 0005's reversibility clause asks of any amendment to its criteria. ADR 0015 already put `book_id` on every journal row and in every `client_order_id` so that a second book would be cheap. The changes below are cheapest before the first real window, for the same reason ADR 0015 gave.

### What blocks each part today (read-only scoping, current `main` at 76082f1)

**1. More than one paper strategy at once.** Four rules, each by design:

- One open window per journal. `store/journal.py:995-1002` `open_window` fails closed on more than one open row; `execution/window.py:683` and `:744` refuse `window_open` before and inside the write. Every command and reader takes *the* open or latest window with no book argument: `run.py:915`, `wrapper.py:1004`, `resume.py:538`, `reconcile_run.py:571`, `switch.py:331`, `window.py:887`, `check.py:420`, `report.py:856`, `ops.py:684`, `dashboard/override_page.py:223`.
- The whole account must be flat. `window.py:555-631` `_check_flat` refuses any open order and any position not explained by the previous window's residues.
- Reconciliation compares the whole account to the one book (`reconcile.py` docstring, lines 10-30: account id, every `positions()` row, `account.cash`, every open order). The ledger's cash base is the account's cash at the last `ok` reconciliation (`ledger.py:18-22`). The buys phase sizes against `account().cash` minus the reserve (`reserve.py` docstring).
- One key pair, one factory. `Settings.alpaca_paper_api_key/secret` (`config.py:1578-1579`); `_paper_credentials` reads exactly those two (`adapters/alpaca_trading_raw.py:142-155`); `build_broker(settings, clock)` takes no book (`execution/brokers.py:24-27`); one run lock per store (`lock.py`, `<store.path>.paper.lock`).

What already exists for a second book (ADR 0015 seam 1, T133 #1280): `paper.book_id` (`config.py:1368`), `book_id` on eight journal tables (`schema.py:1164`, version 17), the book token in every id (`ids.py:50-72`), `paper_windows.account_id` frozen per window and checked on every run (`run.py:1207-1210`). `alerts` has no `book_id` (`schema.py`, the `alerts` DDL).

**2. Non-month_end cadence on paper.** `window.py:290` `_PAPER_CADENCE = "month_end"`; `:656-661` refuses `refused_cadence`; and T136 (#1284) made the path cadence-aware but `start` still passes the constant, not the registered cadence, to `_holdout_end_completed` (`:676`), `_forward_first_session` and `_first_rebalance_session` (`:718-720`). Three readers fall back to `schedule.py`'s `month_end` default (#1286): `report.py:643` `fill_session(t_i)`, `report.py:801` and `check.py:190` `rebalance_sessions(start, end)`. Everything else is already per session or per cadence: the scheduler runs `paper run` every session (runbook, plist `Hour 8 Minute 5`); the submit window and the other timing keys are per session; `planning.due_rebalance` plans only the latest due rebalance, so at `daily` a missed rebalance lapses when the next fill session arrives (`planning.py:217-218`, `:237`), and `paper.max_catch_up_sessions` cannot overlap it. What assumes a month in meaning rather than code: `paper.min_rebalances = 6` (ADR 0005: six rebalances were six months), the report's naming (`MonthlyComparison`, `compare_months`, `_trial_months`; `cli.py:3007-3018`), ADR 0005's "monthly returns", strategy-lab req 11, the paper spec's Definitions amendment of 2026-10-05, ADR 0012 sentence 1, the charter's Scope line, and the scheduling runbook's prose.

**3. `profitability` and `combined` on paper.** The gate is one flag each: `config.FAMILIES["profitability"].paper_ready = False` (`config.py:905`) and `["combined"]` (`:932`), derived into `PAPER_FAMILIES` (`:987-989`), read by `window.py:648` and `planning.py:462`. The thing ADR 0014 point 5 waited for has landed: `Plan.exclusions` is generic, the paper planner writes `excluded_<reason>` for every declared reason (`execution/plan.py:951-997`, T127b #1209), the `signals` CHECK tests only the prefix (`schema.py`, `starts_with(reason, 'excluded_')`), `trial_rebalance_counts` exists (version 14), the paper planner dispatches on the stored family (`planning.py:557-561`, `engine.plan(..., family=family)`), and `strategies.py` has the profitability and combined reads (`statement_facts`, `sics`). The tracking re-run in `paper report` goes through `run_hypothesis` (`report.py:871`), which dispatches on the family too. What is missing: the flip, which ADR 0014 point 5 reserved for the owner's word, and a fixture-store test that a `profitability` plan and a `combined` plan round-trip through `plan_rebalance`, `decisions_from`, `_signals` and the journal (today's tests only assert the refusal: `tests/execution/test_window_start.py:275-284`, `test_planning.py:666`). B4 (`combined`) is a forward exam (ADR 0016 point 5), so it also needs its own book: blocker 1.

**4. The machine-readiness shakedown.** Measured today: `paper check`'s four lines (`check.py`: `rebalance_count` of scheduler-invoked executed rebalances, `tracking`, `chain` with its "once due" rule, `override_reason`); a `reconciliations` row with `status` (`ok`, `mismatch`, `pending_unresolved`, `fills_lagging`) and `mismatches_json` at the start and end of every run (`reconcile_run.py`); the derived kill switch, `paper kill` and `paper resume` (`switch.py`, `resume.py`); the alerts table with `missed_run`, `halted`, `reconciliation`, `stale_data` kinds and one `alert_deliveries` row per channel attempt (`alerts.py`); `paper_runs.invoked_by` and `paper_run_results.status` (`ok`, `halted`, `stale`, `skipped_kill_switch`, `crashed`, `failed`, `no_session`) with `fault_type` holding the exception's class name (`run.py:1098`); `DuplicateClientOrderIdError` at submit and the attempt-numbered id with `orders.client_order_id` as the primary key (`alpaca_broker.py`, `ids.py`); the risk limits per phase, every breach a `LimitBreachError` (`risk.py:514`); `paper lots-reconcile` against a broker export; `tradepartner health` for the data. Not measured as a pass/fail line anywhere: sessions run and sessions with a real fill; mismatch rows that were never explained; two live fills for one decision beyond its planned size, or a fill above its order's submitted size; a kill-switch drill (owner `engaged` row, a scheduled run that submitted nothing while engaged, a `released` row); alert delivery on every listed channel; a run row on every session. None of these needs a new journal table. Each is a query over rows the journal already writes.

**5. The live path.** `alpaca.paper = True` is guarded: the validator refuses `false` with "change only by ADR (Phase 6 live trading)" (`config.py:525`, `:558-563`); the raw client is built with the literal `paper=True` and reads only `ALPACA_PAPER_*` (`alpaca_trading_raw.py:142-159`); `AlpacaBroker` re-checks the guard (`alpaca_broker.py:265-269`); `build_broker` returns the paper adapter and nothing else (`brokers.py`); `cli.py` imports no adapter (T50). The data keys never reach the order path (`config.py:1576`). `paper.live_capital_reference = 100` and `n_orders_below_min_at_live_capital` on every plan report the sizing problem (spec req 7) without settling it. The paper account is a **margin** account (research 2026-10-08, "The paper account is a margin account") while the live account is a taxable **cash** account (#813): `Account` carries `cash`, `buying_power`, `equity` and the seam-3 reservations only (`adapters/broker.py:421-434`); nothing reads settled cash, so a cash account could buy with unsettled sale proceeds in the same session (a good-faith violation at the broker). A live switch therefore needs, technically: its own ADR that lifts the guard for one named book only; live keys under their own names (never the data keys, never the paper keys); a `paper=False` client behind the book's config and a second guard in the adapter; broker facts recorded on the live endpoint (T48b again, with real dollars); a settled-cash read for the buys phase; the stop criteria (#878) frozen into the live window; the lot ledger and 1099-B items (#658); a runbook. Size M for the switch, S for the settled-cash read, plus docs; all `safety-reviewer`.

## Options considered

**Several books**

1. **One Alpaca paper account per book** (this record). Each book has its own key pair, its own account id frozen on its window, and its own run lock. Reconciliation, flatness, the ledger's cash base, the reserve and the risk rules keep today's meaning: one account is one book. Pro: every safety rule on the order path is unchanged in substance; the change is a `book` argument from the CLI to the factory and the journal readers; the live account in Phase 6 is one more book on another endpoint, so this is the live shape too. Con: Alpaca's limit on paper accounts per login. Forum threads report **three paper accounts and one live account per login**, with more only through a second login ([forum 2655](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/multiple-paper-accounts/2655), [forum 18125](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/feature-request-more-paper-trading-accounts/18125)); this is **not verified in Alpaca's official documentation** and must be checked when the owner creates the second account. A fourth book then needs a second Alpaca login and its key pair in `.env`. Size M.
2. **Several books on one account.** Pro: no account limit; one key pair. Con: sleeve accounting on one cash balance: a per-book cash allocation rule and its reconciliation to the account's cash (`ledger.py:18-22`), per-book positions summed to the broker's one position per symbol (`reconcile.py`), flatness per book instead of per account (`window.py:555-631`), the buys phase and the reserve on the book's sleeve instead of `account().cash` (`wrapper.py`, `reserve.py`), `max_gross_exposure` per book against a shared equity, the attribution of a broker order to a book from its id token on every reconciliation, and a decision on whether one book's halt stops the others on the same account. Every one of those is an order-path change under `safety-reviewer`, and ADR 0015's audit priced it L. Rejected now; its trigger stays as ADR 0015's deferred row (a portfolio-level report across books), and option 1 does not close the door.
3. **A second checkout per book** (ADR 0015's interim answer). Pro: nothing to build. Con: two stores to ingest and back up, two sets of commands, no cross-book page, and the owner is the one running it. Rejected for more than one extra book.

**Cadence on paper**

1. **Allow `week_end` and `daily`** (this record): `_PAPER_CADENCE` goes, the registered cadence flows through `start`, and the three #1286 fallbacks pass the window's cadence. Size S, order path at `start`.
2. **Stay monthly.** Rejected by the direction.

**The Phase 6 gate**

1. **Machine readiness** (this record): a fixed shakedown with pass criteria, measured by a command over the journal, independent of any one strategy's tracking result.
2. **Six monthly rebalances of one strategy, tracking within tolerance** (ADR 0005 and the charter as written). Rejected by the direction: it measures the strategy, not the machine, and it takes six months that a daily book makes unnecessary.

## Decision

We will:

**A. Treat paper as an operations test plus evidence, not each strategy's exam.** A paper book proves that the machine trades, reconciles, halts and journals correctly, and it accumulates the strategy's tracking evidence while it runs. A strategy's exam of record stays what ADR 0016 says: a historical holdout spent once, or a forward holdout judged by its paper book over at least the frozen `paper.min_rebalances` rebalances. **Phase 4 exits on part E.** ADR 0005's criterion 1 (the tracking check) leaves the Phase 4 exit: it is reported per book, in rebalance periods of the book's cadence, for as long as the book runs, and it stays required for a forward exam (ADR 0016 point 4) and for a strategy to go live (part F). Criteria 2 to 4 (complete chains, override reasons, no engine run without a trial) stay exit criteria, inside E.5 and `paper check`. The charter's "Success looks like" line is rewritten to say so in this PR. This is done before any paper result exists, so no result is the reason for it (ADR 0005's reversibility clause).

**B. Several books: one Alpaca paper account per book** (option 1). The rules, each the smallest change from today:

1. A **book** is a `book_id` token (`ids.py`'s grammar) with its own key pair, its own Alpaca paper account and at most one open window. `main` keeps today's variables (`ALPACA_PAPER_API_KEY/SECRET`). Every other book's pair is named by its token (the pattern is the task's; the rule is one pair per book, never the data keys, never another book's). A book with no key pair cannot start.
2. `build_broker(settings, clock, book_id)` and `AlpacaBroker` take the book; the raw client reads that book's pair. Every `paper` command takes `--book <token>`, defaulting to `paper.book_id`. `open_window(conn, book_id)` and `latest_window(conn, book_id)` are per book, and `open_window` still fails closed on more than one open window *per book*.
3. The run lock is per book (`<store.path>.paper.<book>.lock`), and `paper run` with no `--book` runs every book that has an open window, in token order, each as its own run with its own lock and `paper_runs` row, under the one scheduled job. The DuckDB write lock stays per chunk, as today. Today's rule holds per book: a book whose sells phase would start outside the submit window trades nothing that session and its rebalance stays pending or lapses (spec req 7 step 6). So the books' runs share the submit window, and the runbook records each book's run time; see open question 4 for the stagger.
4. `paper_windows.account_id` stays the identity check (`run.py:1207`): a book can never trade another book's account. Reconciliation, flatness, the ledger, the reserve and every risk rule are unchanged: one account, one book.
5. The kill switch stays derived per window (`switch.py`); `paper kill` takes `--book` or `--all`. A reconciliation fault halts the book whose account it is.
6. `alerts` gains `book_id` (one additive journal schema version), so `no_window` and `locked` dedupe per book and the page can filter.
7. `paper status`, the operations page, `paper check`, `paper report` and the override page take the book; the page gets a book selector and a one-row-per-book summary.
8. The live account in Phase 6 is one more book on the live endpoint, in the same journal, under its own ADR.

**C. Cadences allowed on paper: `month_end`, `week_end` and `daily`.** `_PAPER_CADENCE` and `refused_cadence` go. `start` reads the hypothesis's frozen `schedule.rebalance_cadence` and passes it to the holdout-end check, T_0 and the forward first session; `report.py:643`, `report.py:801` and `check.py:190` pass the window's cadence (#1286). `paper.min_rebalances` stays frozen per window and is counted in the window's cadence units; ADR 0016 point 4's "at least `paper.min_rebalances` months after `holdout.start`" reads "rebalance periods at the hypothesis's cadence"; the default per cadence is open question 2. The timing keys, the submit window and the scheduled job are per session and do not change. `paper.max_catch_up_sessions` keeps its value; `due_rebalance` already bounds a catch-up by the next fill session. This record is the amendment strategy-lab req 11 waits for; its sentence and the paper spec's cadence lines are rewritten (P0 below) before the gate is removed (P1).

**D. Families on paper.** `profitability` and `combined` become `paper_ready = True`, which is the owner's "says so" that ADR 0014 point 5 reserved, accepted with this record. The change lands with the round-trip test named above, as the class-A, `hold`-labelled one-liner ADR 0014 point 5 describes, so the owner still lands it himself. Which hypothesis of each family starts a book, and when, stays the owner's (ADR 0016 point 5, #1301).

**E. The machine-readiness gate for Phase 6.** Phase 4 exits, and Phase 6 may start, when a command `paper shakedown`, run on the real store over every book, prints every line below as passed, and the Phase 4 retro records the output. (`shakedown`, not `readiness`: the paper spec's req 15 already uses "readiness read" for `paper stop`.)

*The span.* The owner opens the shakedown with one `owner_decisions` row of kind `shakedown_span` (`tradepartner decision shakedown-span --sessions N --order-sessions M --reason`), which records the two thresholds. The **span** is every XNYS session from the session after that row's `made_at` through the last completed session, and it is long enough only once it holds at least N sessions. The thresholds are read from the row, never from live settings, so the bar cannot move after a failing span; a new row restarts the span. **Decided (owner, 2026-10-09, open question 1): N = 10 and M = 5**, two weeks of sessions with real fills on at least half of them; a longer span scales M the same way, one order session for every two sessions. A `mismatch` reconciliation (E.2) also restarts the span: the span then starts at the session after the `released` kill-switch row that followed it.

*The criteria.* Every line names the rows it reads.

1. **Sessions.** For every session of the span and every book whose window is open on that session (a book opened or stopped inside the span counts for the sessions it was open), a `paper_runs` row with `invoked_by = 'scheduler'` exists whose `paper_run_results.status` is `ok`, or `skipped_kill_switch` during the drill of E.4, or `stale` with an owner note (an `owner_decisions` row of kind `shakedown_note` naming the alert). A session with no row passes only with a note on its `missed_run` alert; the line prints how many sessions passed by note. Across all books together, on at least M sessions a scheduler-invoked run submitted at least one order that has a live fill (`fills.superseded_by IS NULL`).
2. **Reconciliation.** Zero `reconciliations` rows with `status = 'mismatch'` in the span, in any book (a mismatch restarts the span, above). `pending_unresolved` and `fills_lagging` rows are allowed only when the next reconciliation of that book is `ok`; the line prints their count.
3. **Orders.** In the span: (duplicate) for no `decisions` row do the live fills of its `orders` (`fills.superseded_by IS NULL`) sum to more than `decisions.planned_quantity` plus the frozen `risk.reconcile_quantity_tolerance` for a quantity decision, or more than `decisions.planned_notional` plus the frozen `risk.reconcile_cash_tolerance` for a notional decision; (mis-sized) no order's live fills sum to more than `orders.quantity` plus the quantity tolerance, or `orders.notional` plus the cash tolerance; (breach) no `paper_run_results` row of the span has `fault_type = 'LimitBreachError'` (every risk rule, `refused_position_side` and `refused_order_shape` halt with it).
4. **Kill-switch drill.** In the span, in at least one book: a `kill_switch` row with `state = 'engaged'` and `source = 'owner'`; then a `paper_runs` row with `invoked_by = 'scheduler'` and `kind` in (`rebalance`, `catch_up`) whose result is `skipped_kill_switch` and which wrote no `orders` row; then a `kill_switch` row with `state = 'released'`. The drilled rebalance becomes `missed` and is listed separately by req 10, so the drill is run on a book that is not a forward exam (open question 5). A real halt-path engagement in the span is printed beside it as evidence, not required; but the halted run's result is `halted`, not an allowed status of E.1, so a real halt restarts the span once the owner has resumed, exactly as a mismatch does (E.2). The machine is ready when it runs a whole span without one.
5. **Journal completeness.** `paper check`'s `chain` and `override_reason` lines pass for every book: every chain is complete once its outcome is due (`check.py`'s rule), and no live fill is known after its terminal event.
6. **Alerts.** At least one `alert_deliveries` row with `ok = TRUE` in the span for every non-store channel in `alerts.channels`.
7. **Data.** No `stale_data` alert in the span lacks a `shakedown_note`, and `tradepartner health` passes on the day the command runs.

The command is pure over a read-only connection (the `check.py` pattern), prints one line per criterion with its query and the thresholds it read, exits 0 only when all pass, and writes nothing.

**F. The existing live gates stay**, in addition to E: the employer compliance check (charter line 27), the written stop criteria (#878: the memo's Q1 (b), Q2 (a), Q3 (a) are the planning team's recommendations and still the owner's call), the taxable cash account (#813), and a `safety-reviewer` pass on the live path's PRs. Nothing in this record lifts the `alpaca.paper` guard. A strategy goes live only from a book whose tracking check has been reported at least once; how many rebalances it needs is the Phase 6 ADR's question, not this one's. **The live strategy is not chosen by comparing paper books.** A book's tracking check judges the machine against that strategy's own backtest, not the strategy against the others; the choice of what goes live first is made from each strategy's exam of record (ADR 0016 point 3: never pick among several strategies on one exam), and any cross-book comparison that informs it is a research comparison counted under the registry's rules. Open question 8 recommends naming it before the span opens.

## Open questions for the owner (a recommendation beside each)

1. **Shakedown thresholds. Decided (owner, 2026-10-09): two weeks, N = 10 sessions with real fills on at least M = 5 of them.** The order-session count scales with the span the same way, one for every two sessions, so a span the owner lengthens keeps half its sessions with fills. (The draft recommended 20 and 10, one calendar month; the owner chose two weeks: a `daily` book gives it in two weeks, a `week_end` book in about ten weeks.) Smaller values would make the gate a formality; larger ones re-create the six-month wait.
2. **`paper.min_rebalances` per cadence.** Today one key, default 6, meaning six months. Recommend a per-cadence default (`month_end` 6, `week_end` 13, `daily` 63: six months, a quarter, a quarter) frozen into the window as today. The trade-off: a forward exam at `daily` over 21 sessions would be one month of evidence, thin for the tracking check and for ADR 0016's exam; 63 keeps every cadence's exam at a quarter or more. The alternative, one number for every cadence, is simpler and wrong at `daily`.
3. **The book cap.** If the forum-reported limit (three paper accounts per login) is real, the fourth book needs a second Alpaca login. Recommend: verify the limit when the second account is created; until then plan three books (H1 at `month_end`, B3, and one fast candidate), and open B4's book when an account is free or a second login exists. The owner names the three.
4. **One scheduled job or one per book.** Recommend one job: `paper run` iterates the books in token order, each under its own lock, so the runbook's wake, log and exit-code procedure stays one procedure; a crash in one book's run is that book's `crashed` row and does not stop the next book. The cost is that the books share the submit window (`[open − 90 min, open + 30 min]` today): each run reconciles, checks staleness and plans before its sells phase, so with many books the last may start its sells phase outside the window and trade nothing that session. Recommend the job starts at the window's open as today, the runbook records each book's run time from the first week, and one plist per book, staggered, is the fallback once a book is seen to miss the window.
5. **A kill in one book.** Recommend per-book switches (today's derivation, per window) plus `paper kill --all` for the owner; the accounts are separate, so nothing in one book can move another's cash. Run the E.4 drill on the fastest book that is not a forward exam.
6. **ADR 0005's `tracking_k` at faster cadences.** The modelled cost per rebalance is small at `daily`, so `k × cost` is a tight band. Recommend keeping `k = 2` and reading the first fast book's report before changing anything; a changed k is a new ADR 0005 amendment with that evidence, as its reversibility clause asks.
7. **The live book's home.** Recommend the same journal and store, book token `live`, so one page and one check cover paper and live; the Phase 6 ADR decides.
8. **Which strategy goes live first.** Recommend naming it in the `shakedown_span` row's reason, before the span opens, from its exam of record (H1 is the registered MVP with a spent holdout), so the paper books cannot be read as a contest.

## Consequences

- Good: several strategies run on paper at once with no change to reconciliation, flatness, the ledger or any risk rule; a daily or weekly book makes the shakedown a matter of weeks; the Phase 6 gate measures the machine with queries over rows the journal already writes, against thresholds frozen before the span; every safety guard on the live path stays exactly as it is; the book token ADR 0015 put in every id now has a second book to attribute.
- Bad / accepted risks: one more Alpaca account per book, each with a key pair in `.env`, and a forum-reported cap of three per login; two additive schema versions before T71 (`alerts.book_id`, then the two decision kinds, both through `store/schema.py`'s one version counter, P5 then P8); the `paper` commands gain a `--book` option that the runbook must explain; books share one submit window under one job; a `daily` book's tracking check is a tight band and may fail for reasons that are the cost model's, not the machine's (which is why A separates the two); the shakedown criteria are the owner's first list and will be tuned at the Phase 4 retro; the charter's success line loses "paper tracks backtest" as an exit criterion, which stays a reported number on every book.
- Reversibility: cheap. Books are rows and key pairs; a single book is this design with one entry. The cadence gate is one constant; the family flags are one line each. The shakedown command is read-only, and its thresholds are a decision row.
- Revisit if: Alpaca's account limit is lower than three or a second login is refused; a book misses the submit window because of the books before it (open question 4's fallback); the first fast book shows that the per-session rules (submit window, catch-up, `sell_wait_seconds`) do not fit `daily`; the Phase 4 retro finds a criterion in E that passed while something was wrong, or one that never passes for a reason that is not the machine's; or the owner wants a portfolio-level report across books (ADR 0015's deferred row).
