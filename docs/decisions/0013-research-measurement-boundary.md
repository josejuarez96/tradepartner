# 0013. Research-measurement boundary: pretrained classifiers may label facts for human review, never write them

**Status:** Accepted (owner decisions of 2026-10-05 on the five questions, in chat to the orchestrator and recorded on #946; by merging PR #950); owner decisions of 2026-10-06 added as a dated section (#1026: the $40 owner-funded pilot budget, the gold-items reading of point 5, the review page under point 8)  ·  **Date:** 2026-10-05  ·  **Issue:** #946  ·  **Amends:** ADR 0008 points 1 and 2 (readings, point 9 below; ADR 0008 itself is not edited)

*ADR number 0012 is reserved by strategy-lab plan task T92 (cadence as a hypothesis parameter, #939); this record is 0013.*

## Context

[ADR 0008](0008-llm-role.md) decided the LLM's role: none in the runtime until Phase 5, advisory at most, never a number and never an order. Three of its rules bear on this record. Point 1 forbids any LLM client or SDK among the runtime dependencies before the Phase 5 spec is accepted, and makes a PR that adds one out of scope. Option 4 rejects, "for the record", an LLM as an **extractor** that pulls facts out of filings into the store, because that makes a model-produced value an input to a decision. Point 3 says the one way a value an LLM produced may enter the store or config: a reviewed code or config change whose PR states the value's source, and only if that source is a human decision or a record with a `known_at`.

The 2026-10-03 research program then proposed experiments that those rules bar. The [ML validation brief](../research/2026-10-03-ml-validation-methodology-brief.md#governance-gates) §2 lists them: any pretrained language model in an experiment, "even offline", is not allowed today, and what unlocks it is "a new ADR defining a research-measurement boundary: a segregated research environment, model outputs that can never reach the store, decision tables or config, and the ADR 0008 point 2 style import/table tests. Plus a research spend ceiling above $0." Its open question 1 asks whether ADR 0008 covers non-generative hosted models and local pretrained encoders at all; the brief assumed yes until the owner says otherwise. The [prospective feature spec](../research/2026-10-03-prospective-semantic-feature-spec.md) §2 names this ADR as precondition 1 of any model capture and says what it must state: model calls happen only in a segregated research job; outputs go only to a research store; no code path reads them into the runtime store, the decision, order or fill tables, config or the risk-gated wrapper; and tests enforce it. The [research-registry spec](../specs/research-registry.md), accepted 2026-10-05 (#931), built the registry those experiments need and drew its own line (req 13): the registry **records** runs and authorizes nothing; it stores no model output and no filing text; research data (label exports, inference records, raw responses) lives outside the runtime store in a content-addressed `data/research/` directory (#810), and the registry points at it by hash. Its Definitions already carry the provenances `model_historical` and `model_prospective`, and its req 2 refuses a `confirmatory` registration with `model_historical` provenance and a `test` split on an exploratory one, both of which shape point 5 below.

**The owner's intent** (Jose, 2026-10-05, in chat to the orchestrator) is narrower than the brief's arms 5 to 9 and different in kind. He does not want a model for sentiment, trade signals or anything in the strategy or decision path. He wants to test the Jev typed classification model (vendor TypeSafe; [typed-classifiers research](../research/2026-10-03-typed-classifiers-financial-disclosures-research.md), cited below as **TC**) for **factual labeling that refines our data, with a human reviewing**: a second opinion on the security master (read a Form 25 or an 8-K12B and give a verdict to compare with our rules, then review a shortlist of contradictions); security types the rules cannot place; planned versus discretionary insider trades before the 2023 Form 4 checkbox; 8-K item subtypes (a 5.02 that is a CEO leaving, a CFO leaving or a new director); event types on collected GDELT headlines (earnings, lawsuit, recall, M&A, management change). He accepts that this needs ADR amendments and fits after a successful backfill and the other tests.

**Why the pain is real.** The security master's rules are patched by hand, one filing at a time: #921 pinned Valaris's `VAL*` footnote row after a Form 25; #905 fixed a cover page after a stopped line keeping a delisted span alive; #893 and #918 filled rename-lead bar holes; #943 found a repair dry run that would delete live Seagate (STX, 1,714 bars) and MiMedx (MDXG, 1,484 bars) because the rules read their filings wrong. Each fix began with one person reading a filing and deciding what it said. The orchestrator's inventory of the same shape of work: 845 pre-2024 price jumps on the real-run copy awaiting triage for `UNIVERSE__ACCEPTED_PRICE_JUMPS`; corporate actions Alpaca lacks (#473); the GDELT headline-entity matching rule, frozen as v2 on 2026-10-05 to cut v1's common-word false matches (Swift, Box) at a cost in coverage that #882's T3 gate (at least 50% of the top 1,000 companies matched over the week) measures from 2026-10-06 (#882 issue thread); ticker and name changes evidenced only in 8-K text (#869). A classifier that reads the filing and says "merger", "bankruptcy" or "compliance delisting" does not fix any of these. It turns a pile into a shortlist.

**What the research says** (TC, sources and grades as given there; nothing here is new evidence):
- Jev is a hosted typed decision model: a `Choice` call takes a state (the text) and a question with up to 255 enumerated options and returns the selected option, a probability over the options and a separate confidence, **and nothing else**: no span, no explanation, no free text (TC §4; TC §9 lists "limited evidence generation" among the class's limits). The published independent benchmark ran pinned `jev-1.13.0` on 37 datasets, 346,009 requests, "reportedly costing under $10"; on Financial PhraseBank (n = 970) accuracy was 0.730 with a 95% interval of [0.699, 0.756] and an expected calibration error of 0.138 (TC §3). Financial-domain calibration is therefore a measured concern, not a hypothetical one.
- The vendor's confidence is **not** the selected class probability: for K options it is (p_max − 1/K)/(1 − 1/K), so "confidence ≥ 0.90" is underspecified. Both quantities should be stored; calibration is evaluated on the probability vector (TC §5).
- List price $0.042 per million input tokens, output free; a 64k-token request budget; moving aliases resolve to versioned ids that the response reports; no fine-tuning (TC §4). The vendor's own cookbook classifies 60 SEC business sections into SIC groups at 39/60 forced, 27/30 in the confident subset, with a sample filtered to support the label (TC §6): an integration example, not validation.
- TC's architecture hypothesis is a cascade with an **explicit `unresolved` outcome** (TC §1, §17), and its evaluation rules are the ones adopted below: pinned version, frozen options, raw responses kept, the observed model id and billed cost recorded, a prespecified routing statistic, perturbation and drift probes (TC §4, §16, §18). TC did not verify vendor billing, made no API call and measured nothing on our data (TC §2); the vendor's terms are read in point 6.
- The only published typed-model result on financial text is one unreplicated preprint ([claims register](../research/claims.toml), topic 9b); whether typed labels add anything to deterministic features is INSUFFICIENT (ER-10, ER-14 in the [hypothesis backlog](../research/hypothesis-backlog.md) B8).

**What is already fixed and this ADR cannot loosen:**
- Charter principle 1 (any edge comes from strategy, data or discipline, not from using an LLM) and principle 3 with process rules 4 and 5: code computes numbers; model output never reaches order placement without deterministic risk rules; secrets live only in `.env`, which agents may not read, and are never logged or sent to a model.
- ADR 0008 points 2 and 3 and option 4, as quoted above. Point 3 is **the** path from a model label to the store or config, and this ADR adds no other.
- [ADR 0009](0009-price-vendor.md) point 1: spend by the running system is $0 per month from Phase 3 until a later ADR changes it; [ADR 0005](0005-objective-benchmark-stop-criteria.md) scopes its spend stop criterion to the running system, has the owner record each month's spend, and has him record build and review hours in each retro.
- [ADR 0003](0003-data-adapters-local-first.md) rule 3: the security master is ours and is the single place that answers "what existed on date T"; it is populated from EDGAR and adapter metadata by code.
- Owner tasks gate the edge ([development-process.md](../ways-of-working/development-process.md) rule 4: keys, the real store); research-registry spec "Users & usage": only the owner runs anything against the real store, agents run on fixture or temp-file stores and every such run is synthetic. Research-registry req 13: nothing under `scripts/` imports `tradepartner.research`; `tradepartner.research` imports no adapter; `tradepartner.research` and `tradepartner.execution` never import each other.
- Research reports are frozen once merged ([docs/README.md](../README.md)); this ADR answers the brief's open question 1 rather than editing it.
- Ingest does not fetch Form 25 primary documents (`edgar_source.py`, T11f) and the event-data collector fetches no 8-K primary document in v1 ([event-data spec](../specs/event-data.md) req 2 (a)). The filing **text** every task below reads is not in the store today; getting it is a corpus build, named where it matters (points 3 and 5, question 3).

The subject is pretrained classification models used **by a research job the owner runs**, on public text, with outputs a human reviews. The Claude Code build agents are out of scope, as in ADR 0008; their rule (point 3 there) is unchanged and is restated in point 4 below because it is the hinge of this design.

## Options considered

1. **Keep ADR 0008 as written: no pretrained model anywhere, in any form, until Phase 5.** Pro: nothing to build, no vendor, no spend, no terms to read; the one boundary already written stays the only one. Con: the brief's arms 5 to 9 never run, so B8 and the typed-classifier question stay INSUFFICIENT for want of our own data; the data-refinement work the owner named stays one filing read at a time by one person; a cheap, bounded, human-reviewed use is refused by a rule written against an unbounded, automated one (a memo or a veto on the portfolio), which is not the risk here.
2. **Read a typed classifier as "not an LLM" and let it extract into the store.** This is the vendor's own cookbook use and the most convenient: a `delisting_reason` column filled by the model. Rejected by ADR 0008 option 4 and charter principle 3 without needing this ADR: whatever the model is called, its output would be a fact the universe, the gap metric and the backtest consume. The security master's "what existed on date T" would then rest on a 73%-accurate, uncalibrated reader with no `known_at` of its own. Listed so the rejection is on record.
3. **A research-measurement boundary with a human decision in the loop** (this ADR). A model labels a passage of public text that code selected with one option from a set we fix, plus a probability vector. The labels live in the research store and nowhere else. A job compares them with what our rules say and produces a shortlist of disagreements. The owner reads the filing behind each item and decides; his decision, not the label, is the source of any fix, which enters the store or config as a reviewed change exactly as today. Every task is first a registered experiment with pre-registered bars scored against labels the owner made blind. Pro: the owner's intent, met; the first question (is this worth his time) is answered by the pilot against blind gold labels and then reported on every batch he reviews; the store stays what ADR 0003 made it; ADR 0008's isolation becomes a test again rather than a reading. Con: spend above $0 and a vendor account; a new dependency group and a module that talks to the network; terms to read; a corpus of filing text to build; a second place (the research store) whose contents must never be mistaken for facts; the vendor sees public filing text we selected, which reveals what we are looking at, not what we hold.
4. **Local open-weight models only, same boundary.** Pro: nothing leaves the laptop; no vendor terms, no per-call spend; a chronologically consistent model is possible later. Con: zero-shot classification over an option set is exactly what a typed hosted model does well and a local encoder does not without training labels, which the pilot is what produces; a local model still needs the same boundary, registration, pinning and drift probes, so it saves the terms line and little else. Treated below as a model class under option 3, not a separate path, and put to the owner (question 5).

## Decision

We will take option 3. A pretrained classification model may be used only as described in points 1 to 9; anything else is a new ADR (point 10).

1. **Which models.** This ADR covers **classification models whose output is one option from a set we enumerate, with a probability over that set**: hosted typed classifiers such as Jev, local pretrained encoders with a classification head, and classical classifiers over pretrained embeddings. They are not the "LLM" of ADR 0008 when used this way: the model returns no free text, writes no memo, and cannot produce a number, a date, a sentence or a span. **Generative models (any model that returns free text, hosted or local) stay under ADR 0008** in every role, including as a classification arm in a benchmark; TC §9's recommendation to compare against a structured-output generative model therefore waits for an amendment of this ADR or for Phase 5, and the comparison that can run now is against deterministic rules, classical classifiers and local encoders. Whether the owner reads ADR 0008's "LLM" this way is **owner question 1**; this point is written on the recommended answer (b), and answer (d) there turns the reading into an explicit amendment with the same effect.

2. **Which tasks: categorical factual labels over a code-selected passage of public text.** In scope is a label from a closed option set we fix, over a **passage** (a filing, an item of one, an exhibit or a headline) that **code selects** from a registered research corpus, with `unresolved` as an option in every set. The model returns the option and the probability vector; the passage it was asked about is the evidence the shortlist shows, and it is ours, not the model's. **Out of scope, whatever the model:** sentiment or tone in any form; return prediction; ranking or scoring of names; any label consumed by the universe, signal, backtest, paper or risk code or by a hypothesis file; any label shown on a decision or operations page; and the **extraction of numbers, dates, ratios or identifiers**, which code parses or the owner reads from the filing, never a model. **Research use of a label beyond the human path:** a model label may be the *treatment* in a registered `economic` or `return` research run (brief §2 row 2, stages 5 and 6, under the brief's stage gates and the registry's rules), because that is a measurement of the label, not a use of it; it may never be a feature in a backtest trial, a ranking, paper or live (brief §2 row 4: a charter amendment and its own ADR; point 10 here). Every task has one of two **shapes**, fixed at registration: **comparable**, where a rule of ours already answers the question and the registration declares a **crosswalk** from the rule's output onto a partition of the option set, so that "disagreement" is defined on the partition; or **proposal**, where no rule answers and the model's label is a proposal the owner confirms against a blind gold sample. 3. **The boundary: where labels live, what the job reads, who reads the labels, and the tests that hold it.**
   - **Where.** Every model output (selected option, probability vector, vendor confidence, raw request and response) is an **inference record in the research store** (`research.data_dir`, a config key the labeling spec adds, default `data/research/`, the gitignored content-addressed directory #810 chose), written append-only (prospective spec §7) and registered as a dataset version under research-registry req 11. **No model-produced value is ever written to** `store.path` or `events.path` (any table in `TABLE_NAMES`, `JOURNAL_TABLE_NAMES`, `MASTER_CHECK_TABLE_NAMES`, `REGISTRY_TABLE_NAMES` or the spec's `RESEARCH_TABLE_NAMES`), to config, to `.env`, or to a file under `docs/`. The registry rows a labeling run writes are the ordinary run and result rows of the research-registry spec, which hold metrics and ids, never a label or a passage (req 13).
   - **What the job reads.** Packets are built from a **registered research corpus dataset**: filing or headline text exported by a reviewed corpus-build script (the corpus spec the brief's B5 names; for task A, Form 25 and 8-K primary documents fetched through the existing EDGAR raw client at its paced rate into `collect.raw_dir` or `edgar.cache_dir` and exported from there), registered under req 11 with its event span. The labeling job **never fetches at labeling time** and never imports an adapter (req 13); it reads the corpus through `research.load_dataset` like any other research input, and the runtime store only through the as-of API for the rule's answer in a comparable task.
   - **Who reads the labels.** Only `tradepartner.research` (the labeling job, the comparison that builds the shortlist, the scoring against gold labels, the drift probe) and the **review command** (`tradepartner research review <run>`, in `cli`, which calls into `tradepartner.research` and prints or pages the shortlist with each item's passage, the answers and the filing's accession or URL). The dashboard's research view shows registry rows (metrics, counts, costs) and never a label, a passage or a document. No page or command offers an "apply" action: there is nothing to apply to, because the fix is a PR (point 4).
   - **Enforcement, extending ADR 0008 point 2 and research-registry req 13.** ADR 0008 point 2's tests are not yet in `tests/` (only `tests/test_ready_pr.py` names the spec path); **the labeling plan's first task writes them**, keyed on `docs/specs/llm-analyst.md` as ADR 0008 says, together with the cases below, keyed on the labeling spec at **`docs/specs/research-labeling.md`**. Each is an AST or text test in the suite, pinned by a fixture that fails it:
     (a) **Dependency test.** `project.dependencies` in `pyproject.toml` contains no model-client package; the test holds the list and the labeling spec names it. A model client may appear only in a dependency group named `research` (`[dependency-groups]`, installed on request, never by the default `uv sync`), and only while `docs/specs/research-labeling.md` exists with status Accepted; the check keys on the spec, as ADR 0008's does, so creating the group cannot switch it off.
     (b) **Import scan.** (i) **No module under `src/tradepartner/` outside `tradepartner.research` and `tradepartner.cli` imports `tradepartner.research`**; the dashboard in particular never does (`backtest.results` and `store.registry` read `store.research`, not `research`, per req 13). (ii) The model client, and `tradepartner.research.models` (the one module that may construct one), are imported by nothing outside `tradepartner.research`. (iii) `tradepartner.research` imports no adapter and no store writer (`store.db.open_for_write` or any ingest, repair, master or collector writer), its registry writes going through `store.research` only. (iv) Req 13's mutual ban between `tradepartner.research` and `tradepartner.execution` holds, and nothing under `scripts/` imports either.
     (c) **Text scan.** The vendor's API host names and the key's environment-variable name appear in source only in `tradepartner.research.models`, `config.py` and `.env.example`, so a client written as a plain `httpx` call (already a runtime dependency) is caught where (a) and (b) would not see it. The packet-builder module or modules the spec names contain no table name from `TABLE_NAMES`, `JOURNAL_TABLE_NAMES`, `MASTER_CHECK_TABLE_NAMES`, `REGISTRY_TABLE_NAMES` or `RESEARCH_TABLE_NAMES` except an allowlist fixed in the test (expected empty, since packets come from the corpus dataset), and no name of a gold-label or review dataset. `research.data_dir` is resolved in `tradepartner.research` only (as `DatasetRecord.path` is read only in `research.load_dataset`); no module outside it names the research store's directory or its file names.
     (d) **Store-unchanged test.** A labeling run and a completed review, executed on a fixture store, leave every table outside `RESEARCH_TABLE_NAMES` byte-identical (row counts and content hashes before and after), the events file untouched, and no file under the repository changed.
     (e) **Fake client and the zero default.** Tests run against a recorded-response fake whose recordings are made by an **owner task** (the key is his) and stored as test fixtures loaded only by tests (ADR 0008 point 3, last sentence). The real client is constructed only when `research.spend_ceiling_usd_month > 0` and the key is present; the default is `0.0` in code, a non-zero value comes **only from the owner's `.env`** (`RESEARCH__SPEND_CEILING_USD_MONTH`), and with the default the module cannot make a network call, which a test asserts. A PR that changes the `0.0` default or that test is labelled `hold` (git-workflow rule 7, class B), so it lands only on the owner's word.
   The labeling plan may swap the scans for import-linter contracts through ADR 0004's process, as ADR 0008 allows.

4. **The human path: a label becomes a shortlist, a human decision becomes a fix, and the model never writes a fact.** For a **comparable** task the job joins the model's label to the rule's answer through the registered crosswalk and emits a **shortlist**: every disagreement on the partition, every `unresolved`, and a **random sample of the agreements** (size set at registration), because a shortlist of disagreements alone cannot see the cases where the rule and the model are wrong together. For a **proposal** task the shortlist is every proposal, grouped by option. The review command shows each item's passage and, for a comparable task, the two answers **unattributed** (which is the rule's and which the model's is not shown; the job attributes after the decision is saved), so the owner is not anchored on the model (protocol §6). He reads the filing and records one of `a` or `b` (one answer is right; attributed afterwards as `rule` or `model`), `both_wrong`, or `unresolved`, with a reason and the passage of the filing he relied on. **`unresolved` is a first-class outcome**: it means the owner, having read the filing, finds it does not state the fact; it is never defaulted to either side, it is counted on the run's result, and its share is a registered metric (TC §17's cascade ends in a human, and the human may also say "I cannot tell"). The review record is appended to a **review file in the research store**, content-addressed and registered as a dataset version with its `known_at`, by the review command and by nothing else (owner question 4 asks whether it should also be an `owner_decisions` row). **A fix enters the system only as a reviewed code or config change** (a resolver rule with a pinned test as #921 did, a corporate-action row, an exception list such as `UNIVERSE__ACCEPTED_PRICE_JUMPS`), whose PR cites the review file's hash, the filing's accession and the owner's decision as the value's source; the stored fact carries the **filing's `known_at`** (its acceptance time), as every store fact does today, never the review time, and earlier runs see the revision as they see any store change (`store changed during run`). This is ADR 0008 point 3 applied: the source is a human decision reading a record with a `known_at`. The model's label is evidence that was looked at, never the source, and a PR that cites a label as its source is out of scope by this ADR. **Every labeling batch and its review, after the pilot as during it, is one registered run** (ADR 0005 "no id, no run"; an untracked one is the charter's stop criterion): kind `benchmark`, the system being the pinned model configuration and the outcome the owner's review decisions, which are human labels; split `prospective` for filings after the configuration freeze, `pilot` or `full` otherwise; bound to the batch's inference-record dataset; counted against the slug's `budget.runs`. If the registry's pinned kind set proves not to fit a production batch, the research-registry spec is amended with a kind for it **before** the first post-pilot batch, never after.

5. **Evaluation before any use.** No task produces a shortlist for the owner to act on before it has passed a pilot run as a **registered experiment** under the research-registry spec, and no model call is made before the run's `RunHandle` exists (spec req 3; by construction). The registration fixes, before the first call:
   - **Gold labels, made blind and first**: a random sample from the task's sampling frame (for task A the whole population of Form 25 filings since 2016 is small enough to sample from directly), labelled by the owner **before any model output exists**, with the passage he relied on, on the annotation protocol's principles (§4 sampling, §6 blinding, §7 record). For factual tasks the filing adjudicates; where two readers are wanted the protocol's agreement run applies. **Owner time**: the brief's power arithmetic (§3.1, §5) puts the gold sample at about 300 items per task before design effects; at two to four minutes a filing that is **10 to 20 owner-hours per pilot**, before the shortlist review, recorded as review hours in the retro under ADR 0005's time criterion.
   - **Gating bars**, computed **against the blind gold labels on the bound split**, never on the shortlist review: per-option precision and recall with a one-sided lower confidence bound (TC §15's form; the brief §3.1's example: 95 correct of 100 gives a lower bound of 0.898, which fails a 0.90 gate); **recall on a seeded set of known errors**, scored against the rule's outputs **at each fix's parent `code_version`** (the filings behind #921, #943, #905 and #893 for task A, the pre-fix rule function or a frozen pre-fix store snapshot recorded in the registration), all of which must be flagged; and the `unresolved` share at or below a registered maximum. **Reported, not gating:** calibration on the **probability vector** with frozen binning (ECE, Brier, class-wise reliability), never on the vendor confidence (TC §5, §16); and the **shortlist yield**, the share of reviewed shortlist items the owner resolved as `model` (the rule was wrong) or `both_wrong`, reported per batch as the running answer to "is this worth his time". A routing threshold, if any, names which statistic it uses and is frozen with the option set.
   - **Pinned configuration**: the versioned model id requested (never a moving alias), the option set and question text as a versioned object with a hash (prospective spec §3), the passage-selection rule and the packet builder's version, the crosswalk, the seed. Frozen before the bound split is scored.
   - **Per-call log**, as inference records (point 3): requested and returned model id, SDK version, option-set version and hash, document id and the SHA-256 of the exact passage sent, full raw request and response, selected option, probability vector, vendor confidence stored separately, token usage, billed or estimated cost, UTC timestamp, run id. The returned id catches re-routes the vendor reports; the drift probe is the only check for silent ones.
   - **Drift probe**: a fixed probe set with gold labels, re-sent at the start of every later run as a `robustness` run; a flip rate or probability shift above the registered tolerance stops the batch before it starts.
   - **Honesty about provenance and splits**: a benchmark on filings that predate the configuration freeze has provenance `model_historical`, and the registry **refuses `confirmatory` for it and refuses a `test` split on an exploratory registration** (req 2, brief §7). Every pilot on past filings is therefore exploratory by construction; it binds a **sealed non-`test` split** (`pilot`, sealed by period under req 11, drawn under the registered seed), its bars pre-registered and applied all the same, and its verdict is recorded as a `TP-` claim labelled exploratory. A confirmatory record for a task comes only from labels on filings that arrive after the freeze (`model_prospective`, split `prospective`), which the owner's hand-run forward batches accrue (point 8). For a review aid this is acceptable: the thing being decided is whether the shortlist is worth the owner's time, which the gold-label bars decide once and the yield reports thereafter.
   A task whose pilot misses its bars is recorded as a negative result and stays closed to use; the option set may be narrowed and a new registration opened, never the bars after the fact.

6. **What is sent to a vendor, and the terms.** The packet a hosted model receives contains only: the code-selected passage of a public SEC filing or exhibit, or a headline's metadata already collected under its own terms, identified by accession or URL, plus the question and the option set. **Never:** positions, orders, fills, decisions, overrides, hypotheses, trials, journal rows, config values, any Alpaca bar or series derived from one (Alpaca's terms forbid redistribution, and a price is a number the model has no business with), the owner's gold labels or review decisions, or anything from `.env`. Test (c) in point 3 holds the packet builder to this by keying on the store's table-name constants and the gold and review dataset names, and the corpus-dataset rule in point 3 means the builder has no other source to draw from. Filing text is untrusted input: the option set is ours and the document cannot add to it, so prompt injection can at worst mislabel one passage, which a human then reads. `safety-reviewer` runs on every PR touching `tradepartner.research.models` (LLM inputs and secrets, CLAUDE.md). The key lives in `.env` under the name the spec gives (added to `.env.example`), is loaded through `config.Settings` as a `SecretStr`, is accessed only by that module, is added to the redaction set `cli_record` uses, and is never logged. **Before the first real call**, a terms line for the vendor is added to the terms report (the [free-data terms report](../research/2026-09-25-free-data-terms.md) or a paid-vendor sibling), recording whether the vendor retains or trains on inputs by default and how to opt out, who owns the outputs, whether personal non-commercial use fits the plan, and how data is deleted; a vendor that trains on inputs without an opt-out is not used.

7. **Cost: a research spend ceiling in config, set by the owner.** `research.spend_ceiling_usd_month` (default `0.0`, at which no real client exists; non-zero only from the owner's `.env`, point 3 (e)) caps model spend by research jobs per calendar month. Before a batch the job estimates its input tokens from the packets and refuses the batch when the month's recorded spend plus the estimate exceeds the ceiling; it records actual billed cost per call where the API reports it and the list-price estimate otherwise; month-to-date spend is computed from the inference records, never configured. The owner records the month's research spend as a row beside the running system's, so ADR 0005's spend check stays one query. This ceiling is **research spend by jobs the owner starts by hand**; ADR 0009's $0 for the running system is unchanged, and the running system (ingest, paper, dashboard, alerts) makes no model call. **Order of magnitude at Jev's list price** (TC §4; vendor billing unverified), per task, not in general: task A's whole population (every Form 25 since 2016 with its surrounding 8-K, roughly 5k to 10k tokens each, a few thousand filings) is a few dollars; task C's 845 jumps at about 20k tokens each are about 17M tokens, about $0.71; but tasks G and J over **every** 8-K or 10-K and 10-Q since 2016 run to billions of tokens, hundreds of dollars, and a whole 10-K exceeds the 64k request budget, so those tasks pilot on registered samples of code-selected sections and never on the full corpus until a per-task estimate is registered. A ceiling of $10 a month covers the pilots **as sampled**; what it really caps is a repricing, a mistake in a batch loop or a later generative fallback. The number is **owner question 2**; the charter's Budget line gains one sentence when he sets it (point 9).

8. **Who runs what.** Only the owner runs a labeling job against the real corpus and a real vendor (development-process rule 4; the key is his), and only he records the fake's responses (point 3 (e)). Agents build and test against fixtures and the recorded-response fake, and a run on any store other than the real one is `synthetic` under the registry's rule. Review is a CLI first; a dashboard page is built only if the owner asks for one. Jobs are started by hand (`tradepartner research label <registration> ...`): nothing in this ADR schedules a model call, so the prospective record (point 5) accrues at the pace of the owner's batches; a scheduled capture job is a revisit item (Consequences), because it would make the running system a spender.

## Consequences

- Good: the owner's intent is met in the form that keeps every existing rule intact: the store, the events file and config are written by code and by reviewed human decisions, exactly as before; the model's whole authority is to shorten a reading list. The gating question for a solo owner, whether the shortlist is worth his time, is decided once against labels he made blind and then reported on every batch as yield. The typed-classifier and B8 questions start accruing our own evidence inside the registry that already exists, with provenance labelled honestly. The isolation is tests, not a reviewer's memory, and they extend the two ADR 0008 already calls for rather than inventing a parallel regime. The cost is small enough to be a config value the owner alone can raise.
- Bad / accepted risks: a vendor sees which filings we read, which reveals interest, not holdings; accepted because the text is public and the packet carries nothing else. A research store full of plausible labels is a standing temptation to join it to the master "just for a report"; tests (b) to (d) exist for that temptation, and the dashboard shows no label on purpose. Every benchmark on past filings is exploratory forever (req 2), so a confirmatory record comes slowly, at the pace of the owner's forward batches. The gold sample costs the owner 10 to 20 hours per task before any shortlist, and reviewing shortlists is his time too: if a pilot's bars are missed, the honest outcome is a negative result and a closed task. Every task needs a corpus of filing text that does not exist yet, so the first pilot starts with a fetch, not a model call. The vendor can reprice, retire or re-route the model; the pinned id, the raw responses and the drift probe make that visible, not impossible. A $0 default means nothing runs until the owner sets a number in his own `.env`, which is intended.
- Reversibility: cheap. Deleting `tradepartner.research.models`, the dependency group and the research store's inference records removes everything this ADR added, because nothing depends on them; fixes that entered through point 4 stand on their own as reviewed changes citing filings, and survive the deletion unchanged. Costly only if point 10 is ever crossed, which is why it needs a new ADR.
- Revisit if: the first pilot's gating bars are missed (then narrow the option set or close the task, never lower the bars); the owner wants a scheduled capture job (an amendment naming where its spend counts, and whether ADR 0009's running-system budget moves); a generative comparison arm is wanted (owner question 1 (c)); the #618 taxonomy passes its agreement gate and the owner wants model arms on it; the vendor's terms change on retention or training; independent evidence appears on typed classifiers for financial filings beyond the one preprint; Phase 5 is entered (then the two boundaries, memo and label, are reconciled in the Phase 5 spec, each keeping its own tables and tests); or the owner's budget or objective changes materially (ADR 0005 "revisit if").
