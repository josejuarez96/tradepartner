# 0013. Research-measurement boundary: pretrained classifiers may label facts for human review, never write them

**Status:** Proposed  ·  **Date:** 2026-10-05  ·  **Issue:** #946

*ADR number 0012 is reserved by strategy-lab plan task T92 (cadence as a hypothesis parameter, #939); this record is 0013.*

## Context

[ADR 0008](0008-llm-role.md) decided the LLM's role: none in the runtime until Phase 5, advisory at most, never a number and never an order. Three of its rules bear on this record. Point 1 forbids any LLM client or SDK among the runtime dependencies before the Phase 5 spec is accepted, and makes a PR that adds one out of scope. Option 4 rejects, "for the record", an LLM as an **extractor** that pulls facts out of filings into the store, because that makes a model-produced value an input to a decision. Point 3 says the one way a value an LLM produced may enter the store or config: a reviewed code or config change whose PR states the value's source, and only if that source is a human decision or a record with a `known_at`.

The 2026-10-03 research program then proposed experiments that those rules bar. The [ML validation brief](../research/2026-10-03-ml-validation-methodology-brief.md#governance-gates) §2 lists them: any pretrained language model in an experiment, "even offline", is not allowed today, and what unlocks it is "a new ADR defining a research-measurement boundary: a segregated research environment, model outputs that can never reach the store, decision tables or config, and the ADR 0008 point 2 style import/table tests. Plus a research spend ceiling above $0." Its open question 1 asks whether ADR 0008 covers non-generative hosted models and local pretrained encoders at all; the brief assumed yes until the owner says otherwise. The [prospective feature spec](../research/2026-10-03-prospective-semantic-feature-spec.md) §2 names this ADR as precondition 1 of any model capture and says what it must state: model calls happen only in a segregated research job; outputs go only to a research store; no code path reads them into the runtime store, the decision, order or fill tables, config or the risk-gated wrapper; and tests enforce it. The [research-registry spec](../specs/research-registry.md), accepted 2026-10-05 (#931), built the registry those experiments need and drew its own line (req 13): the registry **records** runs and authorizes nothing; it stores no model output and no filing text; research data (label exports, inference records, raw responses) lives outside the runtime store in a content-addressed `data/research/` directory (#810), and the registry points at it by hash. Its Definitions already carry the provenances `model_historical` and `model_prospective`, waiting for this ADR.

**The owner's intent** (Jose, 2026-10-05, in chat to the orchestrator) is narrower than the brief's arms 5 to 9 and different in kind. He does not want a model for sentiment, trade signals or anything in the strategy or decision path. He wants to test the Jev typed classification model (vendor TypeSafe; [typed-classifiers research](../research/2026-10-03-typed-classifiers-financial-disclosures-research.md), cited below as **TC**) for **factual labeling that refines our data, with a human reviewing**: a second opinion on the security master (read a Form 25 or an 8-K12B and give a verdict to compare with our rules, then review a shortlist of contradictions); security types the rules cannot place; planned versus discretionary insider trades before the 2023 Form 4 checkbox; 8-K item subtypes (a 5.02 that is a CEO leaving, a CFO leaving or a new director); event types on collected GDELT headlines (earnings, lawsuit, recall, M&A, management change). He accepts that this needs ADR amendments and fits after a successful backfill and the other tests.

**Why the pain is real.** The security master's rules are patched by hand, one filing at a time: #921 pinned Valaris's `VAL*` footnote row after a Form 25; #905 fixed a cover page after a stopped line keeping a delisted span alive; #893 and #918 filled rename-lead bar holes; #943 found a repair dry run that would delete live Seagate (STX, 1,714 bars) and MiMedx (MDXG, 1,484 bars) because the rules read their filings wrong. Each fix began with one person reading a filing and deciding what it said. The orchestrator's inventory of the same shape of work: 845 pre-2024 price jumps on the real-run copy awaiting triage for `UNIVERSE__ACCEPTED_PRICE_JUMPS`; corporate actions Alpaca lacks (#473); a headline-entity rule whose v2 lost coverage to keep precision (#882 T3); ticker and name changes evidenced only in 8-K text (#869). A classifier that reads the filing and says "merger", "bankruptcy" or "compliance delisting" with an evidence span does not fix any of these. It turns a pile into a shortlist.

**What the research says** (TC, sources and grades as given there; nothing here is new evidence):
- Jev is a hosted typed decision model: a `Choice` call takes a state (the text) and a question with up to 255 enumerated options and returns the selected option, a probability over the options and a separate confidence (TC §4). The published independent benchmark ran pinned `jev-1.13.0` on 37 datasets, 346,009 requests, "reportedly costing under $10"; on Financial PhraseBank (n = 970) accuracy was 0.730 with a 95% interval of [0.699, 0.756] and an expected calibration error of 0.138 (TC §3). Financial-domain calibration is therefore a measured concern, not a hypothetical one.
- The vendor's confidence is **not** the selected class probability: for K options it is (p_max − 1/K)/(1 − 1/K), so "confidence ≥ 0.90" is underspecified. Both quantities should be stored; calibration is evaluated on the probability vector (TC §5).
- List price $0.042 per million input tokens, output free; a 64k-token request budget; moving aliases resolve to versioned ids that the response reports; no fine-tuning (TC §4). The vendor's own cookbook classifies 60 SEC business sections into SIC groups at 39/60 forced, 27/30 in the confident subset, with a sample filtered to support the label (TC §6): an integration example, not validation.
- TC's architecture hypothesis is a cascade with an **explicit `unresolved` outcome** (TC §1, §17), and its evaluation rules are the ones adopted below: pinned version, frozen options, raw responses kept, the observed model id and billed cost recorded, a prespecified routing statistic, perturbation and drift probes (TC §4, §16, §18). TC did not verify vendor billing or terms, made no API call and measured nothing on our data (TC §2).
- The only published typed-model result on financial text is one unreplicated preprint ([claims register](../research/claims.toml), topic 9b); whether typed labels add anything to deterministic features is INSUFFICIENT (ER-10, ER-14 in the [hypothesis backlog](../research/hypothesis-backlog.md) B8).

**What is already fixed and this ADR cannot loosen:**
- Charter principle 1 (any edge comes from strategy, data or discipline, not from using an LLM) and principle 3 with process rules 4 and 5: code computes numbers; model output never reaches order placement without deterministic risk rules; secrets live only in `.env`, which agents may not read, and are never logged or sent to a model.
- ADR 0008 points 2 and 3 and option 4, as quoted above. Point 3 is **the** path from a model label to the store or config, and this ADR adds no other.
- [ADR 0009](0009-price-vendor.md) point 1: spend by the running system is $0 per month from Phase 3 until a later ADR changes it; [ADR 0005](0005-objective-benchmark-stop-criteria.md) scopes its spend stop criterion to the running system and has the owner record each month's spend.
- [ADR 0003](0003-data-adapters-local-first.md) rule 3: the security master is ours and is the single place that answers "what existed on date T"; it is populated from EDGAR and adapter metadata by code.
- [teams.md](../ways-of-working/teams.md): agents run on fixtures; only the owner runs anything against the real store or the real data. Research-registry spec req 13: nothing under `scripts/` imports `tradepartner.research`; `tradepartner.research` and `tradepartner.execution` never import each other.
- Research reports are frozen once merged ([docs/README.md](../README.md)); this ADR answers the brief's open question 1 rather than editing it.

The subject is pretrained classification models used **by a research job the owner runs**, on public text, with outputs a human reviews. The Claude Code build agents are out of scope, as in ADR 0008; their rule (point 3 there) is unchanged and is restated in point 4 below because it is the hinge of this design.

## Options considered

1. **Keep ADR 0008 as written: no pretrained model anywhere, in any form, until Phase 5.** Pro: nothing to build, no vendor, no spend, no terms to read; the one boundary already written stays the only one. Con: the brief's arms 5 to 9 never run, so B8 and the typed-classifier question stay INSUFFICIENT for want of our own data; the data-refinement work the owner named stays one filing read at a time by one person; a cheap, bounded, human-reviewed use is refused by a rule written against an unbounded, automated one (a memo or a veto on the portfolio), which is not the risk here.
2. **Read a typed classifier as "not an LLM" and let it extract into the store.** This is the vendor's own cookbook use and the most convenient: a `delisting_reason` column filled by the model. Rejected by ADR 0008 option 4 and charter principle 3 without needing this ADR: whatever the model is called, its output would be a fact the universe, the gap metric and the backtest consume. The security master's "what existed on date T" would then rest on a 73%-accurate, uncalibrated reader with no `known_at` of its own. Listed so the rejection is on record.
3. **A research-measurement boundary with a human decision in the loop** (this ADR). A model labels public text with one option from a set we fix, plus an evidence span and a probability vector. The labels live in the research store and nowhere else. A job compares them with what our rules say and produces a shortlist of disagreements. The owner reads the filing behind each item and decides; his decision, not the label, is the source of any fix, which enters the store or config as a reviewed change exactly as today. Every task is first a registered experiment with pre-registered bars. Pro: the owner's intent, met; precision is measured directly on the shortlist he reviews, so the first question (is this worth his time) is answered by the pilot; the store stays what ADR 0003 made it; ADR 0008's isolation becomes a test again rather than a reading. Con: spend above $0 and a vendor account; a new dependency group and a module that talks to the network; terms to read; a second place (the research store) whose contents must never be mistaken for facts; the vendor sees public filing text we selected, which reveals what we are looking at, not what we hold.
4. **Local open-weight models only, same boundary.** Pro: nothing leaves the laptop; no vendor terms, no per-call spend; a chronologically consistent model is possible later. Con: zero-shot classification over an option set is exactly what a typed hosted model does well and a local encoder does not without training labels, which the pilot is what produces; a local model still needs the same boundary, registration, pinning and drift probes, so it saves the terms line and little else. Treated below as a model class under option 3, not a separate path, and put to the owner (question 5).

## Decision

We will take option 3. A pretrained classification model may be used only as described in points 1 to 9; anything else is a new ADR (point 10).

1. **Which models.** This ADR covers **classification models whose output is one option from a set we enumerate, with a probability over that set**: hosted typed classifiers such as Jev, local pretrained encoders with a classification head, and classical classifiers over pretrained embeddings. They are not the "LLM" of ADR 0008 when used this way: the model returns no free text, writes no memo, and cannot produce a number, a date or a sentence. **Generative models (any model that returns free text, hosted or local) stay under ADR 0008** in every role, including as a classification arm in a benchmark; TC §9's recommendation to compare against a structured-output generative model therefore waits for an amendment of this ADR or for Phase 5, and the comparison that can run now is against deterministic rules, classical classifiers and local encoders. Whether the owner reads ADR 0008's "LLM" this way is **owner question 1**; this point is written on the recommended answer.

2. **Which tasks: categorical factual labels over public text, with an evidence span.** In scope is a label from a closed option set we fix, over the text of a public filing, exhibit or headline, returned with the span the model's probability attaches to and with `unresolved` as an option in every set. **Out of scope, whatever the model:** sentiment or tone in any form; return prediction; ranking or scoring of names; any label consumed by the universe, signal, backtest, paper or risk code; any label shown on a decision or operations page; and the **extraction of numbers, dates, ratios or identifiers**, which code parses or the owner reads from the filing, never a model. The candidate tasks named so far, all of this shape, none authorized by this ADR alone (each is one registered experiment, point 5, taken in the order the owner sets):

   | # | Task | Text the model reads | Option set (always plus `unresolved`) | What code or the owner keeps | Feeds |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | A | Security-master second opinion: departure reason | Form 25 and the surrounding 8-K (items 3.01, 1.01, 2.01, 8.01), 8-K12B | merger or acquisition; going private; redomicile or reorganisation; bankruptcy; compliance delisting; voluntary delisting or exchange transfer; other | effective dates, successor ids; the resolver's status and `listing_ends_as_of` | #943 (Seagate, MiMedx), #921, #905, #893 |
   | B | Security type the rules cannot place | cover page and listing text | common; preferred; warrant; unit; depositary receipt; fund or ETF; debt; right; other | `class_title`, par value, exchange | unplaced listings in the master |

3. **The boundary: where labels live, who reads them, and the tests that hold it.**
   - **Where.** Every model output (selected option, probability vector, vendor confidence, evidence span, raw request and response) is an **inference record in the research store** (`research.data_dir`, default `data/research/`, the content-addressed directory of #810), written append-only (prospective spec §7) and registered as a dataset version under research-registry req 11. Nothing a model produced is ever written to `store.path`, to `events.path`, to any table in `TABLE_NAMES`, `REGISTRY_TABLE_NAMES` or `RESEARCH_TABLE_NAMES`, to config, to `.env`, or to a file under `docs/`. The registry rows a labeling run writes are the ordinary run and result rows of the research-registry spec, which hold metrics and ids, never a label or a span (req 13).
   - **Who reads.** Only `tradepartner.research` (the labeling job, the comparison that builds the shortlist, the scoring against gold labels, the drift probe) and the **review command** (`tradepartner research review <run>`, in `cli`, which calls into `tradepartner.research` and prints or pages the shortlist with each item's evidence span, probability vector and the filing's accession or URL). The dashboard's research view shows registry rows (metrics, counts, costs) and never a label, a span or a document. No page or command offers an "apply" action: there is nothing to apply to, because the fix is a PR (point 4).
   - **Enforcement, extending ADR 0008 point 2 and research-registry req 13**, each an AST or text test in the suite, pinned by a fixture that fails it:
     (a) **Dependency test.** `project.dependencies` in `pyproject.toml` contains no model-client package (the test holds the list: the vendor SDK the labeling spec names, and the generative SDKs ADR 0008 already lists). A model client may appear only in a dependency group named `research` (`[dependency-groups]`, installed on request, never by the default `uv sync`), and only while the accepted labeling spec exists at its recorded path; the check keys on the spec, as ADR 0008's does, so creating the group cannot switch it off.
     (b) **Import scan.** The model client, and `tradepartner.research.models` (the one module that may construct one), are imported by nothing outside `tradepartner.research`; `tradepartner.research.models` imports no store writer (`store.db.open_for_write` or any ingest, repair, master or collector writer) and no adapter; `tradepartner.research` and `tradepartner.execution` keep req 13's mutual ban; nothing under `scripts/` imports either.
     (c) **Path and name scan.** `research.data_dir` is resolved in `tradepartner.research` only (as `DatasetRecord.path` is read only in `research.load_dataset`); no module outside it names the research store's directory or its inference-record file names in source. The source of `tradepartner.research.models` names no table from the decision path (`decisions`, `orders`, `fills`, `overrides`, `positions_daily`, `signals`, `hypotheses`, `trials`, `trial_*`, `owner_decisions`): the packet it sends is built from the research corpus and the raw filing cache, not from the journal.
     (d) **Store-unchanged test.** A labeling run and a completed review, executed on a fixture store, leave every table outside `RESEARCH_TABLE_NAMES` byte-identical (row counts and content hashes before and after), the events file untouched, and no file under the repository changed.
     (e) **Fake client.** Tests run against a recorded-response fake. The real client is constructed only when `research.spend_ceiling_usd_month > 0` and the key is present; with the ceiling at its default of `0.0` the module cannot make a network call, and a test asserts that.
   The labeling plan may swap the scans for import-linter contracts through ADR 0004's process, as ADR 0008 allows.

4. **The human path: a label becomes a shortlist, a human decision becomes a fix, and the model never writes a fact.** For a task whose labels can be compared with ours (A to G, J, K), the job joins the model's label to the rule's answer for the same document and emits a **shortlist**: every disagreement, every document where the rule has no answer and the model has one, and every `unresolved`, each with the evidence span, the probability vector and the document id. It also draws a **random sample of the agreements** (size set at registration), because a shortlist of disagreements alone cannot see the cases where the rule and the model are wrong together. The owner reviews each item against the filing itself and records one of `rule` (ours is right), `model` (the model is right; a fix follows), `both_wrong`, or `unresolved` (the filing does not settle it), with a reason and the span he relied on. **`unresolved` is a first-class outcome**: it is never defaulted to either side, it is counted on the run's result, and its share is one of the registered metrics (TC §17's cascade ends in a human, and the human may also say "I cannot tell"). The review record is appended to a **review file in the research store**, content-addressed and registered as a dataset version with its `known_at`, by the review command and by nothing else (owner question 4 asks whether it should also be an `owner_decisions` row). **A fix enters the system only as a reviewed code or config change** (a resolver rule with a pinned test as #921 did, a corporate-action row, an exception list such as `UNIVERSE__ACCEPTED_PRICE_JUMPS`), whose PR cites the review file's hash, the filing's accession and the owner's decision as the value's source. This is ADR 0008 point 3 applied: the source is a human decision reading a record with a `known_at`. The model's label is evidence that was looked at, never the source, and a PR that cites a label as its source is out of scope by this ADR.

5. **Evaluation before any use.** No task produces a shortlist for the owner to act on before it has passed a pilot run as a **registered experiment** under the research-registry spec, and no model call is made before the run's `RunHandle` exists (spec req 3; ADR 0005 "no id, no run", by construction). The registration fixes, before the first call:
   - **Gold labels**: a random sample from the task's sampling frame, labelled by the owner blind to any model output, with the evidence span, on the annotation protocol's principles (§4 sampling, §6 blinding, §7 record). For factual tasks the filing adjudicates; where two readers are wanted the protocol's agreement run applies.
   - **Bars**, as values with the inference method named: precision of the shortlist (the share of flagged items the owner resolves as `model`, `both_wrong` or a true `unresolved`, that is, items worth his read) with a one-sided lower confidence bound (TC §15's form; the ML brief §5's example: 95 correct of 100 gives a lower bound of 0.898, which fails a 0.90 gate); recall on a **seeded set of known errors** (the filings behind #921, #943, #905 and #893 for task A), which must all be flagged; the `unresolved` share; and, as a report rather than a gate, calibration on the **probability vector** with frozen binning (ECE, Brier, class-wise reliability), never on the vendor confidence (TC §5, §16). A routing threshold, if any, names which statistic it uses and is frozen with the option set.
   - **Pinned configuration**: the versioned model id requested (never a moving alias), the option set and question text as a versioned object with a hash (prospective spec §3), the packet builder's version, the seed. Frozen before the sealed split is scored.
   - **Per-call log**, as inference records (point 3): requested and returned model id, SDK version, option-set version and hash, document id and the SHA-256 of the exact text sent, full raw request and response, selected option, probability vector, vendor confidence stored separately, token usage, billed or estimated cost, UTC timestamp, run id. The returned id catches re-routes the vendor reports; the drift probe is the only check for silent ones.
   - **Drift probe**: a fixed probe set with gold labels, re-sent at the start of every later run as a `robustness` run; a flip rate or probability shift above the registered tolerance stops the batch before it starts.
   - **Honesty about provenance**: a benchmark on filings that predate the configuration freeze has provenance `model_historical`, and the registry **refuses `confirmatory` for it** (req 7, brief §7). Every pilot on past filings is therefore exploratory by construction, its bars pre-registered and applied on a sealed split all the same, and its verdict recorded as a `TP-` claim labelled exploratory. A confirmatory record for a task comes only from labels on filings that arrive after the freeze (`model_prospective`), which the owner's hand-run forward batches accrue (point 8). For a review aid this is acceptable: the thing being decided is whether the shortlist is worth the owner's time, and he measures that directly every time he reviews one.
   A task whose pilot misses its bars is recorded as a negative result and stays closed to use; the taxonomy or the option set may be narrowed and a new registration opened, never the bars after the fact.

6. **What is sent to a vendor, and the terms.** The packet a hosted model receives contains only: text of public SEC filings and exhibits, public Federal Register documents, or headline metadata already collected under their own terms, identified by accession or URL, plus the question and the option set. **Never:** positions, orders, fills, decisions, overrides, hypotheses, trials, journal rows, config values, any Alpaca bar or series derived from one (Alpaca's terms forbid redistribution, and a price is a number the model has no business with), the owner's labels or review decisions, or anything from `.env`. Test (c) in point 3 holds the packet builder to this. Filing text is untrusted input: the option set is ours and the document cannot add to it, so prompt injection can at worst mislabel one document, which a human then reads. `safety-reviewer` runs on every PR touching `tradepartner.research.models` (LLM inputs and secrets, CLAUDE.md). The key lives in `.env` under the name the spec gives (added to `.env.example`), is read only by that module, and is never logged. **Before the first real call**, a terms line for the vendor is added to the terms report (the [free-data terms report](../research/2026-09-25-free-data-terms.md) or a paid-vendor sibling), recording whether the vendor retains or trains on inputs by default and how to opt out, who owns the outputs, whether personal non-commercial use fits the plan, and how data is deleted; a vendor that trains on inputs without an opt-out is not used.

7. **Cost: a research spend ceiling in config, set by the owner.** `research.spend_ceiling_usd_month` (default `0.0`, at which no real client exists) caps model spend by research jobs per calendar month. Before a batch the job estimates its input tokens from the packets and refuses the batch when the month's recorded spend plus the estimate exceeds the ceiling; it records actual billed cost per call where the API reports it and the list-price estimate otherwise; month-to-date spend is computed from the inference records, never configured. The owner records the month's research spend as a row beside the running system's, so ADR 0005's spend check stays one query. This ceiling is **research spend by jobs the owner starts by hand**; ADR 0009's $0 for the running system is unchanged, and the running system (ingest, paper, dashboard, alerts) makes no model call. **Order of magnitude at Jev's list price** (TC §4; vendor billing unverified): a Form 25 with its surrounding 8-K is roughly 5k to 10k tokens, so the whole security-master pass over every delisting since 2016 is a few dollars; 845 price jumps at about 20k tokens each are about 17M tokens, about $0.71. A ceiling of $10 a month covers every candidate task at list price; what it really caps is a repricing, a mistake in a batch loop or a later generative fallback. The number is **owner question 2**; the charter's Budget line gains one sentence when he sets it (point 9).

8. **Who runs what.** Only the owner runs a labeling job against the real corpus and a real vendor (teams.md; the key is his). Agents build and test against fixtures and the recorded-response fake, and a run on any store other than the real one is `synthetic` under the registry's rule. Review is a CLI first; a dashboard page is built only if the owner asks for one. Jobs are started by hand (`tradepartner research label <registration> ...`): nothing in this ADR schedules a model call, so the prospective record (point 5) accrues at the pace of the owner's batches; a scheduled capture job is a revisit item (Consequences), because it would make the running system a spender.

9. **Amendments and placement.** ADRs are append-only (docs/README.md), so ADR 0008 is not edited here. On acceptance, its header gains one dated line pointing at this record, and the following readings hold:
   - **ADR 0008 point 1**: "no LLM SDK among the runtime dependencies" means `project.dependencies`. A classification-model client for the models in point 1 of this ADR may live in the `research` dependency group, importable only in `tradepartner.research.models`, once the labeling spec is accepted (test (a)). The sentence "a PR that adds an LLM client or an LLM SDK dependency before Phase 5 is out of scope" keeps its full force for generative clients and for any client outside that group; `safety-reviewer` runs on either.
   - **ADR 0008 point 2**: its import and dependency tests gain the research cases of point 3 (a) to (e).
   - **ADR 0008 point 3**: unchanged, and named here as the only path from a label to the store or config.
   - **ADR 0008 option 4**: the extractor stays rejected: a model whose output enters the store or config, or whose output is a number or a date, is still that. A labeler under this boundary is not an extractor because nothing it produces enters the store. "Structured facts come from structured sources, parsed by code" stands.
   - **ADR 0008 points 4 to 9**: unchanged. Nothing here is an advisory memo, nothing is shown next to a decision, and Phase 5's entry conditions (point 7) are not touched; Phase 5 may never happen and this ADR still stands.
   - **ML validation brief §2**: rows 2 and 3 of its governance table ("any pretrained language model in an experiment"; "prospective capture") are unlocked by this ADR plus the labeling spec, for the models and tasks in points 1 and 2 and with capture by hand only; row 4 (a model-derived feature in a ranking, portfolio or paper run) still needs a charter amendment and its own ADR. Open question 1 is answered by point 1, pending owner question 1. Open question 2 (where research code, dependencies and outputs live) is answered by point 3 and #810.
   - **Charter, Budget line**: when the owner sets the ceiling, one sentence is added, as ADR 0009 amended principle 4: research labeling spend by owner-run jobs is capped by `research.spend_ceiling_usd_month` under this ADR; the running system's $0 is unchanged.
   - **Roadmap**, "Calendar-bound phases and what fills them": one item is added after the existing ones: the labeling spec and plan under this ADR, then the first pilot, **after T45b's real-store run has completed** (the backfill is done and the master has been exercised), beside Phase 4 and touching no Phase 4 code; **no phase's exit criteria depend on it**, and it does not shorten or lengthen Phase 4's six paper rebalances.

10. **What needs a new ADR, not an amendment.** Any model output reaching `store.path`, `events.path` or config by a path other than point 4; any model-derived feature in a universe, signal, ranking, backtest, paper or risk computation (charter principle 3; a charter amendment too); a generative model in any role (ADR 0008); a scheduled job that calls a model; a spend ceiling above what the owner sets in question 2 by more than the ADR 0005 retro records as a deliberate change. An amendment of this ADR, in a dated section as ADR 0010 did, covers: adding a task to the table in point 2 (if it is the same shape), adding a model class that returns an enumerated option with a probability, or changing a test's mechanism without changing what it forbids.

**What each option rejects, for the record.** Option 1 rejects spending a few dollars and a bounded vendor surface to turn hand-patched resolver work into a reviewed shortlist, and rejects ever answering the typed-classifier question with our own data. Option 2 is rejected by ADR 0008 and the charter and stays rejected. Option 3 rejects every form of authority for the model, including the implicit one of a pre-filled label in a review screen, and rejects treating a historical benchmark as confirmatory. Option 4 is not rejected; it is a model class under option 3 and the owner chooses the order (question 5).

## Consequences

- Good: the owner's intent is met in the form that keeps every existing rule intact: the store, the events file and config are written by code and by reviewed human decisions, exactly as before; the model's whole authority is to shorten a reading list. The pilot's primary metric is the one that matters to a solo owner, the precision of the shortlist he reads, and it is measured on every review. The typed-classifier and B8 questions start accruing our own evidence inside the registry that already exists, with provenance labelled honestly. The isolation is tests, not a reviewer's memory, and they extend the two ADR 0008 already has rather than inventing a parallel regime. The cost is small enough to be a config value.
- Bad / accepted risks: a vendor sees which filings we read, which reveals interest, not holdings; accepted because the text is public and the packet carries nothing else. A research store full of plausible labels is a standing temptation to join it to the master "just for a report"; tests (b) to (d) exist for that temptation, and the dashboard shows no label on purpose. Every benchmark on past filings is exploratory forever (req 7), so a confirmatory record comes slowly, at the pace of the owner's forward batches. Reviewing a shortlist is still the owner's time: if the pilot's precision is low, the honest outcome is a negative result and a closed task. The vendor can reprice, retire or re-route the model; the pinned id, the raw responses and the drift probe make that visible, not impossible. A $0 default means nothing runs until the owner sets a number, which is intended.
- Reversibility: cheap. Deleting `tradepartner.research.models`, the dependency group and the research store's inference records removes everything this ADR added, because nothing depends on them; fixes that entered through point 4 stand on their own as reviewed changes citing filings, and survive the deletion unchanged. Costly only if point 10 is ever crossed, which is why it needs a new ADR.
- Revisit if: the first pilot's precision bar is missed (then narrow the option set or close the task, never lower the bar); the owner wants a scheduled capture job (an amendment naming where its spend counts, and whether ADR 0009's running-system budget moves); a generative comparison arm is wanted (owner question 1 (c)); the vendor's terms change on retention or training; independent evidence appears on typed classifiers for financial filings beyond the one preprint; Phase 5 is entered (then the two boundaries, memo and label, are reconciled in the Phase 5 spec, each keeping its own tables and tests); or the owner's budget or objective changes materially (ADR 0005 "revisit if").
