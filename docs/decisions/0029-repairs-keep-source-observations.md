# 0029. Identity repairs keep source observations: retract, never delete; every release checks coverage; an independent answer key judges the resolver

**Status:** Accepted (on merge; the owner approved the direction on #3275 on 2026-11-11 and left the technical questions to the orchestrator, who decided them the same day)  ·  **Date:** 2026-11-11  ·  **Issue:** #3275  ·  **Related:** [#3264](https://github.com/josejuarez96/tradepartner/issues/3264) (the diagnosis), [#3265](https://github.com/josejuarez96/tradepartner/issues/3265) (orphan guard), [#3269](https://github.com/josejuarez96/tradepartner/issues/3269) (line withdrawal, T388), [#3274](https://github.com/josejuarez96/tradepartner/issues/3274) (release coverage check, size S), [#3250](https://github.com/josejuarez96/tradepartner/pull/3250) (T387), [ADR 0022](0022-no-vendor-security-master.md) (no vendor; the free answer key), [ADR 0023](0023-break-first-fast-books-paper-exam.md), [ADR 0028](0028-one-ingest-pipeline.md), [data-foundation plan](../plans/data-foundation.md) amendment 2026-11-11 (#3275)

## Context

In plain words: the store decides which security owns a price bar by reading its own listing and delisting rows. A repair then deletes the bars that decision says are on the wrong security. If the decision is wrong, the deletion is the only copy of the loss, and the same wrong decision judged it.

**What happened (#3264, `~/tradepartner-probes/sitting-inputs/apa-hole-3264.md`).** On the sitting rehearsal copy, step 9's EDGAR write added two June 2020 Form 25s for Apache (CIK 0000006769). They withdrew its NYSE and NYSE Chicago lines while the Nasdaq line stayed. The resolver read them as a delisting and ended APA's span on 2020-06-17. `repair-resolution` then deleted every bar outside the span: **101,698 bar keys on 177 securities, of which 26,889 keys on 81 securities have no id that receives them.** APA (2020-06-18 to 2021-03-12) and MAR (1,541 sessions, 2020-09-21 to today) are the two largest; BATRA, BATRK, LLYVA, LLYVK, DDS, TCBI, IBOC, CECO, CAE and others are also in the list. Nothing in the run noticed: its counts (`--expect-bar-rows`) prove the size of the repair, not that it is right.

**The circularity.** #3250 (T387) made a bar known at the time of a Form 25 evidence that the security kept trading. That is a sound use of bars. But `repair_resolution` deletes bars on the strength of the same listing ends. So a wrong end deletes the bars, and the deleted bars were the evidence that the end was wrong. The diagnosis calls the cut "self-reinforcing": once the bars are gone, the Nasdaq row's own end also becomes 2020-06-17, and nothing in the store shows the security kept trading. A rerun of the resolver cannot find the mistake, because the data that would show it was removed by the mistake.

**What we checked, and what we did not.**
- The real store has not run a blanket `repair-resolution`. The orchestrator read the release log on 2026-11-11: only targeted `repair-bars` releases are on it. (`data/releases.toml` is untracked, so this is the orchestrator's reading, not reproducible from the repo. The rules for what is a release are in the [data-releases runbook](../runbooks/data-releases.md), which lists `repair-resolution`, `repair-bars`, `repair-dividends`, `delete-master-rows` and `master-retract` as the row-changing commands.) The harm so far is on the rehearsal copy. That is why this is cheap to decide now and expensive after the first real run.
- **Alpaca bars and actions are not in the raw archive.** `store/raw_archive.py` is written for EDGAR (the daily index and Form 4 bodies; `ingest.py` calls it only from the index and Form 4 recorders), and [ADR 0028](0028-one-ingest-pipeline.md) says in so many words that "the archive is not a cache of public data for every source": Alpaca's data terms are checked before that source adopts the archive. So a deleted Alpaca bar can only come back by refetching it from the vendor, under whichever id the resolver then assigns. Where the resolver assigns no id (the 26,889 keys), it cannot come back at all, and the vendor's history for a delisted name may not be served for ever. Deleting is therefore the one step in the pipeline with no way back.
- Nothing is yet known about which bars were *correctly* deleted. The diagnosis lists groups that are probably correct (warrant and unit tickers, closed-end funds with old Form 25 tails, about 20 one-session boundary keys) beside the APA-type holes. A rule that cannot be undone cannot be told apart from one that was right.

## Options considered

1. **Buy or adopt an external security master (a vendor crosswalk and listing events).** Pro: an independent source of identity. Con: ADR 0022 already decided this on 2026-10-14: no vendor, USD 69 to test and about USD 499 a year against a USD 0 data budget ([ADR 0009](0009-price-vendor.md)), a medium-to-large migration, and a vendor snapshot is current so our own `known_at` is still needed. The defects here are in our rules and our repair, not in a missing source, and a vendor would not have stopped a delete from our own tool. Not chosen. Its one useful idea, an independent answer, is taken in option 3 without the cost.
2. **A full evidence-graph identity model.** Every fact (bar, filing, listing) becomes an observation with its source, and identity is a derived view that can be recomputed at any time, nothing ever deleted. Pro: the cleanest answer to the circularity, since the evidence can never be removed. Con: a rewrite of the master, the resolver and every reader; it is the migration ADR 0022 rejected for the same reason; it would block the data sitting for weeks; and most of its benefit comes from one rule (never destroy an observation) that can be taken alone.
3. **Hybrid (chosen).** Three changes that add to the existing design and rewrite none of it:
   - (a) repairs stop destroying observations: they retract them;
   - (b) every release proves, before it closes, that no security lost trading days it should still have;
   - (c) a small answer key, built from sources outside our resolver, judges the resolver itself.
   Each is useful alone, they cost about ten small tasks, and none of them blocks T387, T388 or #3265.
4. **Do nothing but fix the rule (T388) and add the orphan guard (#3265).** Pro: the cheapest. Con: it fixes the line-withdrawal case and refuses orphans, but the next wrong rule would still delete bars that were the only evidence, and nothing would notice a liquid name losing months of bars if the guard's resolver is wrong in the same way. Kept as the floor: T388 and #3265 go ahead regardless. Not enough alone.

## Decision

We will do the following. The owner approved the direction on #3275 on 2026-11-11.

**1. Repairs retract; they do not delete.** `repair-resolution`, `repair-bars`, `repair-dividends`, `repair-spinoff-splits` and `delete-master-rows` stop issuing `DELETE` on `prices_daily`, `corporate_actions`, `listings` and `delistings`. A row a repair moves out is **retracted**: it leaves the fact table, so every reader, which never sees it, is unchanged, and it is kept in full in a side table, with:
- the table and key it came from, and the whole row (every column, including `known_at`, `source`, `provenance`);
- the **reason** (the repair command's reason text);
- the **release name** (the repair already refuses without an open release);
- the **rule that judged it**: for `repair-resolution`, the resolver's verdict (the listing end or chain row, and the ticker, that put the key outside the span); for the others, the manifest line or review entry;
- the retraction time, and a `restored_at` that is NULL until the row is put back.

A retraction is **reversible row by row**: `repair restore` puts one retracted row, or all rows of one release, back into its table (it refuses if the logical key is now held by any revision, whatever its `known_at`, or if another id already holds that ticker and session; it runs only in its own open release, never inside an open paper window, and the release's close records the restored sessions so trials that read them go stale). A restore is itself recorded. The retracted rows of a release are the "after" evidence the coverage check reads.

*Reuse and difference.* The master tables already retract by a `retracted` flag on an append-only revision (schema version 11, `store/retraction.py`, `master-retract`; `corporate_actions.cancelled` does the same for actions). That model withdraws a key *from its `known_at` on*, which is right for a master rule that changed: reads before the correction still return what was believed then. A bar or action moved by `repair-resolution` is different: it was never true on the wrong id, so the old delete removed it from reads at every instant, and the retraction keeps exactly that effect. A revision flag would instead need every reader of `prices_daily` (adjusted prices, universe, gap, health, backtest, paper) to filter it, and a reader that missed the filter would silently read a bad bar. The side table needs no reader change and cannot be missed. `delete-master-rows` also moves to it, because it removes rows "never right at any instant" (its own test); `master-retract` keeps the flag, which is the right model for rows that were right once. T389 pins both with a test that every existing as-of reader returns equal frames with a row retracted and with it deleted. The choice is settled in the decisions below (question 1).

Schema: one additive migration (new table, no existing row changes), following the version-11 and version-48 pattern: a read-only connection on an older version is accepted and sees no retractions.

**2. Every release runs a coverage check.** #3274 builds the small first version: a read-only `tradepartner release coverage <release>` comparing each security's trading days in the release's pre-release backup (opened read-only) with the current store, listing every security that lost more than a configured threshold, and whether the bars moved to another id; exit 1 on an unmoved loss. This ADR makes it **mandatory**: `decision data-release close` refuses a release that changed bars, actions or master rows unless a passing coverage record for that release exists, and the sitting and releases runbooks run it after the repair steps and stop on exit 1. The plan task extends the check where #3274 stops: "moved" means a bar actually exists on the other id for that session (a comparison of sets of days, not counts, and not the resolver's say-so); any bar or dividend left with no owner fails whatever the threshold; a row retracted with no receiving id counts as a loss (exit 1), a retracted row is reported with its reason and rule, and a count-only release (no bars touched) passes without a backup. A release that cannot produce a pre-release backup does not close.

**3. An independent answer key for hard security histories.** About 20 histories, each a security whose identity is hard and whose true outcome is known from outside sources, not from our resolver or any of our stored rows. Each history lists (a) the **inputs** that reach our store (the filing events: Form 25s, 8-K12Bs, cover-page listings, and the sessions that had bars, because T387 and T388 read bars as evidence; the bar sessions are cited too, from the exchange's or a vendor's published history) as a minimal fixture, and (b) the **expected outcomes** (which entity holds the ticker on which sessions, which sessions have bars, when the line ended), and **each fact carries a citation** (a filing accession, an exchange or company notice, a dated press release; with its date). The expected outcomes are written down from the cited source alone, before and without running the resolver, and a fact without a citation is refused by the loader. Starting list, to be confirmed in T394 against sources:
- **Holdco reorganisations:** APA Corp / Apache (the holdco took the ticker on 2021-03-01; Apache's NYSE Chicago and NYSE lines withdrawn in 2020 while Nasdaq stayed), plus two or three more.
- **Line withdrawals with a continuing primary:** Marriott (MAR; NYSE Chicago line withdrawn 2020 while Nasdaq continued), FISV/FI, HTZ-type moves.
- **Multi-class and tracking-stock names:** Atlanta Braves Holdings BATRA and BATRK, Liberty Live LLYVA and LLYVK, and two or three more split-off or dual-class cases.
- **Bankruptcies with new equity:** three or four names whose old equity ended and whose new equity started under the same or a new ticker.
- **National exchange to OTC moves:** three or four names whose bars continue after a Form 25.
- **Exchange transfers:** DDS, TCBI, IBOC-type moves, where a Form 25 does not mean the end.

The key is stored as fixtures (a packaged file under `src/tradepartner/store/`, loaded and matched by a pure module, in the pattern of T189's index key: [ADR 0022](0022-no-vendor-security-master.md)). **A test runs the resolver over each history's inputs and compares its output with the cited expectations.** A history the resolver does not yet pass is marked as an expected failure with an issue number, so the test is green today and turns red when a fix makes it pass without the mark being removed (strict). The key is **check-only**: no universe, signal, backtest or research module may import it (a source scan, as for the index key; the same scan bars research readers from the retracted-rows table). It also runs read-only against the real store as a command (T396), so the sitting can read the real result.

**4. T246 runs after the sitting.** T246's RSP index check (the known-answer check of S&P 500 membership against the store's universe, backtest plan, #1598) runs **after the data sitting**, as the **economic cross-check**: it asks, with a source we did not build, whether a month-end universe lost members to the sitting's repairs. It does not replace the answer key (it covers index members only, not the delisted, multi-class or OTC names), and the answer key does not replace it. The plan amends T246's line to say so.

**What this does not change.** T388 and #3265 go ahead. The APA Corp chain entry (2021-03-15 to 2021-05-05) remains the owner's config call (T388's amendment). No store is touched by this decision; no task here reads the real store: T396 is a read-only command and T397 runs it on the sitting copy.

## Consequences

- Good:
  - A wrong identity ruling can no longer destroy the evidence that would show it wrong. Every moved row can be put back, one at a time.
  - A repair's effect is checked by what the store now has, not by a count the repair printed itself.
  - The resolver is judged by outcomes written down from outside sources, not by its own output. A rule fix (T388, T387) gets a standing regression set instead of one fixture each.
  - No reader changes, no new dependency, no vendor cost, and the real store has not yet run the destructive repair.
- Bad / accepted risks:
  - The side table grows by every retracted row (the rehearsal's 101,698 bar keys are on the order of ten MB; a larger run is bounded by the store's size, and retention is decision 4 (90 days, purge through a named release); the purge command is not built here).
  - A retraction is still not undone automatically: a restore of a row onto an id whose span is wrong only puts the bar back where the wrong rule left it. The check (2) and the key (3) are what find the wrong rule.
  - The answer key is hand-built and about 20 histories is a sample, not a proof. It catches the known hard shapes (APA, MAR, multi-class) and nothing else. Citations can be wrong; a wrong fact is found when the resolver and a second source disagree, and is fixed in one line.
  - The coverage check needs a pre-release backup. A repair whose backup is missing cannot close; that is a deliberate cost.
  - Vendor bars for a delisted name may stop being served, so a bar refetched later may not exist. Retraction keeps the one copy we have.
- Reversibility: **cheap** for 1 and 2 (a table and a refusal; reverting is a PR); **cheap** for 3 (a check-only file and a test). The retracted rows themselves are the reversibility.
- Revisit if: the side table passes 10% of `prices_daily` (the design is then wrong, not the data); a vendor's terms start allowing archival of Alpaca bars (then ADR 0028 can archive them and retraction becomes less important); the answer key finds a defect class none of its histories cover (grow it); or a retracted row is restored more than once for the same release (the rule that judged it is wrong).

## Decisions on the open questions (orchestrator, 2026-11-11, #3275)

The owner said these technical questions are the orchestrator's to decide. Decided on #3275:

1. **Where a retracted bar or action lives:** a side table, `retracted_rows`, so every reader's view stays exactly what the delete gave and none can miss a flag.
2. **`delete-master-rows`:** moves to the same side table. `master-retract` keeps its `retracted` flag.
3. **The answer key's histories:** the builder proposes about 20, with sources, on the T394 issue; the orchestrator reviews; no names are required by the owner.
4. **Retention:** retracted rows are kept until the producing release has been closed for 90 days with a passed coverage record; a purge goes only through a named release.
5. **T397:** `identity-check` runs read-only on the sitting copy right after T388 and T396 merge.
