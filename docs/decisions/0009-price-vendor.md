# 0009. Price vendor: none for Phase 3; Alpaca SIP bars plus EDGAR, with the survivorship gap measured and gated

**Status:** Accepted 2026-09-26 (PR #241)  ·  **Date:** 2026-09-26  ·  **Issue:** #240 (plan task T29)

## Context

ADR 0003 deferred the price-vendor choice to the start of Phase 3 and set the terms (rule 8): the ADR records the data budget; it may say "no vendor", but then it amends charter principle 4 ("point-in-time data, including delisted names") in the same document, and Phase 4 may not start until the owner has signed off on the survivorship gap in the trial registry.

What is known now that was not known then:

- **Alpaca's free plan serves consolidated SIP daily bars from 2016-01-04**, and for a probed sample of eight delisted names (five acquired, three failed) the bars run to each name's last regular session ([#104](../research/2026-09-25-alpaca-delisted-bars.md)). A zero-volume placeholder bar can follow the last real session, and a renamed ticker's history is served under both symbols; the price adapter drops both. The sample is eight large, well-known names delisted 2019 to 2023, with bars checked for the last 60 sessions only; names delisted 2016 to 2018, small caps and reused tickers were not probed.
- **Alpaca's corporate actions matched** every ground-truth split, reverse split and spin-off, and returned four dividends a year (count only, not amounts or ex-dates), for seven large-cap symbols in every year 2016 to 2025 ([#101](../research/2026-09-25-alpaca-open-and-depth.md)); smaller names, delisted names and dividend amounts were not checked. No announcement dates are carried, so #83 stamps each action at the close before its ex-date.
- **Ingest staleness is not a problem**: on 20 sessions in August and September 2026, for currently listed names only, Alpaca delivered a row (zero-volume placeholders included) for all but at most 0.03% of names, median 0.01% ([#106](../research/2026-09-25-alpaca-missing-bars.md)). This bears on staleness at ingest, not on historical or delisted coverage.
- **EDGAR is the point-in-time source for delistings** (Form 25 and 25-NSE, stamped at acceptance) and for the security master, so "which names existed on date T" does not depend on the price vendor at all.
- **The survivorship gap is measured, not assumed.** `survivorship_gap` (Phase 2 T15) counts, per rebalance, the names listed at some session in the window that have no bar at T or were delisted in the window with a stale tail, and a holdout run is refused when its count share crosses the frozen threshold (`gap.count_share_threshold`, default 0.05) at any rebalance close, unless the owner overrides with a reason that is written to the trial ([Phase 3 spec](../specs/backtest.md), gap gate); in-sample runs record the gap but are never refused. The gap is a lower bound: it misses names the security master never learned of, and names excluded for missing history (universe rule 6, `truncated_history`) are reported beside it, not in it.
- **The first hypothesis fits the history.** H1 rebalances first on 2017-01-31 with a 12-month formation window, so it needs bars from January 2016 ([H1](../hypotheses/h1-momentum-12-1.md)). Its holdout ends 2026-09-30. *Note 2026-10-04 (#851):* since H1's #842 amendment its first rebalance was 2019-11-29 (the first month-end at which the universe's size cut binds on the owner's store), so the 2016 bars give the lookback about three years of slack; the start is set by listings, not by prices, and this ADR's decision stands as written. *Note 2026-10-05 (#976, records #975):* #975 re-pinned H1's `in_sample_start` to 2020-08-31 for the #974 first-span bar gap, so its first rebalance is now 2020-08-31; the slack is about four and a half years and the point above stands unchanged.
- **No paid vendor is verified.** The G8 report ([2026-09-25-price-vendors.md](../research/2026-09-25-price-vendors.md)) graded every paid vendor an individual can buy "claimed" for delisted coverage, on the vendor's word; none records when a corporate action became known; every adjusted series whose method is documented (Sharadar, EODHD, Massive) is restated backwards; and Norgate, Tiingo, Sharadar and Massive all require deleting local data when the subscription ends, with Sharadar's direct licence the only one that explicitly lets derived works (results, statistics, trade logs) survive. Alpaca's terms allow private storage for personal, non-commercial use and say nothing about deletion ([free-data terms](../research/2026-09-25-free-data-terms.md)).

The store keeps raw bars and corporate actions with `known_at`, and adjusts at read time (ADR 0003 rule 1), so a vendor's restated adjusted series would never be stored as truth anyway; what a vendor could add is history before 2016 and delisted names Alpaca lacks.

## Options considered

1. **No paid vendor for Phase 3: Alpaca SIP daily bars from 2016 plus EDGAR, gap measured and gated** (this ADR). Pro: zero cost; delisted names observed present on the sample; corporate actions matched for the seven large caps probed; no deletion clause hangs over the store, the Parquet export or tagged backtest artefacts; the gap gate makes any bias visible per rebalance instead of assumed away. Con: history starts 2016-01-04, so no hypothesis can form a signal before that; the delisted coverage is verified on eight names only, not on the population; the free plan's SIP history access is Alpaca's to withdraw.
2. **Sharadar SEP, direct, Prices plan (USD 39/mo, history from 1998).** Pro: delisted names claimed since 1998; the only licence read that explicitly keeps derived works; bulk download; a `closeunadj` field, so raw storage holds. Con: about USD 468/yr at the monthly rate (the annual price is unverified) for history no registered hypothesis needs yet; delisted coverage still unverified; local data must be deleted within thirty days of cancellation, so the store would have to be rebuilt from Alpaca if the subscription lapses; adds a second price source to reconcile.
3. **Norgate Platinum (USD 630/yr, delisted names to 1990).** Pro: the deepest delisted coverage claimed, "essentially complete" from late 1992. Con: the updater is Windows-only (a VM on the owner's Mac, unresolved); deletion on expiry; whether its adjusted series are restated backwards is undocumented.
4. **Massive (formerly Polygon), 20+ year plan (USD 199/mo).** Pro: full history claimed for delisted tickers, flat files. Con: USD 2,388/yr (about 1,910 billed annually); Tier 3 reports dispute the delisted coverage; no dividend adjustment; deletion at termination; storage during the subscription not addressed in the terms.
5. **EODHD (USD 199/yr) or Tiingo (USD 300/yr).** Con: EODHD's names delisted before 2018 have prices only and its post-termination clause is unresolved; Tiingo's delisted support excludes recycled tickers by its own account and its free tier forbids persistent storage. Neither is better than option 1 for the period H1 covers.
6. **Alpaca Algo Trader Plus (USD 99/mo).** Pro: the incumbent, no second source. Con: USD 1,188/yr buys real-time SIP data and higher limits; the same "7+ years" of history and no documented delisted coverage, so nothing this decision needs.
7. **One month of Sharadar SEP direct (USD 39) as a cross-check**, reconciling Alpaca's delisted names and 2016 to 2023 closes and keeping only the derived statistics, which Sharadar's licence allows after deletion within thirty days. Pro: the cheapest route to a grade better than "observed". Con: not needed before T45b, because H1's in-sample run walks every Form 25 delisting on the universe exchanges through the gap's `truncated_tail` count at each rebalance; it becomes the first step if a trigger fires.

## Decision

We will run Phase 3 with **no paid price vendor**. The `PriceSource` for every registered hypothesis, including H1's real-store run (T45b), is **Alpaca's free plan, SIP daily bars** (`alpaca.historical_feed = sip`), with EDGAR as the source of delistings and the security master, exactly as Phase 2 built it.

1. **Budget: USD 0 per month for data, APIs and LLM calls by the running system, from Phase 3 until a later ADR changes it** (the charter's Budget line defers to this ADR). The budget is re-opened only by a trigger below, and a vendor then enters through a new ADR and a plan amendment adding its adapter, never by editing this one.
2. **Delisted-coverage grade for Alpaca: observed on a sample of eight large, well-known names delisted 2019 to 2023, bars checked for the last 60 sessions only (#104).** Not "verified" in G8's sense (no vendor documentation and no independent source), and not measured for 2016 to 2018 delistings, small caps or reused tickers. The population check is the `truncated_tail` component of `survivorship_gap`, read at every in-sample rebalance of H1's run: it flags a Form 25 name on the universe exchanges whose last bar is more than `gap.missing_tail_sessions` sessions before the session preceding its filing, or which has no bar at all. It does not check bars up to the final session; the data-health page's delisted-names list (T18) shows the derived end session per name, not a comparison. The owner reads the per-rebalance `truncated_tail` counts before T45b. A full population check against final sessions (G8 follow-up 2) is a small follow-up issue if the owner wants it before T45b.
3. **The gap is the guard.** Every trial records `survivorship_gap` per rebalance; a holdout run that crosses `gap.count_share_threshold` is refused unless overridden with a reason (Phase 3 spec, gap gate). Per ADR 0003 rule 8, **Phase 4 does not start until the owner has signed off that the recorded gap is acceptable, as an `owner_decisions` row of kind `gap_signoff` referencing H1's in-sample trial** (`tradepartner decision gap-signoff --trial <id> --reason`, spec req 12, CLI in T42).
4. **History starts 2016-01-04.** A hypothesis whose formation window needs an earlier session cannot be registered under this ADR; registering one is a trigger.
5. **Terms and derived artefacts.** Alpaca data is stored privately for the owner's own account under Alpaca's personal, non-commercial terms; nothing is redistributed. The Parquet export and tagged backtest artefacts (ADR 0003) carry no deletion obligation. If a vendor is later added, its deletion clause applies to its rows and to any export containing them, and the ADR that adds it says what happens to artefacts at cancellation.
6. **Charter principle 4 is amended**, per ADR 0003 rule 8, to say what "including delisted names" means under this ADR: delisted names from 2016-01-04 as served by Alpaca SIP bars and stamped by Form 25 filings, verified on a sample, with the survivorship gap measured per rebalance and gated. The text is in `docs/charter.md` with a reference to this ADR.

**Triggers that re-open the budget (any one):**
- H1's in-sample run, or any later registered hypothesis, records a gap count share above `gap.count_share_threshold` at any month-end and the owner does not sign it off;
- H1's in-sample run records any `truncated_tail` name the owner cannot explain as a data error in EDGAR;
- the `truncated_history` side count, excluding names whose first listing is less than `universe.min_history_months` before the rebalance (new listings fail rule 6 by construction), exceeds `gap.count_share_threshold` of the listed set at any in-sample rebalance;
- a hypothesis the owner wants to register needs bars before 2016-01-04;
- Alpaca withdraws SIP history from the free plan or changes its storage terms.

If a trigger fires, the first step is option 7 (one month of Sharadar to reconcile), and the candidate order for a subscription from G8's evidence is Sharadar SEP direct (derived works survive cancellation), then EODHD, whose pre-2018 delisted names lack splits and dividends and whose deletion clause is unresolved, then Norgate if pre-1998 history is needed and the Windows updater is acceptable. Massive is not a candidate at its price.

## Consequences

- Good: Phase 3 starts now with no spend and no licence deadline over the store; every bias the free source carries is measured per rebalance and refused above a threshold instead of assumed; a vendor remains a one-adapter change (ADR 0003).
- Bad / accepted risks: no signal can form before 2016-01-04, which rules out long-history hypotheses and limits the in-sample window to about seven years before the 2024 holdout; delisted coverage rests on eight probed names until the data-health metric runs on the full store; Alpaca can change its free plan, in which case the store keeps what it holds and a vendor decision follows.
- Reversibility: cheap for the data, one-way for a spent holdout. Adding a vendor is a new ADR plus one adapter task, and the store's raw layout is vendor-agnostic. But a holdout spent on Alpaca data (gate passed or overridden) cannot be re-spent cleanly for that hypothesis after a vendor arrives: a second spend is a `holdout_repeat` (spec req 11, ADR 0003 rule 6). And "the store keeps what it holds" assumes Alpaca's terms stay silent on deletion; the free-data terms report read a 2021-dated market-data terms page and not the customer agreement, and notes the terms can change.
- Revisit if: any trigger above fires, or at the Phase 3 close-out (T45b) if the recorded gap or the data-health metric is worse than the sample suggested.
