# 0003. Data and broker access go through adapters; local-first; paid price vendor deferred to Phase 3

**Status:** Accepted  ·  **Date:** 2026-09-24  ·  **Issue:** #5  ·  **Amended:** 2026-10-07 (#1199, the stale-listing rule in the survivorship gap; owner decision 2026-10-07 on #1199)

## Context

Phase 1 originally required choosing a price-data vendor before building, because survivorship-bias-free prices decide whether a backtest can be trusted ([handoff](../research/2026-09-24-initial-research-handoff.md) D3, G8). That choice depends on a monthly budget the owner has not set. Vendor pricing has not been researched (G8 is open); the working assumption, unverified, is that any paid plan costs more per year than the ~$100 of planned live capital.

The owner wants to build and test the system's own logic first, locally, without depending on a vendor, and to be able to swap data sources later without rewriting the system. The parts that must be correct regardless of vendor are the point-in-time store, the security master, the calendar, the trial registry, the journal, the cost model and the backtester. None of them needs vendor data to be built or tested.

Two facts make a local-first approach honest rather than a shortcut:
- **Point-in-time correctness cannot be tested on real data**, because real data does not say what the right answer was. It is tested on synthetic fixtures where the answer is known.
- **SEC EDGAR is point-in-time by construction** (acceptance timestamps) and free. It includes Form 25 delisting notices (on EDGAR since the mid-2000s) and XBRL facts such as shares outstanding and public float, so a delisting record and a rough size measure can be built without a vendor. What free sources lack is *price history for delisted names*, especially the final decline before delisting, which is where survivorship bias in a momentum strategy lives.

## Options considered

1. **Pick the vendor now, build against real data.** Pro: real data from day one. Con: forces the budget decision before anything exists; couples the system to one vendor's schema; point-in-time tests would still need fixtures.
2. **Adapters with local defaults; vendor deferred** (this ADR). Pro: the owner's code is built and tested without spending; the vendor becomes a one-adapter change; the survivorship gap is bounded and documented rather than hidden. Con: Phase 3 backtests on free data are biased upward until a vendor is added, and the deferral must not become permanent.
3. **Free data only, permanently.** Pro: zero cost. Con: momentum backtests on survivors only overstate returns by an unknown amount; charter principle 4 ("including delisted names") is violated.

## Decision

We will define three interfaces in `src/tradepartner/` and put every external system behind one:

| Interface | Local default (Phase 2) | Later adapters |
|---|---|---|
| `PriceSource` | synthetic fixtures; Alpaca free market data | paid survivorship-free vendor (Phase 3 ADR) |
| `FilingSource` | synthetic fixtures; SEC EDGAR via `edgartools` | none expected; EDGAR is the primary source |
| `Broker` | in-memory fake broker | Alpaca paper (Phase 4), Alpaca live (Phase 6) |

### Rules

**1. Point-in-time storage.** Every stored record carries `known_at` (when the fact became knowable, UTC, tz-aware) and `ingested_at` (when we stored it) plus a source identifier. Prices are stored **raw** (unadjusted open, high, low, close, volume; the open is required because execution is at T+1 open per ADR 0006) alongside **corporate actions** (splits, dividends, ticker changes, delistings), each with its own `known_at`. Adjusted series are computed at read time "as of T", never stored as truth, because vendors rewrite adjusted history after the fact.

**2. Canonical timing rule.** A daily bar for session T has `known_at` equal to that session's close on the exchange calendar. A signal computed from session T's close may trade no earlier than session T+1. This rule lives in the core, not in adapters. An adapter whose source cannot supply a real `known_at` applies this rule and says so in its docstring.

**3. Security master.** Owned by us. Keyed by our own security ID, mapped to CIK and to dated ticker ranges, so a ticker reused by a new company never returns the old company's history. Populated from EDGAR (company facts, Form 25) plus adapter metadata. It is the single place that answers "what existed on date T". Adapters resolve securities through it, never by bare ticker.

**4. Fixture universe.** A checked-in synthetic dataset with at least: one delisting, one split, one ticker change, one restated fundamental (two `known_at` values for the same period), one exchange holiday and one half day. Two test layers:
- the **core no-look-ahead suite**, run against the fixture `PriceSource` and `FilingSource`;
- **per-adapter contract tests**, run against recorded responses, checking that every record has `known_at` and `ingested_at` filled per rules 1–2.

**5. Survivorship gap.** Tracked as two numbers, computed from filings dated before T and reported as a **lower bound**:
- *count share*: names in the security master that were listed on date T but are **missing price history**, divided by all names listed on T. A name is missing if it has no prices, or if its price history ends more than N sessions (config, proposed 5) before its Form 25 effective date;
- *size share*: the same, weighted by the last known `EntityCommonStockSharesOutstanding` (or public float) × the last price we have for that name, computed identically for survivors.

Both numbers appear on the Phase 2 data-health page and in every backtest result. The revisit threshold is on the **count** share (proposed 5% at any rebalance date), because the bias sits in small names.

*Amendment 2026-10-07 (#1199):* a listing whose last bar is more than `gap.stale_listing_sessions` sessions before T counts, for this rule only, as ended at its last bar. The rule, its argument, its limits and the open questions are in the [amendment section](#amendment-2026-10-07-1199-the-stale-listing-rule) below.

**6. The holdout is not spent on free data.** Backtests on a `PriceSource` whose survivorship gap exceeds the threshold may not unlock the locked holdout. The single holdout run per hypothesis waits for a source below the threshold, or for an explicit owner decision, logged in the trial registry, to spend it anyway.

**7. Risk rules sit outside every broker.** The core sends orders only through a risk-gated wrapper around `Broker` (position limits, kill switch, idempotency). Adapters never contain or bypass risk logic, so swapping paper for live cannot skip it.

**8. Deferral has a deadline.** The price-vendor ADR, with the data budget, is due **at the start of Phase 3**, before the first registered hypothesis runs. It may record "no vendor", but then it must also amend charter principle 4 through the same ADR, and Phase 4 may not start until the owner has signed off on the gap in the trial registry.

### Verify before the Phase 2 plan

Unknown as of this ADR and to be checked in the plan, not assumed:
- Alpaca free plan: historical depth (believed ~2016), IEX-only vs consolidated daily bars, delisted-symbol coverage, corporate-actions depth, dividend data for SPY and MTUM total return, whether the terms allow local storage, whether the daily **open** is the official opening-auction price or an IEX print, and whether a fractional DAY order submitted before the open joins the opening auction or fills at NBBO afterwards. An account and API key are needed even for data.
- EDGAR: a declared `User-Agent` with contact details and a rate limit of about 10 requests per second.

## Consequences

- Good: the owner's code is testable and correct before any money is spent; vendor lock-in is limited to one adapter; the bias from free data is bounded and visible; the holdout stays clean.
- Bad / accepted risks: Phase 3 numbers on free data are optimistic and must not be used to size positions or claim an edge. Alpaca's free history (if ~2016) gives roughly nine usable years after the 12-month lookback, far below the ~16 years the handoff estimates to confirm a Sharpe of 0.5. `edgartools` has one maintainer; pin its version. The gap metric misses delistings the security master never learns about.
- Reversibility: cheap for adapters. The storage rules (1–3) are costly to change once data is loaded, which is why they are fixed here.
- Revisit if: the count-share gap exceeds the configured threshold at any rebalance date; the owner sets a data budget earlier than Phase 3; or Alpaca's terms or coverage fail the checks above.

## Amendment 2026-10-07 (#1199): the stale-listing rule

**Owner decision (Jose, 2026-10-07, on #1199):** treat the survivorship gap as one problem with one target, holdout and in-sample count share **≤ 0.05 at every rebalance**, and adopt the "stale listing" rule (scenario D of the [2026-10-06 sizing spike](#the-evidence), #1017/#1018) through this amendment. This section writes the rule down for review; the code change in `gap.py`, its no-look-ahead test and the re-measure follow as step 2 of #1199, their own task, after this amendment lands. Nothing here changes code, config or a plan line.

### The rule

For the survivorship gap at T (rule 5 above; [data-foundation spec](../specs/data-foundation.md), "Survivorship gap"), with `session` the last completed session at T and W = (previous rebalance session, `session`]:

1. **Stale listing.** A security's current listing that the gap today treats as *live* at `session` (status `listed`, or `transferred` with an end session on or after `session`; a `delisted` listing is never one) is **stale at T** when the name has at least one traded bar known at T on or before `session`, that last bar is **before the first session of W**, and the number of sessions strictly after it and up to `session` is **greater than `gap.stale_listing_sessions`**. The bar is the latest revision known at T with positive volume, as the gap reads bars today; a zero-volume halt marker is not a trade. Sessions are XNYS sessions from the trading calendar (`calendar.all_sessions`, the count `gap._sessions_after` already uses for `truncated_tail`), never weekdays or calendar days. The "before W" condition makes the rule safe for any N: a name dark more than N sessions whose last bar is still inside W is not stale, it is live and `no_bar_at_t` as today, so a value of N below the sessions in W acts as if it were equal to them and never turns a death into a survivor.
2. **Ended at its last bar.** A stale listing counts, *for the gap only*, as ended on the session of its last bar, which is before W, so the security is **not in L** on that listing's account and **never in M** (never `no_bar_at_t`): it contributes to neither the count share nor the size share. Another listing of the same security active in W (an older `delisted` row whose Form 25 was filed inside W, or a `transferred` row ending inside W) keeps the security in L on that row's account and is judged by today's rules, so a Form 25 filed inside W for a long-dark name is still tested as `truncated_tail`.
3. **Reported, never hidden.** The securities the rule removes are returned as a fourth side category, `stale_listings` (security id, last bar, sessions dark), reported beside `unclassifiable`, `truncated_history` and `stale_shares` on the data-health page and in `SurvivorshipGap`, and never in M. A name is counted once: a stale security is in none of the three other side categories (they read names active in W, and a stale security is not, unless another of its listings is, rule 2, in which case it is not stale-reported either).
4. **The key.** `gap.stale_listing_sessions`, an integer number of sessions, frozen per hypothesis with the rest of `gap.*` (backtest spec req 10; see open question 1). **Proposed default: 63 sessions** (a quarter of the 252-session year; the spike's 90 calendar days). It is listed with the other keys in the data-foundation spec's "Config keys" and lands in `config.py` with step 2.
5. **Unchanged.** A listing with **no traded bar at all** known at T stays in M as `no_bar_at_t` while live, as today (open question 2). The `truncated_tail` test for a Form 25 filed inside W, with its own `gap.missing_tail_sessions`, is unchanged. The threshold `gap.count_share_threshold`, rules 6 and 8 and the holdout gate are unchanged.

### Why 63 sessions

- **Three monthly rebalances.** At the `month_end` cadence W holds about 21 sessions, so a name that goes dark is counted missing (`no_bar_at_t`) at the three month-ends after its last bar, then treated as ended. At a weekly cadence (ADR 0012) that is about thirteen rebalances. The window in which a dying name's missing tail would have mattered to a monthly strategy is visible in the gap in full.
- **Longer than any ordinary halt.** An SEC trading suspension under Exchange Act section 12(k) lasts at most ten business days; an exchange halt pending news lasts hours to days; a compliance halt pending a delisting determination that runs longer than a quarter ends, in nearly every case, in the delisting the rule assumes.
- **Shorter than the dead group.** Of the 564 names in M at 2026-09-30 (measured, #1199; the cause table's buckets are calendar days, so a name near a boundary may sit on the other side of 63 sessions, about 88 to 92 calendar days), 404 (72%) stopped more than a year ago and 58 (10%) between 91 and 365 days ago, with only about 22 of the 564 carrying any delisting row. Both groups read as dark names whose Form 25 the master never matched or that never filed one (SPAC liquidations, mergers, reverse mergers, moves to OTC): dead to a strategy at T. 63 sessions ends the count for about both groups; the 34 names dark 11 to 90 days (6%) and the 17 recent holes (3%) stay counted, so a fresh price hole still shows.
- **Why not 5** (`gap.missing_tail_sessions`): every data hole longer than a week would be read as a death, and the gap exists to show holes.
- **Why not 252:** the 91-to-365-day group would stay counted for up to a year after the name died, for no information the three rebalances did not already give; arithmetic on the measured table (not a measurement) puts the 2026-09-30 share near 0.038 at 252 sessions against about 0.025 at 63.
- **The spike used this value**, so its scenario D is the same rule; its estimate below, measured on top of scenarios A to C, is not the number step 2 alone should reproduce (see "Expected effect" for the comparison point). Any other value is the owner's to set in config; the ADR fixes the shape of the rule, not the number.

### The argument

**What the gap is for.** Rule 5 bounds the survivorship bias of a free price source: the share of the names a strategy could have held at T whose prices the store lacks. The bias that matters for a momentum strategy is losing a dying name's final decline (Context above): if the store drops a name before its delisting, a backtest that held it exits at a price it never got, and a universe built from the store leans towards survivors. The gap is the guard for that, read at every rebalance and gating the holdout (rule 6).

**Why a name dark for more than N sessions is not tradable at T.** A listing row stays `listed` until the master learns of its end, and the master learns of an end only from a matched Form 25 (rule 3; [data-foundation spec](../specs/data-foundation.md) req 4). The 2026-10-06 spike and the #1199 baseline show that most of M is not missing prices for a tradable name: it is names whose bars stopped for good, months or years ago, under a listing row that still reads live because the Form 25 was never matched (13,868 unmatched 25-NSE filings in the 10-04 backfill log, 91 compound class titles the never-guess rule refuses) or never filed. No order could have been placed in such a name at T, and `universe_as_of(T)` already excludes it under ADR 0006 rule 6 (no bar in the history window). Counting it as "listed at T but missing" measures the master's Form 25 coverage, not the price source's survivorship coverage, and the count grows every year (154 such names in 2023-10, 404 in 2026-09), which is why the holdout reads worse than the in-sample window with no change in the source. After N sessions without a trade, the more likely reading of the listing row is "ended, filing unmatched", and the gap takes it. That reading is argued from the cause table, not yet checked name by name, so **step 2 includes a sample check**, recorded on #1199 before the re-measure is taken as the new baseline: a random sample of about 30 of the names the rule removes at 2026-09-30, each checked against EDGAR (a 10-Q or 10-K filed after the last bar with a cover page still naming the ticker and exchange means the company is alive and the hole is ours; a Form 15, a merger 8-K, a liquidation or an OTC cover page means it is not) and, where EDGAR is silent, an outside quote. If more than about a tenth of the sample turns out to be still trading on a universe exchange, the premise is wrong for this store and the owner decides on #1199 before the rule gates anything (see "Consequences and reversibility").

**Why the delisting-return bias is still caught.** The bias lives in the sessions between a name's last stored bar and its real end. Under the rule those sessions are counted: at every rebalance inside the N-session window after the last bar the name is live, in L and in M as `no_bar_at_t`, so a source that drops names early raises the gap at the rebalances where a strategy would have held them. A Form 25 filed later, inside some W, still tests the tail against `gap.missing_tail_sessions` (`truncated_tail`), as today. And the names the rule removes are reported in `stale_listings`, so a sudden rise in that count at the health page is visible even when the shares are not.

### What the rule does not change

- **Universe membership.** `universe_as_of` is untouched. A dark name already fails rule 6 (no traded bar in its `min_history_months` window; a bar is a traded bar since #787), so it is in no universe and no backtest buys it, before and after this amendment.
- **Returns and the backtest's delisting handling.** The engine reads the gap only to record it per rebalance and to gate the holdout ([backtest spec](../specs/backtest.md) req 5 and req 11). A held name whose listing ends exits at its last close (`backtest.delisting_exit=last_close`); a held name still listed with no bar in the last `backtest.stale_exit_sessions` (5) sessions exits as `stale_exit`. Neither reads `gap.stale_listing_sessions`; no trial's returns move.
- **The master and the ingest.** No `listings` row, span, resolver verdict or `listing_ends_as_of` result changes; a stale listing is still `listed`, the daily ingest keeps asking the source for it, a resumption lands as bars, and `--fill-holes` still lists it. The ingest's own staleness and dark-share checks (`ingest.max_missing_share`, `ingest.max_dark_share`) are unchanged and remain the first line against a source that stops serving live names.
- **Point-in-time reads.** Every input is a row with `known_at ≤ T`: the listing rows, the bars and the calendar. Truncation invariance for `survivorship_gap` (data-foundation spec, "Look-ahead") must still hold, and step 2's no-look-ahead test asserts it: a bar on a session after `session`, or one first known after T, cannot make a name live at T. A bar for a session on or before `session` that is fetched later but stamped at its session's close (`--fill-holes`, spec amendment #831) changes the verdict at T retroactively, which is the property every as-of read of bars already has, not a new deviation.
- **Trial rows.** The gap values stored on every past trial stand as recorded, with that trial's `code_version`. A run after step 2 is a new trial under the new code, as for every code change.

### Residual risk accepted

1. **A live name the source stopped serving.** A vendor-side hole longer than N sessions on a name that still trades (a dropped symbol, a symbol form the resolver does not map, a rename the master missed, #869) is read as a death and leaves the gap. This is the cost of the rule: the gap can no longer tell "dead, filing unmatched" from "alive, bars not fetched" once the dark stretch passes N. Handled by: the three rebalances during which it is counted; the `stale_listings` list on the health page, which the owner reads when the count moves (no alert on a rise in that count is planned; `health --check` has no rule on it, and adding one is the owner's call); the ingest's dark-share check at the chunk the name disappears; the step 2 sample check above; `--fill-holes` and steps 4 and 5 of #1199, which chase exactly these names (dual-class and dotted tickers; the recent holes).
2. **A halt longer than N sessions, or a relisting.** A name halted for more than N sessions that resumes (bankruptcy-pending halts; MiMedx-type OTC spells and returns, `alpaca.accepted_relistings`) is read as ended during the dark stretch after N sessions and is live again from its first new bar at a later T. The listing row is never altered, no backtest can hold it meanwhile (rule 6 keeps it out of the universe; a held position exits as `stale_exit` after 5 sessions), so the only effect is that the gap under-counts it in that stretch. Accepted; such halts are rare on the universe exchanges.
3. **A late Form 25.** A Form 25 filed more than N sessions after the last bar, inside a later W, is still tested as `truncated_tail` at that rebalance, so the name can be counted at the three rebalances after its last bar and once more at the filing. Accepted; it keeps the Form 25 check honest and costs at most one count per such name.
4. **The number is a choice.** 63 is argued above, not measured as optimal; a larger N keeps dead names in the gap for longer, a smaller one hides holes sooner. The value is frozen on every registration and printed with the frozen set, so a trial's gap is reproducible under its own N. A registration takes N from live config at registration time, as it takes `gap.count_share_threshold` today, so the frozen set printed at `hypothesis register` is where a reviewer checks it.

### Expected effect

- **Measured baseline** (#1199, the store backup before the 2026-10-07 re-ingest). Method: `tradepartner.gap.survivorship_gap` itself, read-only on the store, at the read time of every month-end rebalance session of the in-sample (2020-08-31..2023-12-29) and holdout (2024-01..2026-09-30) windows, with `universe.top_n_by_cap=1000` (which touches only the side categories, through `universe_as_of`, never L or M; the orchestrator's `gap/gap.py`, the spike's `gap1.py` re-pointed, is a loop over those calls and nothing more); L is every listed common name, about 3,200 in 2020-08 and about 4,600 in 2026. Result: in-sample max 0.084, 17 of 41 rebalances above 0.05; holdout max 0.122, 33 of 33 above 0.05 (0.120, 0.119, 0.122 for 2026-07..09). M at 2026-09-30 is 564 names: 404 dark for more than a year, 58 dark 91 to 365 days, 50 with no bar ever, 34 dark 11 to 90 days, 17 recent holes, 1 truncated tail. Step 2 re-measures with the same call over the same sessions, so the two numbers compare.
- **Spike estimate** (2026-10-06, read-only on a store copy; an estimate, not a measurement): scenario D, this rule at 90 calendar days, **on top of scenarios A to C** (Form 25 closes, CIK and ticker successors, and the cached unmatched 25-NSEs resolved, that last one approximated from the EDGAR cache): in-sample max about 0.020, holdout max about 0.017. Scenarios A to C together, without D, left the holdout's last three months at about 0.056. The A-to-D figure is the combined effect, not what step 2 alone should reproduce; A to C are steps 3 to 5 of #1199 or later.
- **Comparison point for step 2, arithmetic on the measured table** (not a measurement): the rule alone removes about 404 + 58 names from M and from L at 2026-09-30, leaving about 102 of about 4,160 names, a share near 0.025 (near 0.038 at 252 sessions); the 50 with no bar ever stay in M until step 4 maps them. A re-measure far from 0.025 at 2026-09-30 means the table and the rule disagree and is investigated before the number is used.
- The number that counts is the **re-measure in step 2** against the ≤ 0.05 target at every rebalance of both windows, recorded on #1199 as the new baseline with the sample check. If it stays above the target anywhere, steps 3 to 5 of #1199 follow, each re-measured before the next is built.

### Consequences and reversibility

- **The gate is looser, by design, against one failure.** A price source that stops serving a live name more than N sessions before its real end is no longer held against the gap after those N sessions. Before this amendment such a name counted at every later rebalance; now it counts at about three. The gap still bounds a source that drops names inside the N-session window, and the Form 25 tail test is unchanged. The owner accepts this on #1199 with the sample check as the evidence that the names removed are dead.
- **The threshold was not re-derived.** `gap.count_share_threshold` (0.05) was set in rule 5 for the old count, which mixed dead names with missing prices; it now applies to a narrower count. It stays at 0.05 because 5% of live names with no price at a rebalance is the level rule 5 argued from (the bias sits in small names) and the rule removes names that were never missing prices, not prices that were missing. The owner can move it in config, frozen per registration as today.
- **The rule makes the holdout gate passable with no change to the source.** That is the point of the owner decision (the measured 0.122 is mostly master coverage, not price coverage), and it is also the consequence to watch: a holdout spent under this rule is spent for good (domain rule 3), H1's included under open question 1(b). The sample check and the re-measure come before any spend, and the spend's trial row records the gap it saw and the frozen N.
- **Reversible, cheaply, except for a spent holdout.** The key is config; setting it very large restores today's count on every new registration, and an old registration keeps the N it froze, so no past trial moves. The reported `stale_listings` category costs one field and one health line. What cannot be undone is a holdout trial run under the rule; that is why the owner questions below are answered before step 2 is claimed.

### Open questions for the owner

Listed, not decided; each is answered on #1199 before step 2 is claimed.

1. **How the frozen key treats H1.** `gap.*` is frozen per hypothesis (backtest spec req 10) and `frozen_keys()` enumerates the section, so `gap.stale_listing_sessions` becomes a frozen key the moment it lands, and the strategy-lab spec (Definitions, "Frozen-key defaults") requires a `FROZEN_KEY_DEFAULTS` entry for it "with the default that reproduces the old behaviour". Taken literally, that default is *off*, so **H1's registration, which predates the key, would keep today's gap** (holdout max 0.122) and its holdout run would stay `refused_gap` unless overridden with `--override-gap --gap-reason`. Options: (a) the table entry is "off" (the literal rule; off means `null`, since 0 would make every `no_bar_at_t` name stale, so the key's type is `int | None` and the pinned JSON value `null`) and H1's holdout spend records a gap override, with this amendment as the reason; (b) the table entry is 63, i.e. the rule applies to every registration, H1 included, with a one-line amendment of the strategy-lab spec's Definitions saying that a `gap.*` key measuring the store rather than the strategy enters at its real default (the pinning test checks entries by value, so mechanically it is an entry like any other); (c) the key is not frozen at all (a backtest spec change to req 10's "`gap.*`"). **Recommendation: (b)**: the gap measures the store's coverage at each rebalance, not a design choice of the hypothesis, past trial rows keep the gap they recorded, and no live setting can move H1's N (the registration lacks the key, so the pinned table default is read, never live config).
2. **Listings with no bar at all.** The rule as written needs a last bar, so the 50 never-priced names (9% of M at 2026-09-30, BF.A/BF.B, MOG.B, AGM.A, SENEB and the other dual-class and dotted tickers) stay in M. Proposed: keep them counted, because an empty listing is a mapping defect the gap should show and step 4 of #1199 is its fix; the alternative is to count a listing as ended `gap.stale_listing_sessions` sessions after its `valid_from` when no bar has ever landed.
3. **Storing the stale count per rebalance.** `stale_listings` is reported by `survivorship_gap` and the health page. Should `trial_rebalances` also store its count (a new column, a schema version, and a backtest-page change, which the 2026-10-07 B3 path asks to avoid for now), or is the health page enough? Proposed: the health page and the result object now; a column only if the owner wants it on the backtest page.

### The evidence

- #1199: the owner decision, the measured baseline and the cause table.
- The 2026-10-06 sizing spike for #1017/#1018 (orchestrator c0498f01, read-only on a store copy; summary in the owner's local research library, `gapspike-1017-1018/summary.md`): scenarios A to D and their simulated shares; the conflicts it lists (Form 25 close versus the #820 relisting rule; compound titles versus the never-guess rule; successions never persisted) are the residual causes steps 3 to 5 of #1199 address, not this rule.
- `src/tradepartner/gap.py` as of this amendment: `live` is `status == LISTED or (status == TRANSFERRED and end >= session)`, and `no_bar_at_t` is `live and last_bar != session`. Step 2 changes, in that module only: the `active` set that builds L (per security, over all its listings; a stale current listing no longer makes the security active, another listing active in W still does, rule 2), which means the last-bar read moves before L is built; the `no_bar_at_t` verdict (never for a stale listing); the side-category filter, which reads `active` and so excludes a stale security from all three; and `SurvivorshipGap`, which gains `stale_listings` (security id, last bar, sessions dark) and its `settings` echo gains the new key. `health.py` and the health page gain the category's count and list. Nothing else reads the key.

### Not chosen

- **Fixing every Form 25 first** (scenarios A to C alone): the spike's simulated holdout max after all three was 0.056, above the target, and each step is a master change with its own conflicts; the owner chose the rule as the one lever and the master fixes as correctness work under #1199.
- **A calendar-day window** (the spike's 90 days): a count in sessions is what the module already does for `truncated_tail` and what CLAUDE.md requires (a trading calendar, never weekdays).
- **Dropping stale names from L silently**: the reported `stale_listings` category is what keeps the rule honest.
