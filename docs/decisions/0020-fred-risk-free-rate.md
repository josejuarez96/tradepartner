# 0020. FRED/ALFRED is the source of a stored, point-in-time T-bill rate (DTB3), the risk-free rate the metrics read

**Status:** Proposed (accepted by the owner's merge of this PR; owner approval of the direction 2026-10-11)  ·  **Date:** 2026-10-11  ·  **Issue:** #1498

## Context

Every Sharpe ratio the system stores is computed against `metrics.risk_free_rate`, a constant 0.0 (`MetricsConfig`, backtest spec req 7 and "Config keys": "ADR 0005 measures against benchmarks, not cash"). Since March 2022 the 3-month T-bill has paid 1 to 5.5% a year, so a raw Sharpe of 0.3 under the constant is a Sharpe near zero against cash. The headline deflated Sharpe is on the `excess_spy` basis (backtest spec open question 7), which a cash rate does not touch, so nothing promoted so far rests on the constant; but the raw Sharpe is stored, shown and fed to V.

Two reports now need a T-bill series the store does not hold. The [B11 report](../research/2026-10-10-b11-beta-alpha-split.md) (#1461) fetched FRED's `DTB3` by hand on 2026-10-10 to measure every logged trial's alpha against bills, and says B12 "should store a proxy with `known_at` ... not fetch it ad hoc"; the [beyond-long-only report](../research/2026-10-10-beyond-long-only.md), step 0, says the same, and the shorting candidates that follow it (financing, a hedged line against bills, a cash benchmark for market-neutral books) all read a rate. A hedged or market-neutral book measured against a constant zero would look 4 to 5 pp/yr better than it is.

The [research-program page](../ways-of-working/research-program.md) ("When a claim would change the tool itself ... new data to ingest ... it goes to an ADR") and [development-process.md](../ways-of-working/development-process.md) ("Always requires an ADR, whatever the size: choosing a ... data source") require a decision record for a new external source. The owner approved the direction on 2026-10-11 (orchestrator 189cc6ad, relayed on #1498) and put his `FRED_API_KEY` in `.env`.

**What a read-only probe found on 2026-10-11** (recorded on #1498):

- FRED series `DTB3`, "3-Month Treasury Bill Secondary Market Rate, Discount Basis", daily, percent, not seasonally adjusted; source the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, release H.15 Selected Interest Rates.
- ALFRED holds vintages of it from 2005-06-28: 5,128 vintage dates, one per business day from 2016-01-04. FRED gives **dates only**, for observations and for vintages; no time of day.
- As of real-time date 2022-03-15 the latest observation was dated 2022-03-14: a one-business-day posting lag. The Board posts H.15 "daily Monday through Friday at 4:15pm" (Eastern), with the previous business day's rates; FRED's series page showed "Last Updated: 2026-10-09 3:16 PM CDT" with the latest observation 2026-10-08, the same lag.
- The values as known on 2022-03-15 equal today's values for the same dates: **DTB3 is not revised**. The vintage machinery still matters, because the rule below stamps every row from its vintage date, so a revision, should one ever come, is a new row with its own `known_at` and never a back-dated one.
- The API: `fred/series/observations` with `realtime_start`/`realtime_end`, `output_type` 1 to 4, `vintage_dates`; `limit` up to 100,000 observations per request; at most 2000 vintage dates per request for JSON and XML (1000 for CSV and XLSX; 450 and 225 for output type 2 on a daily series). One request with a real-time window and `output_type=1` returns every (observation, value) with the real-time period in which that value was current, which is exactly the store's revision model. The whole DTB3 history from 2016 is about 2,700 rows: one request a backfill, one a day after that. FRED's API pages publish no numeric request ceiling; the terms say the Bank "may impose or adjust the limit on the amount of bandwidth you may use or the number of transactions you may send or receive through the FRED® API".

**FRED's terms** (read 2026-10-11; [API terms](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/docs/api/terms_of_use.html), [legal terms](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/legal/); the API terms incorporate the legal terms). The lines that bear on a local store and a public repository, quoted:

- Redistribution (same section): "Except as expressly provided in these Terms of Use, FRED® Content may not be modified, duplicated, copied, distributed, framed, reproduced, rebranded, republished, uploaded, downloaded, scraped, displayed, posted, transmitted on any Internet, Intranet or Extranet site, nor may the FRED® Content be incorporated in any other database or compilation or sold in any form or by any means, in whole or in part, without the Bank's prior written permission".
- Storage (legal terms, the "You may not, without the Bank's prior written consent" list, item (p)): "Store, cache, or archive any portion of the FRED® Services or FRED® Content; provide any stored, cached, or archived portion of the FRED® Services or FRED® Content to any third party; or incorporate any FRED® Content in any database, compilation, archive, cache, or other medium."
- Third-party series (API terms, "Property Rights"): "Data series available through the FRED® API, may be owned by third parties and subject to copyright restrictions. ... Before using data series owned by third parties for anything other than your own personal use, you must contact the data owner to obtain permission."
- Attribution (API terms, "Requirements"): "Place the following notice prominently on your application: 'This product uses the FRED® API but is not endorsed or certified by the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis.'"

Two of these pull against each other: the licence grants a personal, non-commercial download, and item (p) forbids storing without consent. The data itself is the Board of Governors' H.15 release, a work of the United States government (17 U.S.C. §105) that the Board also serves from its own [Data Download Program](https://www.federalreserve.gov/datadownload/) under no such terms, but without vintages. FRED adds the vintages and the convenient API; it owns neither the numbers nor a copyright in them. The owner of the series is the Board, not a third-party licensor, so the third-party permission clause does not apply to DTB3. That point settles the third-party clause only: item (p) is a term of the contract the key accepts, and no copyright argument lifts it. The decision below rests on the owner accepting that risk (Consequences (a)), not on the clause being void.

## Options considered

1. **FRED/ALFRED `DTB3` through the API, stored point-in-time** (this ADR). Pro: vintages from 2005 give a real `known_at` per value, which no other free source does; one request a day; the same discount-basis quote the B11 report used; free. Con: the storage clause above, read against the personal-use licence; the Bank can change or withdraw the API; FRED's day is Central time and its dates carry no time, so the stamp is a rule, not a timestamp.
2. **The Board's H.15 Data Download Program directly.** Pro: public domain, no terms; the same numbers. Con: no vintages at all, so `known_at` would rest on the posting rule alone with no way to detect a late posting or a revision; a CSV/XML package format with its own parser; nothing a backfill can use to prove when a value appeared. Kept as the **fallback** if FRED's terms or availability change: the table and the stamping rule below are source-agnostic, and only the adapter changes.
3. **The Treasury's Daily Treasury Bill Rates** (treasury.gov). Pro: public domain; the primary quotes. Con: no vintages; posting time not documented; a second format.
4. **A T-bill ETF's bars from Alpaca SIP** (BIL from 2007, SGOV from 2020), the beyond-long-only report's other option. Pro: the same price source as everything else, stamped as bars. Con: a return series, not a rate, so it serves a cash benchmark but not financing, hedging or a yield-based model; carries an expense ratio and price noise; the store holds no such bars and the universe excludes funds, so it would need benchmark-style seeding and a dividend-correct total return; SGOV's history is too short and BIL's expense ratio understates bills by about 14 bp a year.
5. **Kenneth French's `RF` series.** Pro: the academic convention (one-month bill, monthly). Con: monthly only; revised and republished without vintages; a derivative of a commercial series; downloads, not an API.
6. **Keep the constant.** Pro: nothing to build. Con: every raw Sharpe since 2022 is overstated by the bill yield, and the hedged and market-neutral work the owner has asked for cannot be measured honestly.

## Decision

We will add **FRED/ALFRED as a `RateSource`** behind an adapter, store the **`DTB3`** series point-in-time in a new `rates` table, and let the backtest metrics read a per-period risk-free series from it, with the constant kept as a frozen fallback. The rules are in the [data-foundation spec amendment of 2026-10-11](../specs/data-foundation.md#amendment-2026-10-11-1498-the-rates-table-and-the-fred-rate-source) and the [backtest spec amendment of the same date](../specs/backtest.md#amendment-2026-10-11-1498-the-risk-free-series-from-the-store); the tasks are the data-foundation plan's T169 to T170d. What this ADR fixes, because it is costly to change once rows are loaded:

1. **One table, source-agnostic.** `rates(series, observation_date, value, vintage_date)` plus the four common columns. The adapter interface is `RateSource.observations(series, realtime_start, observation_start)`; FRED is its first adapter, the fixture its second, and the H.15 download (option 2) is the named fallback adapter if FRED goes away: it writes its own `source`, and, having no vintage, a derived `vintage_date` (the first business day after the observation, the release that first carried it) that its docstring declares, under ADR 0003 rule 2's clause for a source with no real `known_at`; the cost is that a late posting cannot be seen. No code outside the adapter knows FRED's field names.
2. **The stamping rule is the store's date-only rule.** FRED gives a vintage date and no time; H.15 posts at 16:15 Eastern, after that day's close. A row's `known_at` is the **close of the first XNYS session strictly after its vintage date**, the same rule data-foundation req 5 applies to a corporate action known by date only. Why not the end of the vintage day in New York time: FRED's day boundary is Central time and its posting hour is not in the data, so a stamp at New York midnight is a second rule that can be early by an hour in principle. Why not the next session's open: no consumer of this table reads between an open and a close (the metrics read at a rebalance close; the paper path does not read it), so the open and the close of the next session are the same stamp for every read the system makes, and the close is the rule the store already has. The cost is one session: a rate published at 16:15 on day D+1 for day D is readable from close(D+2), not from close(D+1). For a series that moves by basis points a week, the Sharpe cannot tell.
3. **Vintages are revisions.** A later vintage with a different value for the same observation date is a new row with its own `known_at`; a vintage that repeats the stored value writes nothing. Rows are never updated or back-dated. DTB3 is not revised today; the rule costs nothing and makes a future revision honest.
4. **The constant stays and nothing moves retroactively.** `metrics.risk_free_rate` keeps its meaning; a new frozen key `metrics.risk_free_source` (`constant` or `store`) enters the frozen-key table at `constant`, so every existing registration, H1 and its open paper window included, computes exactly what it computes today. The flip of the live default to `store` happens in the owner's backfill task, after the real store holds the series, as T78 flipped `edgar.statement_facts_enabled`.
5. **Terms.** The rows are the owner's personal, non-commercial copy under the licence quoted above, as ADR 0009 holds Alpaca's bars under Alpaca's personal-use terms. Nothing is redistributed: no `rates` row, fixture recorded from FRED, or FRED payload enters the public repository (fixtures are synthetic, generated like the rest of the fixture universe); the Parquet export and backups stay private, as they are for Alpaca data. The attribution notice above is placed on the health page's rates block and in the README's data-sources line. The owner accepts item (p) as a residual risk (Consequences); if the Bank objects or the API is withdrawn, the fallback is option 2 and the store keeps its stamping rule. **Termination** ("destroy all copies"): the FRED-sourced rows, and every export and backup holding them, are deleted under a named data release, the one deletion the table allows; the release's record lists the trials whose frozen set reads `store`, which then no longer reproduce from the store, and the owner decides their rerun (data-foundation amendment, "Terms").
6. **Budget.** USD 0, unchanged (ADR 0009 rule 1): the API is free with a registered key.

## Consequences

- Good: a real cash rate with a `known_at` behind every Sharpe from the flip on; the B12 decomposition, the hedged line and any market-neutral benchmark read one table through the as-of API instead of an ad-hoc fetch; a revision, if FRED ever makes one, is a row the health page counts, never a silent rewrite.
- Bad / accepted risks: (a) FRED's storage clause, read against its personal-use licence; the owner accepts a private copy for his own research, and the Board's own download is the fallback with no vintages. (b) The raw Sharpe of a trial registered after the flip is not comparable with one registered before it, and both feed the family's V; the `excess_spy` basis, the headline, is unaffected. (c) The stamp is a rule from a date, not an observed time; a day on which the Board posts late (after a later session's close) would make the rule early for that one value, which the data cannot show; H.15's daily schedule makes this rare and the value at stake is one day of a bill yield. (d) A new frozen `metrics.*` key costs a frozen-key table entry and one sentence in the strategy-lab spec, as every frozen key does. (e) The table sits outside the data vintage, so a row that lands with a past `known_at` after a `store` trial ran (a revision, a refetched hole, a backfill re-run, or the termination deletion above) does not mark that trial stale; health counts such rows and the owner decides the reruns. (f) Termination of the key means deleting the rows, which breaks the store's "never delete" rule once, under a release, and leaves the `store` trials irreproducible from the store (their exports go with the rows) until the fallback source is loaded.
- Reversibility: cheap. The adapter is one module; the table is additive (no existing row changes); dropping the series is a config change (`metrics.risk_free_source = constant`) that no registration before the flip would notice. What cannot be undone is a trial registered under `store` after the flip: it stays a trial counted in N, as every trial does.
- Revisit if: FRED changes its terms or withdraws the API or the `DTB3` vintages (switch the adapter to option 2); the health page shows a DTB3 revision (decide whether the vintage rule needs more than the next-session close); a shorting or financing model needs a second series (a new series is a config change under this ADR, a new source is a new ADR); the owner wants the engine's `backtest.cash_rate` to read the same table (out of scope here: the engine refuses a non-zero cash rate today).
